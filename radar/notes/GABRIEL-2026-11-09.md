# Gabriel India — Radar Deep Dive

Private note for the multibagger radar. Not published, not deployable, not part of the website. One company, one Screener page (consolidated), paced. Every figure is read off an exchange filing, computed from the exchange's own bhavcopy, or labelled as an estimate.

**The one-line case.** Gabriel spent 2026 converting itself from an 8–9% margin shock-absorber maker into a group holding company for braking, steering, ADAS and seating — buying two promoter-owned businesses with ₹1,881 Cr of its own shares and ₹1,000 Cr of 8.15% debt. The assets bought are better than the asset doing the buying (Mando earns 25.7% on capital against Gabriel's 25.6% on a much lower margin base, and was acquired at 21.5x earnings against Gabriel's own 65x). The transformation is rational and earnings-accretive. The price already pays for it: 65x trailing, 107x on the parent's own operations, eleven times book, with H1 margins down 90bps.

## 2. What the Company Does

Gabriel is the flagship listed company of the ANAND Group and, since 2026, effectively the group's automotive holding vehicle. The legacy business is ride control — shock absorbers, struts, front forks — with **88% of the Indian commercial-vehicle market, 33% of 2W/3W, 61% of electric 2W and 20% of passenger vehicles** (company presentation, H1 FY27). It sells to every Indian OEM, runs an aftermarket network of 873 business partners and 30,000 retailers, and exports 3% of revenue.

The mix, H1 FY27, standalone: 2W/3W 66% of sales, passenger cars 21%, commercial vehicles plus railways 12%, trading 1%. OEM channel 91%, replacement 9%.

What changed in 2026 is the perimeter. The company now consolidates or equity-accounts nine entities:

| Entity | Stake | Business | Basis |
|---|---|---|---|
| Inalfa Gabriel Sunroof Systems (IGSS) | 100% (proposed 65%) | Sunroofs | Subsidiary |
| Gabriel Europe Engineering Centre | 100% | R&D, Belgium | Subsidiary |
| Anand CY Myutec (ACYM) | 76.1% | Synchroniser rings, aluminium forgings | Subsidiary |
| Jinhap Gabriel (JGAIPL) | 51% | Fasteners, precision forgings | Subsidiary |
| Dana Anand India (DAIPL) | 25.1% | Axles, driveshafts | Associate |
| Henkel Anand India (HAIPL) | 49% | Body-in-white, NVH | Associate |
| SK Enmove Gabriel | 49% | Lubricants and fluids | Associate |
| **HL Mando Anand** | **28.99%** | **Braking, steering, suspension** | **Associate, from 11 Sep 2026** |
| **HL Klemove India** | **29.99%** | **ADAS radar, cameras, AVAS** | **Associate, from 25 Aug 2026** |

Plus a 50%-less-one-share JV with FORVIA's Faurecia for passenger-vehicle seat frames and complete seats, incorporated 19 Oct 2026, closing targeted by 31 Dec 2026.

Two structural facts matter more than the product list.

**The economics now arrive through two doors.** Consolidated revenue is Gabriel's own factories plus four subsidiaries. But a third of pretax profit arrives below the operating line as share of profit from associates — ₹91 Cr in H1 FY27, ₹158 Cr in FY26. That income carries no revenue and no EBITDA, so EBITDA margin understates the group's true profitability, and any "core P/E" that strips it out overstates how expensive the parent is. Both numbers are shown in §10.

**The acquisitions were bought from the promoter, paid for with shares and debt.** On 11 Sep 2026 Gabriel issued **1,44,04,204 shares at ₹1,305.89** — ₹1,881.03 Cr — to Asia Investments Pvt Ltd, its own promoter, as part of the ₹2,231 Cr purchase of a 28.99% stake in HL Mando Anand; the balance ₹350 Cr was cash. On 25 Aug 2026 it bought 37,844,999 shares of HL Klemove India for **USD 98.44 mn** (about ₹860 Cr at ₹87.3/USD, an assumed rate). The cash came from **₹1,000 Cr of unsecured NCDs at 8.15% for three years**, allotted 10 Sep 2026.

The arithmetic of that is worth holding in mind: Gabriel paid ₹2,231 Cr for 28.99% of a business earning ₹358 Cr, i.e. **21.5x earnings and 13.1x EBITDA**, using shares the market valued at roughly 74x FY26 reported earnings. That is accretive to Gabriel's EPS — and it is also the single largest related-party transaction in the company's history, approved by shareholders on 19 Aug 2026 on KPMG and BDO valuations.

## 3. Why It Cleared the Gates

The brief calls them eight. `scripts/radar-scorecard.py` defines **seven** mechanical gates; the eighth is the judgement call — whether a second engine exists — and it is recorded as unknown rather than passed.

The ledger says five. That was true on the 26 October snapshot. On today's numbers it is **four**, and the reason is worth stating plainly: the September financing moved two of the seven gates from pass to fail.

| Gate | Test | GABRIEL today | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| Headroom | Market cap under ₹25,000 Cr | **₹26,345 Cr** on 19.163 Cr shares (Screener's ₹24,732 Cr uses the old share count) | **Fail** |

| Valuation | P/E ≤ 1.5x industry, or ≤ 35 | **64.9x vs industry 28.4x** | **Fail** |

Three fails is a different company from the one that entered the ledger. The headroom gate fails because the stock is up 36% year-to-date and because the Mando shares added 7.5% to the count; the balance gate fails because the acquisition was part debt-funded; the valuation gate fails on its own terms and would fail at any price above ₹1,000.

The eighth gate — does a second engine exist — is the one that passes most clearly. Ride control is a mature, 9% margin business growing with vehicle production. Braking and steering (Mando, ₹5,886 Cr revenue, 10.5% EBITDA, 25.7% ROCE) and ADAS (Klemove, ₹1,049 Cr revenue, 12.3% adjusted EBITDA margin) are separate engines with higher content per vehicle and a regulatory tailwind. That is the whole investment case, and it is why the note is a WATCH rather than a pass.

Four things in these tables matter more than the growth rate.

**One accounting warning on that "other income" row.** Screener folds the share of profit from equity-method investees into Other Income in the quarterly columns — ₹48.3 Cr of Q2's ₹55 Cr is associate profit, not treasury income — but not in the FY26 annual column, where the consolidated statement shows associate profit of ₹158 Cr separately. The line is therefore not comparable between the two series, and TTM (₹149 Cr) is lower than the associate income alone (₹175 Cr) because Q3 FY26 carried a small negative other-income charge. The derived "core" numbers in §10 use PBT minus the filing's associate line, not this row.

**Margins peaked in FY26 and are now going backwards.** Standalone EBITDA margin went 7.2% → 8.8% → 8.9% → 9.2%, then printed **8.3% in H1 FY27 and 8.2% in Q2**. Consolidated is the same story: 9.6% in H1 FY26 to 8.7% in H1 FY27. Management's explanation is a commodity spike from the West Asia crisis, a lag in pass-through to OEMs, and the higher-revenue/lower-margin mix from the merged fluids business (Anchemco, whose raw material is urea). That is a coherent explanation. It is also the second consecutive year in which the margin has failed to hold its high.

**Working capital has quietly doubled as a share of revenue.** 18 days in FY23 to 32 days in September 2026. On ₹5,593 Cr of TTM revenue, 14 extra days is roughly **₹215 Cr of additional cash locked in the cycle** — and it is the reason CFO (₹110 Cr in H1, per Motilal Oswal) is now well below EBITDA.

**The balance sheet changed character in one quarter.** Borrowings went from ₹148 Cr in March 2026 to ₹1,269 Cr in September, and equity attributable to the parent from ₹1,908 Cr to ₹2,259 Cr (plus ₹1,881 Cr of shares issued, offset by ₹350 Cr of cash paid and the accounting for the acquisition). Net debt of ₹1,088 Cr is 4.1% of market cap — small — but the interest bill is now ₹82 Cr a year and rising, against ₹14 Cr in FY26.

**Cash conversion is genuinely good in the core.** CFO/EBITDA of 97% in FY26, free cash flow positive at ₹108 Cr, and a five-year record that has never printed negative operating cash. The FCF outflow of ₹1,050 Cr in H1 FY27 is acquisitions, not operations. That distinction matters and is the difference between a growth burn and a distress burn.

The EPS line is the trap in this quarter. Consolidated PAT rose 15.4%, but EPS fell 6.5% year-on-year because the share count went from 14.36 Cr to 19.16 Cr. Every per-share number in 2026 is distorted by dilution; the absolute rupee numbers are not.

**The margin story is the whole quarter.** Revenue grew 23%, EBITDA 12%, PBT 14% — a classic case of a company growing its top line faster than its profit because input costs rose faster than prices. Motilal Oswal attributes it to the West Asia crisis commodity spike and the lag in pass-through; management says the same and guided recovery over the coming quarters. Q2 margin did improve 10bps sequentially, which is the first sequential improvement in three quarters.

**Associate income is now a third of pretax profit.** ₹48.3 Cr of the ₹148.2 Cr Q2 PBT came from Dana, Henkel, SK Enmove and — for about three weeks — Mando. That is 32.6%, up from 26.6% a year ago. From Q3 the Mando contribution becomes a full quarter: 28.99% of ₹358 Cr annual PAT is ₹104 Cr, or roughly ₹26 Cr a quarter, so associate income should step up to **₹70 Cr or more** in Q3. That step-up is the single most testable part of the bull case, and it is two months away.

**Segment detail, H1 FY27 (standalone, company presentation):** 2W/3W sales +25.4% against industry production +19.5%; passenger vehicles **+7.3% against industry +21%**; commercial vehicles +12.8% against industry +24.7%. The 2W/3W franchise — two thirds of revenue — is taking share. The PV business is losing it.

Management's answer on PV, from the transcript: *"the lack of growth is primarily on account of the passenger car, the smaller cars… the models where we are present, unfortunately, have not done so well as compared to the models where we have not been there. So it is more of a mix issue."* That is honest and also an admission that the PV book is a model-concentration bet, not a market-share franchise.

**Guidance is qualitative, deliberately.** Management gave no FY27 revenue or margin number. What it did give: consolidated capex of **₹1,000–1,500 Cr a year** for the next three years, and a Vision 2030 target of **₹50,000 Cr of revenue** — 8x the FY27 run-rate, in four years, with no year-by-year path. Treat the ₹50,000 Cr as an ambition, not guidance. The capex number is the one to underwrite, because it is 2x current EBITDA.

**Sunroofs are the soft spot inside the new portfolio.** IGSS revenue fell 12.7% in H1 to ₹199 Cr and PAT fell 71% to ₹4.6 Cr, after a fire at a Hyundai supplier derailed the programme the sunroofs were built for. Management expects the Bayon launch and the AI4 programme next year to fix it. At 4% of revenue it does not move the consolidated number, but it is a reminder that the new segments are not yet additive to margin.

**The percentage-point moves are mostly dilution, not selling.** The share count rose **33% in six months** — 14.36 Cr at March 2026 to 19.16 Cr at September, of which 3.36 Cr came in Q1 FY27 for the merged ANAND automotive undertaking and 1.44 Cr in September for Mando. In absolute terms:

- FIIs held 0.95 Cr shares in March and 1.13 Cr in September — **they added about 20%**, even as their percentage fell.
- DIIs held 2.34 Cr in March and 2.31 Cr in September — **down 1%**, not the 26% reduction the percentage implies.
- Public holding fell from 3.17 Cr to 3.01 Cr shares — a genuine but modest 5% reduction.

So the correct reading is: the promoter took 11.3 percentage points of the company in exchange for two group businesses, minority holders were diluted but did not flee, and the institutional register was broadly stable in absolute share terms. Shareholder count fell 5.6% from the March peak, which is the ordinary churn of a stock that went up 100% and then spent three months going sideways.

**The promoter increase is not a buy signal.** Screener lists "promoter holding has increased by 2.74% over last quarter" as a **pro**. It is a share issuance for an asset purchase, not insider accumulation. The playbook's own warning applies: a promoter percentage that rises through preferential allotment tells you nothing about confidence and everything about dilution.

## 11. Bull Case

1. **The acquisitions are earnings-accretive at the transaction price, and the maths is public.** Mando was bought at 21.5x PAT and 13.1x EBITDA; Gabriel's own shares were issued at ₹1,305.89, or about 74x FY26 reported earnings. Paying with expensive paper for cheap earnings adds roughly ₹104 Cr of PAT against 7.5% dilution — about **16% EPS accretion** even before any growth. That is the cheapest capital raise the company could have done.
2. **Mando is a genuinely good business.** ₹5,886 Cr of revenue, ₹588 Cr of EBITDA (10.5%, 10.9% adjusted), ₹358 Cr of PAT, **25.7% ROCE and 19.5% ROE**, three plants near Chennai, 4,600 employees, and a customer base moving beyond Hyundai/Kia (60–65% of revenue) into Tata, Maruti and Mahindra. It is a brake-and-steering franchise in a market where content per vehicle rises with every safety regulation.
3. **ADAS is a regulated growth market and Klemove is inside it.** India's first localised ADAS radar and smart-camera lines, 87% OEM revenue, ₹1,049 Cr of sales at a 12.3% adjusted EBITDA margin. The draft ADAS mandate extends to commercial vehicles and 2W/3W by 2032; management says it is doing product mapping across all four segments.
4. **The core franchise is close to unassailable where it matters.** 88% of CV, 61% of electric 2W, 33% of 2W/3W; two thirds of revenue comes from segments where Gabriel is the leader and where H1 growth (25.4%) beat industry production (19.5%).
5. **The balance sheet can carry the plan.** Net debt ₹1,088 Cr is 4.1% of market cap, CFO/EBITDA is 97%, the ₹1,000 Cr NCD is fixed at 8.15% for three years, and there is ₹272 Cr of cash and current investments. Dividend policy survives (₹2.15 interim).
6. **The seating JV is an option on the interior, not just the seat.** FORVIA brings global seat-frame technology, ANAND brings the OEM relationships; the JV starts with a ₹150 Cr revenue base at ~10% margin and a target of 10% Indian seating share in five years. Gabriel's cash commitment is only ₹50 Cr.

## 12. Bear Case

1. **65x trailing and 54x EV/EBITDA for a 9% margin component maker is a price that requires everything to work.** The industry trades at 27x, Nifty Auto at 32x. Even on my FY28 estimate the stock is 45x — for a business that grew consolidated PAT 9.4% in H1.
2. **Margins are going the wrong way at exactly the moment the multiple expanded.** 9.6% → 8.7% consolidated, 9.2% → 8.3% standalone, with management citing commodity spikes and pass-through lags. Input-cost pass-through recovers cost, never margin, so a revenue mix shifting toward traded and fluids business structurally dilutes the percentage. The 9%+ margin that justifies the multiple is not in the current numbers.
3. **Both acquisitions were bought from the promoter, paid in stock.** ₹1,881 Cr of shares went to Asia Investments for 28.99% of Mando; the ANAND automotive undertaking came in earlier in 2026 for another 3.36 Cr shares. Each step was valued by an independent firm and approved by minorities, and the Mando price is defensible. The pattern is still the pattern: the listed company is the group's exit route, and minority holders own 33.7% of a company whose asset base is being set by related-party transactions.
4. **The parent's own business lost PV share.** Passenger-vehicle sales grew 7.3% against industry production of 21% in H1 — a 14-point gap that management attributes to model mix. Two thirds of revenue is in the segments that are winning, but the PV book is the one with the highest content per vehicle and it is shrinking in relative terms.
5. **Klemove is very expensive for what it currently earns.** 29.99% for USD 98.44 mn values the whole company at roughly ₹2,868 Cr — **22x adjusted EBITDA and about 260x reported PAT of ₹11 Cr**, on an ROE of 5.9% and an ROCE of 6.6%, below the cost of capital. It is part-funded by debt at 8.15%: the ₹1,000 Cr NCD costs ₹82 Cr a year in interest, against the ₹3 Cr that Klemove's ₹11 Cr PAT gives Gabriel at a 30% share. That only works if ADAS scales hard, and the company has not put a number on when.
6. **Free cash flow is negative and will stay negative.** H1 free cash outflow ₹1,050 Cr, capex guided at ₹1,000–1,500 Cr a year against ₹550 Cr of EBITDA, working capital at 32 days and rising. The dividend and the acquisitions are being funded by debt and equity, not by the business.
7. **The tape says distribution.** +14% on 11.5% delivery, +2.6% on ₹398 Cr of turnover and 10.3% delivery, up/down volume ratio 1.02, DIIs down 1% in absolute share terms and 4.2pp in percentage, and a stock 14% below its high with MACD negative and RSI below 50.

## 13. Kill Criteria

Observable within two quarters. Any one of these ends the thesis as written.

1. **Consolidated EBITDA margin below 8.5% in Q3 or Q4 FY27.** The entire forward valuation assumes a return to 9%+; a third consecutive sub-9% half makes the FY29 EPS of ₹47.6 that brokers are paying 35x for arithmetically unreachable.
2. **Share of profit from associates below ₹55 Cr in Q3 FY27.** With a full Mando quarter it should print ₹70 Cr or more. A number below ₹55 Cr means either the Mando stake is not earning what the presentation says or the other associates are deteriorating, and the ₹2,231 Cr purchase price loses its justification.
3. **Passenger-vehicle segment growth below industry for a second consecutive half.** PV is 21% of revenue and the highest-content slice; two halves of underperformance makes it structural rather than model mix.
4. **Net debt above ₹2,000 Cr, or debt/equity above 0.8.** The balance gate has already failed once at 0.60; failing it harder turns a growth story into a leverage story.
5. **A further promoter-asset purchase paid in shares without a fresh independent valuation.** One related-party injection is a strategy; two is a pattern; three without scrutiny is a governance problem.
6. **A close below ₹1,268**, the September low and the bottom of the three-month range. Below it the 100-EMA at ₹1,334 is already lost and the next reference is the 200-EMA at ₹1,231.

## 17. Verdict

**WATCH — the transformation is real and rational; the price has already paid for it.**

Gabriel is doing the right thing. It is moving from a 9% margin ride-control business into brakes, steering, ADAS and seating — categories with two to five times the content per vehicle, better returns, and a regulatory tailwind. It bought Mando at 21.5x earnings using shares the market prices at 73x, which is the single smartest piece of capital allocation in the company's recent history, and the acquired businesses earn 19.5% and 25.7% on equity and capital.

What it is not doing is growing profit at the rate the price implies. H1 PAT grew 9.4%. EBITDA margin fell 90bps to 8.7%. Working capital went from 18 days to 32 over four years. Free cash flow was negative ₹1,050 Cr in the half, capex is guided at 2x EBITDA for three years, and a third of profit now arrives as associate income that the parent does not control.

At ₹1,374.80 the stock is 64.9x trailing, 48x my FY27 estimate, 45x FY28, 11.7x book and 54x EV/EBITDA. Three of the radar's seven gates now fail — headroom, balance and valuation — and two of those fails were created by the September financing itself. The 25-point score is 17.0 with a critical fail on breakout confirmation, and the largest volume days of the last six months carried 10–12% delivery, which is the opposite of accumulation.

The next two quarters resolve it, and both tests are binary. **Q3 needs associate income above ₹70 Cr** (a full Mando quarter) **and an EBITDA margin back above 9%.** If both land, the ₹47.6 EPS that brokers are paying 35x for stops looking like a stretch, FY28 earnings move toward ₹650 Cr, the forward multiple falls into the low 40s, and a break above ₹1,600 becomes a buy rather than a chase. If either fails, the stock is a 45x cyclical with a 9% margin and a promoter who has been paid ₹1,881 Cr of paper for two group companies — and the September low of ₹1,268 is the level that matters.

**What I would do:** nothing at ₹1,375. Set alerts at **₹1,600** (breakout, needs 2x volume and 40% delivery) and at **₹1,334** (100-EMA, first pullback shelf). Take whichever arrives, size at no more than 5% of portfolio, and re-underwrite after Q3 against the six kill criteria — of which the associate-income test is the one that matters most.

**Radar disposition:** mark `analysed` with outcome `watch`. The business is better than it was twelve months ago; the entry is not.
