# Marksans Pharma — Radar Deep Dive

Private note for the multibagger radar. Not published, not deployable, not part of the website. One company, one Screener page (consolidated), paced. Every figure below is read off an exchange filing, computed from the exchange's own bhavcopy, or labelled as an estimate.

**The one-line case.** Marksans is the cheapest business in its own peer table at 25.0x trailing earnings while earning a higher return on capital than the median (18.8% ROCE against 14.9%) and growing profit four times faster (Q2 PAT +44.8% against a peer median of +33.7%) — a combination that normally commands a premium, not a 25% discount. The record September quarter was driven by UK and Europe (+64.9%) rather than the United States (+8.7%), which is the point: this is no longer a single-market generics exporter. What stops it being a buy today is the tape and the guidance, in that order: the stock is 11.9% below its September high, sits below all four of its short moving averages with an ADX of 14, and management has just walked second-half gross margin down to 55-56% from the 58.5% it printed in Q2 because freight has doubled and the cheap inventory is gone.

## 2. What the Company Does

Marksans is a Mumbai-headquartered, Goa-manufactured **consumer healthcare and generics maker that sells almost entirely outside India**. It began life in 2001 as a Glenmark subsidiary, was spun out, and has spent twenty years buying small front-end distribution businesses in regulated markets so that it owns the label rather than supplying someone else's.

**The business is a store-brand OTC manufacturer.** 80% of FY26 revenue was OTC, 20% prescription. Of the OTC book, roughly 85% is **store brands** — private label for large retailers in the US and UK, where the retailer's name is on the box and Marksans' plant is behind it — and 15% is its own labels. The therapeutic mix is concentrated exactly where private label works: pain management ₹1,311 Cr (44% of FY26 revenue), cough and cold ₹461 Cr (16%), gastrointestinal ₹384 Cr (13%), cardiovascular ₹183 Cr, anti-allergic ₹172 Cr, CNS ₹172 Cr.

**Where the money comes from, Q2 FY27:**

|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| US & North America | **420.9** | +8.7% | +11.6% | 44.8% | Time-Cap Laboratories (Farmingdale, NY) plus India-made exports; store-brand OTC and a smaller Rx book |
| UK & Europe | **404.6** | +64.9% | +13.7% | 43.0% | Relonchem and Bell, Sons & Co; now also QliniQ (Netherlands) and ABCnow (Germany) |

**Four plants, 26 bn units of capacity.** Goa Unit 1 is the old workhorse — 2.4 bn softgel and hard capsules plus 6 bn tablets, fully automated, 18,000 sq m, and the reason the company can absorb retailer volume at a low unit cost. Goa Unit 2 was bought from Teva in 2023, spans 47,597 sq m, and is the growth option: it is being fitted to make tablets, ointments, liquids and creams with a plan to double Indian output to 8 bn units, taking group capacity toward the 16 bn-unit Indian target. Southport in the UK makes liquids, ointments and sachets (2 bn bottles, 1 bn tubes, 1 bn sachets). Farmingdale in the US makes 6 bn tablets and capsules and is the reason "Made in the USA" is available to a US retailer worried about tariffs.

**The pipeline is the growth engine and it is filed, not promised.** 50+ ANDAs/MAs approved, 30+ filed and awaiting approval, 200+ products in the pipeline, 350+ scientists across four R&D centres (Navi Mumbai, Goa, Southport, Farmingdale). The UK alone has 24 products awaiting approval as of 31 March 2026 with 200+ to be filed over four years; the US launched 112 new SKUs in FY26 with 51 in the pipeline; Canada filed 14 products in FY26 and has begun commercial approvals. R&D has run at 2.5-3.0% of revenue — low by Indian pharma standards, and deliberate: this is a formulations business competing on cost and speed to shelf, not a risk-taking innovator.

**Three structural facts that matter more than the product list.**

**Growth in FY27 is partly bought, and the market has been told exactly how much.** QliniQ B.V. (Netherlands, €7.50 mn) and ABCnow GmbH (Germany, €1.10 mn) were acquired in H1 for about ₹95 Cr and consolidated from Q1 FY27. QliniQ contributed ~₹40 Cr in Q2 and ~₹84 Cr in H1. Strip it out and Q2 revenue growth was 25%, and UK/Europe still grew 48.6% organically. That is the cleanest sentence in the whole disclosure: the acquisition is a kicker, not the story.

**Europe is now a platform, not an export destination.** Ireland (Marksans Pharma Europe Ltd — filings started, warehouse and distribution licensing in progress), Germany (Marksans GmbH plus ABCnow), the Netherlands (QliniQ), Canada (incorporated, initial approvals received). Management was explicit that these are country-by-country assets, not a pan-European operation: *"When you talk of QliniQ, it is Netherlands; you talk of ABCnow, it's Germany. These markets do not represent the whole of Europe."* The stated European revenue ambition is ₹1,000 Cr in five years, confirmed on the call.

**A fifth of profit arrives below the operating line if you count other income as profit — and it is mostly currency.** Other income was ₹82 Cr in FY26 and ₹99 Cr TTM. The notes to the accounts disclose net foreign-exchange gains (including forwards) of ₹47.4 Cr in FY26, ₹15.6 Cr in H1 FY27 and ₹5.1 Cr in Q2 alone. This is a real but volatile earnings stream from a company that earns in dollars, pounds and euros and reports in rupees, and it is the single largest adjustment needed before comparing its P/E to a domestic peer's.

## 3. Why It Cleared the Gates

The brief calls them eight. `scripts/radar-scorecard.py` defines **seven** mechanical gates; the eighth is the judgement call — does a second engine exist — and it is recorded rather than passed. Marksans cleared all seven, the only name in this week's cohort to do so (Quess failed durability). What each gate actually saw:

| Gate | Test | MARKSANS | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| Headroom | Market cap under ₹25,000 Cr | ₹14,012 Cr | **Pass** |
| Reinvestment | ROCE ≥ 18% | **18.8%** as Screener computes it (17.9% on my recomputation of the same basis; 25.4% ex-cash) | **Pass, marginal** |
| Durability | 5Y sales CAGR ≥ 15%, margin not compressing | 16.5% (₹1,376 Cr FY21 → ₹2,951 Cr FY26); OPM 25% → 20% on annual closes but **23% on TTM**, so expanding on the series the gate reads | **Pass** |
| Quality | CFO/PAT ≥ 0.8, debtor days ≤ 90 | 1.09x FY26, ~1.25x TTM (not 0.81x — see §1); debtor days 79.7 | **Pass** |
| Balance | Debt/equity ≤ 0.5 | 0.14 including leases, 0.01 excluding | **Pass** |
| Management | Promoter ≥ 35% and stable | 43.87%, identical for four years | **Pass** |
| Valuation | P/E ≤ 1.5x industry, or ≤ 35 | 25.0x against industry median 33.4x | **Pass** |

**The reinvestment gate is the one worth arguing about, because the company and the website disagree by 600bps and both are right.** Screener divides EBIT by all capital employed and gets 18.8%. My own recomputation on Screener's own capital-employed figure (equity ₹3,332 Cr + borrowings ₹465 Cr) with TTM EBIT of ₹678 Cr gives **17.9%** — so the gate is a marginal pass, not a comfortable one, and it sits on a definition that charges the company for holding ₹1,124 Cr of cash. The company reports **24.7% trailing ROCE** (from 18.7% a year earlier), which is a return-on-operating-capital measure; strip the cash out of my calculation and it is 25.4%, so the company's number is internally consistent. The honest reading: **the operating business earns 25%, the balance sheet dilutes that to 18%, and the gate passes on either basis but only just on the diluted one.** A company sitting on 8% of its market cap in cash should be judged on the operating return and watched on what it does with the cash.

**The durability gate passes on a technicality that happens to be true.** On annual closes, operating margin fell from 25% in FY21 to 20% in FY26 — that would have failed the gate. The scorecard reads the TTM column, where margin is 23%, and TTM is the correct lens here because FY26 was the trough year of a deliberate inventory and freight cycle and FY27 has already reversed it (H1 24.0% against 18.2%). The right way to say it: **margin bottomed at 20.2% in FY25 and has recovered to 23-24%, and the recovery is one half-year old.**

**The eighth gate passes more clearly than any of the seven.** The second engine is not a new product line, it is a change in the shape of the company: from a US-centric generics exporter into a multi-country consumer-health front-end that manufactures its own store brands. The evidence is arithmetic, not narrative — **72% of Q2's ₹220 Cr of incremental revenue came from UK and Europe, and only 15% from the US**, which was 100% of the story five years ago. Add a branded Rx business in Australia growing 64% off the Nova base, a Canadian entity with approvals in hand, and a 26 bn-unit manufacturing base that is being doubled in India, and there is a genuine second act. The risk attached to it is that all four new markets (Germany, Netherlands, Ireland, Canada) are pre-scale, and Europe's ₹1,000 Cr target is five years out against roughly ₹700 Cr annualised today including the UK.

Four things in that table matter more than the growth rate.

**The cash-flow turnaround in FY26 is the single best fact in the file.** CFO went ₹207 Cr → ₹458 Cr (+121%) on a 13% revenue increase, free cash flow went ₹35 Cr → ₹329 Cr, and the FY25 wobble (CFO/PAT 0.54x) reversed completely. H1 FY27 continued it: ₹322 Cr of operating cash against ₹75 Cr in H1 FY26, a 75% EBITDA-to-cash conversion against 31%, and ₹275 Cr of free cash after ₹46 Cr of capex. Over three years the company has converted 79% of reported profit into operating cash on average — not spectacular, but consistent, and improving.

**Working capital is the cost of the model and it is large in absolute terms.** The company's own cycle measure is ~122 days in Q2 FY27, down from ~132 in Q1 and ~138 in FY26 — genuine improvement. On ₹3,392 Cr of TTM revenue, 128 days is **₹1,190 Cr of cash locked in working capital**, and Screener's wider definition (149 days) implies ₹1,385 Cr. Receivables alone are ₹757 Cr at September, or 81 days; inventory is ₹996 Cr, roughly 3.5 months of revenue. Set that against ₹1,124 Cr of cash and the picture is clear: **the cash pile is financing the working capital, not sitting idle.** That is not a crisis — inventory is a deliberate buffer for a business that must ship a winter cough-and-cold season on schedule, and payables of ₹393 Cr fund part of it — but it does explain why a company with ₹1,095 Cr of net cash and 25% growth is not buying back stock.

**The margin is the variable that decides the next two years.** 21.1% → 20.3% → 20.4% → 23.1% TTM. The step-up is real but it is partly inventory arithmetic: Q1 FY27 consumed cheaply-bought stock, Q2 began repricing into higher-cost inventory, and management has guided gross margin to 55-56% for H2 against the 58.5% printed in Q2 and 58.8% in H1. A 300bps gross-margin give-back on ₹950 Cr of quarterly revenue is about ₹28 Cr of gross profit per quarter — roughly 13% of Q2's EBITDA. **The company is telling you, in plain language, that Q2 was the margin peak of this cycle.**

**Interest and lease costs are rising faster than revenue, and it is not distress.** Finance cost went ₹12 Cr → ₹24 Cr → ₹26 Cr TTM; lease liabilities went from ₹271 Cr to ₹436 Cr in six months, of which ₹120 Cr is a new UK lease (management confirmed the reason on the call). Debtor days, credit rating and net cash all improved at the same time, so this is IFRS-16 arithmetic and a distribution footprint, not leverage. Both ratings were affirmed or improved: **CARE AA- Stable and India Ratings AA- with the outlook revised to Positive**, short-term A1+.

**The quarter was a record on every operating line and a miss against consensus.** Reported EPS of ₹3.11 came in 11.1% below the ₹3.50 consensus (Yahoo/Refinitiv compiled estimates: actual 3.11, estimate 3.50). That is why the stock fell 4.33% on results day on 5.96 mn shares — four times normal volume — and why the 6-week range has not broken upward since. The three reasons PAT fell 10% sequentially, all given by the CFO on the call, are legitimate and all are visible in the table: higher depreciation after acquired intangibles at QliniQ and ABCnow started amortising over ten years (+₹7 Cr), lower other income as currency moves stabilised (−₹7 Cr), and a higher effective tax rate of 25.8%. **None of the three is a deterioration in the business. All three are permanent, which is the problem** — depreciation will stay at ₹32 Cr a quarter, so the EPS base has reset lower.

**Q1 was the hidden peak.** 25.3% EBITDA margin in Q1 was flattered by consuming inventory bought before the crude and freight spike. Management said it plainly: *"During Q1, we benefited from consumption of lower-cost inventory. However, we are now seeing an increase in inventory costs, resulting in gross margin moving to 58.5% from 59.1% in the previous quarter."* Freight has nearly doubled, adding ₹20 Cr to other expenses sequentially, plus ₹8 Cr of employee cost and a couple of crore of German start-up cost. That Q2 still printed a record ₹214 Cr of EBITDA against those three headwinds is the bull case in one line; that the headwinds are structural into H2 is the bear case.

**The geographic mix is the most important number in the release.** US revenue of ₹420.9 Cr was the highest ever for the region but grew only 8.7% — against a US consumer environment management described as one where *"medicines are literally the last priority on everyone's mind"* as food and gas take precedence. UK and Europe grew 64.9% to a record ₹404.6 Cr, of which ~₹40 Cr was QliniQ; the base business still grew 48.6% on new launches after a flat FY26. Australia and New Zealand grew 64.0% on the branded Rx push under Nova. Rest of World fell 47.0% on Middle East shipping disruption, worth ₹12 Cr of lost revenue — small in group terms, and management expects to recover to ₹20-25 Cr a quarter as logistics normalise.

The reasonable conclusion is **Level 2 credibility on margins (5-15% risk of miss) and Level 3 on revenue specifics** — the multi-year numbers are directional, not a model.

**The promoter stake has not moved, and the one time it did move it was dilution, not a sale.** Screener shows 43.85% across March 2023 to September 2026 — flat for three and a half years. The 4.4pp drop from 48.25% in FY22 to 43.85% in FY23 is the 2023 warrant conversion that raised ₹372.40 Cr in equity from OrbiMed **and the promoters themselves**, i.e. a capital raise in which the promoters participated, not a sell-down. Using that step to argue "promoters are diluting" would be wrong; using it to note that a 48.25% stake became 43.85% through a raise is accurate and neutral.

**The FII line contains one artefact and one real trend.** The apparent collapse from 22.19% (March 2025) and the recovery from 8.13% (December 2025) are not capital flows: look at public holding in the same two quarters — it jumps from 33.93% to 42.08% and back to 32.78% in perfect mirror image. On the clean comparisons: **FIIs went from 16.92% (Sep 2025) to 19.06% (Sep 2026), +2.14pp**, and 17.47% → 19.06% in the September quarter alone, the strongest quarterly institutional accumulation in the table.

**DIIs are the ones trimming.** 6.57% at March 2026 (the high) to 6.19% then 5.54% — **−1.03pp over six months, or a 16% reduction in their holding**. That is trimming, not an exit; the 50%-reduction threshold that would make it critical is nowhere near. It is also explained by the bulk-deal record: in June 2026 **OrbiMed sold ₹256.8 Cr of stock through a block, and Unifi Capital bought 72.4 lakh shares (1.6%) at ₹231.50 for ₹167.6 Cr** — a foreign fund handing stock to a domestic AMC at a price 25% below today's. Unifi's analysts were the most active questioners on the Q2 call, so the new holder is engaged.

**Shareholder count is flat at 2.47 lakh, not rising.** That is unusual for a stock up 60% in a year and it is a point in the thesis's favour: this has not been a retail momentum name. Public holding has actually fallen from 35.81% (Dec 2023) to 31.53%, with institutions absorbing 4.9pp. **Retail-to-institution transfer is the healthiest form of ownership change there is.**

### 11c. Forward estimates — built from guidance, checked for arithmetic

## 12. Bull Case

**1. The multiple has compressed while earnings grew — the rarest setup in a momentum name.** The stock is 11.9% below its September high while TTM EPS went from ₹11.50 to ₹12.36. That is a 30.5x stock becoming a 25.0x stock without the price falling much, which is the definition of de-risking a valuation. If EPS simply holds and the multiple returns to its own September level, that is a 22% move with no new information.

**2. Growth is now geographically diversified, and the proof is in the increment.** 72% of Q2's incremental revenue came from UK and Europe and only 15% from the US — against a history where the US was everything. The US still grew 8.7% to a record ₹421 Cr in a consumer environment management described as prioritising food and gas over medicine. A company whose *slowest* market is growing 9% in a bad year has a different risk profile from one that depends on it.

**3. The cash machine turned on.** FY26 CFO ₹458 Cr against ₹207 Cr, free cash flow ₹329 Cr against ₹35 Cr, and H1 FY27 already at ₹322 Cr of operating cash with 75% EBITDA conversion. Net cash rose ₹135 Cr in H1 *after* ₹95 Cr of acquisitions and ₹41 Cr of dividends. That is a business funding its own growth, its own M&A and a dividend simultaneously.

**4. The balance sheet is a call option on capacity.** ₹1,095 Cr of net cash against a ₹14,012 Cr market cap, AA- ratings with the outlook revised to positive, and a stated plan to double Indian capacity to 16 bn units by buying a plant or building a block within twelve months. Capacity bought at a cyclical low in a business with 20%+ EBITDA margins is the highest-return use of that cash, and management has said it is in dialogue with two companies.

**5. The OTC store-brand model is structurally advantaged in an inflation.** When retailers' customers trade down, store brands gain share, and Marksans is the low-cost manufacturing partner behind those labels — 80% OTC, 85% of that store brands, with 26 bn units of capacity and 98-99% of revenue made in its own plants. The 2026 tariff regime, which currently exempts generics, penalises imported *branded* drugs while the US facility in Farmingdale offers a "Made in the USA" option. The company is on the right side of both the consumer trade-down and the tariff wall.

**6. Execution credibility on the things it controls.** The ₹3,000 Cr total-income target, the 19-20% margin range, the net-cash balance sheet and the OTC growth focus were all promised and all delivered. Working capital improved 16 days in a year. Debtor days are 80 and stable. The promoter has not sold a share in four years. Those are the markers of a management that does what it says, which is what makes the aspirational ₹5,000 Cr target worth taking seriously at all.

## 13. Bear Case

**1. Q2 was the margin peak and the company has said so.** Gross margin guided to 55-56% for H2 against 58.5% printed; EBITDA margin guided to 21-22% for the year against 22.8% in Q2 and 24.0% in H1. Freight has nearly doubled (+₹20 Cr sequentially), API prices for paracetamol and ibuprofen are elevated, and the cheap inventory that flattered Q1 is gone. **A 300bps gross-margin give-back is ~13% of Q2's EBITDA.** In the conservative case (§11c) FY27 EPS is ₹11.37 — *below* the trailing ₹12.36, meaning no earnings growth at all this year on a P/E of 27x.

**2. The EPS base has structurally reset lower.** Acquired intangibles at QliniQ and ABCnow now amortise over ten years instead of being tested only for impairment, adding ₹7 Cr a quarter of depreciation; the CFO said this is the new run-rate. Combined with lower other income and a 25.8% tax rate, Q2 PAT fell 10% sequentially on a 11.8% revenue increase. Goodwill rose ₹57 Cr and intangibles ₹35 Cr in six months from two acquisitions bought for ₹95 Cr — small, but the amortisation tail is now permanent.

**3. A fifth of profit is currency and interest, so the operating multiple is 30x, not 25x.** ₹99 Cr of TTM other income, of which ₹47.4 Cr was FX gains in FY26 alone. Core P/E is **~30.4x**, not 25.0x. A rupee that strengthens against the dollar and pound — the natural consequence of the same crude-price relief that would fix the margin — takes this income away at exactly the moment margins recover. The two hedges the company does not have are a hedged other-income line and a domestic revenue base.

**4. The US, still 45% of revenue, is slowing and structurally price-eroded.** 8.7% growth in Q2 and 11.7% in H1, with single-digit price erosion in the Rx book that management expects to continue. The $300 mn order-book target is three years out from $225 mn — a 10% CAGR on the largest single market. Store-brand OTC is a low-price game where the retailer holds the pricing power; management confirmed there are **no price increases** in the base portfolio. Volume is the only lever and the consumer is trading down hard.

**5. The European platform is four pre-scale entities bought at ~20x.** Ireland has not started trading. Canada has approvals but "commercial revenue expected in the coming quarters". Germany and the Netherlands are single-country front-ends contributing ₹84 Cr in a half. The ₹1,000 Cr European target is five years out. Management itself said: *"it might take up to three years to see the visible changes"* — and each of these is a distribution business with no manufacturing moat, which is precisely the kind of asset that produces goodwill rather than returns if the launches do not land.

**6. Working capital absorbs the cash pile.** ₹1,190 Cr locked at 128 days on ₹3,392 Cr of revenue, receivables at ₹757 Cr, inventory at ₹996 Cr. The ₹1,124 Cr of cash is not excess capital — it is the working-capital float for a business that must ship a seasonal cough-and-cold peak. That is why no buyback has happened and why management answered the buyback question with "if nothing else is happening".

**7. The tape has not confirmed anything.** 11.9% below the high, below the 10/20/30/50 EMAs, ADX 14, RSI 49, a rejected ₹328.70 on 13 November, and three-month relative underperformance of 10.3pp against the Nifty. Eight moving averages inside a ₹1.32 band means the next move is a coin flip with a 4% daily range on the losing side.

## 14. Kill Criteria

Written before the outcome is known, so that a review is a measurement rather than an argument. Any one of these closes the thesis:

**Operational**
1. **US revenue declines year-on-year for two consecutive quarters**, or US H1 revenue growth falls below 5%. This is 45% of the book and the order-book target's anchor.
2. **Gross margin below 55% for two consecutive quarters.** That is the company's own floor; below it, the 20%+ EBITDA structure is broken and the FY29 ₹5,000 Cr target is unachievable at a sensible margin.
3. **EBITDA margin below 20% for a full year** while revenue grows — the definition of buying growth with margin.

**Balance sheet**
4. **Working-capital cycle above 150 days**, or debtor days above 95. The cycle has improved for two quarters; a reversal means the growth is being sold on credit.
5. **Net cash (company definition) below ₹500 Cr without a disclosed, return-accretive use** — i.e. cash spent on M&A that cannot be shown to earn its cost of capital.
6. **Goodwill impairment on QliniQ or ABCnow**, or cumulative European M&A above ₹500 Cr before the platform shows ₹150 Cr of annual revenue.

**Ownership**
7. **Promoter stake below 40%, any pledge, or any promoter share sale** — 43.87% flat for four years is a large part of why this passed the management gate.
8. **FII holding below 15% for two quarters** (currently 19.06%), which would mark the end of the institutional accumulation that has absorbed retail supply since 2023.

**Price**
9. **A monthly close below ₹267** (the 200-day EMA at 267.00, and 23.5% below spot). That invalidates the long-term uptrend, not just the trade.
10. **A close above ₹351 that fails within five sessions on delivery below 30%** — a false breakout at the old high would confirm distribution and reset the base clock.

## 18. Verdict

**WATCH — a genuine quality compounder at a fair price with no entry signal.**

The business case is as clean as anything in this ledger. Marksans passed all seven mechanical gates, the only name in this week's cohort to do so, and the eighth — the judgement gate — passes on evidence rather than narrative: 72% of Q2's incremental revenue came from UK and Europe where the US used to be the only engine, with a branded Rx business in Australia growing 64% and four new front-door entities in Germany, the Netherlands, Ireland and Canada. It earns 18.8% on total capital and 25.4% on the capital it operates, holds ₹1,095 Cr of net cash against ₹29 Cr of borrowings (₹659 Cr net if the ₹436 Cr of leases is counted), converts profit into cash at 1.09-1.25x, has not breached a positive-cash-flow year in twelve years on record, and trades at 25.0x against an industry median of 33.4x while growing profit four times faster than that median. Everything the company said it would do by FY26 — the ₹3,000 Cr income target, a 19-20% margin, a net-cash balance sheet — it did.

The problems are timing and arithmetic, and both are measurable. **Arithmetic:** other income is ₹99 Cr, or 17.5% of attributable profit, and ₹47.4 Cr of the FY26 figure was pure currency gain — so the operating multiple is **~30.4x, not 25.0x**, and the earnings yield of 4.00% sits 3.31pp *below* the 7.31% ten-year G-sec. On Howard Marks' four tests it fails all but return on capital, with an earnings power value of ₹103 against a ₹309.20 price — two-thirds of the market capitalisation is a claim on growth not yet earned. **Timing:** management has just guided second-half gross margin to 55-56% from the 58.5% it printed in Q2, doubling freight and elevated paracetamol and ibuprofen prices have already taken ₹28 Cr a quarter out of gross profit, and the amortisation of acquired intangibles has permanently reset depreciation ₹7 Cr a quarter higher. In the conservative case FY27 EPS of ₹11.37 is *below* the trailing ₹12.36, on 27.2x. And the tape agrees with the caution: 11.9% below the September high, below all four short moving averages, ADX 14, RSI 49, and a 10-week base between ₹296 and ₹329 that has not resolved.

What makes this a WATCH rather than a pass is that the accumulation is real and visible. **20-session average delivery is 45.8%**, ten of the last twenty sessions above 45%, only one below 30% — buyers are taking stock home on almost every up-day, which is the opposite of the churn that marks most 60%-in-a-year names. FIIs are up 2.14pp over twelve months and 1.59pp in the September quarter, the promoter has not sold a share in four years, shareholder count is flat at 2.47 lakh while institutions absorbed 4.9pp from retail, and the two 2026 block events — OrbiMed's ₹256.8 Cr exit to Unifi at ₹231.50 in June, and the Microcurves same-quantity cross at ₹313.24 in August — are rotations, not distributions.

**What I would do.** Nothing today. Set the two triggers and let the market choose: a close above **₹330** on more than 2 mn shares with delivery above 40% is the breakout buy, with a ₹312 stop and ₹351/₹385 targets; a flush into **₹283-290** that holds is the better buy, with a ₹267 stop where the 200-day EMA sits. For a long-horizon holder already convinced by the FY29 arithmetic — ₹5,000 Cr revenue at 22% EBITDA is ₹16.72 of EPS, 18.5x today's price — accumulating in thirds between ₹285 and ₹330 is defensible and the 4% position cap is the discipline. For anyone valuing on book or on yield, this is a pass and will remain one.

**The one thing that would kill the thesis:** gross margin failing to hold 55% for two consecutive quarters. That single line decides whether FY27 EPS grows or shrinks, whether the 21-22% EBITDA structure is real or was an inventory artefact, and whether the ₹5,000 Cr target is reachable at a sensible margin. It is also the one number management has already told you to expect lower — which is why this is a WATCH and not a buy at 25x.
