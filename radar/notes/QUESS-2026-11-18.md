# Quess Corp — Radar Deep Dive

Private note for the multibagger radar. Not published, not deployable, not part of the website. One company, one Screener page (consolidated), paced. Every figure below is read off an exchange filing, computed from the exchange's own bhavcopy, or labelled as an estimate.

**The one-line case.** Quess is no longer the conglomerate that lost money on facility management and job portals. Since the three-way demerger it is a pure workforce-management business: 504,000 associates deployed, ₹16,400 Cr of trailing revenue, zero financial debt, ₹465 Cr of net cash, 24% ROCE and its first genuine volume inflection in three quarters (21,000 net associates added in Q2, the best in a year). The stock has responded — +62% in twelve months against a Nifty that fell 2%. What stops it being a buy is arithmetic that the headline P/E hides: **18.3x trailing is computed on a 3.3% effective tax rate that the income-tax department has disallowed, that the auditor has qualified four quarters running, and that carries ₹388 Cr of crystallised demands and another ₹845 Cr of FY24-FY25 deductions still to be assessed.** On the normal 25.17% rate the same earnings give **~24.9x**, which is a 2.2x premium to TeamLease at 11.3x for a business with the same 1.5% operating margin on general staffing.

## 2. What the Company Does

Quess is **India's largest private-sector staffing company**: it employs people and deploys them into client premises, billing the client per associate per month and paying the associate a wage. FY26 revenue was ₹15,305 Cr; employee-benefit expense was ₹14,500 Cr, or **94.7% of revenue**. The operating margin is 2.0%. This is a volume, working-capital and execution business, not a margin business, and everything about the investment case follows from that.

**Three segments, one of which is almost all of it (Q2 FY27):**

| Segment | Revenue ₹ Cr | YoY | EBITDA ₹ Cr | Margin | What it is |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **General Staffing** | **3,823** | +15% | **57** | 1.5% | 490,000 associates across consumer/retail/telecom, manufacturing, BFSI, construction, value-added services. 74% of it is "collect & pay" — Quess collects from the client before it pays the associate, so this segment *generates* float rather than absorbing it |
| **Professional Staffing** | 254 | +13% | 27 | 10.5% | IT staffing, GCC services, recruitment, international mobility. 7,566 associates; 71% of headcount and revenue from Global Capability Centres |
| **Overseas** | 318 | +10% | 22 | 6.9% | Singapore, Malaysia, Philippines, Vietnam, Sri Lanka, Dubai/Abu Dhabi. 6,000+ associates |
| **Digital platforms** | 1.6 | — | +1.5 | — | BCPL blue-collar job/HRMS platform. First EBITDA-positive quarter |
| **Total** | **4,396** | **+15%** | **86** | **2.0%** | Adjusted for the ₹6 Cr ESOP charge, EBITDA ₹92 Cr at 2.1% |

**The segment split is the whole valuation argument.** General Staffing is 87% of revenue and 54% of EBITDA. Professional Staffing plus Overseas is 13% of revenue and 46% of EBITDA — the presentation's own line is "high-margin portfolio contributes 48% of total profitability". A 10bps improvement in General Staffing margin is worth ₹16 Cr a year, roughly 5% of group EBITDA. A 15% growth rate on a 1.5% margin adds only the margin, not the revenue.

**The unit economics, stated plainly.** Management put "PAPM" — profit after personnel cost per associate per month — at **₹650-700**, unchanged, with the higher-value tracks (value-added services, construction, manufacturing) above that and the traditional consumer/retail/telecom/BFSI book a flat fee. The traditional book is still **80-82% of General Staffing**; the higher-margin businesses are 18-20% and growing faster. That mix number is the single best forward indicator in the whole disclosure, because margin repair here cannot come from pricing (there is none) — only from mix.

**The demerger is the reason the financials look different from the history.** The scheme, approved by NCLT Bengaluru on 17 March 2025 and effective 31 March 2025 with an appointed date of 1 April 2024, split the old Quess three ways:

- **Quess** kept workforce management: general staffing, IT staffing (minus Brainhunter), overseas staffing.
- **Digitide Solutions** took the BPM/payroll-processing business (GTS) and Brainhunter.
- **Bluspring Enterprises** took facility management, industrial asset maintenance and the foundit job portal.

Shareholders received one share of each for every Quess share, and both siblings listed in June 2025. **This matters for two reasons.** First, Quess today is a *cleaner* business than any historical comparison suggests — the low-margin facility-management drag and the loss-making foundit portal are gone. Second, the siblings are now the closest thing to a live read on what the market pays for adjacent pieces of the same group: **Digitide trades at ₹60.10 (₹914 Cr market cap) on a trailing loss, Bluspring at ₹153.77 (₹2,354 Cr) on a trailing loss.** Both are value-destructive at the bottom line, and both have de-rated hard since listing. That is a caution about how the market treats this group's non-core pieces, not a support for Quess's multiple.

**What management says the next leg is — "Quess 2.0".** Three tracks, all early:
1. **Global corridors** — GCC hiring, international mobility and "digital-led mobility". Quess is a founding member of GSMAI, has signed an MoU with IOM (the UN migration agency) and had mobilised 135 workers to the UAE in H1.
2. **Digital platforms** — BCPL broke even at the EBITDA level in Q2 (after a ~₹6 Cr burn last year), with paying clients up from 14 in March to 54 in September across enterprises, MSMEs and government. An AI-enabled SaaS payroll module is being built. Management expects full-year breakeven for FY27 and profits from FY28.
3. **Quess IT Solutions** — the IT staffing business repositioned with practice leaders in cybersecurity, data/AI and programme management, targeting a first solutions project in H2 FY27.

The stated ambition, from the Q1 call, is **dollar-linked revenue rising to 20-25% of the total over three to four years** — which on today's numbers means roughly ₹4,000 Cr, against ₹650 Cr of Overseas plus ₹500 Cr of Professional Staffing now. That is aspirational, not guidance, and it is treated as such in §11.

**Two operating facts that carry more weight than the strategy slides.** The **AI sourcing engine ("The Brain") already accounts for ~13% of General Staffing hiring** and generated 1.4 lakh candidate leads and ~24,700 offers since launch, with 35,000-40,000 offers expected in H2 — that is a real cost-per-hire lever in a business where sourcing is the main variable cost. And **DSO in General Staffing is 24 days with AR at 15 days**, i.e., the collection engine is tighter than the growth. Both are the kind of thing that shows up in margin two quarters later.

## 3. Why It Cleared the Gates

The brief calls them eight. `scripts/radar-scorecard.py` defines **seven** mechanical gates; the eighth is the judgement call — does a second engine exist — and it is recorded rather than passed. Quess cleared **six of seven** in week 2026-W47, failing durability. What each gate actually saw:

| Gate | Test | QUESS | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| Headroom | Market cap under ₹25,000 Cr | ₹5,037 Cr | **Pass** |
| Reinvestment | ROCE ≥ 18% | **24.0%** (Screener, all capital); 24.8% on my recomputation (EBIT ₹326 Cr ÷ capital employed ₹1,313 Cr) | **Pass** |
| Durability | 5Y sales CAGR ≥ 15% and margin not compressing | **Fail** — see below | **Fail** |
| Quality | CFO/PAT ≥ 0.8, debtor days ≤ 90 | 1.04x FY26 (not 0.83x — see §1); debtor days **37.7** | **Pass** |
| Balance | Debt/equity ≤ 0.5 | **0.11**, and that is leases only; zero borrowings | **Pass** |
| Management | Promoter ≥ 35% and stable | **56.81%**, unchanged for five quarters, no pledge | **Pass** |
| Valuation | P/E ≤ 1.5x industry **or** ≤ 35 | 17.9x against industry 15.7x (threshold 23.5x) | **Pass, marginal** |

**The durability fail is correct, and the two published growth rates for it are both wrong.** The scorecard recorded 3.7%. Screener prints 7.15% over five years (FY21 ₹10,837 Cr → FY26 ₹15,305 Cr) and flags it as a CON. Neither is a clean measure, because FY21-FY23 include Digitide and Bluspring, which Quess no longer owns. On the only comparable base — continuing operations, restated — the record is:

- **FY24 ₹13,695 Cr → FY25 ₹14,967 Cr (+9.3%) → FY26 ₹15,305 Cr (+2.3%) → TTM ₹16,400 Cr (+8%)**

A 5.7% two-year CAGR with a 2.3% year inside it is not a 15% compounder, and the gate is right to fail it. What the gate cannot see is that the +2.3% year was the first clean post-demerger year, and that FY27 has started at +15% (of which ~3.5pp is the Labour Code pass-through, so ~11.5% underlying). **The honest framing: Quess failed durability on a record that is two years old and one quarter into a better trend.** That is a reason to watch, not a reason to dismiss.

**The eighth gate — the second engine — passes on evidence, and it is the strongest part of the case.** Three pieces:
1. **The mix shift inside General Staffing is measurable.** Value-added services, manufacturing and construction are 18-20% of the segment and growing faster than the 80-82% traditional book; construction EBITDA grew **71%** year-on-year in Q2 and manufacturing added 7,800 associates in the quarter (+11% QoQ). PAPM on those tracks is above the ₹650-700 group average.
2. **Professional Staffing's gross margin has structurally improved** from 17.5% to **20.5%** in two years, with a quarter of IT associates now in niche skills earning "significantly higher" gross margin and 62 new MSAs signed in H1 — more than all of FY26.
3. **Overseas is now a margin business, not a presence.** 6.9% EBITDA margin, up from 6.2% in Q1, with the Middle East at 14.2%, the Philippines at 10.5% net and Malaysia growing 50%.

The risk attached to the eighth gate is that all three are small in absolute terms and none has yet changed the group number: group EBITDA margin has been 1.9-2.2% for eight quarters.

Five things in that table matter more than the growth rate.

**The tax line, not the operating line, is what changed.** FY24 PAT of ₹280 Cr carried a *negative* tax charge and ₹159 Cr of other income — a year that is not comparable to anything. FY25 PAT of ₹46 Cr absorbed ₹164 Cr of exceptional items (₹119 Cr expected credit loss on discontinued projects, ₹26 Cr goodwill impairment, ₹19 Cr demerger costs) and a −₹199 Cr other-income line. FY26 is the first clean year: ₹230 Cr PBT, ₹76 Cr of tax — **a 3.3% effective rate** — and ₹222 Cr of PAT. The step from ₹46 Cr to ₹222 Cr is not a doubling of the business; it is the absence of one-off hits plus a tax rate that has fallen to almost nothing. H1 FY27 has continued it: ₹151 Cr PBT, a ₹6 Cr **tax credit**, ₹157 Cr PAT.

**Cash conversion is good on a matched basis and mediocre on a clean one.** FY26 net operating cash ₹230 Cr against ₹222 Cr PAT is 1.04x — healthy for a staffing business. But it included ₹68 Cr of tax paid against a ₹76 Cr charge, i.e., the reported profit is genuinely turning into cash. H1 FY27 looks spectacular (₹366 Cr net operating inflow) only because **₹229 Cr of it was an income-tax refund**; cash generated from operations before that was ₹137 Cr against ₹157 Cr of PAT, or 0.87x. The refund is real money — it is in the balance sheet as cash — but it is not repeatable and it should not be annualised.

**The balance sheet is the best in the company's history and it is genuinely unlevered.** Borrowings went from ₹838 Cr (FY24) to ₹113 Cr (FY25) as the demerger transferred debt with the undertakings, and the company repaid the residual working-capital line. Today: **zero financial debt, ₹417 Cr of cash, ₹28 Cr of bank balances, ₹20 Cr of current investments against ₹128 Cr of lease liabilities.** Net cash ₹465 Cr is **9.2% of market capitalisation**. Equity is ₹1,185 Cr, of which ₹1,034 Cr is reserves.

**The Labour Code has put ₹300 Cr of air into both sides of the balance sheet, and this is the single most important accounting fact in the interim accounts.** H1 revenue includes a **one-time ₹298 Cr** (₹120 Cr in Q2) from the new Labour Code. It is a pass-through with no profit. Look at what it did to the balance sheet:

- **Unbilled revenue went from ₹774 Cr (Mar-26) to ₹1,311 Cr (Sep-26)** — +₹537 Cr.
- **Non-current provisions went from ₹322 Cr to ₹626 Cr** — +₹304 Cr.
- **Other financial liabilities went from ₹989 Cr to ₹1,297 Cr** — +₹308 Cr.

The company told the analyst who asked: *"almost ₹300 crores of unbilled is because of the Labour Code provisions that we have made… half of the [unbilled] buildup is because of the Labour Code implementation."* In the cash-flow statement the same entry appears as a ₹571.5 Cr increase in receivables and unbilled revenue offset by a ₹550.3 Cr increase in other liabilities and provisions. **It is balance-sheet-neutral, it carries no cash, and it inflates reported revenue growth by roughly 3.5 percentage points in H1.** Strip it out and H1 revenue grew ~11.5%, not 15%.

**Working capital is the business model and it is currently a source of cash, not a use.** Working-capital days are 7 and debtor days 38; ₹2,150 Cr of receivables plus unbilled is 48 days of revenue, funded by ₹1,297 Cr of other financial liabilities and ₹364 Cr of other current liabilities — the associates' wage float. **74% of General Staffing is "collect & pay", meaning Quess is paid before it pays.** That is why a business with ₹16,400 Cr of revenue needs no debt, and it is also why the model is fragile in exactly one direction: if clients push terms out or the mix shifts toward "pay & collect" contracts with 15-30 day credit, the float reverses and cash becomes a use. Management said the collect-and-pay share fell about 1% in the quarter and called it "moving parts". It is worth watching precisely because it is the funding mechanism.

**Decompose the +44% before believing it.** Q2 pre-tax profit rose 12.5%, from ₹57.5 Cr to ₹64.6 Cr. The ₹23 Cr of PAT growth came almost entirely below the pretax line: **tax went from a ₹5.7 Cr charge to a ₹10.0 Cr credit, a ₹15.7 Cr swing that alone accounts for roughly two-thirds of the reported growth.** The credit itself is a **₹14.7 Cr deferred-tax credit** against ₹4.7 Cr of current tax. In plain terms: the operating business grew pretax profit 12.5% and the earnings per share grew 44% because the tax rate was 7% in the year-ago quarter and negative in this one. Anyone quoting "PAT +44%" without that sentence is selling something.

**Core operating profit grew faster than PBT, which is the real positive.** Strip other income out of both quarters: core PBT was ₹62.6 Cr against ₹53.4 Cr, **+17.2%**. That is a genuinely good number against a 1.5% segment margin, and it is the arithmetic behind the "EBITDA outpacing revenue" claim on the call.

- **General Staffing is the inflection.** 20,857 net associates added in the quarter — after "nearly three quarters" without additions, in the CEO's words — on the highest-ever sourcing of 56,576 and 116 new contracts. Manufacturing added 7,800 (+11% QoQ), construction EBITDA grew 71%, and collections were a record ₹4,285 Cr with DSO down to 24 days from 26. Management's steady-state guidance is **10,000-15,000 net adds a quarter**, and it did 21,000.
- **Professional Staffing is deliberately in investment mode and it cost 170bps of margin.** EBITDA fell 2% on 13% revenue growth because of a planned investment in leadership, sales, delivery and practices — the first of that scale since FY21-22. Management says without it the margin would have been **11.7%** rather than 10.5%, guides **11-12% for the year with an exit near 12%**, and points to 62 new MSAs in H1 (more than all of FY26) and 437 net headcount additions, the best quarter since FY22. The gross margin (20.5%) is intact. This is the one place where a genuinely higher-quality revenue stream is being built, and it is being paid for out of current earnings.
- **Overseas margin improved to 6.9% from 6.2%** with revenue up 10% despite the Middle East being flat on geopolitics (at a 14.2% margin), the Philippines up 15% at a 10.5% net margin and Malaysia up 50% at a lower margin.

**Q1 FY27 was the quarter that flattered everything.** Revenue ₹4,182 Cr (+15%, including ₹176 Cr of Labour Code pass-through), EBITDA ₹85 Cr, PAT ₹82 Cr (+61%). Three non-recurring items sat inside it: the Labour Code one-off, **₹24 Cr of one-time other income from income-tax refunds** (₹22 Cr of interest), and a low tax charge. That is why Q2 PAT fell 9% sequentially on 5% more revenue, and it is why the Q1 growth rate should not be extrapolated.

**Guidance credibility: Level 2.** This management does what it says on the operating items it controls — the demerger completed, the debt was repaid, the dividend was maintained through a year of ₹46 Cr PAT, and the headcount guidance for the quarter was beaten. Against that, the Q1 to Q2 revenue path was carried by a pass-through that nobody forecast, the "PAT growth" being marketed is a tax artefact, and the "Quess 2.0" targets are directional rather than numeric. **Addressing each one, because three of the four are load-bearing.**

**"Tax rate seems low" is the most important sentence on the page and Screener gives it one line.** The 3.3% FY26 rate is the §80JJAA deduction for additional employment, and it is contested. Deloitte's limited-review report on the Q2 FY27 results carries a **qualified conclusion**: certain deductions claimed by the parent "have been disallowed by the Income Tax Authority", the parent has challenged them, and "as a result of the uncertainty… we are unable to comment whether any adjustments are necessary." **That qualification has been repeated in every set of results since at least Q2 FY26.** The annual report quantifies it: **₹3,879.94 mn (₹388 Cr) of contingent liability** for demands including interest, up from ₹296.4 Cr a year earlier, and **₹844.8 Cr of FY24 and FY25 80JJAA deductions for which assessment is not yet complete**. Management's defence is real — four favourable tribunal rulings (two Bombay, two Delhi ITAT), external legal opinions, and an own hearing deferred from October 2026 to **January 2027** — and the ₹229 Cr tax refund received in H1 FY27 is evidence that the position has prevailed somewhere. But the reader should hold both facts: **the P/E is 18.3x because the tax rate is 3%; at the statutory 25.17% it is 24.9x.** That is not a valuation nuance, it is the valuation.

**"Poor sales growth of 7.15% over past five years" is correct on the number and wrong on the reason.** The five-year window straddles the demerger; FY21-FY23 include revenue Quess no longer owns. On continuing operations, FY24 ₹13,695 Cr → FY26 ₹15,305 Cr is a 5.7% two-year CAGR, and FY26 alone grew 2.3%. So the criticism stands, just with a different number — and it is why the durability gate failed.

**"4.26 times book" is fair and mostly irrelevant here.** The business has no fixed assets (₹395 Cr of PPE and intangibles against ₹16,400 Cr of revenue) because its asset is a workforce it does not own. Book value is not a floor for a staffing company; 4.3x book is the price of a 24% ROCE on a thin equity base.

**"Low return on equity of 13.5% over last 3 years" is arithmetically right and analytically misleading.** The three-year window includes FY25's ₹46 Cr PAT year, which carried ₹164 Cr of demerger-related exceptional items. TTM ROE is **23.5%** and the company reports 25%. The three-year average is a museum piece.

## 12. Bull Case

**1. The volume inflection is real and it is the first in a year.** 20,857 net associates added in Q2 after "nearly three quarters" with none; 56,576 sourced, the highest ever; 116 new contracts; headcount 504,000, up 4% YoY and 5% QoQ. In a business where revenue follows headcount with a one-quarter lag, that is the cleanest forward indicator available — and management has already said the September additions will "flow into Q3".

**2. The mix shift gives margin repair without needing price.** Traditional consumer/retail/telecom/BFSI is 80-82% of General Staffing at flat-fee economics; value-added services, manufacturing and construction are 18-20% and growing faster, at higher PAPM. Construction EBITDA grew 71% in the quarter, manufacturing added 7,800 associates (+11% QoQ), and the CEO's own framing is that as the higher-margin block grows "the margin profile for Quess will move upwards". A shift from 18% to 25% of the segment over three years is worth 15-20bps of group EBITDA margin — on ₹17,000 Cr of revenue, ₹25-34 Cr of extra EBITDA, or 10-14% of the current base.

**3. The balance sheet is a strategic asset, not a lazy one.** ₹465 Cr of net cash, zero borrowings, ICRA/CRISIL AA (stable)/A1+ ratings, and a payout record of ₹11 in FY26 against ₹14.84 of EPS (74%). The company can fund the Quess 2.0 build (BCPL, the payroll SaaS module, corridor infrastructure) out of cash flow without dilution, and still return most of free cash flow. In a sector where the closest peer carries 0.14x debt/equity and the demerged sibling Bluspring carries 0.84x, a net-cash staffing business is an unusual thing.

**4. Professional Staffing is a genuinely better business being built inside a worse one.** Gross margin 20.5% against 17.5% two years ago, 71% of revenue from GCCs, a quarter of associates in niche skills, **62 new MSAs signed in H1 — more than all of FY26** — and 437 net headcount additions, the best quarter since FY22. EBITDA margin is temporarily down to 10.5% because of a deliberate investment that management says would have been 11.7% without it. If that converts, the segment is worth more than its 6% of revenue implies: at 46% of group EBITDA it is already the profit engine.

**5. The formalisation tailwind is regulatory, durable and measurable.** 15+ Mn net EPFO additions in FY25, the flexi workforce compounding at 12.6% to a projected 9.16 Mn by FY27, the Labour Codes implemented (which raised Quess's revenue line by ₹298 Cr in H1 purely as a compliance pass-through), and the EPF wage ceiling doubled from ₹15,000 to ₹25,000 — each of which pushes workers from informal to formal, which is exactly Quess's addressable market. Very few Indian businesses have a government policy actively expanding their TAM.

**6. The technology is already in the P&L, not just in the deck.** "The Brain" is 13% of General Staffing hiring with 1.4 lakh leads and 24,700 offers, targeting 35,000-40,000 offers in H2 at "much lower cost and faster time to service". The AI query system handled 18,000 queries in four weeks with more than one in five fully resolved by AI that previously took 24 hours, targeting 50% by December and 80% by March. In a business where sourcing and associate servicing are the two variable costs, that is a margin lever that does not need pricing power.

**7. Institutional ownership is rising while retail exits.** FIIs +1.95pp in the September quarter and +1.68pp over six months; public holding down 2.7pp in six months; shareholder count down 23% in eighteen months. That is the transfer you want to see in a re-rating — and the promoter has not sold a share in five years.

## 13. Bear Case

**1. The earnings are a tax artefact and the auditor says so four quarters running.** Q2 PAT grew 44% on PBT growth of 12.5% because tax swung ₹15.7 Cr, and ₹14.7 Cr of that was a *deferred* tax credit. The FY26 effective rate was 3.3%; H1 FY27 was a net credit. Deloitte's conclusion is qualified on exactly this point, the contingent liability is **₹388 Cr** (7.7% of market cap, 1.4x TTM PAT), and a further **₹845 Cr** of FY24-FY25 deductions is still to be assessed. **On the statutory rate the P/E is 24.9x, not 18.3x.** If the January 2027 hearing goes the wrong way, both the multiple and the earnings fall at once.

**2. A 2% margin business has no room for error.** Employee costs are 94.7% of revenue. A 10bps move in the operating margin is 5% of EBITDA. The company has held 1.9-2.2% for eight quarters — that is stability, not improvement, and the entire re-rating has happened without a margin expansion. Any wage-cost shock, statutory increase or pricing concession goes straight to the bottom line.

**3. Revenue growth is flattered and the balance sheet has absorbed the flattery.** ₹298 Cr of H1 revenue is a Labour Code pass-through with no profit, worth ~3.5pp of the 15% growth. It also added **₹537 Cr to unbilled revenue** and roughly **₹300 Cr to provisions**, taking unbilled to ₹1,311 Cr and non-current provisions to ₹626 Cr. That is a balance-sheet entry dressed as growth, and it will take quarters to unwind.

**4. Professional Staffing is deteriorating at the margin and the excuse may not hold.** EBITDA fell 2% on 13% revenue growth; margin 10.5% against 12.2%; MSP declined ~10%; top-10 client concentration rose from 40% to **46%**. Management calls it a planned investment with returns in 3-6 months and a 12% exit margin. If the exit is 10.5%, the segment is worth less than the market assumes, and it is 46% of group EBITDA.

**5. The closest peer says the sector multiple is too high, not that Quess is cheap.** TeamLease is at 11.3x and 48% below its high; SIS at 29.8x with 6.8% ROE; the two demerged siblings loss-making and de-rated 15-61%. Quess at 24.9x normalised is the most expensive quality staffing name in the set, and it has re-rated into a sector that is de-rating.

**6. The float model is the funding, and it can reverse.** 74% of General Staffing is collect-and-pay — Quess is paid before it pays. That is why the balance sheet needs no debt. But the collect-and-pay share slipped ~1pp in the quarter and management's own explanation is that the newer, higher-margin work (construction, manufacturing, VAS) carries **15-30 day credit**. Growth in the good business therefore consumes the float that funds the bad one. If that ratio keeps falling, the ₹465 Cr of net cash becomes ₹465 Cr of working capital.

**7. Other liabilities are doing a lot of work.** ₹1,297 Cr of other financial liabilities plus ₹364 Cr of other current liabilities against ₹28 Cr of trade payables. Statutory dues, wage accruals and the Labour Code provision all sit there. None of it is improper; all of it is opaque, and it is the mechanism by which a 2% margin business shows a 24% ROCE.

**8. The tape has not confirmed anything, and the biggest session had no disclosed cause.** 13.3% below the high, on R1, ADX 16.7, rejected at ₹356.60, with the August high built on a 43%-of-equity, 10%-delivery session and the 9 November +8% pop on 19% delivery with no filing and no index event. Four months of consolidation, no breakout, and the last month has lagged the Nifty by 5.7pp.

**9. Governance history deserves a line.** The 80JJAA claims were the subject of an **NFRA order against the external chartered accountant** who certified Form 10DA for FY19-21 (subsequently stayed by the Delhi High Court), and the group carries a **₹71.66 Cr provident-fund demand** disclosed as an emphasis of matter. Neither is a finding against Quess's management. Both are reminders that this company's tax and statutory-compliance function has been aggressive enough to attract regulator attention.

## 18. Verdict

**WATCH — an improving franchise with a clean balance sheet, priced on a tax rate that is under challenge.**

The operating case is better than it has been in three years. Quess is a focused workforce-management business now, not a conglomerate: 504,000 associates, ₹16,400 Cr of trailing revenue, **21,000 net associates added in Q2 after three quarters of none**, the highest-ever sourcing of 57,000, 116 new contracts, DSO at 24 days and record collections of ₹4,285 Cr. It earned 24% on capital, holds **₹465 Cr of net cash against zero borrowings**, paid ₹11 a share in FY26 and has declared ₹5 for FY27. It cleared six of seven mechanical gates, failing only durability on a growth record that predates the demerger and is one quarter into a better trend, and the eighth gate — the second engine — passes on evidence: 18-20% of General Staffing now sits in higher-margin verticals growing three to four times faster than the base, Professional Staffing's gross margin is 300bps higher than two years ago with 62 new MSAs signed in six months, and the digital platform broke even.

The problems are arithmetic and they are concentrated in one line of the P&L. **The 18.3x that the screen shows is computed on a 3.3% effective tax rate.** Q2's celebrated 44% PAT growth came on 12.5% PBT growth, with a ₹15.7 Cr tax swing doing two-thirds of the work and ₹14.7 Cr of that being a deferred-tax credit. Deloitte has **qualified the accounts on exactly this point for four consecutive quarters**; the statutory contingent liability is **₹388 Cr**; another **₹845 Cr of FY24-FY25 deductions is unassessed**; and the company's own hearing has slipped to **January 2027**. At the statutory 25.17% rate, TTM earnings give **24.9x, not 18.3x** — a 2.2x premium to TeamLease at 11.3x for the same 1.5%-margin economics, and an earnings yield of 4.02% against a 7.27% government bond.

What makes this a WATCH rather than a pass is that the operating inflection is not a story — it is in the headcount, and headcount leads revenue by a quarter. Management has guided 10-15k net adds a quarter and just did 21k; the higher-margin mix is measurable at 18-20% and growing; and the balance sheet gives the company the option to build Quess 2.0 without dilution. None of that depends on the tax position. **But the price already pays for all of it and for the tax position too:** on the base case the stock is 18.3x this year's reported earnings, 22.5x normalised, 2.98x earnings power value, and it sits exactly on monthly R1 with ADX at 16.7, 13% below a 52-week high that was made on a session where 43% of the equity changed hands at 10% delivery.

**What I would do.** Nothing today. Set the two triggers: a **close above ₹357 on more than 0.6 mn shares with delivery above 40%** is the breakout buy, with a ₹330 stop and ₹389/₹420 targets; a **flush into ₹296-305 that holds** is the better buy, with a ₹285 stop. For a long-horizon holder, accumulating in thirds below ₹310 is defensible because the FY28 arithmetic at 21x normalised is not demanding and the dividend is real — but size it at 3% and treat the January hearing as the event that decides whether this is a 20x compounder or a 25x value trap. For anyone valuing on book, on yield, or on clean earnings, this is a pass.

**The one thing that would kill the thesis:** an adverse ruling on the 80JJAA deduction. Everything else in this note is a reason to watch. That one line is a reason to be careful about size.
