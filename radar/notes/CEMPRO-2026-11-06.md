# Cemindia Projects — Radar Deep Dive

Private note for the multibagger radar. Not published, not deployable, not part of the website. One company, one Screener page (consolidated), paced. Every figure is read off an exchange filing, computed from the exchange's own bhavcopy, or labelled as an estimate.

**The one-line case.** A 95-year-old heavy-civil contractor that the Adani group bought in May 2025, which has since tripled its order book to ₹32,715 Cr and grown revenue at a 27.5% compound rate for four years on 33.5% return on capital — trading at 38x trailing earnings, 22% below its July high, with a results-day candle that went from ₹1,414 to ₹1,287 and a half-year in which operations consumed ₹117 Cr of cash. The franchise is real. The price already pays for it, and the growth now depends on one group's order book.

## 2. What the Company Does

Cemindia Projects is the company formerly known as ITD Cementation India. It has been building in India since 1931 — as a branch of the British Cementation Company, then Trafalgar House, Kvaerner, Skanska, and from 2005 the Italian-Thai Development subsidiary that gave it the ITD name. In May 2025 an Adani group entity, Renew Exim DMCC (now Renew Exim FZCO), bought the 67.46% promoter stake, and the company was renamed in August 2025 and re-tickered CEMPRO in September 2025.

It is a heavy-civil EPC contractor, not a general builder. Eight verticals, with the order book split as follows at 30 Sep 2026:

| Segment | Order book (₹ Cr) | Share | Representative work |
|---|---|---|---|
| Maritime structures | 7,529 | 23.0% | Vadhvan near-shore reclamation, Mundra container terminal, Dahej LNG berth, Vizhinjam, Abu Dhabi LNG jetty, Bangladesh transmission foundations |
| Urban infra, MRTS & airports | 6,432 | 19.7% | Underground metro in Chennai, Bengaluru, Pune, Delhi, Kolkata; Jaipur and Trivandrum airport works |
| Industrial structures & buildings | 6,160 | 18.8% | SAIL Burnpur steel plant civil works, HVDC substation, CPWD redevelopment |
| **Data centres** | **4,905** | **15.0%** | 220 MW in Maharashtra, 100 MW in Andhra Pradesh — **all from Adani Infra (India)** |
| Water & wastewater | 3,375 | 10.3% | Morsagar reservoir, Ganga raw-water intake, Ahmedabad micro-tunnelling |
| Highways, bridges, flyovers | 2,577 | 7.9% | Four/six-laning in Bihar and UP — all from Adani Road Transport |
| Foundation & specialist | 1,257 | 3.8% | Piling at Dahej, Dhamra yard works |
| Hydro, dams, tunnels | 480 | 1.5% | Sivok–Rangpo tunnels, 500 MW pumped storage |

Client mix is 11% government, 25% PSU, **64% private**. Geography is 99% India. What the company actually sells is the ability to do things few Indian contractors can: 8 tunnel boring machines, 5 micro-TBMs, 3 trench cutters, 46 hydraulic piling rigs, 279 cranes and 4 jack-up barges. Management puts the domestic marine segment at three serious players, five including aspirants. That scarcity is the moat, and it is the reason a company with ₹10,000 Cr of revenue earns a 38x multiple.

**The structural fact that dominates everything else.** Adani group entities are the promoter, the largest customer, and the source of nearly all the growth. Management told analysts on 29 July 2026 that of ₹8,000 Cr of Q1 inflows, "₹6,000 crores from Adani group and ₹2,500 crores from the outsider", and that **"today, backlog of ₹31,000, Adani will be around 53%"**. CARE and CRISIL both confirm 52-53% at 30 June 2026. The FY26 related-party note in the annual report quantifies it: **₹2,834.21 Cr of revenue from Adani group entities out of ₹9,982.72 Cr standalone revenue — 28.4%** — in a year when Adani owned the company for only ten months.

The scorecard defines seven quantitative gates. Cemindia passes **six**; valuation is the one it fails.

| Management | Promoter ≥ 35% and stable | 62.84%, one owner, no pledge | Pass |
| Valuation | P/E ≤ 1.5x industry, or ≤ 35 | 38.1 vs industry 14.2 (limit 21.3); also above the 35x absolute cap | **Fail** |

Two of those passes deserve an argument rather than a tick.

**The quality gate passes on a full-year number that the current half-year contradicts.** FY26 CFO of ₹500 Cr against PAT of ₹598 Cr is 0.83x — acceptable for a contractor. But H1 FY27 CFO is **−₹116.65 Cr** against PAT of ₹271 Cr, a swing of ₹413 Cr from the ₹296.46 Cr generated in H1 FY26. The gate is measuring last year.

**The durability gate is measuring a low base.** The 30% five-year figure starts at FY21 revenue of ₹2,728 Cr, a COVID year. From FY22's ₹3,809 Cr the compound rate is 27.5%. Both are true; 27.5% is the honest number.

The eighth gate — the judgement one no ratio captures — is whether the growth has a floor. It does, and it is an unusual kind of floor: a ₹32,715 Cr order book worth 3.25 years of revenue at FY26's run rate. What the ratios cannot tell you is that more than half of that floor sits inside the promoter's own group.

Four things are worth more than the table.

**1. The margin has not expanded. The "EBITDA margin" has.** The investor presentation shows EBITDA margin rising 9.0% → 8.8% → 10.5% → 10.2% → 11.9% across FY22–FY26 and calls it "consistently expanding". That EBITDA line is operating profit *plus other income*. Strip other income and the operating margin is 9.7%, 9.3%, 10.1%, 9.9% — flat for three years and now drifting. Q2 FY27's headline 10.2% is 9.7% once its ₹12.26 Cr of other income is removed. This is a 10% margin business, and it has been for a decade.

**2. FY26's profit contains roughly ₹104 Cr of money that will not recur.** The other-income note breaks ₹176.96 Cr down honestly: ₹34.76 Cr of deposit interest, ₹6.68 Cr on income-tax refunds, **₹50.46 Cr of interest on a settlement/award**, **₹53.05 Cr of foreign-exchange gain**, ₹12.75 Cr of profit on asset disposals, and small items. The award interest is the Delhi International Arbitration Centre award; the FX gain is a translation artefact. Add the disposal profit and about ₹116 Cr of the ₹177 Cr is non-recurring.

**Core P/E, derived, and the derivation matters.** TTM consolidated PAT is ₹625 Cr. One-off other income in the TTM window is ₹103.5 Cr pre-tax (award interest ₹50.46 Cr + FX gain ₹53.05 Cr); at the FY26 effective tax rate of 26.9% that is ₹75.7 Cr post-tax. **Core TTM PAT ₹549 Cr, Core P/E = ₹23,738 Cr ÷ ₹549 Cr = 43.2x**, against a reported 38.0x. This is not a Screener field — it is MCap ÷ (PAT − non-recurring other income), with the other-income composition taken from Note 25 of the FY26 consolidated accounts. Recurring other income (deposit interest, writebacks) is left in, which is why the adjustment is ₹76 Cr and not the full ₹103 Cr.

**3. The balance sheet is the good part.** Borrowings have been flat at ~₹1,000 Cr for three years while revenue grew 30% and equity grew from ₹1,494 Cr to ₹2,619 Cr. TOL/TNW fell from 2.66x to 2.31x; interest cover rose from 3.65x to 4.97x (5.5x in Q1 FY27); 70% of mobilisation advances are interest-free. That is what took the ratings from A+ to AA. It is why a working-capital-intensive business can grow 25% without a leveraged balance sheet.

**4. Working capital is now absorbing cash, and the definitions disagree.** The H1 FY27 cash-flow statement is unambiguous: contract assets up ₹499 Cr, trade receivables up ₹138 Cr, inventories up ₹87 Cr, and after taxes paid, **operating cash of −₹116.65 Cr**. Contract assets on the balance sheet went from ₹2,263 Cr to ₹2,738 Cr in six months. Management reports net working capital of ~120 days (69 receivable days including retention, 30 inventory, 107 WIP). CRISIL independently puts "net working capital intensity" at 65-70 days and expects it below 90. CARE puts gross current asset days at 211, up from 190. These measure different things — CRISIL is net of payables and advances, management is gross of WIP — but the direction is the same in all three: working capital is lengthening as the nascent order book mobilises.

**Distinguishing growth burn from distress burn** (the skill's test): CFO is negative because contract assets are up ₹499 Cr — unbilled revenue on projects mobilising, not receivables going bad. Debtor days improved to 47 from 57. Borrowings fell. Ratings were upgraded twice during exactly this period. This is **growth burn, 🔴 High, not 🔴 Critical** — with one condition: it stops being growth burn the moment receivables, rather than contract assets, become the driver.

**H1 FY27:** revenue ₹5,469.57 Cr (+14.6%), EBITDA ₹565 Cr (10.3%), PAT ₹271.42 Cr (+10.8%), EPS ₹15.80.

**The growth rate is not what the headline says, and this is the single most important number in the note.** Consolidated Q2 revenue grew 25.2%. Standalone — the parent, which is the actual contracting business — grew **17.65%** to ₹2,582.65 Cr. The ₹166 Cr difference is the subsidiary Ceminfra Construction Ltd, which contributed **nil** revenue in Q2 FY26. Same pattern in Q1: consolidated +5.6%, standalone **−0.9%**. For the half year, consolidated grew 14.6% and the parent grew **7.7%**. The subsidiary's ₹166 Cr/quarter comes at a near-zero margin — it added ₹3 Cr of PAT.

So the honest sequence is: parent revenue fell 0.9% in Q1, grew 17.7% in Q2, and grew 7.7% over the half. Against FY27 guidance of 25% for the year, that is a gap, not a trajectory — and it means H2 must deliver **+34%** revenue growth on H2 FY26's ₹5,288 Cr to hit the guidance (Q3+Q4 would need ₹7,106 Cr). A more defensible full-year number is ₹12,000–12,300 Cr, or +19–22%. Q3 FY26 was a soft comparable (+2%), so Q3 FY27 will look good; Q4 is where the guidance lives or dies.

**The margin is compressing, not expanding.** 11.1% → 10.2% on the company's own EBITDA definition, 90 bps in a year. Management's guidance is "10% plus, double digit… I hope that will be maintained." It is being maintained, barely, and only with other income helping.

**What the order book did.** Record ₹32,715 Cr at 30 September, up from ₹24,545 Cr at FY26-end and ₹18,820 Cr a year earlier. H1 inflow ₹12,005 Cr (Q1 ₹8,519 Cr, Q2 ₹3,485 Cr) against a full-year target of ₹25,000 Cr — so H1 has delivered 48% of the year's target. Q2's inflow of ₹3,485 Cr is a step down from Q1's ₹8,519 Cr, and it is worth watching: management said in July that of ₹8,000 Cr of Q1 inflow, ₹6,000 Cr came from the group. Q2's number implies the non-group pipeline is thinner than a ₹90,000 Cr bid book at a 15% hit ratio would suggest.

**The QIP.** ₹1,400 Cr raised on 19 October at ₹1,108/share — a 4.37% discount to the ₹1,158.58 floor price — for 1.26 Cr shares, 6.85% of the post-issue capital. Oversubscribed 2.3x. Paid-up capital rose from ₹17.18 Cr to ₹18.44 Cr. Participants included Motilal Oswal MF, Mirae, BOI MF, Tata MF, ABSL MF, Invesco, Union, Trust, ITI, Bandhan, Edelweiss Life and Nuvama. Note the sequence: the board's enabling resolution was for **₹5,000 Cr** (23 July); the company raised **₹1,400 Cr** — 28% of it. Management had described it as "an enabling thing… completely dependent on the orders which we are trying to secure". That is consistent, but it also tells you the market would not have absorbed ₹5,000 Cr at this price.

⚠️ **Screener's CON — "Promoter holding has decreased over last quarter: −4.62%" — is mechanically wrong as a signal.** Renew Exim held 11,58,92,883 shares before the QIP and holds 11,58,92,883 shares after it. The percentage fell because the denominator grew from 17.18 Cr to 18.44 Cr shares, not because a single share was sold. Verify: 11.589/18.44 = 62.84%. This is dilution, and dilution at ₹1,108 when the stock trades at ₹1,287 is accretive to book value per share. Do not read it as promoter selling.

The DII line is the same event from the other side: 4.79% in September to 11.06% in October is the QIP allocation landing. Motilal Oswal MF crossed 5% — 58,66,463 shares on 21 October through the QIP and the open market, taking its total to 5.16%, disclosed under SAST Regulation 29(1). FIIs drifted down 1.1 percentage points over the year, which for a stock up 49% is rebalancing, not rejection.

Shareholder count is up from 90,782 to 1,03,365 in four months while the stock corrected 35% and then recovered — retail entering on the recovery, not on weakness. No promoter pledge is disclosed anywhere in the FY26 annual report or on the exchange.

**The related-party concentration, quantified from the audited note.** FY26 revenue from Adani group entities, standalone: Adani Road Transport ₹992.72 Cr, Adani Renewable Energy Forty-Two ₹350.47 Cr, Dhamra Port ₹343.59 Cr, Colombo West International Terminal ₹196.45 Cr, Mundra Petrochem ₹175.78 Cr, Jaipur Airport ₹109.24 Cr, Adani Enterprises ₹103.02 Cr, Adani Green Energy Six ₹102.48 Cr, Adani Container Terminal ₹98.54 Cr, and fourteen more — **total ₹2,834.21 Cr of ₹9,982.72 Cr, or 28.4%**. That was ten months of ownership. The order book share is 53%.

This is the thing to hold in your head alongside the 33.5% ROCE. A contractor earning 33% on capital because its parent gives it work at competitive-bid prices is a different proposition from one earning 33% in an open market — and CARE's stated negative rating sensitivity is literally "weakening linkages with Adani group", which tells you the agency sees the linkage as the credit strength. It cuts both ways.

## 7. Delivery and Price Structure

From NSE bhavcopy, 280 unique sessions from 17 Sep 2025 to 5 Nov 2026. Duplicate holiday files (weekends carrying a copy of Friday's bar) were removed — this matters, an un-deduplicated series shows an 18 Jan 2026 bar that does not exist and inflates the one-year return from +49% to +90%.

**Delivery is genuinely high.** Twenty-session average 45.7%; sixty-session average **53.5%**, with 48 of the last 60 sessions above 45%. Against the ~37% NSE large-cap baseline, that is real accumulation, and it held through the 35% correction. This is not the Syrma pattern of a 600% volume spike on 15% delivery.

The results day is the textbook trap: **3.17x the prior 20-session average volume** (527,959 shares) with the lowest delivery of the last six sessions, a 145-point intraday range, and a close 9% under the high. Institutions do not take delivery at 37% on a 16.8-lakh-share day. That is churn — and it happened at a price 14% above the previous close, which is where a seller would want it.

**Price structure.** All-time high ₹1,649.20 on 21 July 2026 (closing high ₹1,639.60). Then a three-month, 35% drawdown to ₹1,066 on 23 October — and the drawdown was orderly on delivery, which is why the recovery from ₹1,066 to ₹1,414 in nine sessions was possible. But 5 November's ₹1,414.30 failed to reach July's ₹1,649.20, so the daily structure since July is a **lower high**, not a new high. The stock is 22% below its peak and has now been rejected once at ₹1,414.

Volume context: 20-session average 5.28 lakh shares ≈ ₹68 Cr/day of turnover. Adequate for a retail position; thin for anything institutional.

## 10. Bull Case

**1. The order book is the highest-quality thing here, and it is 2.7 years long.** ₹32,715 Cr at 30 September, up 74% year on year, with a segment mix where no single vertical exceeds 23%. Private clients are 64% of the book; 90% of contracts carry price-escalation clauses, which is why a 10% operating margin survives a commodity cycle. At ₹12,000 Cr of FY27 revenue the book covers all of FY27, all of FY28 and most of FY29 before a single new order.

**2. Data centres are a genuine second engine, not a slide.** ₹4,905 Cr of order book (15%) across 220 MW in Maharashtra and 100 MW in Andhra Pradesh, with management putting current work at 320–400 MW and claiming capacity for ~500 MW a year on a 24–27 month cycle. This is the highest-margin, least-competitive work in Indian construction right now, and Cemindia has a two-year head start inside the group that is building the most data-centre capacity in the country.

**3. Capital efficiency is in the top decile of Indian construction.** 33.5% ROCE on 0.42x gearing, with borrowings flat at ₹1,000 Cr for three years while revenue grew 30%. TOL/TNW down to 2.31x (CRISIL computes 1.9x on its own definition), interest cover up to 5.5x, and two rating upgrades plus a fresh CRISIL AA in the last eight weeks. Contractors that earn 33% on capital and don't need equity are rare; this one just raised ₹1,400 Cr anyway, which is optionality rather than necessity.

**4. The market has paid for the story with real money.** ₹1,400 Cr QIP oversubscribed 2.3x; ten domestic mutual funds and two insurers on the register; Motilal Oswal MF through 5%; DII holding from 1.94% to 11.06% in seven months. Delivery averaged 53.5% over 60 sessions through a 35% drawdown. That is not retail enthusiasm.

**5. Scarcity plus a policy cycle.** Eight TBMs, four jack-up barges and three real competitors in Indian marine contracting. ₹12.22 lakh Cr of infra capex in the FY27 Budget at 4.4% of GDP, with ports, metros, water and data centres all expanding. A ₹90,000 Cr bid pipeline at a 15% hit ratio implies ₹13,500 Cr of inflow without a single new relationship.

## 11. Bear Case

**1. 53% of the order book and 28% of revenue is the promoter.** This is the thesis risk, not a footnote. CARE's negative rating sensitivity is "weakening linkages with Adani group" — the agency treats the parent's order flow as the credit strength, which means the same variable is simultaneously the bull case and the concentration risk. Related-party revenue was ₹2,834 Cr in ten months of FY26. If group ordering slows, or if the group's own leverage bites — the acquisition debt sits at promoter level, ring-fenced from Cemindia with no recourse per CRISIL, against a holdco portfolio CARE values at over ₹4 lakh Cr — there is no independent order book of the size required to replace it. Q2's ₹3,485 Cr inflow was a quarter at half Q1's pace; the first data point that might be that slowing.

**2. The parent business is growing at 7.7%, not 25%.** Consolidated revenue growth of 14.6% for H1 is flattered by a subsidiary that had nil revenue last year and earns almost nothing on it. Standalone Q1 was −0.9%. Hitting the 25% guidance requires H2 revenue up 34% — Q3 looks easy against a +2% comparable, which means Q4 carries the entire year.

**3. The half-year consumed cash.** CFO of −₹116.65 Cr against ₹271 Cr of PAT, with contract assets up ₹499 Cr in six months. CARE puts 71% of the order book at under 20% progress; that is precisely the phase where unbilled revenue balloons and cash goes out before it comes in. It is growth burn, but it is burn — and it now has to reverse in a six-month window.

**4. Margins are flat and compressing at the margin.** Operating margin 9.7% → 9.3% → 10.1% → 9.9%, with Q2 EBITDA margin down 90 bps year on year. The 11.9% FY26 "EBITDA margin" in the presentation is an artefact of ₹104 Cr of one-off other income. Consensus FY27 EPS needs ~11% margins to work, and management has guided only "10% plus".

**5. The stock failed exactly where it needed to hold.** ₹1,414.30 on 5 November — 3.17x volume, 37.4% delivery, close 9% below the high, 22% below the July peak. The correction from ₹1,649 was 35%, which is not a base. The 20 and 50-day EMAs are inverted. Without a confirmed breakout there is no asymmetry: the downside to the October low is 17%, the upside to the July high is 28%, and the entry is 14% above where the smart money was buying nine sessions earlier.

**6. It is not cheap on any cash-based measure.** Enterprise value of ₹24,511 Cr on ₹1,260 Cr of TTM EBITDA is 19.5x. Earnings yield 2.63% against a 7.12% risk-free rate. Price/EPV 4.7x. On the Howard Marks tests it fails four of five.

## 12. Kill Criteria

The thesis dies — not deteriorates, dies — on any one of these:

1. **Adani group's share of the order book goes above 60%, or group receivables/advances reverse.** The first is visible quarterly; the second shows up as related-party receivables rising faster than related-party revenue in the FY27 annual report. Either converts the growth engine into a related-party credit exposure.
2. **FY27 operating cash flow below ₹350 Cr on PAT above ₹600 Cr** (CFO/PAT under 0.6x) for a second consecutive year. One half-year of negative CFO is mobilisation; a full year is a working-capital problem, and CARE's own trip-wire is interest cover below 3x.
3. **Q4 FY27 revenue growth below 20%** (i.e. below ₹3,568 Cr against ₹2,973 Cr). That would mean FY27 lands under ₹12,000 Cr and the 25% guidance has failed twice in two years — the Kaynes pattern.
4. **Vadhvan still not started at FY27-end.** The ₹7,529 Cr maritime book is 23% of the order book and the single largest segment; management said in July that execution "is yet to start… not in our hands". A second year of zero revenue from the flagship port would strand the largest vertical.
5. **Operating margin below 9.0% for two consecutive quarters.** That is the level at which a contractor's fixed costs stop being absorbed and the 33% ROCE starts to erode.
6. **Any rating outlook revision to negative, or a downgrade.** With borrowings at ₹1,000 Cr and working capital lengthening, the AA is now load-bearing.

## 13. Verdict

**WATCH. Quality business, wrong price, unconfirmed chart, and one counterparty holding half the order book.**

The operating record is not in dispute. Revenue from ₹5,091 Cr in FY23 to ₹10,061 Cr in FY26, ROCE from 19% to 33.5%, borrowings unchanged, ratings from A+ to AA, and an order book that has gone from ₹18,820 Cr to ₹32,715 Cr in twelve months. A company that owns eight tunnel boring machines and competes with two others in Indian marine contracting deserves a premium multiple, and the market has given it one.

But three things are true at once. It trades at 38x reported and 43x core earnings — with a 2.63% earnings yield against a 7.12% government bond — because the market has already priced the order book converting into revenue at better than 10% margins. The parent business, as distinct from its new subsidiary, grew 7.7% in the half just reported. And on 5 November, on the single largest volume day in six months, the stock ran to an all-time-high-attempt of ₹1,414 and closed at ₹1,287 on 37% delivery, which is what distribution looks like when it happens politely.

The honest framing is that this is a **₹12,000 Cr revenue business with a 10% margin and a 53%-related-party order book, priced at 36x forward earnings**. That can be a wonderful investment if the Adani pipeline keeps filling and H2 collects the cash. It is not a trade to enter the morning after the candle that failed.

**What I would actually do.** Do not buy here. Two setups would change that:

- **Breakout:** a daily close above ₹1,414.30 with delivery above 45% and volume above 6 lakh shares. That confirms the July high is in play and puts ₹1,649 on the table with a stop at ₹1,327.
- **Retest:** a pullback that holds the ₹1,230 cluster (20 and 50-day EMAs, S3 at ₹1,216) on delivery above 45% and contracting volume. Entry ₹1,240–1,270, stop ₹1,190, first target ₹1,414, second ₹1,649. Risk/reward roughly 1:2.9 to the second target.

Position sizing for a ₹23,738 Cr contractor whose earnings depend on one group: **maximum 2–3% of portfolio**, not the 5% the small-cap band would normally allow. The concentration is a portfolio-level risk, not just a company one.

**What to watch before the Q3 print (late January 2027):** today's 11:00 IST concall for any change to the 25% guidance or the 53% group share; Q3 revenue against the ₹2,894 Cr that +25% requires; H2 CFO against the ₹350 Cr the year needs; and whether Vadhvan moves.
