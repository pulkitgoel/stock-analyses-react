## 2. What the Company Does

CPCL runs one refinery, at Manali in north Chennai, with 10.5 million tonnes a year of capacity. It is one of the more complex refineries in India — fuel, lube, wax and petrochemical feedstock units in one site — which is why its gross refining margin runs a few dollars above the Singapore benchmark (FY26: $9.28 against a Singapore $5.83, a premium management attributes to crude optimisation, product mix and group procurement).

The product slate is petrol, high-speed diesel, ATF, LPG, naphtha, lube oil base stocks, wax, hexane, mineral turpentine and specialty defence grades including JP-5, JP-7 and ISROSENE. There is a second, 1.0 MMTPA refinery at Nagapattinam (Cauvery Basin) that is being replaced: a 9 MMTPA grassroot refinery and petrochemical complex is being built in a joint venture, Cauvery Basin Refinery and Petrochemicals Ltd, in which CPCL holds 25% and Indian Oil 75%. Those project assets sit on CPCL's balance sheet as a disposal group held for transfer at ₹1,484 Cr.

Ownership: 67.29% with the Government of India and Indian Oil Corporation; foreign institutions 15.79%, domestic institutions 1.18%, public 15.76%. The free float is 32.7%, but with 15.8% sitting with foreign institutions and 1.2% with domestic ones, only about 15.8% of the equity is genuinely in public hands — the single most important structural fact about the stock, and the reason its moves are violent.

**There is no second engine.** Throughput is capped by a 10.5 MMTPA site running at 103-112% utilisation. Growth comes from three places: cracks, the value-added product mix, and a debottlenecking of Manali towards roughly 280,000 barrels a day (about 14 MMTPA) that is talked about but not yet a funded, dated project. The 9 MMTPA Cauvery refinery is a 25%-owned associate on a 2030-31 timeline, which means it is an option on the next decade, not a driver of this one.

**The durability fail is also arithmetically wrong and directionally right.** The 13.3% comes from dividing TTM sales of ₹80,427 Cr by March 2022 sales of ₹43,068 Cr — four years, not five, with a TTM numerator. **The eighth gate fails.** CPCL is one asset, at capacity, whose earnings are the crack spread. The 25% Cauvery stake is real but distant.

| **Net cash from operating activities** | **1,393.24** | **24.86** | **2,944.96** |

₹3,278 Cr of profit produced ₹25 Cr of operating cash. The mechanics:

- **Receivables rose ₹1,772 Cr**, from ₹235 Cr to ₹2,008 Cr. That is nine days of sales, up from one day. In absolute terms it is not a crisis for a refiner; in cash terms it is ₹1,772 Cr.
- **Payables fell ₹2,767 Cr**, from ₹3,890 Cr to ₹1,122 Cr. Creditor days halved from about 22 to about 6. Crude suppliers are being paid faster, which is what happens in a tight physical market where cargoes are scarce and everyone wants cash.
- **Cash and equivalents went from ₹905 Cr to ₹0.28 Cr.** Borrowings rose from ₹1,964 Cr to ₹2,202 Cr. Net debt went from about ₹707 Cr to ₹1,857 Cr, up ₹1,150 Cr, in a half-year in which the company earned ₹3,278 Cr and paid ₹923 Cr of dividends.

So the record half-year was funded by drawing down cash and by not paying suppliers late. Working capital days went from 10 at March 2026 to roughly 41-44 at September 2026 — inventory ₹8,110 Cr, receivables ₹2,008 Cr, payables ₹1,122 Cr against TTM sales of ₹80,427 Cr.

Two honest readings, and they point the same way.

**Benign reading:** this is timing. Receivables at nine days and payables at six days are both extreme, and both revert when the physical market loosens and PSU payment cycles normalise. The company earns this cash back in FY28.

**Uncomfortable reading:** the boom is not self-funding. At $111 Brent, inventory alone is ₹8,110 Cr of working capital and falling prices turn it into a loss. A dividend policy of 30% of profit on peak earnings, paid out of borrowed money, is the classic way a cyclical PSU returns capital it has not yet collected. Both readings imply the same posture: do not pay peak-cycle prices for these earnings.

## 11. Bull Case

**1. The cracks are structural, not just a spike.** The physical market is broken: a war in the Gulf, a Chinese fuel export ban, diesel scarcity, and Western refineries that deferred maintenance and now have to take it. BP's refining indicator margin was 46.7 in the third quarter and 43.6 quarter-to-date in the fourth — the margin has not faded into October. EIA raised its Q4 Brent forecast by $14 to about $105 on 6 October. CPCL earns $9-11 a barrel above Singapore in a normal year, and Singapore is running at record spreads.

**2. The earnings are real and the balance sheet proves it.** FY26 repaid ₹7,274 Cr of debt since FY22 while paying ₹62 a share. Net worth is ₹13,593 Cr, debt/equity 0.16, and ICRA reaffirmed A1+ commercial paper in September. This is not a leveraged refiner that will be wiped out by one bad year; FY25 was a bad year and the company still made a profit.

**3. CPCL is the cleanest way to own Indian refining.** Indian Oil, BPCL and HPCL are losing money on retail because pump prices are frozen. MRPL and CPCL are not — they sell at market. The peer table shows the split: −124%, −23% and −6% for the marketers against +217% and +212% for the refiners. If you want the crack, you buy the refiner without the marketing drag.

**4. The dividend is large and the payout is consistent.** 30% of profit for three years, ₹62 in FY26, ₹25 already declared for FY27. At a 30% payout on FY27 EPS of ₹339-430, the FY27 dividend is ₹100-129 a share — 6.6-8.5% at today's price. Even at a normalised ₹205 of EPS, a 30% payout is ₹62, a 4.1% yield.

**5. The operating base is intact and slightly improvable.** Throughput of 11.71 MMT in FY26 was a record at 112% utilisation; H1 FY27 ran at 103% despite a planned turnaround. Debottlenecking towards 280,000 bpd is a genuine if undated option, and the value-added slate — lube base stocks, hexane, wax, ATF — earns a premium over fuel. Crude blending has been improved specifically to reduce dependence on a single grade for LOBS.

## 12. Bear Case

**1. You are buying the best margin in the company's history at the multiple the market pays for a normal one.** Q2's $20.41 included $3.82 of inventory gain — that is a price move, not a refining achievement. Strip it out and the operational GRM was $16.59, still extraordinary. Normalise to $8-9 and EPS is ₹205-265, and the stock is at 6-7.5x, roughly where it has always traded. There is no re-rating here, only an earnings cycle.

**2. The profit is not turning into cash.** H1 FY27: ₹3,278 Cr of PAT and ₹24.86 Cr of operating cash flow. Receivables up ₹1,772 Cr, payables down ₹2,767 Cr, cash down from ₹905 Cr to ₹0.28 Cr, net debt up ₹1,150 Cr. Working capital went from 10 days to about 42. If the physical market stays tight, working capital stays locked; if it loosens, margins fall. Either way the investor is not being paid in cash today.

**3. Nobody is accumulating this in the tape.** Average delivery 21% and falling; the four biggest October volume days delivered 9.0%, 12.6%, 15.7% and 13.3%. The 7 October gap of 14.7% on 5.4x volume was reversed within six sessions. With 67.3% held by the government and Indian Oil, only about 15.8% of the equity is in public hands, so the price is set by whoever is trading that day — and in October that has been day traders, not funds.

**4. There is no growth engine.** One site, at capacity. The 9 MMTPA Cauvery refinery is 25%-owned through an associate, has been reconfigured and delayed to a 2030-31 completion, and carries a cost reported at ₹36,354 Cr — a project that consumes capital in this decade and produces earnings in the next. Capex on CPCL's own books is only ₹930 Cr a year, which is the number of a business with nowhere to invest.

**5. Policy is a live risk, not a theoretical one.** The government owns 67.29% and sets the dividend. Export duty on diesel and ATF was already "higher than the cracks" and hurt FY26, per management. In 2022-23 the same government asked refiners to share the cost of holding retail prices down. An extended war that pushes OMC losses further into the open raises the probability of a windfall levy or a subsidy-sharing mechanism that reaches CPCL's export margins — the single fastest way to convert a $20 GRM into a $10 one.

## 13. Kill Criteria

Written down before the outcome is known, so the review is not an argument with hindsight.

1. **Q3 FY27 GRM below $10/bbl.** That is roughly half the Q2 operational margin and would take FY27 EPS towards ₹300. Any print below $10 says the crack has normalised and the stock's 4x becomes a 6-7x on falling numbers.
2. **Brent sustained below $85 with cracks compressing.** There is ₹8,110 Cr of inventory on the balance sheet. A $20 fall in crude with a lagging product price is an inventory loss, not a gain — FY25 is the template, when a $4.22 GRM turned ₹63,148 Cr of sales into ₹214 Cr of profit.
3. **Any government move to extend subsidy-sharing or a windfall levy to refiners.** One notification and the earnings base halves. This is the risk that cannot be modelled from the accounts, only observed in Delhi.
4. **FY27 operating cash flow below 50% of PAT.** If the working capital released in H1 does not come back by March, the dividend is being funded by borrowing, and the balance sheet repair of FY22-FY26 reverses.
5. **A cash call from Cauvery Basin Refinery and Petrochemicals.** A ₹36,354 Cr project with a 25% partner needs equity. If CPCL is asked to fund more than its share, or to guarantee debt, the "modest capex" virtue disappears.

## 16. Verdict

**WAIT. Do not open a position at ₹1,521.40.**

CPCL is a good business having its best year, at a price that already reflects it.

The bull arithmetic is genuinely attractive: 3.97x trailing, 2.96x EV/EBITDA, a 4.08% yield, 34.9% ROCE, debt/equity 0.16, and a physical product market where the world's diesel is short and the refiners are the toll collectors. The peer table makes the point better than any argument: the refiners grew profit 212-217% while the marketers lost money, and CPCL has the highest return on capital in the group.

Three things stop it being a buy here.

**The multiple is not a discount, it is the norm.** CPCL traded at 4.6x on FY26 earnings in March and 3.4x on FY24 earnings before that. The stock doubled because EPS went from ₹208 to ₹383; the multiple actually compressed. On a normalised $8-9 GRM the stock is at 6-7.5x — mid-pack against Indian Oil at 5.5x and MRPL at 7.7x. Anyone buying "4x PSU refiner" is buying the peak of a war-driven crack and calling it value.

**The profit is not cash.** ₹3,278 Cr of H1 profit produced ₹25 Cr of operating cash flow, receivables rose ₹1,772 Cr, payables fell ₹2,767 Cr, cash fell to ₹0.28 Cr and net debt rose ₹1,150 Cr — while ₹923 Cr of dividends went out. That is a company returning capital it has not collected, in the quarter its margins peaked.

**The tape says the same thing.** Average delivery 21%, the four largest volume days at 9-16%, a 14.7% gap on 7 October fully reversed within six sessions, and a 25-point checklist score of 16.5 with a critical Fail on breakout confirmation. This stock is being traded, not accumulated, on a 15.8% public float.

**What to do instead.** Let it come to you. The two levels that matter are ₹1,377-1,430 — the 22 October low and the 50-day average, which is where the last two pullbacks stopped — and ₹1,677.50, the 11 September high. Buy the first with delivery above 30% on the turn, or buy the second with delivery above 35% and volume above 1.5x, and size for a 6.2% average daily range. If neither happens, the correct outcome is to have missed a stock that was already up 98% in a year.

For an existing holder the case is different and simpler: the dividend and the balance sheet justify holding through the cycle, with a stop below ₹1,325 — the 5 October low — because below that the October structure has failed and the next reference is the 200-day average at ₹1,185, which is 22% lower.

**Suitable investor profile.** This is a cyclical income position, not a compounder. The right owner is someone who wants a 4-7% dividend yield from a debt-light PSU refiner and accepts that the capital value will oscillate with a crack spread set in the Gulf. It is not suitable for anyone who needs the earnings they are buying to be repeatable next year, because on the company's own history they are not.

**Where this note could be wrong.** If the war-driven product shortage runs through FY28 — BP's margin indicator has not faded in October, and EIA's 2027 Brent forecast of $84 assumes shut-ins fall — then FY27 EPS of ₹430 and FY28 of ₹500+ make ₹1,521 a 3x stock, and the delivery data is just the noise of a thin float. The evidence for that outcome is the durability of the crack, which is a geopolitical question this note cannot answer. What it can say is that at this price the investor is not being paid to guess.
