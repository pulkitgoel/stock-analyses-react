## 2. What the Company Does

LTTS is Larsen & Toubro's listed engineering research and development arm. It does not run IT operations or write business applications for banks; it does product engineering — designing, testing and sustaining the physical and embedded products its clients sell. 24,035 employees across 21 design centres, 31 sales offices and 111 innovation labs, serving 430 active clients including 69 Fortune 500 companies. 55.9% of delivery is offshore, 35.6% is fixed price, and the top five clients are 15.7% of revenue (top 20: 41.0%).

Three reporting segments, Q2 FY27:

| Segment | Revenue ₹ Cr | Share | EBIT margin | YoY revenue |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Sustainability | 1,156.6 | 38.0% | 29.5% | +13.4% |
| Mobility | 981.9 | 32.3% | 16.9% | +13.5% |
| Tech | 901.9 | 29.7% | 12.0% | −0.6% |

**Sustainability** is the profit engine and the surprise. It is not ESG consulting — it is plant engineering for oil and gas, chemicals and LNG, plus industrial products: data centres, electrification, energy transition, robotics, smart manufacturing and CPG. It earns a 29.5% EBIT margin, roughly double the group, and has gone from a volatile project business to 38% of revenue on the back of large multi-year deals won over the last six quarters. Management has reorganised it into four buckets (oil and gas/chemicals, FMCG, energy and automation, industrial machinery) and says the annuity share has risen. Half of Q2's large-deal wins were here.

**Mobility** is automotive (about half the segment), aerospace, rail, and trucks and off-highway. US auto is executing, aerospace is growing strongly on production expansion, rail is growing on rolling-stock modernisation. European auto is the hole: "muted", with management expecting no further decline but recovery only in 9-12 months.

**Tech** — semiconductors, telecom infrastructure, media, MedTech, and the Systems & Platforms business bought with Intelliswift — is the laggard at a 12% margin. One MedTech programme ramped down on schedule and the follow-on phase does not start until H2. The offset is the new Data Center Engineering business line and the AI-led telecom win, which began ramping in Q2.

**The strategic bet is "Engineering Intelligence" (EI).** AI-led revenue is $120M annualised, 9.5% of the quarter, and management says more than half of Q2's $203M of large-deal TCV was AI-led. 16,000 engineers (67% of the workforce) have been upskilled, 1,808 patents are filed, and there are partnerships with NVIDIA (a joint EI lab), Anthropic, Databricks, Cognite and MIT Media Lab. Whether this is a real moat or a relabelling exercise is the central question in the bull and bear cases below.

**Ownership:** Larsen & Toubro 73.51%. Free float 26.49%, of which domestic institutions hold 13.76% and foreign institutions 4.43% — roughly 2.8 crore shares, about ₹27 Cr of daily traded value before the October re-rating. This is a thin float for a ₹44,000 Cr company.

| Second engine | Human read | Qualified pass | Three segments, but one business model |

Three of those need reading carefully.

**The durability fail is arithmetically wrong and directionally right.** The script divides TTM sales of ₹11,626 Cr by March 2022 sales of ₹6,570 Cr — four and a half years, not five. The true five-year CAGR (FY21 ₹5,450 Cr to FY26 ₹10,996 Cr) is 15.1%, which would pass. But the three-year CAGR is 11.1% (FY23 ₹8,014 Cr to FY26 ₹10,996 Cr), profit grew 2.9% over the same three years (₹1,174 Cr to ₹1,281 Cr), and the operating margin fell from 22% in FY22 to 18% in FY26 before recovering to 19% now. The gate reached the right conclusion by accident: this has not been a durable compounder since FY23.

**The valuation pass is a technicality that has now expired.** At ₹4,174 the continuing TTM P/E is 33.0x, which is 1.67x the industry median of 19.8x and above the 29.7x ceiling the relative test allows. The gate passes only because its fallback clause accepts any P/E under 35. On the gate's own logic, the stock is at the limit.

**The eighth gate is a qualified pass, and the qualification matters.** LTTS has three revenue segments, and two of them are growing double digits with expanding margins — that is more than most engineering outsourcers have. But all three are the same engine: billable engineers, billed in dollars, delivered from India. When the Rupee moves 8% the whole company moves 8%; when a client insources, a segment disappears. The diversification is by end-market, not by business model.

One caveat on this table before it is read: FY26 is on the continuing-operations basis the company now reports, while FY24 and FY25 are as originally reported and still include Smart World. The company has not published restated FY25 revenue, so the 14.0% growth figure is not strictly like-for-like — the base carries a business the numerator does not. The distortion is small (Smart World contributed ₹5-12 Cr of annual profit) but it means the underlying growth was somewhat better than 14%.

Read vertically, this is a company that doubled revenue between FY21 and FY24 and then stopped making more money. EPS was ₹110.77 in FY23 and ₹120.68 in FY26 — 2.9% a year for three years. The reason is visible in the margin line: EBITDA margin fell from 21.5% in FY22 to 17.6% in FY26 as wage inflation outran pricing, and EBIT margin fell from 18.3% to 14.5%. FY26's 14% revenue growth was bought at the cost of 100 basis points of margin.

**Q2 FY27 is the first quarter that breaks that pattern:** EBIT margin 16.3%, up 220 basis points year on year, with gross margin up in all three segments. That is the entire investment case in one number.

Three other things are worth stating plainly.

**Cash conversion is genuine and has always been genuine.** CFO/EBITDA of 75-83% and free cash flow above ₹1,250 Cr in each of the last three years, on capex of ₹100-240 Cr. This is an asset-light business that converts profit into cash — the opposite of the capex-heavy names this ledger usually carries.

**Debtor days fell from 95 to 67 in FY26.** That is the Smart World (SWC) business leaving the consolidation — a city-surveillance and smart-infrastructure unit with government and municipal receivables. Selling it did more for the balance sheet than for the P&L.

**Other income is shrinking even as cash grows.** ₹283 Cr in FY25, ₹200 Cr in FY26, ₹111 Cr on a continuing TTM basis, against ₹4,590 Cr of cash and investments. Some of that is forex losses netted into the line — the CFO said the ₹17 Cr of Q2 other income was struck "while absorbing forex volatility". Falling treasury income at a company with rising cash is worth watching; it means the yield or the currency hedging is costing something.

**The headline is an accounting event, not a business event.** Reported profit rose 66.8%. Continuing profit rose 19.6%. The difference is ₹168.6 Cr of profit from discontinued operations, of which ₹165 Cr is the one-time gain on the sale of the Smart World business that closed in the quarter. The company said so on the call, in the release, and in note 4 of the filing. Broker notes, wire copy and the tape all led with the 66.8%.

**And most of the revenue growth is currency.** The realised rupee went from ₹88.40 to the dollar in Q2 FY26 to ₹95.90 in Q2 FY27 — an 8.5% depreciation. Dollar revenue grew 3.3% (4.8% in constant currency). 1.033 × 1.085 = 1.121 — which is precisely the 12.1% rupee growth. So of every ₹100 of incremental rupee revenue, roughly ₹70 is the exchange rate and ₹30 is more work. This is not a criticism of the company — everyone in Indian IT had the same tailwind — but it is the difference between a business growing 12% and one growing 3% in the currency its clients pay in.

**What is genuinely improving is productivity and mix.** Headcount went from 23,359 to 24,035 (+2.9%) while rupee revenue grew 12.1%, so revenue per employee rose from ₹11.62 lakh a quarter to ₹12.65 lakh, up 8.9%. Offshore mix rose from 52.1% to 55.9%. Fixed-price mix rose from 32.1% to 35.6%. Sustainability — the 29.5%-margin segment — went from 34.7% to 38.0% of revenue. Those three things, not currency, are why EBIT margin rose 220 basis points.

**The Q2 quality-of-earnings detail:** other income of ₹30.9 Cr is 8% of continuing PAT, well below the 20% threshold that requires a Core P/E adjustment. Interest of ₹13.9 Cr is lease interest. There is no exceptional item in Q2. The quarter was clean; the headline was not.

## 6. Where the Profit Came From

This section exists because Screener and every screen built on it will mislead you on this stock, and because the same trap caught the CG Power note in this ledger last month.

Screener's quarterly table shows "Other Income" of **₹200 Cr** for September 2026, against ₹34 Cr in the prior quarter and ₹77 Cr a year earlier as Screener reports them (the filing's restated continuing figures are ₹29.1 Cr and ₹65.7 Cr). A reader applying the standard rule — other income above 20% of PAT means the profit is not operating — would conclude that 36% of this quarter's profit was non-operating and mark the stock down accordingly.

**That ₹200 Cr is ₹30.9 Cr of real other income plus ₹168.6 Cr of profit from discontinued operations,** which Screener folds into the same line. The gain is real cash — ₹493 Cr of proceeds landed in investing cash flow — but it is a one-time item from a business that no longer exists, and it will not recur.

The correct treatment:

| | ₹ Cr |
|---|---|
| Reported PAT, Q2 FY27 | 548.7 |
| Less: profit from discontinued operations | (168.6) |
| **Continuing PAT, Q2 FY27** | **380.1** |
| Continuing other income | 30.9 |
| Other income as % of continuing PAT | **8.1%** |
| Core P/E adjustment required? | No |

So: no Core P/E adjustment on operating grounds. But a **basis** adjustment is essential, because the reported trailing P/E of 28.8x carries about ₹18.6 of discontinued EPS — Smart World's four quarters including the ₹165 Cr gain — and Screener's 30.6x treats the same items differently. On continuing operations the trailing multiple is **33.0x**, and every valuation statement in this note uses that number.

## 12. Bull Case

**1. The margin inflection is real and it is being driven by mix, not accounting.** EBIT margin went from 14.1% to 16.3% year on year, with gross margins up in all three segments. The drivers are visible in the operational table: offshore mix 52.1% to 55.9%, fixed price 32.1% to 35.6%, and the 29.5%-margin Sustainability segment from 34.7% to 38.0% of revenue. Revenue per employee rose 8.9% on 2.9% headcount growth. Management hit its mid-16% aspiration two quarters early and has guided to hold it through a Q3 wage hike of 50-80 basis points.

**2. The order book is converting, which was the market's specific complaint.** For six quarters the knock on LTTS was that it announced large-deal TCV that never showed up in revenue. Q2 answered it: $203M of TCV, more than half AI-led, with the $75M, $30M+, $25M and six $10M+ wins now ramping. Pipeline is up more than 25% year on year and backlog is up. Management also conceded it owes $100M from a light Q1 and has committed to making it up in Q3-Q4 — a specific, checkable promise, which is better than a vague one.

**3. AI revenue is quantified, not narrated.** $120M annualised, 9.5% of the quarter, growing from a much smaller base last quarter, on a definition the COO gave on the record ("a significant part of the deliverable is created through leverage of the AI stack"). That is more disclosure than most Indian IT companies provide. If AI-led revenue compounds from $120M while the legacy book shrinks, the mix shift alone lifts margins, because management says it prices at a premium.

**4. The balance sheet is a genuine asset.** ₹4,061 Cr of net cash, no borrowings, ₹1,455 Cr of operating cash flow against ₹1,281 Cr of profit, free cash flow above ₹1,250 Cr in each of the last three years on capex of ₹100-240 Cr. A 48% payout with room to raise it. In a market where the rest of this ledger's names are funding growth from debt, LTTS funds a dividend from operations and still sits on 9% of its market value in cash.

**5. The sector and the currency are both tailwinds.** Nifty IT is up 12.8% over six months against the Nifty's −4.8%, and the rupee at 95.90 gives every Indian IT exporter an 8.5% revenue tailwind that flows almost straight to margin. LTTS leads its own sector on relative strength by 10.7 points over six months.

## 13. Bear Case

**1. You are paying 33x for 3% dollar revenue growth.** This is the bear case and everything else is a detail. Revenue rose 12.1% in rupees and 3.3% in dollars; constant-currency growth was 4.8%. The company's own five-year aspiration is a 13-15% revenue CAGR, which on current evidence is a hope rather than a trajectory: the three-year revenue CAGR is 11.1%, the three-year profit CAGR is 2.9%, and the only quarter in three years with meaningful margin expansion is the one just reported. At 33x, the multiple already assumes the aspiration is met.

**2. The market bought a number that does not exist.** Reported PAT rose 66.8%; continuing PAT rose 19.6%. The ₹165 Cr Smart World gain is one-time, the business is sold, and the ₹168.6 Cr of discontinued profit will not appear again. Every headline on 21 October led with the 66.8%. When a stock gaps 10.7% on a number that has to be adjusted before it means anything, the move is built on a misreading — and misreadings get corrected.

**3. The tape says this was trading, not accumulation.** Delivery of 8.01% on 108x volume, 20.4% average across the five sessions against a 42.1% baseline, 39% of the free float turned over with 11% of it taken for delivery. ₹3,280 Cr of turnover on 21 October of which roughly ₹263 Cr changed ownership. No block or bulk deal explains it. This is what a thin float does when a headline lands: the same shares change hands twenty times.

**4. The research community is below the price.** Consensus target ₹3,764 — 10% below the current price. JM Financial raised to ₹3,890, Motilal Oswal is Neutral at ₹3,850, Prabhudas Lilladher is Hold at ₹3,390. Only Anand Rathi, at ₹4,336, is above the market. When a stock trades through the sell side's average target, either the sell side is about to revise or the stock is ahead of itself; both resolve, and the second resolves downward.

**5. The float, the beta and the liquidity are a trap for position sizing.** 26.5% free float, beta 0.22, ₹26 Cr of daily traded value before the re-rating and ₹155 Cr after. A ₹44,000 Cr company that trades ₹26 Cr a day is a company where a mid-sized fund cannot build or exit a position without moving the price — which is precisely why the October move was violent in both directions. The 4.4% average daily range is not a temporary condition; it is what a thin float does.

**6. Tech is not fixed.** The segment grew −0.6% year on year at a 12% margin, the MedTech follow-on programme does not start until H2, management guided Tech to be "muted" in Q3, and media is described as "steady" in the way managements describe businesses that are not growing. A third of revenue is stuck.

## 14. Kill Criteria

Written down before the outcome is known, so the review is not an argument with hindsight.

1. **Q3 FY27 EBIT margin below 15.5%.** The entire bull case is that 16.3% is a floor, not a peak. Management already flagged a 50-80 basis point wage-hike drag; a print below 15.5% means the drag was larger than guided and the "mid-16%" claim was a two-quarter artefact.
2. **Constant-currency revenue growth below 4% for two consecutive quarters.** Dollar growth, not rupee growth, is the business. At 3-4% CC the 13-15% aspiration is dead, and 33x on 4% growth is indefensible.
3. **Tech segment revenue declines again in Q3.** Management guided it to be muted, which allows flat. A second consecutive decline means the segment is shrinking, not pausing.
4. **Delivery staying below 25% for two more weeks while the price holds above ₹4,000.** That would confirm the October move as a churn event with no institutional buyer behind it, and would make the ₹3,830-3,950 retest a probability rather than a possibility.
5. **DII holding falling below 12.5% in the December quarter.** Domestic institutions have been the marginal buyer for three years (8.41% to 14.64%). The September trim of 0.88 points was small; a second consecutive cut would mean they are selling into the rally, and there is no other buyer of size.
6. **Any large acquisition paid for in cash or stock at a premium.** LTTS bought Intelliswift and has now sold Smart World; the ₹4,061 Cr of cash is 9% of market value and the market is pricing the possibility that it gets spent. A dilutive deal at 33x would remove the balance-sheet support for the multiple overnight.

## 17. Verdict

**WAIT. Do not open a position at ₹4,174.20.**

LTTS is a good business having its first genuinely good quarter in three years, at a price that has already paid for it.

The bull arithmetic is real: a 27% ROCE, no debt, ₹4,061 Cr of net cash, 1.14x cash conversion, a 48% payout, EBIT margin up 220 basis points with the drivers visible in offshore mix and segment mix, $203M of TCV converting to revenue, AI revenue quantified at $120M annualised, and a five-year Nokia engagement announced this week. Against Cyient at 33.7x on a 12.3% ROCE or Tata Technologies at 41.4x on 3.6% growth, LTTS at 33.0x is the cheapest quality name in its own peer group.

Four things stop it being a buy here.

**The growth is thinner than the headline.** Revenue rose 12.1% in rupees and 3.3% in dollars, because the rupee went from 88.40 to 95.90. Profit rose 66.8% as reported and 19.6% on continuing operations, because ₹165 Cr of the gain was the Smart World sale. Both adjustments are in the filing, neither was in the headlines, and the stock gapped 10.7% on the headlines.

**The multiple is not a discount, it is the middle of the range.** LTTS traded at 40x in October 2024, 39x at its December 2025 peak, 26x at the March 2026 bottom, and 33.0x now. Earnings were ₹123.28 in FY24 and ₹120.68 in FY26 — flat. Two years of share-price movement was entirely multiple. At 33x the price already assumes two years of guided growth and a 25x exit; on the Howard Marks tests it fails all four.

**Nobody accumulated this move.** Delivery was 8.01% on the largest volume day in the stock's recent history, 20.4% average over five sessions against a 42.1% baseline, with 39% of the free float turned over and 11% of it taken for delivery. ₹3,280 Cr of turnover on 21 October, ₹263 Cr of it real. There was no block deal, no promoter sale, no index event — just a thin float and a headline.

**The score is 14.0 out of 25.** Four fails, on volume quality, price action, EMA alignment and delivery. The trend has turned; the entry has gone.

**What to do instead.** Let it come back.
