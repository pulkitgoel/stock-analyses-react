# Mastek — Radar Deep Dive

Private note for the multibagger radar. Not published, not deployable, not part of the website. One company, one Screener page (consolidated), paced. Every figure below is read off an exchange filing, computed from the exchange's own bhavcopy, or labelled as an estimate.

**Why this note starts with an apology to the screen.** The radar added Mastek on 26 October at ₹1,761.60 — the close of 23 October, the same evening the company filed its Q2 result. The seven gates are balance-sheet and P&L ratios; not one of them reads an order backlog, a dollar revenue line, or a margin guidance. By the 3 November close the stock was ₹1,458.30, **17.2% below the price the screen used**, and it fell 22.5% from its 21 October high of ₹1,888 in six sessions. That is the story this note has to answer: not "is Mastek cheap", but "what did the screen fail to see, and is the cheapness now real".

## 2. What the Company Does

Mastek is a 44-year-old Ahmedabad-founded IT services firm that has been rebuilt twice: first as an Oracle applications implementer, then as a UK public-sector specialist, and now as what management calls an "AI-native" engineering and transformation company. Revenue in Q2 FY27 was ₹1,018.7 Cr ($107.3m).

The shape of the business matters more than the description:

- **Geography is the risk.** UK & Europe is **67.8%** of revenue, North America 21.2%, AMEA 11.0%. The UK share has risen, not fallen, through the year (64.5% → 67.3% → 67.8%). A single government's procurement mood moves two-thirds of the P&L.
- **Vertical mix:** Financial Services 44.4%, Government & Education 16.9%, Manufacturing & Technology 18.4%, Healthcare & Life Sciences 11.1%, Retail/Consumer 9.2%. Financial services is the growth engine (+16.4% QoQ), and it is mostly UK — Bank of England, FCA and similar arms-length bodies.
- **Contract mix:** 57.1% time-and-material, 42.9% fixed-price. The T&M majority is why UK rate-card policy passes straight into margin.
- **Concentration:** top 5 clients 33.3% of LTM revenue, top 10 45.5%. Moderate for the size.
- **People:** 4,810 employees, of whom 3,171 are in India; utilisation 83.4%; LTM attrition 15.2%, down from 18.5% a year ago.
- **Balance sheet:** net cash, no leverage, DSO 74 days (management calls it best-in-class), and a business that converted ₹542 Cr of operating cash flow in FY26 on ₹404 Cr of PAT.

The bull and bear case both sit in one sentence: a cash-generative, net-cash, 10x-earnings UK public-sector IT franchise whose dollar revenue has started to shrink while AI compresses the price of the work it sells.

## 3. Why It Cleared the Eight Gates

The radar runs seven mechanical gates plus one judgement gate. Mastek scored **5 of 7**.

| Gate | Result | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Headroom | Pass | Market cap ₹5,062 Cr at the screen price, well under the ₹25,000 Cr ceiling |
| Reinvestment | **Fail (marginal)** | ROCE 17.7% against an 18% bar — misses by 0.3 points |
| Durability | **Fail** | 5-year sales CAGR 12.0% on the gate's own window (TTM ₹3,848 Cr vs FY22 ₹2,184 Cr), margin 16% against 18% four periods earlier |
| Quality | Pass | CFO/PAT 1.34x, debtor days 57 (gate: ≥0.8x and ≤90 days) |
| Balance | Pass | Debt/equity 0.12 against a 0.5 ceiling |
| Management | Pass | Promoter 35.75%, above the 35% floor, and flat across five periods |
| Valuation | Pass | P/E 11.5 against an industry screen of 18.7 and a 35x absolute ceiling |

Both failures deserve to be read precisely, because one is an artefact and one is real:

- **Reinvestment (17.7% vs 18%)** is a hair's breadth miss and not meaningful. ROCE was 32% in FY22 and has halved as the company bought growth through acquisitions and as margins slid from 21% to 16%. The direction is the signal, not the 0.3-point gap.
- **Durability** is the honest failure. Revenue compounded 16.5% from FY21 to FY26, which would clear the gate — but profit compounded 9.9% over the same five years, and the last two years delivered 10.0% revenue CAGR and 14.0% profit CAGR. The gate is measuring a decelerating business, not a compounder.

**The eighth gate — judgement — does not pass either.** The test is whether the second engine has revenue attached. Mastek's second engine is AI: 53 AI deals closed in Q2, ~45% of the order book, a $25m US federal Salesforce win. But AI order bookings *fell* from $64m in Q1 FY27 to **$41.3m in Q2 (−35.5% QoQ)**, and the company will not disclose AI revenue until Q3. The engine exists; the revenue line is not yet visible.

**What the gates could not see.** All seven read the annual P&L, the balance sheet and the shareholding pattern. None reads the 12-month order backlog, the dollar revenue trend, or the margin guidance — the three things that broke the stock. The screen priced a 12.8x P/E on 23 October; the market re-priced it to 10.6x on facts the screen does not ingest. That is a design limit worth recording in the ledger, not a one-off.

## 7. Where Growth Is and Is Not

Geography, Q2 FY27:

| | QoQ (₹) | YoY (₹) | Share of revenue | Total backlog ($m) Q2FY26 → Q1FY27 → Q2FY27 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| UK & Europe | +4.1% | +14.0% | 67.8% | 334.8 → 360.5 → **321.9** |
| North America | +0.9% | +1.3% | 21.2% | 82.8 → 113.5 → **122.3** |
| AMEA | +3.6% | −7.8% | 11.0% | 63.7 → 54.1 → 60.3 |

The UK grew and its backlog fell ₹38.6m in a quarter (−10.7%). That combination — current growth strong, forward book shrinking — is what a business looks like when it is executing an old order book and not replacing it at the same rate. The causes management gave are specific and checkable:

- A **$25m US federal department deal** won last quarter has had its start deferred until after the US mid-term elections (held 3 November 2026, the day before this note). That is a live catalyst either way.
- A **UK contract already won** is being consumed slower than planned; management hopes for a Q4 pickup.
- **UK public-sector decisions stalled** through the change of prime minister and cabinet; several pipeline orders were put on hold or cancelled pending the budget.
- **Healthcare & Life Sciences in the UK is down more than 25% YoY.** Management's explanation is a phase gap: projects that finished discovery/alpha need re-contracting before beta, and the government has been slow to organise the bills. A six-month bridge contract is in place; the larger replacement being bid is "north of £100 million" and is an H2 event.

Offsetting: North America's backlog is up 47.7% YoY to $122.3m, the third consecutive quarter of growth, and management is sticking to a second-half turnaround there. AI order intake is 45% of the book. Management also disclosed a pipeline of "$100 million plus" deals to be adjudicated over six to nine months — a number that could reset the backlog in a single transaction, in either direction.

**The AI order-booking series is the most misread number in the quarter.** From the company's own decks: $7m (Q2 FY26) → $16.9m (Q3) → $27.5m (Q4) → **$64.0m (Q1 FY27)** → **$41.3m (Q2 FY27)**. Deal count rose 43 → 53 over that last step, so the average AI deal fell from **$1.49m to $0.78m**. The headline "53 AI deals, 45% of the order book" is true and is being read as acceleration; the value of AI bookings halved while the count rose. That is consistent with management's own description — transformative AI deals run $0.5–5m, and the large AI *productivity takeouts* that could move the needle are still being pitched, not won.

Note what is *not* here: no order wins were announced through the exchange in the forty-five days to 3 November. The NSE filing record across that window is results, transcript, an investor-meet notice, a leadership appointment, and a press release about expanding the Trichy delivery centre by 150–200 seats. No contracts. For a company whose bull case rests on deal momentum, the announcement flow is thin.

## 8. Cash Flow and Working Capital

Nothing in this section is broken, and that is the point. CFO ₹542 Cr in FY26 against EBITDA ₹586 Cr (121% conversion), FCF ₹515 Cr, net cash ₹552 Cr, DSO 74 days improved by one day in the quarter. Mastek collected $120m from customers in a $107.3m revenue quarter.

The one caution: DSO of 74 days is high for the peer set (Screener's debtor-days calculation on the fiscal year is 57, which nets something the quarterly DSO does not). The difference between 57 and 74 is the difference between annualised and period-end receivables; the company's own number is the more conservative one and it is stable. The Q2 write-off of old receivables inside the exceptional item is a small reminder that UK public-sector collections are not always clean.

**Working capital is not a risk here.** Unlike a project or metering business, there is no inventory, no commissioning cycle, and no customer-funded capex. The cash conversion is the reason the stock has a floor at all.

Three facts, in order of importance:

1. **Foreign money has been leaving for two years.** FIIs 14.40% → 5.93% since March 2024, a 59% relative reduction and 8.5 percentage points absolute. This is not one quarter of profit-booking; it is a full cycle of de-ownership, and it started long before the Q2 miss.
2. **Domestic institutions have absorbed all of it and more.** DIIs 7.07% → 22.01% over the same span, including +3.0pp in the September quarter alone. The marginal buyer of this stock is an Indian mutual fund. That is a support, not a vote of confidence in the growth story — and it means the DII flow is now the thing to watch.
3. **Promoters are flat at 35.75%** — eight quarters of 35.75–35.79%. Stable in the sense the radar's gate means. But in December 2025 the promoter group was reported (CNBC-TV18, confirmed by ICICI Direct on 3 Dec 2025) to be **exploring a sale of roughly 20% of the company at up to a 25% premium**, citing succession planning and leadership stability. No transaction has been announced nine months later. Treat it as a standing binodal option: a strategic buyer at a premium re-rates the stock; the absence of one leaves it where it is.

Shareholder count has drifted from 1.03 lakh (Mar 2025) to 87,785 — weak hands leaving a falling stock, which is normal and mildly healthy, and not a signal on its own.

**Bulk and block deals: essentially none in 2026.** Trendlyne's full history shows one 2026 entry — 28 August 2026, Microcurves Trading Pvt Ltd, 264,122 shares bought and sold intraday at ₹1,839–1,842, an HFT round trip, not accumulation. Every other 2025–26 entry is Graviton, Musigma, NK Securities, XTX or QE — all algo desks, all both-sides. The last genuine institutional block was ICICI Prudential MF buying 500,000 shares on 4 September 2024 at ₹2,751. **Nothing in the block-deal record supports the idea that institutions have been quietly accumulating this fall.**

I cannot attribute it from the public record, so I am not building any conclusion on it; it is recorded because a 17%-of-equity turnover day with 6.5% delivery is not normal and should be re-checked against the annual report's shareholding schedule when it is published.

## 15. Bull Case

1. **The price already assumes stagnation.** At 10.6x trailing and 9.4x FY28E, with ₹552 Cr of net cash (12% of market cap) and ₹515 Cr of FY26 free cash flow, the operating business is being valued at roughly ₹3,971 Cr — 6.6x EBITDA and 1.03x sales. You are buying a net-cash, dividend-paying franchise for less than the market pays for most industrials with cyclically-peaked earnings.
2. **The UK, which is two-thirds of revenue, is still growing.** +4.1% QoQ, +14.0% YoY in Q2, with EBIT held at 18%. Financial services there grew 16.4% QoQ on the Bank of England/FCA franchise.
3. **North America is finally inflecting on the leading indicator.** Total backlog $82.8m → $113.5m → $122.3m over three quarters, +47.7% YoY. If it converts, it diversifies the UK concentration for the first time in five years.
4. **The backlog miss is timing, not cancellation.** Two identified causes — a $25m federal start deferred past the mid-terms and one UK contract consuming slowly — both of which management says will be consumed. The mid-term election is now past (3 November), which converts a "when" into an "if".
5. **Domestic institutions are buying.** DIIs 7.07% → 22.01% over nine quarters, +3.0pp in the September quarter alone, absorbing a 59% relative cut by FIIs. Somebody with a research desk is underwriting this.
6. **A promoter stake sale is a live, unpriced option.** The promoter group was reported in December 2025 to be exploring a ~20% sale at up to a 25% premium for succession reasons. Nine months on, nothing has happened — but a strategic or PE buyer at a control premium is a genuine re-rating path that has nothing to do with the order book.

## 16. Bear Case

1. **The dollar top line is shrinking, and that is the thesis-breaker.** Q2 USD revenue −0.8% YoY, H1 −1.6%. The 8.3% rupee growth is FX, not demand. A company at 10x earnings with falling dollar revenue is not cheap; it is priced for decline.
2. **Margin has broken the 16% floor and management will not date the recovery.** 14.4%, down 121bps YoY, with the company's own words being "the velocity of improvement will depend on macro and geopolitical factors". The 200bps of mix damage is *structural* — UK localisation and security-cleared-resource requirements raise the onsite cost base permanently. You do not fix that by being more efficient; you fix it by re-pricing contracts the government buys on rate cards.
3. **AI is deflating the price of the work faster than it is adding volume.** Management said in April 2026 that renewals are being won at **10–15% discounts** because of AI-driven competition. Its own AI productivity deals are described as "price aggressive" with compressed margins by design, against 35–70% productivity commitments. The AI transformation deals carry 40–50% gross margin; the productivity deals, which are the volume, do not.
4. **The 68% UK concentration is a single point of policy failure.** A new prime minister, a new cabinet, a budget in the same week, stalled departmental decisions, several pipeline orders "put on hold or cancelled", and a healthcare vertical down more than 25% YoY because the government is slow to re-contract. UK total backlog fell $38.6m in one quarter.
5. **Order intake is deteriorating at the leading edge.** 12-month backlog −7.7% QoQ. AI order bookings $64m → $41.3m (−35.5% QoQ) while the deal count rose from 43 to 53 — the average AI deal halved. Healthcare the weakest vertical. No order wins announced to the exchange in 45 days.
6. **The technical and flow picture is uniformly bad.** Below nine of nine MAs, RSI 35.6, −32.7% against a sector at −9.2%, distribution-grade delivery on the down days, and foreign institutions down 8.5 points in two years with no block-deal evidence of anyone accumulating the fall.

## 17. Kill Criteria

The thesis as a *value* idea dies if any one of these happens. Each is observable within two quarters.

1. **FY27 dollar revenue comes in below FY26** — H1 is already −1.6%; a second half that does not grow in dollars makes the full-year decline a fact rather than a risk.
2. **12-month backlog prints below $270m in Q3** — a third consecutive sequential decline, or UK backlog below $300m, means the order book is being consumed faster than it is replaced.
3. **Q3 EBITDA margin below 14.0%** — that would make 14.4% a peak rather than a trough, and puts the "back to 16%" ambition out of reach.
4. **DII holding falls in the December quarter** — the marginal buyer stopping is the flow signal that removes the floor.
5. **Promoter holding drops below 35%** without an announced buyer — that fails the radar's own management gate and removes the strategic-sale option at the same time.
6. **A close below ₹1,334.20** — the 52-week low, and the last technical reference. Below it there is no level until the FY24 range.

## 20. Verdict

**WATCH — do not buy. The valuation is genuinely cheap; the business it is valuing is shrinking.**

Mastek fails the multibagger test on its own terms. A multibagger needs a growing top line and a rising return on capital; this company has −1.6% dollar revenue in H1, a margin down 121bps, ROCE halved from 32% to 17.7% in four years, and 13 of 25 technical checks failing. What it does have is real: ₹515 Cr of free cash flow, ₹552 Cr of net cash, a 1.65% dividend, a 10.6x trailing multiple and a UK franchise that is still growing. That combination makes it a candidate for a value portfolio. It does not make it a candidate for this one, and the 9.0/25 technical score says the market has not finished repricing it.

The honest framing of the next two quarters: this is a **binary wait on two numbers**. If Q3 shows the 12-month backlog stabilising above $300m and the margin back above 15%, the market has mispriced a cash machine and the stock re-rates toward the 13–14x it traded at in July. If Q3 shows a third sequential backlog decline and a 14% margin, then 10.6x is not cheap — it is the correct multiple for a business with a shrinking dollar revenue base, and the stock goes to its FY24 range.

**What I would do:** nothing today. Re-underwrite after Q3 (late January 2027) against the six kill criteria. If the backlog and margin both hold, the entry is above ₹1,600 on a confirmed reclaim of the 50-DMA, not at ₹1,458 on the way down; the risk of catching a value trap at 10x is that 10x becomes 8x on the next print. If someone already owns it, the position is defensible on cash flow alone, but the DII flow is now the only thing holding the price and that is not a thesis.

**Radar disposition:** leave in the ledger as `analysed` with outcome `watch`. It does not graduate to the watchlist for accumulation until the dollar revenue line turns, and it should not be scored as a multibagger candidate — the durability gate failed for the right reason.
