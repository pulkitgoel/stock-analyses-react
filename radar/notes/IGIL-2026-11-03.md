## 3. What the Company Does

IGI is an independent gemological laboratory. It grades and certifies diamonds, coloured gemstones and finished jewellery, and issues a report that fixes the 4Cs — carat, colour, clarity, cut — that the trade prices against and the consumer relies on. Founded in Antwerp in 1975, headquartered in Mumbai, owned by Blackstone since 2023.

Three revenue lines, all fee-based:

- **Lab-grown diamond certification (54% of certification revenue in Q2 FY27).** Loose stones and finished jewellery. IGI was the first laboratory to certify lab-grown diamonds, in 2005, and industry estimates put its share of global lab-grown grading above 70%. This is the growth engine.
- **Natural diamond certification (roughly 30%).** A mature, flat-to-slow market where IGI is taking share rather than riding growth.
- **Coloured gemstones and other (6% and rising).** Built around American Gemological Laboratories (AGL), acquired for US$9 million cash and consolidated from January 2026.

The economics are those of a certification monopoly in miniature: revenue is a fee per report (average realisation ₹891 per report in Q2 FY27), the cost base is laboratories and graders, and incremental volume drops through at very high margin. EBITDA margin was 61.4% in Q2 FY27 and has not been below 57% in any of the last twelve quarters. Capex was ₹81 Cr against ₹624 Cr of operating cash flow in the fifteen months to March 2026.

Where the work physically happens is the structural point. The stones are grown, cut and polished in India, and graded in India; the retail demand that creates the need for a certificate sits in the United States, Europe and the Gulf. India standalone produced ₹3,056 Mn of ₹3,980 Mn group revenue in Q2 FY27 — three quarters of the revenue and effectively all of the grading capacity.

Why the two failures are artefacts:

- **Durability.** IGI listed in December 2024. Screener's P&L shows Dec 2022, Dec 2023, Dec 2024 and a 15-month period to March 2026, because the company changed its financial year from calendar to March. There is no five-year series to test, so the gate returns "unknown" and scores it a fail. The underlying record is 46% sales CAGR from CY2022 to CY2024 and 27% revenue growth in H1 FY27 — but the honest position is that a company with twenty-two months of listed history has not demonstrated durability through a full cycle.
- **Management.** The screener file holds three shareholding columns. The company's own filings show 76.55% in all eight quarters since listing — perfectly flat. The gate's "falling" label is a data gap. A Blackstone-controlled promoter at 76.55% is stable in the sense the gate means, though it carries a different risk the gate does not test (section 9).

The eighth gate is judgement, and it passes clearly: the **second engine** has revenue attached. AGL contributed ₹244 Mn of certification revenue in Q2 FY27, up from ₹57 Mn a year earlier, taking gemstones from 2% to 6% of revenue; Italy opened this year; Saudi Arabia is being prepared; a Botswana company was incorporated in September 2026.

**The momentum score is still wrong and still worth recording.** The radar's score for IGIL was 41.0/100, component detail reading "sales -8.8% YoY (0/10), profit -6.3% YoY (0/10)". Those figures compare TTM revenue of ₹1,457 Cr against the **fifteen-month** FY26 column of ₹1,598 Cr. A twelve-month period measured against a fifteen-month period always shows a decline. IGIL's latest quarter grew revenue 30.9% and profit 43.1%. Any name in the universe that has just changed its financial year is under-scored the same way; the scorecard should normalise the last annual column to twelve months before computing growth. I have not changed the scorecard — that is a separate decision — but the score should not be read as a verdict on the business.

Three observations:

1. **This is a lab-grown story and the fee is holding.** The bear case for a lab-grown grader is that the stone price collapses, the certificate becomes disproportionate to the asset, and fees get competed away. ARP rose 4% year on year. That is the single most important data point against the bear case.
2. **Natural diamond certification is not growing and is 30% of revenue.** Natural jewellery +3%, natural loose +8%, in a market management described as flat, with high and volatile gold prices making buyers slower to commit. IGI is taking share in a pool that is not growing, which works for a while and then stops.
3. **Gemstones is small but is why the "second engine" gate passes.** AGL went from ₹57 Mn to ₹244 Mn in four quarters, the only line not a function of diamond volumes.

## 8. Cash Flow and Working Capital

H1 FY27: operating cash flow ₹4,157 Mn, 89% of EBITDA and 118% of PAT, helped by a ₹662 Mn working-capital release as receivables fell from a peak of ₹2,885 Mn to ₹2,402 Mn. Cash and bank ₹10,749 Mn at September 2026.

Screener's two standing warnings, addressed rather than repeated:

- **"Working capital days have increased from -103 days to 113 days."** True, and misleading as a red flag. A certification business collects on or before delivery and carries almost no inventory, so working capital days were negative in the IPO year. The move to 113 days is receivables building as the customer mix shifts towards larger growers and retail chains who negotiate credit. The absolute amount is ₹451 Cr on TTM revenue of ₹1,457 Cr. It has stabilised — debtor days 66 at March 2026, receivables down 17% in the September half, conversion 94% of EBITDA. A business-model change, not a liquidity problem, but it does mean the days-of-cash cushion is thinner than the headline margin suggests.
- **"Stock is trading at 9.83 times its book value."** Descriptive and structurally expected. A fee business with 69% ROCE and almost no fixed capital will always trade far above book.

## 16. Bull Case

1. **A certification franchise on the volume side of a structural shift.** Lab-grown loose certification +37% and lab-grown jewellery +47% in the latest quarter, with grower capacity still being added. IGI has graded lab-grown since 2005 and industry estimates put its global share above 70%.
2. **The pricing bear case has not appeared in the numbers.** ARP rose 4% year on year in a quarter when lab-grown prices were flat to falling. The fee is 2-3% of retail — small enough that a retailer will not risk an uncertified stone to save it.
3. **Operating leverage is measurable.** Revenue +31%, EBITDA +39%, PAT +43% in Q2. Incremental certification revenue lands at roughly 70% margin because the labs, equipment and graders are already there.
4. **Balance sheet with options.** Net cash ₹925 Cr, ROCE 69%, CFO/PAT 0.94, capex 5% of revenue. It can open labs (Italy, Saudi Arabia, Botswana), buy capability (AGL for US$9 million) and pay dividends out of cash flow without touching debt.
5. **Guidance was raised, not cut.** The May guidance of 15% revenue and 20% EBITDA growth has been effectively replaced by "similar to H1" — 27% and 34%. For a stock that de-rated on growth doubts through 2025, that is the specific thing that had to change.
6. **A second engine with revenue attached.** AGL took gemstones from 2% to 6% of revenue in four quarters, and its methods are being pushed into the other laboratories.
7. **The market agrees, with money.** Four analysts, mean target ₹504, no sell ratings, and a Q2 EPS beat of 17% against the ₹3.55 consensus (₹4.16 printed).

## 17. Bear Case

1. **One segment. Half the revenue. A deflationary end market.** 54% of certification revenue is lab-grown loose stones, and the sequential number was flat (₹2,095 Mn to ₹2,111 Mn). YoY growth is coming from grower capacity running hotter, not from price. If growers stop adding reactors or the wholesale price resumes falling, the fee pool stops growing — and the market is paying 25.8x for a business that would then be a 12% grower with a 46% net margin and no second act.
2. **GIA is attacking the price point and management's answer is assertion.** GIA moved away from full 4Cs reports for lab-grown towards a simplified assessment priced at about US$15 per carat. Asked directly, the CFO said the 4Cs "are not going to go away at all" and that the trade needs them to establish commercial value. That may be right. It is not evidenced in the transcript, and a cheaper accepted certificate is the one thing that breaks a 61% margin.
3. **Grading integrity is the entire franchise and it has been questioned publicly.** Two analysts on the latest call raised grading inconsistency and asked whether certificate-printing rights had been given to large Surat growers. Management denied the printing rights and defended the process as ISO-accredited with multiple review layers. Nothing is proven. But a laboratory's asset is credibility, and a single grading scandal would be terminal in a way a bad quarter never is.
4. **The promoter must sell, and will sell more than it must.** 76.55% held by a Blackstone vehicle, SEBI's 25% minimum public shareholding biting by December 2027, and the prospectus itself flagging promoter disposal for exactly that reason. At minimum ₹267 Cr of stock must come to market; a private equity fund eleven years into a hold will clear more than the floor. Against a ₹4,036 Cr float that is a real supply event.
5. **A third of revenue is a market that is not growing.** Natural jewellery +3% and natural loose +8% in a flat market. Winning share in a shrinking pool has a floor.
6. **No comparable, no anchor.** No listed Indian gemological peer; GIA and HRD are private. Every valuation argument is against the company's own twenty-two-month history or against a peer set of real estate platforms — a genuine information disadvantage.
7. **The quarter had a quiet flaw.** Q2 was the best quarter in history on every headline, and the largest segment did not grow sequentially.
8. **New: the tape rejected the first attempt at a new high.** ₹420.60 was tested on 30 October with 46% delivery behind the selling and the stock closed 5.4% below the high. The stock now needs to absorb that supply before the breakout can extend, and the volume on the pullback has fallen to a third of average — meaning no one is currently building a position to take it out.
9. **New: the consensus is arithmetic-soft.** The ₹504-526 targets rest on FY27 EPS that implies a net margin the business has never printed (50.6% against 46.7% delivered) or a second half 25% larger than the first. If the street is modelling ₹18.72 and the company delivers ₹17.35, the target moves down with the estimates.

## 18. Kill Criteria

Written before the outcome is known, to be judged at the review date rather than argued with hindsight.

- **Primary kill:** lab-grown average realisation per report declines year on year for two consecutive quarters while volumes still grow. That is certification becoming a commodity and it removes the floor under a 61% EBITDA margin. One quarter is noise; two is a repricing.
- **Secondary kill:** any regulatory or exchange action, class action, or sustained press investigation into grading integrity at IGI's laboratories. Nothing is pending; this is the tail risk that ends the thesis outright.
- **Third:** promoter sell-down materially above the minimum-public-shareholding requirement (more than about 3% of equity) in a single transaction, or a block at a discount to market.
- **Fourth:** natural diamond certification turning negative year on year for two quarters, which would mean the 30% base is shrinking faster than lab-grown can compensate.
- **Fifth (new):** two consecutive quarters of flat or declining lab-grown loose-stone revenue. One flat quarter is a stocking pattern; two would mean grower capacity has stopped being added, which is the entire growth assumption.
- **Thesis-neutral but review-triggering:** a close below ₹368, which invalidates the breakout without invalidating the business.

## 21. Verdict

**WATCH, upgraded at the margin — the technical objection has weakened, the valuation objection has not.**

The business case is unchanged and it is the best the radar has surfaced. A 69.2% ROCE, net cash, 94% cash conversion, 61% EBITDA margin and 27-31% revenue growth in a franchise that grades more than 70% of the world's lab-grown diamonds is a real moat, built on being first in 2005 and on 4.35 million reports a quarter of accumulated grading data. Q2 was the best quarter in the company's history on every line and management raised guidance rather than trimming it.

What has changed since 28 October is the evidence about who was buying. The draft's case against entering rested on the 20.6% delivery on the peak-volume day. The three sessions since printed 42.6%, 46.2% and 45.2% on a fraction of that volume — the churn was absorbed, not distributed into. The overbought reading also defused correctly: RSI 79.8 to 74.7 with the price unchanged, and Bollinger %B back inside the band from 1.04 to 0.845.

What has not changed is the price. At 25.8x trailing and 22-24x forward the stock is fairly valued for this quality, not cheap, and the ₹504 consensus target is 29x my base FY27 EPS — it needs 27% growth to continue and a margin the business has never printed. And the tape has now shown its first refusal: ₹420.60 on 30 October with 46% delivery behind the sellers, and a 5.4% intraday reversal.

The business risk that matters is not another laboratory winning share — it is lab-grown certification becoming a commodity priced per carat rather than per report. The evidence says it is not happening: ARP rose 4% year on year. But the largest segment was flat sequentially, GIA is now selling a cheaper simplified grade, and a third of revenue comes from a natural-diamond market that is not growing.

**What I would do:** ₹381-391 is the band, ₹368 is the stop, ₹420.60 and then ₹445-450 are the targets. Risk 18 to make 34-64, roughly 1:2 to 1:3.3. For a long-horizon holder a first tranche at market with a second on a retest into ₹375-385 is defensible at 22-24x. Do not chase above ₹410 into a second test of ₹420.60 — the Friday session is the record of what that looks like.
