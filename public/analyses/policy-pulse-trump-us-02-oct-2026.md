## The September Jobs Report Breaks the Fed's Hike Case: +29,000 Payrolls, Unemployment at 4.2%

**The single most market-moving number of the week printed on Friday morning in Washington: the US economy added just 29,000 nonfarm payroll jobs in September**, against a Dow Jones consensus of 84,000 and an LSEG poll estimate of 90,000. The unemployment rate rose to **4.2%** from 4.1%, leaving **7.1 million people counted as unemployed**. Economists had expected the jobless rate to hold at 4.1%.

The revisions were worse than the headline. **July was revised down by 31,000, from a gain of 21,000 to a LOSS of 10,000. August was revised down by 29,000, from 162,000 to 133,000.** Taken together, employment in July and August was **60,000 lower** than previously reported. Average hourly earnings rose just **5 cents, or 0.1%, to $37.81**, and are up **3.0% year-on-year**. The average workweek held at 34.4 hours.

The market reaction was immediate and unambiguous: **stock futures climbed and Treasury yields retreated** as traders took the miss as a signal that the Federal Reserve will hold at its **October 27-28** meeting. Charles Schwab's Joseph Brusuelas put it bluntly — the report "will take an October rate hike off." The September hike, the first in three years, now looks like a one-and-done for the year, with December the next live meeting.

Context matters here. This is the same labour market that produced a 162,000 August print, an ADP estimate of 90,000 for September, and a week of hawkish Fed commentary. Fed Vice Chair **Philip Jefferson** had said the day before that officials "may need more time" before deciding on another hike. New York Fed President **John Williams** said there was "no need for urgency." The 29,000 print does the rest of the work for them.

## Trump Goes Direct on Diesel: Europe Agrees to Release Reserves, Brent Breaks Below $100

**President Trump announced on Truth Social that Europe has agreed to release emergency diesel stocks.** Quoting the post: *"Europe has just agreed to release a massive amount of their heavily stocked Diesel Oil. The process will begin immediately."*

It follows a Reuters report that EU countries were **considering tapping diesel reserves** to relieve soaring fuel prices and to head off Trump's threat to **ban US diesel exports**. European trade chief **Maros Sefcovic** said he had discussed tight diesel supplies and price spikes with USTR Jamieson Greer and expressed Europe's desire for a coordinated approach. The White House had been pressing the EU to release emergency stocks.

The market response was sharp: **Brent crude fell more than 3% to below $100 a barrel** at one point on Friday, after settling at **$102.31 on October 1** (up 4.4%) and after WTI had risen 2.7% to $92.87. This is a **product-market intervention, not a crude-supply one** — the tight leg of the global oil market through this conflict has been refined products, particularly diesel, where prices have been setting records.

That distinction matters for reading the trade. Middle East *crude* flows have largely normalised — JPMorgan estimates regional exports at **17.5 million bpd, or 98% of pre-war levels**, and India's West Asian crude imports rose **74% month-on-month to 2.10 mbd in September**. It is the refined-product complex that remains dislocated, and that is exactly what a diesel stock release addresses.
