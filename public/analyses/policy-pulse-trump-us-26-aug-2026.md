### 🇺🇸 Major Story 1: Canada Trade War Escalates — Section 338 Tariffs

President Trump has invoked **Section 338 of the Tariff Act of 1930** — a rarely used provision (first presidential use in 96 years) — to impose **50% tariffs** on Canadian goods including automobiles, dairy products, and alcoholic beverages. The move came after trade negotiations collapsed over the weekend.

**Key Details:**
- **Effective Date:** August 22, 2026 (immediate)
- **Coverage:** ~$20 billion of Canadian exports to the U.S.
- **Canada's Response:** Retaliatory tariffs of up to 50% on $20 billion of U.S. goods, effective September 8, 2026
- **Tariff Structure:** Most rates at 15-25%, but steel/aluminum tariffs doubled to 50%

**Affected Products:**
- Canadian autos, trucks, auto parts
- Dairy products (cheese, milk)
- Alcoholic beverages (beer, wine, spirits)
- Wood products, hockey equipment

**Strategic Context:**
The Section 338 invocation represents Trump's pivot to older trade laws after the Supreme Court struck down IEEPA-based emergency tariffs in February 2026. Unlike Section 301, Section 338 requires a finding of "discrimination against U.S. commerce" and allows tariffs up to 50%. The 30-day implementation window has already begun.
