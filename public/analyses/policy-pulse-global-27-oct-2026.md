Korea's third-quarter GDP is the cleanest available read on where the AI capex cycle actually is, and it landed at 1.0% quarter-on-quarter against a 0.7% forecast. The Bank of Korea attributes roughly half of that growth to chips. The KOSPI rose 3.09%.

That single number explains most of what happened across Asia today, and it explains the split.

## First: Korea grew on factories, not on consumers

**Q3 GDP +1.0% q/q versus +0.7% expected.** The composition is the story:

- **Exports +2.3%**, led by semiconductors and electrical machinery and equipment.
- **Facility investment +4.1%**, driven by machinery for semiconductor manufacturing.
- **Construction +0.7%**, on civil engineering.
- **Imports +2.4%**, on crude oil and chip-making equipment.
- **Private consumption fell — the first decline in nearly four years.**

Chase that through. Korea's expansion is being funded by other people's AI capex, and the machinery line is the tell: the country is buying the tools to make the chips that the world's hyperscalers have already contracted for. The household sector is not participating.

**The index reaction: KOSPI 5,638.34, up 168.87 points or 3.09%.** The Korea Exchange triggered a buy-side sidecar at 10:13am, suspending program buy orders for five minutes — the first such action in four trading sessions, and a mechanical consequence of KOSPI 200 futures rising at least 5% and holding for a minute. SK Hynix rose 5.18% to 1,341,000 won, Samsung Electronics 4.23% to 222,000 won. The KOSDAQ added 2.8%.

A sidecar is not a buy signal. It is the exchange telling you the move was fast enough to need a circuit. What matters is that it fired on the buy side, six weeks after the AI complex was being sold on debt-funding fears.

## Second: Japan up, China down — the split is the signal

**Nikkei 225 63,268.37, up 1,078.72 points or 1.73%**, with Advantest up 9.6% and SoftBank Group up 4.95%. Chip-testing and AI-investment names carried the index.

**Hang Seng 23,041.32, down 170.49 points or 0.73%. Shanghai Composite 3,648.30, down 49.22 points or 1.33%.**

**Two figure corrections worth stating, because both appeared in wire copy today.** The AP report carried Shanghai down 0.3% at 3,648.30, which does not reconcile with Monday's close of 3,697.52 — the arithmetic gives -1.33%, and -1.33% is what is used here. The same report carried the Hang Seng at 23,077.56, down 0.6%, which was a mid-session print; the close was 23,041.32.

So the day's shape is: AI hardware jurisdictions up, Chinese platforms down. That is the opposite of a broad risk-on move, and it is consistent with a market that is paying for compute capacity and not for consumer internet exposure.

## Third: AI capex is being funded with equity now, and that changes the risk

**Meta increased its largest-ever equity fundraising to $97.5 billion** — up from the $95 billion announced a day earlier — to fund AI infrastructure. Roughly $35 billion of the initial tranche is common and mandatory convertible preferred stock, including about $22.5 billion of Class A common and $12.5 billion of Series A mandatory convertible preferred. Nvidia, AMD and Broadcom have together shown interest of up to $5 billion.

**Anthropic filed its S-1 with the SEC on Monday for a Nasdaq listing under the ticker ANT**, disclosing steep losses alongside rapid revenue growth and unspecified security concerns.

**Why this matters more than the individual filings:** through October the AI complex was being sold because the capex was debt-funded into rising borrowing costs, with the 10-year near 4.99%. Equity-funded capex moves the risk from the credit market to the shareholder register. It is a different risk, not a smaller one — and the market has spent two sessions rewarding the switch.

The test is this week. **Alphabet, Microsoft and Amazon all report**, more than a third of the S&P 500 reports this week, and the question being asked of each of them is whether the capex is showing up in revenue. Jefferies has flagged that Magnificent Seven earnings growth could trail the rest of the index — 21% against 32%.

## Fourth: oil fell for a second session on diplomacy, not on supply

**Brent settled Monday at $115.81, down 91 cents, after touching $117 during the session.** Today it traded down again across the session — $114.68 early in Asia (down 0.98%), $114.80 by mid-session (down 1.0%), and $115.15 shortly after the open (down 0.57%) — with WTI between $100.83 and $101.32. Reuters, at 9:57am ET, described Brent slipping for a second straight session while still holding near its highest since late April. Murban was $117.67.

**One figure does not reconcile and is discarded.** An aggregator carried Brent up 0.5% at $116.34. That is internally consistent with the $115.81 settle, but it contradicts Reuters' own description of a second down session and conflicts with three independent quotes in the $114.68-115.15 range. The direction of the trade was down.

**The cause was diplomatic.** Reported high-level contact between Saudi, Turkish and Pakistani officials and Iran's leadership in Tehran on Monday — described as the first such meeting among the four, ending with an agreement to keep talking — eased supply fears. What did not ease: Houthi attacks on Saudi Arabia continued.

**This is the most reversible premium in the market and it is being defended politically.** The President called his own ceasefire a waste of time this week; the White House has ruled out a strike before November 3; and reporting points to prepared plans for afterwards. A diplomatic headline and a strike headline can therefore take $6 off or add it back inside a week.

## Fifth: the Fed decides tomorrow, and the market has already decided

**FOMC decision Wednesday October 28 at 2pm ET, 11:30pm IST.** A hold in the 3.75-4.00% range is close to fully priced, with roughly an 11% chance of a hike this week and about 82% for December. The tradeable part is the December language, not the decision.

Immediately behind it: **September PCE and Q3 GDP on Thursday October 29.** Core PCE is the number that decides whether the December hike pricing survives. The 10-year is at 4.99%, which is the discount rate being applied to every AI capex projection above.

**The frame that ties this article together:** Meta is spending $34.9 billion of quarterly capex against $32.8 billion of operating cash flow, which means negative free cash flow, which is why it just raised $97.5 billion of equity. At a 5% 10-year, that arithmetic is the reason a chip selloff shows up in Tokyo and Seoul and not in Chinese platform stocks.

For India, the transmission is through the dollar and flows rather than through earnings. FPI outflows are already at a record $32.56 billion year-to-date, and a hawkish hold pushes the same direction.
