## RBI Hikes the Repo Rate to 5.50% and Switches to "Calibrated Tightening"

The **Reserve Bank of India's Monetary Policy Committee (MPC)**, meeting **October 5–7** under Governor **Sanjay Malhotra**, **raised the policy repo rate 25 basis points to 5.50%** on **Wednesday, October 7** — the **first hike since February 2023** and the end of four consecutive holds (RBI press release 2026-2027/1264). The vote to hike was **unanimous**.

- **Corridor:** the **Standing Deposit Facility (SDF) rate is now 5.25%**; the **Marginal Standing Facility rate and the bank rate are 5.75%**.
- **The stance was changed from "neutral" to "calibrated tightening"** — reported as a **4–2 majority** decision. Malhotra: **"rate cuts are off the table in the near term… policy action ahead can only be a rate hike or a pause,"** with the extent and duration of the cycle contingent on **underlying inflation, the broadening of price pressures and second-round effects of the supply shock**.
- **Growth revised up, not down:** FY27 real GDP is now projected at **7.1%, a 40 basis point upgrade**, with the quarterly path at **Q2 7.2%, Q3 6.9%, Q4 6.8%**.
- **Inflation revised up:** FY27 **CPI projection raised to 5.2%**.
- **The stated rationale:** inflation has become **"more generalized and persistent"**; supply-side risks remain from **crude and commodity prices and adverse El Niño conditions**; and **high growth momentum gives the MPC space to tighten**.
- **The RBI's own risk list is notable:** **"uncertainty about fair valuation of AI stocks"** and an **"elusive resolution of the West Asia conflict"** are named as downside risks to the global outlook, alongside nervous global financial markets.

## The 57th GST Council Slips a Second Time, to October 8

The GST Council Secretariat issued **Office Memorandum F.No.1392/57th GSTCM/GSTC/2026 dated October 5**, rescheduling the **57th GST Council meeting** — originally September 12, then October 7 — to **Thursday, October 8, at 11:00 a.m. in the Summit Room, Bharat Mandapam, New Delhi**, citing "unavoidable circumstances." This is the **second postponement**.

Finance Minister **Nirmala Sitharaman** has said the agenda is **process reform** across **registration, returns, refunds, dispute resolution and the flow of input tax credit**. Reported (not decided) proposals:

- an **~17-day refund architecture** (up to 10 days for acknowledgement, about 7 for processing, with deemed acknowledgement otherwise) and **~90% automatic release after a risk check**;
- **ITC protection for genuine buyers** under Section 16(2)(c) where the supplier defaults, plus a review of blocked credits under Section 17(5);
- **ITC on group health insurance** and on **telecom towers and pipelines**;
- **refund of tax paid on plant and machinery over five years**; QRMP-style expansion for small B2C suppliers; **no notice below ₹10,000**; fewer e-way bill checks; and treating **work done in India on a foreign client's goods as an export**.

**These are proposals. Treat as law only after the Council recommends and notifications issue.**

## SEBI Eases the Merchant-Banker Requirement for Private Debt

SEBI issued a **circular dated October 7, effective immediately**, allowing **eligible listed issuers to raise debt by private placement without appointing a merchant banker**. Issuers at a **face value of ₹10,000** still require a merchant banker **by default**, but may opt out where **all** of the following hold:

- the issuer is **regulated by an Indian financial-sector regulator — SEBI, RBI, IRDAI or PFRDA**;
- it has been **listed on a recognised exchange for at least one year**;
- it has **no pending fines or penalties** from SEBI or exchanges for listing violations; and
- the instrument carries a **rating of at least AA−** (where multiple ratings exist, the **lowest** counts).

Exchanges will set operational requirements and confirm eligibility at in-principle approval. SEBI's stated intent is **easier debt issuance and wider retail access to high-rated securities**. The step follows feedback from market participants.

## L&T Takes the Chandrapura Main Plant Package — a ₹5,000–10,000 Crore LNTP

**Larsen & Toubro** disclosed on **October 7** that its **L&T Energy CarbonLite Solutions** arm has secured a **Limited Notice to Proceed (LNTP)** from **DVC CIL Power Private Ltd (DVCCIL)** — a **50:50 joint venture of Damodar Valley Corporation and Coal India** — for the **main plant package of the 2×800 MW Chandrapura thermal power project in Jharkhand**.

- L&T classifies the order as **"Major," a band of ₹5,000–10,000 crore** (it has not disclosed an exact figure).
- Scope: **design, engineering, manufacturing and supply, erection, testing and commissioning of boilers, steam turbines, generators and electrostatic precipitators**, plus associated mechanical, electrical, C&I and civil works, using **ultra-supercritical** units.
- **It is an LNTP, not a full order.** DVCCIL will issue the Notice to Proceed only after **environmental clearances** are secured and the LNTP period completes.
