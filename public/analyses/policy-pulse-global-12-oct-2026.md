## Brent Tops $106 as Riyadh's Airport Is Hit Again and a Tanker Is Struck at Hormuz

Brent crude traded above **$106 a barrel** on **October 12**, the highest in more than two weeks, with WTI near **$93.7**. The trigger was a violent weekend in the Gulf.

- Saudi Arabia's civil aviation authority said **12 people were killed and 309 wounded** in Saturday's attack on **Riyadh's King Khalid International Airport**. Strikes continued into Sunday, and CBS reported **at least 20 people killed over a week**, including a US national. Houthi militants also claimed attacks on two Saudi oil tankers and warned refineries.
- A **tanker sailing out of the Strait of Hormuz was struck by an unidentified projectile** on Sunday, sustaining port-side damage with the crew reported safe, per a UKMTO warning. The **IRGC said its navy targeted a tanker** it described as passing through Hormuz illegally. Bloomberg reported a separate tanker attacked deep in the Persian Gulf for the first time in weeks.
- Iran's foreign minister called his Saudi counterpart to discuss the escalation. Trump said he was **considering whether to join Saudi strikes** against the Houthis.
- The offset: Gulf exports recovered to **17.5 million barrels a day, about 98% of pre-war levels**, and Iraq devalued the dinar by roughly 13% as the disruption hit its exports.

## Trump's Diesel Deal Caps the Spike Instead of Reversing It

The crude move was contained rather than reversed because Trump announced a Russian supply deal, and it is thinner than the headline suggests.

- Trump said on **October 9** that Russia would release **300,000 tonnes of diesel immediately, 500,000 tonnes in November and a further 1 million tonnes after that**, and on **October 11** announced a Russia-Ukraine **"energy ceasefire" effective immediately**. Zelenskyy later said Russia struck a power plant in Kharkiv.
- Moscow's planned **500,000-tonne** export compares with **28.6 million tonnes** shipped last year, so this is a marginal addition to a distorted market.
- The **IEA has urged governments to accelerate pledged diesel releases ahead of its October 15 meeting**, and the US Energy Department approved an emergency exchange of up to **4 million barrels** from the Strategic Petroleum Reserve to offset Hurricane Isaias disruptions.
- Senator **Thom Tillis** said the arrangement may breach a 2022 law banning Russian oil imports absent conditions including a full Russian withdrawal. Details are unclear and the plan faces opposition in Washington.

## Canada Sends Its Largest Trade Mission to India in 30 Years

Canadian International Trade Minister **Maninder Sidhu** is leading a nearly **300-member** Team Canada trade mission to India from **October 12 to 17**, with stops in Mumbai and Bengaluru — the largest Canadian delegation to India in more than 30 years.

- The two governments are pushing to conclude a **Comprehensive Economic Partnership Agreement by the end of 2026**, ahead of an expected **Modi visit to Canada in December**. Canada's stated target is to double exports to markets outside the United States over the next decade.
- Delegation sectors: **aerospace and space, clean technologies and the energy transition, information and communication technologies, life sciences and agri-food**. The proposed agreement also covers **energy, advanced manufacturing, artificial intelligence and critical minerals**.
- Canadian farm groups have asked for enforceable commitments rather than tariff cuts that could be reversed at short notice, which is the likely sticking point.
