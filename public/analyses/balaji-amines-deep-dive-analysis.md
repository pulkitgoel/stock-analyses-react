## 2. Business Overview

Balaji Amines Ltd (BAL), incorporated 1988, is India's leading manufacturer of **aliphatic amines and amine derivatives**, with a product portfolio spanning methyl amines (MMA, DMA, TMA), ethyl amines, amine derivatives (DMAE, choline chloride, morpholine, DMF, NMP, EDA, piperazine, DETA, TETA) and speciality chemicals.

**Three reporting segments:** Amines, Amine Derivatives, and Speciality Chemicals.

**Manufacturing:** four plants — three near Solapur (Units I, III and IV at Tamalwadi/Chincholi MIDC, Maharashtra) and one near Hyderabad (Unit II, Bollaram, Telangana). The company also owns a five-star hotel in Solapur (Balaji Sarovar, managed by the Sarovar group) — a legacy diversification that has historically weighed on return ratios.

**Subsidiary:** Balaji Speciality Chemicals Ltd (BSCL) — a material subsidiary driving the current ₹750 Cr expansion into cyanide chemistry and EDA derivatives. Note that **promoters hold 45% of BSCL**, not the listed company; the stated justification is land held in promoters' names.

**End-user industries:** pharmaceuticals and agrochemicals together account for roughly 50-60% of sales, with the balance from paint stripping and resins, rubber chemicals, water treatment, oil refineries, mining and, increasingly, energy-transition applications (LPG blending, aerosol propellants, EV battery electrolytes).

**Competitive structure:** the Indian aliphatic amines market is effectively a **duopoly** between Balaji Amines and Alkyl Amines Chemicals. Balaji is the leader in methylamines. Chinese competition is largely absent in methylamines but has been destructive in DMF, NMP, DMC and EDA — the company had to shut its EDA plant at one point and ran it at 40-50% utilisation after restart before pivoting that capacity into higher-value derivatives (DETA, TETA, piperazine).

**What the company is becoming:** the growth story has shifted from commodity methylamines to (i) high-value speciality derivatives, (ii) import substitution in cyanide chemistry via BSCL, and (iii) new-age chemicals — Dimethyl Ether (DME) as an alternate fuel, and acetonitrile for pharma/agrochemical and battery applications.

### 3.3 Key takeaways — and the one number that matters most

**The inflection is real, and it is margin-led.** Operating margin went from 15% in Q1 FY26 to 25% in Q1 FY27 — a 1,000 bps expansion in four quarters, with net profit up 111% year-on-year.

**But the volume number is alarming.** Q1 FY27 consolidated volume was **21,587 MT against 27,570 MT in Q1 FY26 — down 21.7%** — while revenue rose 25.6%. That means **realisation per tonne rose roughly 60%** (from about ₹1.33 lakh/MT to ₹2.14 lakh/MT). This is not a demand-led recovery; it is a **price-led recovery**.

Management's own press release is candid on this, attributing resilience partly to "adverse scenarios of West Asia's crisis" — i.e. supply-side disruption lifting global amine prices — alongside anti-dumping protection on acetonitrile and improving demand in key end-user industries.

**Why this matters:** price-led chemical recoveries reverse faster than volume-led ones. If West Asian supply normalises or Chinese producers return, the realisation tailwind that produced a 1,000 bps margin swing can unwind with similar speed. The company's own history shows exactly this — revenue fell from ₹2,346 Cr in FY23 to ₹1,389 Cr in FY25 as amine prices normalised, taking EPS from ₹100.47 to ₹48.62 and the stock down 53% over five years.

**The long view is a cycle, not a compounding curve.** FY26 revenue (₹1,419 Cr) is 40% below FY23 (₹2,346 Cr); FY26 PAT (₹169 Cr) is 58% below FY22 (₹418 Cr). TTM EPS of ₹63 is still only 55% of FY22's ₹113.71. This is a cyclical business recovering from a trough, currently priced as though the recovery is durable.

## 7. Growth Pipeline & Revenue Visibility

A chemicals company has no order book: revenue visibility comes from capacity on stream, product approvals and contract pricing. Here is what has actually been commissioned and what remains.

| Project | Status | Scale |
|---|---|---|
| **Dimethyl Ether (DME)** | **Commissioned** (commercial production started 20 May 2026) | 1,00,000 TPA — India's first commercial-scale DME facility. Targets LPG blending and aerosol propellant markets |
| **Acetonitrile (ACN)** | **Commissioned 24 Sep 2026** | Capacity enhanced to **1,440 TPM (~17,280 TPA)**; "advanced technology" under the expansion and modernisation programme at Unit III, Chincholi MIDC. Domestic + export market |
| **N-Methyl Morpholine (NMM)** | Commissioning during FY27 | Broadens the speciality portfolio |
| **BSCL Unit-I (brownfield)** | Under execution, targeted FY27 | EDA-based downstream products (DETA, TETA, Piperazine, AEEA, AEP) |
| **BSCL Unit-II (greenfield, Chincholi)** | Under execution, targeted FY27 | HCN, NaCN, EDTA, EDTA-2Na — import substitution in cyanide chemistry for pharma, agrochemical and mining users |
| Isopropylamine (MIPA/DIPA) | Commissioned (30 Jun 2025) | Smaller, incremental |

**The policy tailwind that makes the ACN bet coherent:** India notified a **five-year anti-dumping duty on acetonitrile imports from China, Russia and Taiwan on 19 June 2025**. ACN is used in pharmaceuticals, agrochemicals and — increasingly — as a battery electrolyte solvent. The new 1,440 TPM line is being commissioned into a protected market, which is a materially better setup than the company's earlier commodity plays.

**BSCL's ₹750 Cr expansion has been granted Mega Project Status** by the Government of Maharashtra under the Package Scheme of Incentives 2019, which brings fiscal benefits and signals state-level support.

**Execution risk is concentrated in one place:** the FY28 earnings upgrade story in the market rests overwhelmingly on BSCL Units I and II commissioning and ramping on time. Both are targeted for FY27. CWIP of ₹512 Cr on the FY26 balance sheet is the money already spent against that plan.

This marks an important milestone in BAL's strategic vision to diversify into new-age chemicals and alternate fuel applications, while reinforcing our commitment towards import substitution and creating long-term value chain for our customers."

> "Our integrated manufacturing platform continues to be a key competitive advantage, providing operational flexibility, supply chain reliability and cost efficiencies across product lines. At the same time, we remain focused on expanding our presence in high-value specialty chemicals and electronic-grade products, positioning the Company to benefit from structural growth opportunities in pharmaceuticals, agrochemicals, alternate fuels and the evolving EV battery chemicals ecosystem."

**Guidance as reported from the earnings calls (secondary sources, attributed):**

- **FY27:** volume growth of **10-15%** with **EBITDA margin of ~23%**
- **Medium term (stated on the Q2 FY26 call):** sustainable EBITDA margins of **17.5%-22%**; low utilisation at new plants, particularly battery-related chemicals, due to slow customer ramp-up and pending regulatory approvals
- 34% of energy sourced from renewable solar (FY26 BRSR), which management cites as supporting the 22-23% margin guidance

**The critical observation:** Q1 FY27's actual 26% EBITDA margin is **above the top of the previously guided 17.5-22% band and above the current 23% target**. Management is therefore guiding for margins to *normalise downward* from the current run-rate, not to hold. Any valuation that capitalises Q1's 26% margin is assuming management is being conservative about its own business.
