China's cabinet spoke at lunchtime. The STAR 50 rose 15.1%.

That is the sort of number that requires an explanation, and the explanation is a policy event rather than an earnings one.

## The statement, and what it did

The State Council, chaired by Premier Li Qiang, issued an unusual midday readout on Friday pledging support for capital markets: new incremental policies, a bigger role for the state-backed "national team" of equity buyers with Central Huijin acting as a stabilisation fund, more long-term capital directed into equities, and encouragement for listed companies to pay higher dividends and buy back more shares. Reuters reported the statement also pledged to strengthen defences against imported risks in capital markets.

An intraday readout of a cabinet meeting is not a normal communications channel, which is why the move was violent:

The CSI 300's move was its best single day in two years. It also started from a 16-month low: as recently as Thursday the index was at its weakest since June 2025 and is still down about 13% year to date, on course for its worst year since 2022. Selling in October and November had erased all of the market's 2026 gains.

So the honest framing is not "China is fixed." It is that Beijing finally addressed the thing that was breaking — Goldman's note points to consumer loans including mortgages falling by **RMB 1.6 trillion** in the first nine months, more than three times the decline in all of 2025, with mortgage balances 8% below their 2023 peak. A state fund buying equities does not fix that. It buys time.

## Korea gapped and held, which is the difference

The Kospi rose 4.34% to 4,915.68 with the Kosdaq up 8.13% — a move that triggered a buy sidecar at 10:22 a.m. Foreign investors bought a net **1.1256 trillion won** of Kospi shares while retail sold **1.7746 trillion won**. Reports said regulators are considering expanding the **10.76 trillion won** stabilisation fund.

Set that against Thursday. On 26 November the Kospi opened +1.01%, was +1.94% in the morning, and closed +0.29% — an index that opened up 2% on futures and closed up a third of a percent had been sold into all day. That was positioning. Friday was information, and the difference is that the index held its gain to the close.

Taiwan is the absence worth noting. The Taiex rose **0.40%** on a day the Kospi rose 4.34%, and foreign funds have sold a record **NT$1.09 trillion** of Taiwanese stocks in November through Thursday. The AI trade's most direct equity expression is not participating in the AI rebound, which is consistent with what Nvidia is doing in the US session today.

## The IT trade was a hedge, and the hedge closed

India's Nifty IT fell **1.88%** on Friday to 32,758.10, a day after rising **3.66%** to 33,386.95. HCL Technologies fell 3.03%, Infosys 2.68%, TCS 2.57%, Tech Mahindra 3.33% intraday.

Four sessions to Thursday had added **7.98%** to the index from 30,919.45 on 19 November — roughly ₹1.70 lakh crore of market value. It has given back 1.9% of that in one session.

The mechanism is not Indian. Thursday's rally happened because USTR published its Section 301 determinations at 6:41 p.m. IST, three hours after the Indian close, and the first session able to price a 25% goods duty bought IT services — which a goods duty cannot reach at any rate. Friday's session had the other half of the information: semiconductors were bouncing, and the reason to own IT as a hedge against semis disappeared with them.

That is what makes this fragile. The marginal November buyer of Indian IT was not buying growth — Bernstein initiated positively on a $2.1 trillion addressable market by FY30 with India at 15%, while Jefferies is Underperform on TCS, HCL Tech, Wipro and Tech Mahindra, and Choice notes that both Infosys and Cognizant cut organic guidance. The marginal buyer was buying an asset class that was not semiconductors. When semiconductors stop falling, that bid does not need to exist.

And the rupee, which firmed to an eight-month high around 92.4 on Thursday, weakened 60 paise to 92.98 on Friday — a revenue tailwind for exactly the sector that was being sold.

## Crude: the level is contested and the direction is unresolved

Indian coverage on Friday put Brent between $96.47 and $98, up on the day, attributing it to Houthi attacks on a Saudi Aramco facility and to vessels struck in the Strait of Hormuz.

The front-month series I can verify does not agree. Brent's settlement on 25 November was $94.61 and the latest print available is **$91.02**, a fall of 3.79% — with WTI at $83.80. A Brent-WTI spread of roughly $7 at a $91 Brent is wider than the normal $3-4 range, and at 25 November's $94.61 against $83.68 it was wider still, which suggests the two series may not be quoting the same contract month.

I could not reconcile an eight-dollar gap between two live sources, so the level should be treated as unresolved. What is not unresolved is that the Indian tape traded on the higher number: energy was the second-best sector, oil marketing names were watched for margin, and the rupee fell on importer dollar demand. If Brent is genuinely at $91 rather than $97, the inflation arithmetic into the 4 December RBI meeting is better than Friday's price action implied.

## The other prices

The rupee settled provisionally at **92.98**, down 60 paise from 92.38, after opening at 92.44 and touching 93.01. It is still the best-performing Asian currency in November, up about 2.6%, and nearly 5% off its all-time closing low of 96.88 on 8 October.

India's 10-year government yield rose to **7.2650%**, its highest in a week. The dollar index was at **103.77**, up 0.16%.

US Treasuries were weaker as haven demand unwound, with the 10-year around 4.92%. A 25bp Fed hike on 9 December is nearly fully priced — roughly 99% — which is why the statement will matter more than the decision, and why the dollar is on course for a third straight monthly gain.

## What to watch

- **30 November** — month-end, and the last session before MSCI deletes MSTR.
- **4 December** — RBI policy decision, with crude's true level the single biggest input into the inflation arithmetic.
- **8-9 December** — FOMC. A hike is priced; the dot plot is the event.
- **9 January 2027** — the 178 extended Section 301 China exclusions expire. **10 January** — the US-China truce deadline and the expiry of China's rare-earth export-control pause. Whether Beijing extends the pause again is a more useful signal than any communiqué.
- **Mid-February 2027** — the 90-day licensing window closes for South-East Asian cloud providers under the 16 November BIS rule, which is when the compute that Asia's data-centre pipeline is built on either gets a licence or does not.
