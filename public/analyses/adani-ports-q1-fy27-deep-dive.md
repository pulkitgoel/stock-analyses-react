## 2. Business Overview — What ₹38,736 Cr of Revenue Actually Is

Adani Ports and Special Economic Zone operates port infrastructure and sells the transport chain that hangs off it. The company's own description is "shore-to-door": cargo is originated (international freight network), handled at a port, moved by rail or road, stored in a warehouse or logistics park, and delivered to the customer's gate. It is the only Indian company running that chain end to end at national scale.

**The five reported segments, Q1 FY27, and what each one is:**

| Segment | What it is | Revenue | EBITDA | EBITDA margin | Share of revenue |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Domestic ports | 15 ports and terminals on India's west, south and east coasts | ₹6,964 Cr | ₹5,152 Cr | 74% | 64.4% |
| International ports | Australia (NQXT), Colombo (CWIT), Israel (Haifa), Tanzania | ₹1,747 Cr | ₹730 Cr | 41.8% | 16.1% |
| Logistics | Rail, trucking, MMLPs, warehousing, international freight network | ₹1,173 Cr | ₹219 Cr | 18.7% | 10.8% |
| Marine | 135-vessel fleet: tugs, dredgers, offshore support vessels | ₹901 Cr | ₹404 Cr | 44.8% | 8.3% |
| Port development and SEZ | Land, SEZ infrastructure, development income | ₹36 Cr | ₹36 Cr | 100% | 0.3% |
| **Total** | | **₹10,821 Cr** | **₹6,541 Cr** | **60.4%** | **100%** |

Source: APSEZ Q1 FY27 media release, 29 July 2026. Margins are on segment revenue before unallocable costs.

**Domestic ports is not a growth story any more; it is a cash machine.** Domestic capacity is 653 million tonnes per annum — 355 MMT on the west coast, 158 MMT south, 140 MMT east — against 115.3 MMT handled in the June quarter. The company counted 27.6% of all India cargo in the quarter (27.8% a year earlier) and 44.8% of all India container cargo (45.2%). Both shares slipped. The 74% EBITDA margin is genuinely best in class; the volume line is not.

**International ports is where the growth is, and it arrived by acquisition.** North Queensland Export Terminal in Australia — 50 MTPA of coal export capacity, bought from a promoter-group entity in December 2025 (Section 10) — contributed 10 MMT of the quarter's 22.8 MMT. Colombo contributed 6.9 MMT, Tanzania 3.7 MMT and Israel 2.2 MMT. International volumes were 7.7 MMT a year ago and the segment margin went from 21.1% to 41.8% in twelve months.

**Logistics is the sub-scale segment and it is not behaving.** Revenue was flat at ₹1,173 Cr (+0.3%) and rail volume fell 19% to 145,310 TEUs. This is the one business with a stated target of 27% EBITDA CAGR to FY31 and a stated 10% RoCE today (up from 6%). Its problem is not demand from customers — trucking revenue grew 26% and the international freight network 28% — it is that rail is the piece exposed to the Middle East disruption, and rail is the piece that carries the volume.

**Marine is the quiet compounder.** 135 vessels against 118 a year ago, revenue up 67%, and the whole fleet largely on take-or-pay contracts with Tier-1 customers, which means this is the only genuinely contracted revenue stream in the group. Management has said steady-state margins belong near 55% and that the current 44.8% is depressed by operating conditions, not competition.

**The moat, stated plainly.** Three things, none of them pricing power in the ordinary sense. First, geography: a deep-draft port is not replicable next to an existing one, and the Indian coast has a limited number of sites with hinterland connectivity. Mundra's rail links into northern India are the reason it is India's largest port, not the water depth. Second, an integrated chain: once a customer's container is moving on APSEZ rail to an APSEZ logistics park with APSEZ trucks on the last mile, switching is an operational project, not a purchase decision. Third, capacity: management's stated view on the Q1 call was that "in the next 5 years, there are very less capacities which are being created in India", which is a claim about the permit pipeline that the company's own ₹12,000–14,000 Cr annual capex is designed to exploit.

What the moat does not produce is a fast-growing domestic volume base. Ports grow with the trade they serve, and India's trade grew slowly enough in the first half of FY27 that APSEZ's domestic tonnage grew 2%.

**Four things in that table matter more than the rest.**

**Revenue has grown year on year in every one of the nine year-on-year pairs the table supports**, and Q1 FY27's ₹10,821 Cr is the second consecutive quarter above ₹10,700 Cr. The 19% YoY growth in the June quarter was 2.1% above the analyst consensus for the quarter.

**Profit growth has decelerated to single digits against revenue growth of nearly 20%.** Q1 FY27 PAT grew 10% on revenue growth of 19%. The gap is the P&L below EBITDA: interest ₹1,087 Cr, depreciation ₹1,711 Cr. Depreciation is up 36% year on year, because NQXT and the Colombo Phase II build are now sitting on the balance sheet.

Q1 FY27's ₹853 Cr is the best single quarter in the series and should not be annualised.

**The balance sheet grew ₹49,656 Cr in one year — 37% — and that is the most important fact in this section.** Fixed assets went from ₹89,616 Cr to ₹1,31,652 Cr (+₹42,036 Cr) and borrowings from ₹51,621 Cr to ₹63,566 Cr (+₹11,945 Cr). Reserves went up ₹33,661 Cr, which is more than the year's ₹12,782 Cr profit, and the difference is explained by one transaction: **the December 2025 all-share acquisition of NQXT, settled by issuing 14.38 crore new shares at ₹1,199 each for a total non-cash consideration of ₹17,244 Cr.** Equity capital rose from ₹432 Cr to ₹461 Cr for exactly that reason.

That structure is worth pausing on. The company added a 50 MTPA Australian coal terminal with a stated enterprise value of AUD 3,975 million (roughly US$2.4 billion) **without spending cash and without adding debt** — it paid the seller, a promoter-group entity, in its own stock. From a balance-sheet standpoint that is elegant: net debt/EBITDA stayed at 1.9x through a year in which capex ran ₹15,320 Cr. From a governance standpoint it is a related-party transaction of ₹17,244 Cr at a price (₹1,199) struck in April 2025, and 14.4 crore shares of dilution is 6.2% of the enlarged share count. Both readings are correct (Section 10).

**Leverage is comfortable and the direction is the right one.** Gross debt was ₹56,776 Cr at 30 June 2026 against cash of ₹12,428 Cr — net debt ₹44,348 Cr, or 1.9x TTM EBITDA, and 1.8x on the company's proforma calculation using NQXT's TTM EBITDA. The stated policy ceiling is 2.5x. Average debt maturity is 5.1 years. Debt-to-equity on the FY26 balance sheet is 0.66x.

**What the leverage does not show is the forward commitment.** FY27 capex guidance is ₹12,000–14,000 Cr, and management's five-year plan (Section 7) is ₹90,000 Cr to ₹1,00,000 Cr of capex through FY31. Against that, FY26 free cash flow was ₹5,074 Cr — down from ₹9,228 Cr in FY25 — and FY26 CFO covered operating profit at 96%, below the 101% of FY25 and the 104% of FY24. The company funds capex from a mix of internal accruals and debt, and the CEO's stated position on the Q1 call was that if the company did no M&A, **"in 5 years, I will be close to 0 net debt-to-EBITDA"**. That is a statement about cash generation capacity, and on the FY26 numbers it is arithmetically plausible: ₹20,356 Cr of CFO against ₹15,320 Cr of capex leaves ₹5,036 Cr, and EBITDA is growing 18% a year.

**Working capital is the anomaly.** Working capital days went from −114 to −33 in one year, and debtor days from 53 to 60. For a ports business, negative working capital is normal — customers prepay, the port collects before it pays. A 81-day deterioration is a ₹8,000 Cr swing in cash at this revenue base, and it is the single largest unexplained movement in the FY26 accounts. It may be consolidation of NQXT and Tanzania, it may be receivable timing, and the annual report would settle it. It is flagged, not explained, and it is the reason FY26 FCF fell 45% while EBITDA rose 24%.

**The answer to "is the profit real" is yes, with a caveat that has widened.** Over five years CFO averaged 107% of operating profit. A port that collects cash ahead of its P&L is exactly what a good port looks like, and this one has done it for a decade. FY26's 96% is the lowest conversion in the five-year window and the first year below 100%.

**The caveat is capex, and it is structural rather than temporary.** FY26 capex of ₹15,320 Cr against CFO of ₹20,356 Cr left ₹5,074 Cr of free cash flow — 1.29% of market capitalisation. FY25's ₹9,228 Cr was 2.35%. The business is in the middle of the largest capacity-expansion programme in its history (653 MMT to 1,000 MMT by December 2030) and it has told the market to expect ₹90,000 Cr to ₹1,00,000 Cr of capex through FY31. **For the next five years this is a company that converts EBITDA into assets, not into free cash.** The dividend is ₹7.50 a share, or ₹1,728 Cr, which is 34% of FY26 free cash flow and 13% of earnings. That is a payout policy designed to preserve the capex budget, and it should be read that way rather than as a signal of confidence.

**Financing outflows have been shrinking even as debt rose** — ₹5,483 Cr in FY26 against ₹7,800 Cr in FY24 — because the company has been refinancing rather than repaying. In FY26 it completed a US$199.57m bond buyback in March and a US$386.03m buyback in August 2025, and extended average maturity from 4.3 years to 5.4 years. Net cash flow was positive ₹1,682 Cr, the second consecutive positive year after two negative ones.

**One check worth doing before trusting the FCF number:** FY26 capex of ₹15,320 Cr is above the ₹11,000–12,000 Cr guidance, and the working-capital swing described in Section 4 consumed cash. The company's own framing is that capex will be funded from internal accruals — the CEO said so on the FY26 release: "Disciplined capital allocation will ensure that future capex is funded via internal accruals". On FY26 numbers that was true by ₹5,036 Cr. On the five-year plan it will not be true in every year, which is why net debt/EBITDA, not FCF, is the metric to watch.

That is a rotation out of the Adani complex as it de-rated, and it is not yet reversing. Public holding is down to 4.78% from 7.39% in March 2024 — the retail float has been absorbed.

## 7. Order Book & Revenue Visibility

**A port operator has no order book, and this one does not pretend to.** Revenue is not contracted in advance the way a defence or EPC order book is; it accrues as cargo arrives. What a port can offer instead is three things: capacity that exists and is being used, capacity being built with a date attached, and the small share of revenue that genuinely is contracted. This section sets out all three, and it does not call any of it an order book.

### 7.1 The capacity pipeline

| Asset | Now | Target | Date | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|

**The domestic capacity number is the load-bearing target and it deserves a sceptical sentence.** 653 MMT to 1,000 MMT by December 2030 is a 53% increase in five years, against domestic cargo of 115.3 MMT in a quarter (roughly 460 MMT annualised). Capacity utilisation across the domestic system is therefore around 70%, which means the company does not need to fill the new capacity to justify it — it needs to fill the 347 MMT it is adding. Management has said domestic traffic should reach 850 MMT by CY30, which is a 14% CAGR from FY26 levels and implies utilisation of 85% on the new base. **That is a demanding assumption, and it is the single number in the entire investment case that the volume data does not currently support** (domestic volume grew 2.1% in Q1 FY27).

### 7.2 The genuinely contracted revenue

**Marine is the only segment with contracted revenue at scale.** The FY26 release describes the fleet as backed by "take-or-pay contracts with Tier-1 customers", acquired through offshore support vessel purchases in the Middle East, Africa, South Asia and Indian waters. Take-or-pay means the customer pays whether or not the vessel is used. FY26 marine revenue was ₹2,681 Cr (+134%) with a 51% EBITDA margin and 13% RoCE. In Q1 FY27 it grew 67% to ₹901 Cr. Recent wins: a partnership with Oceaneering International for deepwater engineering and offshore work in Europe, and a 10-year contract supporting Argentina's first LNG exports to India — the company's entry into South America.

**That is a real order book, it is small relative to the group (8.3% of revenue), and it is the highest-quality revenue APSEZ has.** It is also the segment whose margin is most exposed to the Middle East situation: management said steady-state margin is ~55% against 44.8% now, and that the gap closes when operating conditions normalise rather than when contracts renew.

### 7.3 What the visibility actually is

The honest summary is that APSEZ's revenue visibility comes from **assets under construction with fixed completion dates**, not from customers under contract. Three of those dates are within eighteen months and are checkable:

- **Vizhinjam to 5.7 mn TEU by December 2028** — funded in part by MSC's US$858 mn second tranche, with a strategic operator now holding 49% of the asset and an interest in filling it.
- **Colombo CWIT Phase II, commissioned 30 September 2026** — 3.2 mn TEU live, targeting close to 25% of Colombo's 13 mn TEU target by 2028.
- **Paradip's two dry bulk berths (18 MMT)**, won in September 2026, with the SPV incorporated in October. No commissioning date has been disclosed in the filings reviewed for this note.

### 8.2 On the domestic volume question — the exchange that matters most

Motilal Oswal's analyst asked directly why domestic volumes were muted. The answer is more candid than the release:

Note the specific claim and note its shape: **one customer's plant outage cost 2–2.5 MMT**, on a domestic base of 115.3 MMT. > "In Marine, our margins used to be higher earlier... before the whole Middle East situation actually sprang up. So that could be a swing factor, which could then determine how international port margin could shape up in the future."

**That is the most useful paragraph in the call and it is the closest thing to free optionality in the equity case.** Israel handled 2.2 MMT in the quarter, 9.7% of international volume, at realisations four times the Indian average. If the situation normalises, the international segment's 41.8% margin has room that does not require any new capacity.

### 8.4 On whether to raise FY27 EBITDA guidance

Asked whether a ₹6,541 Cr quarter implied guidance should be raised from ₹25,000–26,000 Cr:

And with the trade growth, which we are seeing, because of the policy reforms, we see that trade will grow and India may be requiring more capacities than planned."

**Guidance was not raised after a quarter that annualises to the top of the band.** That is deliberate conservatism, and it is the reason this note models guidance rather than the run-rate (Section 11).

### 8.5 On M&A, the balance sheet and the ABP rumour

Asked about press reports of a UK port acquisition (ABP):

> "We don't comment on speculation and the rumor, and I repeat that and emphasize on that."

On large-ticket acquisitions and leverage:

> "Today, I am at 1.9x. If I do nothing, if my only capability and capacity to invest in the organic growth, I will be negative in 5 years."

> "The value of asset is not about the value of asset today. The value of asset is what return it will generate for me, considering the power of my balance sheet in the next 5 to 7 years."

**Read together, those answers say: the company intends to buy something large, and it believes its balance sheet can absorb it.** That is a thesis for optionality and it is also the source of the largest single risk to the equity (Section 13) — a ₹50,000 Cr acquisition would take net debt/EBITDA through the 2.5x ceiling the same management reaffirmed in the FY27 guidance.

## 15. Bull vs Bear

### The bull case

**1. The FY31 ambition is a doubling, and the company has hit its revenue guidance two years running.** Revenue ₹38,736 Cr to ₹92,438 Cr at 19% CAGR; EBITDA ₹22,851 Cr to ₹52,278 Cr at 18%. On those numbers and a 16x exit multiple in 2031, an investor at today's price earns roughly **15% a year**.

**2. The growth is now coming from the segments with the most pricing power.** International revenue +80% in Q1 FY27, marine +67%, on assets (NQXT, CWIT, Vizhinjam, the OSV fleet) that are either newly acquired or newly commissioned. The margin drag from mix reverses as those assets mature: management says Australia is heading back toward 65% margins and Israel has 4x India realisation waiting on a geopolitical normalisation.

**3. Vizhinjam is a strategic asset that just got externally valued.** MSC's TiL paid US$1.397 billion for 49% at a US$2.85 billion enterprise value. APSEZ carries Vizhinjam at cost. That is an unmarked gain in the accounts and a de-risked expansion — the partner funding the build is the world's largest container line, whose ships will call there.

**4. The balance sheet is under-levered relative to its cash generation.** 1.9x net debt/EBITDA, 5.1 years average maturity, BBB from S&P at sovereign level, AAA domestically. The CEO's own arithmetic — near-zero net debt in five years if nothing is acquired — is the leverage optionality in the story.

**5. The tape agrees, and it is not just price.** Delivery averaging 62.86% for nine sessions against a ~37% market baseline, FII holding at a record 15.95%, and Capital Group taking a full 2% block at ₹1,632 in May. Institutions are accumulating, not distributing.

**6. The regulatory overhang is being closed, not extended.** Three SEBI settlements in September 2026, all for amounts under ₹1.5 crore each, all without admission. Each one removed a live proceeding.

### The bear case

**1. Domestic volume grew 2.1%, and the 1,000 MMT target needs 14% a year.** Every incremental rupee of capex is justified by a volume assumption that the most recent quarter did not support. The Krishnapatnam explanation is credible; it is also unverifiable until Q2 FY27.

**2. The consensus earnings base is above management's own guidance.** ₹74 of FY27 EPS requires ₹28,500 Cr of EBITDA against a guided ₹25,000–26,000 Cr. If the company delivers what it guided, EPS is ₹60–64, the forward P/E is 27–28x, and the consensus target of ₹2,073 needs a 2.9-turn multiple expansion on top of the earnings.

**3. The tax rate will normalise.** 14% in FY26 against a 25.17% statutory rate. Ten points of normalisation is 12% of forward EPS. This is the single largest unmodelled risk in every bull model on the street, and Screener flags it and the sell side does not.

**4. The capex overrun is a pattern, not an accident.** ₹15,320 Cr against ₹11,000–12,000 Cr guidance. In a business where the return is the spread between the cost of capital and the return on the asset, a 30% capex overrun on a fixed-revenue concession is a permanent return-on-capital problem — and ROCE has already slipped from 15% to 14% reported (though the company reports 16% on its own definition, up from 15%).

**5. Related-party concentration.** The largest asset added in three years was bought from the promoter group, priced in shares the promoter received, at a 30% discount to today's market. A minority holder's protection is the majority-of-minority vote and the disclosure, and neither prices the asset independently.

**6. Free cash flow is 1.29% of market capitalisation and heading lower.** ₹5,074 Cr of FY26 FCF, working capital deteriorated 81 days, and the five-year capex plan is ₹90,000–1,00,000 Cr. This is a business that will not produce meaningful free cash to shareholders before FY30, which means the entire return depends on the terminal multiple.

**7. The chart is not confirming anything.** Below the 9, 20, 50 and 100-day averages, MACD negative and widening, ADX below 20, −DI at nearly twice +DI, CCI below −100. It is a range with a bearish tilt, 1.3% below the bottom of the moving-average band that would change the picture.

## 17. Final Verdict

**Verdict: ACCUMULATE ON DIPS. Staggered buying into ₹1,620–1,680, with the Q2 FY27 print on 28 October as the gate.**

The business case is stronger than the price action and the price action is better than the headlines. Revenue has compounded 20% a year with a 52–60% operating margin held throughout, four international ports and a 135-vessel marine fleet have been added in three years, the largest container line in the world just bought 49% of the flagship transshipment port at an enterprise value that implies ₹2.85 billion of unmarked value, and the balance sheet carries 1.9x leverage with investment-grade ratings at three agencies. Against that, domestic volume grew 2.1%, the stock costs 27.5x FY27 guidance earnings, the tax rate is nine points below statutory, capex has overrun guidance by 30%, and the chart sits below every average shorter than 200 days.

**Neither side of that is a surprise to the market, and the price reflects the compromise.** Ten percent off the high, 29.5x trailing, 4.1x book, consensus target ₹2,073 which is 17.8x FY28 EBITDA — the stock is priced as a good business with a governance discount applied, which is what it is.

**The case for buying on weakness rather than on strength rests on three things the delivery data and the register both support:** 62.86% average delivery for nine sessions, FII holding at a record 15.95% with a new ₹7,486 Cr strategic holder from May, and a symmetrical triangle that has been built with higher lows since August. Institutions are accumulating a stock that retail has been selling.
