## Black Thursday in Seoul: the KOSPI Falls 7.57% and Both Boards Halt

South Korea produced the sharpest single-session loss of the global cycle on **October 15**, and it triggered market-wide halts on both the main and secondary boards.

- The **KOSPI closed at 5,554.22, down 454.59 points or 7.57%** — its first close in the 5,500 range since **July 30** (5,593.56). It was the index's largest point decline and largest percentage decline of this selloff.
- The **KOSDAQ closed at 760.82, down 63.06 points or 7.65%.**
- **Level 1 circuit breakers** — which trigger when an index falls 8% or more from the previous close — halted trading on the **KOSDAQ at 12:23 p.m.** and on the **KOSPI at 12:38 p.m.**, each for 20 minutes. A **sell-sidecar** had already triggered on the KOSDAQ at 9:12 a.m.
- Intraday, the KOSPI fell as much as **8.68%, or 480.76 points, to 5,528.05** before the halt.
- The rout is concentrated in the two names that carry roughly 45% of the index: **SK hynix fell 11.06% to 1.26 million won** and **Samsung Electronics fell 9.48% to 210,000 won.** **Samsung Electro-Mechanics plunged 15.36%**, **SK Square shed 10.21%**, and the losses widened out across the technology complex.
- The won weakened to **1,344.6 per dollar**. Foreign investors had already pulled **$676 million** out the previous day.
- Context on the scale: the **KOSPI has fallen nearly 40% from its June peak**, and on October 13 it had closed 5.46% lower at 6,035.35. This is the seventh session of a continuous decline.

The trigger was not a Korean event. It was imported, and it landed on the most highly-valued market in the world.

## The Actual Trigger: Decelerating Lab Revenue and Leveraged Data-Centre Debt

The immediate cause was a combination of two disclosures from the private side of the AI economy.

- **Anthropic** disclosed third-quarter revenue of **$16.8 billion** at a pre-IPO meeting with institutional investors, up **45% sequentially** from $11.6 billion in the second quarter and $4.7 billion in the first. The absolute figure is enormous; the growth *rate* is decelerating and the number came in **below most estimates**. The company has reportedly pushed its IPO from October to **November**.
- MarketWatch headlined it directly — "Anthropic's slowing growth could be the AI trade's latest red flag" — and AFP reported that the strong results from major AI firms are "failing to lift investors' spirits."
- **CoreWeave** fell **15.5% on October 14, its worst day since February, and another 8% on October 15, with its bonds declining sharply.** CEO Michael Intrator said: "we are confident in our backlog which more than supports all of our debt obligation."
- The macro overlay came from the United States: **September CPI at 3.7% year on year** (released October 14) followed by **September PPI at 6.3% year on year, the biggest jump since 2022**, with a **1.3% month-on-month increase that was the second-largest on record**. Fed funds futures moved to roughly a **50% probability of an October hike** and a **96% probability of at least one more before year-end**.
- That combination is what makes this rout different from the summer's. The concern is no longer whether demand exists — Anthropic's revenue answers that — but whether the **debt taken on to build against that demand can be carried if the cost of capital keeps rising.** Trading Economics described it as stress on "data-centre operators that took record levels of debt to finance capex."
- Japan also added a domestic reason to sell: with energy-driven inflation persistent, the market has begun pricing higher borrowing costs for the corporate sector, and "the sector's credit outlook deteriorated on expectations that policymakers will have to raise borrowing costs."

## Five Days to the Graham Act, and Why India Is the Most Exposed Buyer

The exposure with a documented date attached is not in Asia's chip complex but in the tariff statute signed on **September 18**.

- **Section 112** requires duties of **up to 500%** on goods imported into the US from Russia, explicitly including petroleum products, by **October 18** — a Sunday, so several law firms give **Monday, October 19** as the operative date.
- **Section 113** requires duties of **up to 100%** on all goods from the five largest importers of Russian crude or natural gas in the preceding twelve months, but **only where those buyers knowingly make new purchases on or after the deadline.** Purchases made before it do not count.
- **India is the second-largest buyer of Russian crude, behind China**, according to unofficial rankings by the Kyiv School of Economics and the Centre for Research on Energy and Clean Air. India does not rank among the largest buyers of Russian gas, and Section 113 counts only crude oil and natural gas — not refined products such as diesel.
- **Section 113(g)** requires a written justification to the relevant congressional committees **not later than 10 days before imposing a duty** — around **October 8** for an October 18 start. That deadline passed with **no published target list, no duty and no waiver**, which is the strongest single signal that the tariffs may not land on schedule.
- The Act permits the president to waive any duty by certifying that it is in the US national interest, and it does not affect the Treasury's authority to issue new general licences. **OFAC General License 135**, issued **October 9**, authorises transactions in Russian-origin diesel until **April 2027**, and Treasury Secretary **Scott Bessent** has rejected the claim that the law bars those purchases. Trump said Russia will supply more than **300,000 tonnes** of diesel initially, **500,000 tonnes in November** and **1 million tonnes thereafter**.
- Congress is not settled on it: Representative **Raja Krishnamoorthi** called the arrangement "illegal, ineffective" on October 15, Representative **Mike Levin** cited a New York Times estimate that about **$4 billion** of American money could reach Russia's economy, and a senator has said the president "may be" breaking the law.
