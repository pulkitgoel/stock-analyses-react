Brent fell 7.63% in a single session on Monday, then fell another 3% today. The United States bombed an Iranian island on Monday. Both statements are true, and the distance between them is the entire crude trade right now.

## First: a war premium removed from a war still being fought

**Brent settled Monday at $104.77, down $8.66 or 7.63%**, with a session low of **$103.28**. WTI's low was **$95.15**. Today it continued lower — **Brent down 3% at $101.54, WTI down 2.3% at $94.23** at the time of writing, a second consecutive decline.

The cause was diplomatic, and it is specific. Iranian President **Masoud Pezeshkian** said **Supreme Leader Ayatollah Mojtaba Khamenei had granted the government "certain authorities" for peace talks.** That is the single most material line in the whole complex, because it means the negotiators can actually commit to something. Announcing a willingness to talk is cheap; being authorised to sign is not. Qatari mediation is active, a draft proposal is circulating, and a follow-up round is possible.

Trump's own framing to reporters: Tehran wants *"to make a deal and that could happen."* Vice President **JD Vance** told CNN: *"The president and I both feel really good about the [prospect of potentially coming] to a negotiated settlement."* Secretary of State **Marco Rubio** said on Sunday that Trump would prefer a diplomatic resolution and does not want to use military force.

Now the offsetting facts, all from the same twenty-four hours. **The US attacked Iran's Qeshm Island on Monday**, according to US officials speaking to the Wall Street Journal, and has been carrying out **"limited strikes"** against the country for weeks — deliberately kept low-profile so as not to signal a major escalation. **At least four commercial vessel attacks** have been reported since the start of November, in the Persian Gulf, the Strait of Hormuz and the Gulf of Oman, per the UK Maritime Trade Operations Centre.

**So the market is discounting a deal that has not been signed, while the two parties are still shooting at each other and at shipping.** That is not irrational — the premium being removed was for a *future* escalation, and the authorisation grant genuinely lowers the probability of one. But it is a **probability being repriced, not a risk being retired.**

The scale check that keeps this honest: **oil gained more than 10% in October.** A 7.63% single-day giveback plus today's 3% takes Brent to roughly where the month began. This is a round trip, not a trend change.

**Two Fed presidents said last week that rates will need to move higher to rein in inflation.** That matters here because the entire disinflation argument of the past quarter has rested on energy. If crude round-trips back to where it started the month, the case for the next hike gets stronger without any new data — and that compresses the multiples of exactly the fuel-consuming names that benefit from cheaper oil. The two effects partially cancel. It is worth saying plainly rather than presenting only the favourable half.

## The India leg: the same crude move hit two sets of companies in opposite directions

India is the cleanest mechanical transmission available, because it imports the overwhelming majority of its crude and its refiners price products off a lagging benchmark.

| Stock | Impact | Reason | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| IOC, BPCL, HINDPETRO | 🟢 Positive | Landing costs fall first while retail product prices adjust with a lag, so the spread widens on the way down. This is the single most reliable crude-fall trade in Indian equities and it is why Nifty Oil & Gas led the market on Monday | 3/5 |
| RELIANCE | 🟢 Positive | Refining and petrochemical feedstock both improve with a lower crude. Reliance's own 11.6% move on Monday was a record Q2 result, not a crude trade — but a sustained lower crude is additive to the refining margin | 2/5 |
| ONGC, OIL | 🔴 Negative | Upstream realisations fall directly with the benchmark, with no refining offset. This is the mirror of the OMC trade and the reason the two should never be held as a single "oil" view | 3/5 |

**One divergence to publish rather than smooth over: ONGC actually closed up 4.19% on Monday, on a day crude fell 7.63%.** The market read the crude collapse as a risk-on event for India — lower inflation, better current account, room for the RBI — rather than as an upstream earnings cut. The directional logic in the table above is right on a one-to-three-month view and was wrong on the day. Both facts belong in the record.

Context worth holding: **the RBI raised the repo rate to 5.5% on 7 October** and shifted its stance to calibrated tightening. A sustained fall in crude is the one variable that could loosen that stance without the RBI having to reverse itself — which is a better explanation for four consecutive sessions of Indian gains than anything happening in Korean memory.

## Asia: the chip rout and the Indian rally are the same trade, opposite sides

**South Korea's KOSPI fell 3.59% to 5,648.79. Shanghai fell 0.82% to 3,608.10. The ASX 200 rose 0.34% to 8,886.80. Japan's Nikkei did not trade — 3 November is Culture Day, a national holiday.** India's Nifty rose **1.58%**.

That divergence is the "reverse AI trade" in its mature form. The KOSPI is roughly **21% below its September high**, per Bloomberg, with foreign investors dumping Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix on renewed fears of Chinese memory competition from CXMT and on mounting concern about how the AI build-out is being financed. Korean memory is the purest expression of AI capital expenditure; Indian IT services and domestic cyclicals are the furthest thing from it.

| Stock | Impact | Reason | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|

The honest read on that table is that it is weak. **The money that left Korean semiconductors today did not go into Indian IT — it went into Indian consumer staples.** A 3% FMCG day against a 0.34% IT day is the tape correcting the narrative.

## What to watch

- **Any signed US-Iran document.** Until one exists, every crude decline of this size is a position unwind and can reverse in a session. The single tell to watch is whether negotiators return for a second round.
- **Vessel attacks in Hormuz and the Gulf of Oman.** Four since 1 November is an escalation rate, not a background rate.
- **The 10 November expiry of China's rare-earth export-control pause** — six weeks ahead of the US-China tariff truce deadline, and the next scheduled shock to the same supply chains the KOSPI is already pricing.
- **Indian FII flows.** FIIs were net buyers of ₹3,114.68 crore on 2 November. Three consecutive weeks of that would confirm the Indian rally is a reallocation rather than a bounce.
