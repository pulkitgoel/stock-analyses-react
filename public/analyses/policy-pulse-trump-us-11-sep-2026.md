## August CPI Matches the Headline but Core Runs Hot — September Hike Odds Hold Near 60%

The August consumer price index, released Friday September 11 at 8:30am ET, came in **in line on the headline and slightly hot on the core**: the all-items index rose **0.4% month-on-month** (after +0.1% in July) and **3.4% year-on-year**, exactly as forecast. **Core CPI rose 0.3% month-on-month — a tenth of a point above the 0.2% consensus — while the annual core rate eased to 2.4% from 2.5%.**

The composition is what matters for the trade. Core came in hotter than expected one day after the August producer price index showed **diesel up 24.1%** and energy up 4.2% year-on-year, and with **Brent above USD 104 and WTI near USD 100**. Fed funds futures on the CME FedWatch tool were pricing roughly a **59-62% chance of a 25bp hike at the September 15-16 FOMC** — where the July meeting already produced **three dissents in favour of hiking**.

The bond market is the transmission channel. The **10-year Treasury yield is hovering just under 5% (about 4.95%)**, having come within a whisker of 5% overnight, and the **30-year sits at 19-year highs (about 5.31% on September 10)**. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent's beefed-up buyback operation **failed to calm the long end** — an important signal that the term premium, not just the policy rate, is repricing. Equity futures were up about 0.4% premarket as traders bought the dip: the Dow entered Friday **on course for its second-worst week of 2026**.

## Trump's USD 5,000 "Trump Dividend" Promise Is a USD 1.2-1.35 Trillion Fiscal Event

At the Republican National Committee's midterm convention in Dallas (September 9-10), President Trump pledged to **issue a USD 5,000 "Trump dividend" to every adult US citizen if Republicans hold both chambers of Congress after the November 3 midterms**. The Committee for a Responsible Federal Budget puts the cost at about **USD 1.2 trillion**; an NBC News estimate is **USD 1.35 trillion** (roughly 240-260 million adults).

The funding story does not close. Vice President JD Vance pointed to **tariff revenue**, but the Congressional Budget Office estimates the government has collected only **USD 167 billion** in tariffs so far this fiscal year, against a baseline deficit of **USD 2.1 trillion**. The pledge therefore widens the deficit, which the bond market is already pricing — the WSJ explicitly tied this week's yield surge to "more questions over federal spending". Two second-order effects matter for equities: extra cash would **raise inflation and force the Fed to lean tighter** (the very thing the President says he does not want), and the payment is **legally untested** because it is conditioned on one party winning an election.

This is the fourth time this term the President has floated direct payments; none has been issued. It is a campaign signal, not a policy yet — position it as a deficit/inflation impulse rather than an earnings event.

## Canada Escalation Enters Its Tariff-Scope Phase — Product List Changes September 15

The White House fact sheet responding to Canada's retaliation confirms the mechanics of the escalation: **five Proclamations under Section 338 of the Tariff Act of 1930** to ban certain Canadian products and modify the scope of the July 20 tariffs. Two dates matter:

- **September 15** — product **additions and removals** take effect. **Rock salt and cement come out of the Section 338 tariffs; all-terrain vehicles and additional dairy products go in.**
- **September 29** — the **import bans** on certain Canadian dairy and other products take effect.

These duties apply **regardless of USMCA origin** and stack on top of Section 232. On the aircraft front, Trump posted on Truth Social (September 7) that there will be **"NO MORE SELLING BOMBARDIER IN THE UNITED STATES"** and referenced **Gulfstream (a General Dynamics subsidiary)** being blocked in Canada. Canada's retaliatory tariffs on steel, aluminium, appliances, electronics and wood/pulp/paper took effect at midnight September 9.

## Leader Stock Trades: Congress Trading Ban Floated as Trump's June Disclosure Is Dissected

The midterm convention programme included a **proposed ban on members of Congress trading stocks** — the first time the idea has been platformed at this level during this term. It arrives alongside continued dissection of the President's own filings:

- **June disclosure (filed August 22):** more than **1,000 securities trades** in June, including purchases of **Berkshire Hathaway, Visa, Mastercard and Cintas**, sales of **Meta Platforms and Motorola Solutions**, and a **Vanguard ETF sale in the USD 5-25 million band**.
- **Energy holdings (CNBC, September 9):** Trump's nine largest energy positions — **CVX, COP, XOM, KMI, MPC, OXY, PSX, VLO, WMB** — gained an estimated **USD 1.5-4.4 million during the Iran war**, traded through June 29 with at least 23 sales.
- **Intel reference (September 6 Truth Social):** a claim of "hundreds of billions" of gains, tied to the government's roughly **10% passive stake in Intel bought for USD 8.9 billion**, which has produced large but undocumented paper gains. Intel is up about 190% in 2026, and Trump has publicly said the US has made about **USD 30 billion**.

The White House position is that the portfolio is managed by independent third parties in discretionary accounts and that the President and his family cannot direct trades. For a trader, the utility is the **copy-trade signal**: the disclosed purchases are large-cap quality compounders and defence names, and the government's Intel stake creates an explicit policy-equity link.
