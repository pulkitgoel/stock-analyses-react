## The Senate Kills the CLARITY Act — Crypto's Legislative Route Is Closed for 2026

The 60-vote cloture motion on the **Digital Asset Market Clarity Act (H.R. 3633)** failed **49-50** at 2:19 pm ET on September 15. It did not merely miss the threshold — it failed to win even a simple majority.

The vote split is the story:

- **Every Democrat and both independents voted no**, joined by four Republicans: **Susan Collins (ME), Josh Hawley (MO), Jerry Moran (KS) and Thom Tillis (NC)**. **Rand Paul (KY) did not vote.**
- **Tillis switched his vote from Yea to Nay at the last minute** — a procedural move that, because only a member of the prevailing side can do it, let him immediately file a motion to reconsider. That keeps the negotiation technically alive rather than closing the session on the bill. He posted that "this is not the end for the Clarity Act."
- The calendar is unforgiving: the **House has cancelled its weeks of September 21 and 28**, the **Senate's state work period begins October 5**, and the **midterms are November 3**. CoinDesk's read is that if cloture failed, the next Congress is the likely next opportunity.
- Industry's fallback — **"Plan B"** per Cameron Winklevoss — is to lean on **SEC Chair Paul Atkins and CFTC Chair Michael Selig to "write Clarity through rulemaking."** Atkins said a day earlier that his proposed crypto-asset regulation answers the key question of "when does a covered investment contract cease to exist."

The market repriced specific regulatory exposure rather than crypto as an asset class. **Coinbase fell roughly 9-10% to about $174.42** on the news, **Circle dropped 8-11%**, **Bullish -4.6%** and **Robinhood -3.6%**. In the underlying market, **Bitcoin closed at $75,590 (-3.30%)**, **Ether $2,398 (-4.66%)**, **Solana $96.87 (-5.50%)** and **XRP $1.2833 (-9.83%)** — XRP, the token most directly tied to a pending US regulatory question, fell hardest. Total crypto market capitalisation sits near **$2.6 trillion**, with Bitcoin around **$76,000** on Wednesday.

The failed vote interrupted a Fed-driven relief rally: Bitcoin had gained 1.76% to $78,173 on Monday as nearly every major bank shifted to expecting a US rate rise.

## Leader Trades: The Ethics Clause That Helped Sink the Bill

The CLARITY Act's final text carried a revised **government ethics provision** that would have forced covered officials — **including the President** — to place digital-asset holdings in blind trusts or divest them. That clause is why the vote is a leader-trade story, not just a policy story.

- **Senator Elizabeth Warren** called the ethics language "a tiny little fig leaf that claims they're doing something about corruption, but that is carefully written to make sure that it will not prevent Donald Trump from making his **next $1.4 billion in crypto profits**."
- Trump's **2025 annual disclosure showed more than $1.4 billion in crypto-related income and proceeds** — including **more than $500 million from World Liberty Financial token sales**. Reuters separately estimates the broader family crypto extraction at **$2.3 billion** through April 2026.
- The most recent **periodic transaction report (OGE Form 278-T), covering June 2026 and released August 22**, disclosed **1,051 securities transactions valued between $78.1 million and $263.1 million** — **566-576 purchases and about 475 sales**. Purchases included **Berkshire Hathaway, Visa, Mastercard, Cintas, Home Depot, Fidelity National Information Services**; sales included **Meta and Motorola Solutions**; the largest single transaction was a **$5-25 million sale of a Vanguard dividend ETF on June 22**. Coinbase, RTX, Northrop Grumman and Home Depot all appear in the filing.
- The scale is unprecedented: **more than 21,000 securities trades in 2025 worth an estimated $600 million to $1.86 billion**, and **3,642 trades in Q1 2026 alone worth $220-750 million** — with individual buys of **Nvidia, Microsoft, Broadcom, Amazon and Apple each in the $1-5 million band**.
- The White House position is that these are discretionary accounts run by independent managers using index-replicating models, producing high transaction counts from a passive strategy. **Trump has publicly backed banning members of Congress from trading individual stocks**, and a parallel debate now covers whether any ban should extend to the President and Vice President.

## Standing US Trade Items

- **Canada Section 338:** the revised product lists took effect **September 15** (ATVs and additional dairy in; road salt and cement out). **Import bans on Canadian motor vehicles, dairy and alcohol begin September 29.** Duties apply regardless of USMCA compliance and stack on top of Section 232, producing effective rates near 100% on some lines. Canada retaliated effective September 8 with counter-tariffs of up to 50% on C$27.6 billion (USD 20 billion) of US goods.
- **The January 1, 2027 pledge:** Trump stated on August 24 that tariffs on Canadian cars, trucks, auto parts and steel would rise to **50%**, doubling the existing Section 232 auto rate. No proclamation has been published — treat as a stated pledge, not policy.
