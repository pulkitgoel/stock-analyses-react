## The PM Dedicates ₹3,350 Crore of Ladakh Infrastructure

Prime Minister Narendra Modi inaugurated and dedicated five development projects worth **about ₹3,350 crore in Ladakh on October 15** via video conferencing, spanning aviation, a strategic road, hydro power, irrigation and heritage conservation.

The five projects named in the PMO release:

- **The Kushok Bakula Rinpoche Airport terminal in Leh — ₹650 crore.** It triples flight capacity and lifts annual passenger handling from **10 lakh to 35 lakh**. It is India's first terminal to use **geothermal heating alongside solar** to suit the high-altitude climate.
- **The Nimmu–Padum–Darcha road — ₹2,500 crore.** The PM said it cuts travel time on the corridor **from 12 hours to 3 hours** and "will also greatly help trade and business."
- **The Hanu hydroelectric project and its associated transmission network**, following his earlier inauguration of the Srinagar-Leh transmission line, with further generation planned in the **Puga Valley**.
- **Forty newly constructed reservoirs under Project Amrit Him Sarovar**, aimed at irrigation security in the high-altitude terrain.
- **India's first Petroglyph Conservation Park in Leh**, protecting historic rock art.

The Prime Minister's stated baseline for comparison, since Ladakh became a Union Territory in 2019: the road network has gone **from 1,800 km to over 4,000 km**, major bridges **from 19 to nearly 75**, helipads **from 7 to over 40**, tap-water coverage **from 3.5% to nearly 99% of households**, homestays **from about 150 to 3,000**, and **11,000 greenhouses** are now operational. He also noted that the government approved a **High Court Bench for Ladakh in August 2026** and that more than 40 border settlements are being upgraded under the Vibrant Villages programme.

## The Data-Centre Policy Taking Shape, and the Grid Cost Question Behind It

Two statements on **October 14 and 15** set out the government's approach to the data-centre buildout, and the framing matters more than the timing.

- Minister of State for Electronics and IT **Jitin Prasada** said the government is **rolling out a dedicated policy for data centres**, positioning India as a destination for hyperscale facilities: "**India is a very fertile ground for hyperscale data centres.**" India currently approves data centres under existing environmental and building rules rather than under a sector-specific framework.
- The Modi government views the sector as a **$200 billion opportunity**, and the PM has invited "the whole world's data to reside in India."
- But Prasada set a condition that defines the policy's purpose: data centres in some countries are **taking power away from consumers, who then pay more for electricity**, and "we don't want to get into that." The concern is not hypothetical — the AI data-centre boom is already putting India's power grid under pressure and driving up upgrade costs, with resident protests against facilities in cities including Mumbai.
- Separately, Power Secretary **Pankaj Agarwal** said the government will roll out a **National Electricity Data Sharing Framework in October**, with a national data governance committee to be chaired by the Chairperson of the Central Electricity Authority.
- Prasada did not elaborate on the policy's scope or when it would be unveiled.

The investment case is inverted from the usual AI trade. In India the binding constraint on hyperscale is **power generation, transmission and cooling capacity**, not chips — which is why the listed beneficiaries are grid and equipment suppliers rather than semiconductor names.

## The Cabinet's Festive Cash: a 78-Day Railway Bonus and a Five-Year Welfare Extension

Two Cabinet decisions with direct household cash effects were reported on **October 15**.

- The Union Cabinet approved a **productivity-linked bonus equivalent to 78 days' wages for approximately 11.4 lakh eligible railway employees**, at a total cost of **₹2,009.61 crore** and a maximum of **₹17,951** per qualifying employee. The decision is timed ahead of the festive season.
- The Cabinet also approved the **continuation of the National Social Assistance Programme for 2026-27 to 2030-31 at ₹53,428 crore**, renaming it the **Pradhan Mantri Samajik Sahayata Yojana**, with a revised disability-pension structure of **₹375 and ₹625** against a **40% benchmark**.
- The Supreme Court separately directed Indian Railways to provide **insurance cover for passengers who buy tickets offline**, effective **October 27, 2026**. The existing scheme covers ₹10 lakh for death and permanent total disability, ₹7.5 lakh for permanent partial disability, ₹2 lakh for hospitalisation and ₹10,000 for transport of mortal remains. The insurance is currently attached to online ticketing.

Both Cabinet items put cash into roughly 11.4 lakh railway households and the welfare net in the same fortnight as the festival season. That is a consumption signal, and the market read it as one — the Nifty Auto index rose 1.66% on October 15.

## PM GatiShakti at Five — and One Correction on the ITLA

The PM GatiShakti National Master Plan completed **five years** this week, and the government's own scorecard is worth recording precisely.

- **421 projects worth ₹20.23 lakh crore** had been evaluated under the platform in 2026.
- The platform carried **more than 23,493 data layers** as of **October 12, 2026**.
- Implementation is overseen by an **Empowered Group of Secretaries chaired by the Cabinet Secretary**.
- Union Minister **Piyush Goyal** said the next phase will focus on **multimodal connectivity**.

**One correction that matters for anyone reading the coverage:** the **Integrated Transport & Logistics Authority (ITLA)** — the SPV approved to plan, appraise and monitor transport projects worth ₹500 crore or more across roads, railways, ports, civil aviation and urban mobility, with a mandate to cut logistics costs from 14% of GDP to under 10% — was **cleared by the Union Cabinet on October 6, 2026**, not this week. Reporting that recycled it alongside the GatiShakti anniversary on October 15 is a re-crawl of the earlier release. It is a genuine structural positive for the infrastructure order pipeline; it is not new news.
