The Iran decision moved from analysis to calibration on Thursday. The President said Washington is not negotiating, a report said strikes are being planned for after the midterms, and Brent went to a five-month high. The rest of the tape — a 2.3% Nasdaq decline, a 14% jump in the VIX, a 10-year yield falling through 5.03% — was the market working out which side of that trade it wants to own.

**Speaking on Wednesday, Donald Trump said the United States was not currently negotiating with Iran and that "something one way or the other" would happen.** Separately, the New York Times reported that **US and Israeli militaries have drafted plans to resume strikes on Iran after the November 3 midterm election**, with Tehran's energy infrastructure, nuclear facilities and missile and drone arsenals as likely targets. The State Department has ordered some diplomats and the families of US government employees to leave Saudi Arabia — an "ordered departure" — after a Houthi drone attack on **Saudi Aramco's Shaybah oilfield killed two people** and strikes hit Dammam airport.

**Brent crude rose $3.19, or 2.9%, to $112.01 a barrel by 1311 GMT — its highest since May 18.** West Texas Intermediate traded near $98.70. The UK is reported to be deploying warplanes to help defend Saudi Arabia, and Iran's Revolutionary Guard has described a shift to an "offensive" doctrine.

## The Two-Way Problem With This Trade

The supply story is not one-directional. Crude exports through the Strait of Hormuz have been climbing back toward pre-conflict levels, and some tankers are reportedly switching off tracking systems to move without being targeted. That caps the upside — but it also means the price is being set by the *probability* of an interruption rather than an interruption itself, which is precisely the kind of pricing that reverses hard on a single headline.

For equity positioning the distinction matters: a signed deal retraces this move quickly; a strike campaign does not.

## Second: the de minimis compliance date arrives today

**October 22 is the compliance date for 19 CFR 145.12(a)(2)(v) and (vi)** under the June 24 final rule indefinitely suspending the $800 de minimis exemption for mail shipments. Low-value postal consignments now carry fees, registration obligations and a bond guarantee, with partner-government-agency data required for entry.

The exemption itself has been suspended since June 24, so this is not the day the cost appears — it is the day the paperwork becomes enforceable.

## Third: Meta raised capex and created a second share class — the AI trade did the rest

Operating income fell 37% to $12.99bn and the operating margin halved to 20% from 40%, driven by a **$9.89bn legal charge** tied to the youth-mental-health settlement. Headcount is down 15% year on year to 66,775. The company also announced plans for **a new class of nonvoting stock**, and Q4 revenue guidance is $73-76bn.
