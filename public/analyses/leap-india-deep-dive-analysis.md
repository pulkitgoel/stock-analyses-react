## 2. Business Overview

LEAP India is **India's only national-scale asset pooling company** and the clear market leader in pallet pooling with **~90% share by fleet size**. It does not move goods — it owns the containers goods move in, and rents them out on a pay-per-use or annuity basis.

**What it does, in plain terms:** FMCG, automotive and e-commerce companies need pallets, reusable containers and material-handling equipment (MHE). The traditional model is to buy them, store them, repair them, and lose them. LEAP gives customers the same assets on rent, retrieves them anywhere in the country through its network, repairs them, and re-rents them. Customers convert capex into opex; LEAP earns an annuity on assets it recycles for years.

**Scale and infrastructure (FY26)**
- 14.9 million pooled assets; asset base value ₹1,690 Cr; pallet utilisation 89.2%
- 10,100-10,500 customer touchpoints; 28-29 fulfilment centres
- 1,000+ customers including Hindustan Coca-Cola, Marico, Panasonic, Daikin and Toll Logistics
- **Top-100 customer churn: 0.00% in FY26** (down from 0.75% in FY24) — an extraordinary retention statistic that reflects genuinely high switching costs
- Revenue mix: pallets ~62%, with containers and MHE making up the balance; MHE income grew 29% YoY in Q1 FY27

**How it got to 90% share**
- KKR acquired an 80.63% stake in 2023 through Vertical Holdings II Pte, funding scale-up
- Acquired **CHEP India** in January 2025 — the second-largest pallet pooling player — consolidating the market into effectively one national operator with 7.92 Mn → 14.7 Mn assets and ₹3,719 Mn → ₹7,474 Mn revenue between FY24 and FY26
- Acquired Taron (India's second-largest forklift pooling business, leader in lithium-ion MHE)
- Founder/CMD Sunu Mathew previously ran FMCG sales at CHEP India — he built the competitor and then bought his former employer's India business

**Market runway:** India's palletisation rate is ~17% and pooling penetration only ~9.4%, versus 60-80% palletisation in developed markets. The RHP projects the domestic pallet pooling market growing ~14.9% CAGR from FY26 to FY31, from ~10 million to ~20 million pooled units.

**Moat assessment:** This is one of the more genuine moats in Indian mid-caps. A national pooling network (10,100 touchpoints built over 11 years), retrieval logistics, repair infrastructure and RFID/IoT asset tracking cannot be replicated quickly; the switching cost for a customer is re-tagging every asset and retraining every warehouse. The counter-argument is that pooling is a capital-intensive, commoditised rental product where unorganised regional renters can undercut on price in Tier 2/3 markets.

### The structural story inside the numbers

Three things stand out, and they are the whole investment debate:

1. **EBITDA margin of 49-51%** — exceptional, and it reflects a genuine rental annuity: once assets are placed, incremental revenue drops through at very high margins.
2. **Depreciation of ₹204 Cr against PAT of ₹62 Cr** — the business consumes enormous amounts of capital and depreciates it. EBITDA of ₹379 Cr becomes PBT of just ₹81 Cr after depreciation and interest.
3. **Interest of ₹94 Cr against PBT of ₹81 Cr** — interest expense alone exceeds pre-tax profit. Screener flags "low interest coverage ratio" and, separately, that the company **"might be capitalizing the interest cost."** With CWIP of only ₹8 Cr against fixed assets of ₹1,819 Cr, interest is increasingly being pushed into assets and D&A rather than expensed — relevant because it flatters near-term PAT while raising future depreciation.

## 7. Order Book & Revenue Visibility

This is not an order-book business, and the correct visibility metric is the asset pool:

- 14.9 million pooled assets with an asset base of ₹1,690 Cr and 89.2% utilisation — the installed pool is the revenue base
- Historically adding ~700,000 pallets per year in India over the last six to seven years, a pace management expects to continue
- **India**: palletisation ~17% and pooling penetration 9.4%; market projected to grow ~14.9% CAGR FY26-31 (RHP)
- **GCC expansion** — wholly owned subsidiaries established in Saudi Arabia and the UAE, targeting at least **3 million pallets deployed in the Gulf within 4-5 years** (roughly one-third of the current Indian fleet), on the logic that the GCC has higher palletisation, automation and movement-hire adoption
- **Use of IPO proceeds:** ₹360 Cr debt repayment, ₹120 Cr general corporate — no new capacity is directly funded by the issue, so fleet growth continues to rely on operating cash flow and further borrowings

The visibility is real but it is annuity visibility, not contracted order-book visibility: revenue depends on continuing to place assets and on utilisation holding near 90%.

## 11. Forward Valuation

### Why the P/E is misleading — and what to use instead

At ₹130.50, market cap is ₹5,752 Cr on FY26 PAT of ₹62.34 Cr → **~91x trailing earnings** (Screener's 97.2x, 112x at the IPO price). That looks indefensible against a sector P/E of 29.5x.

But P/E is the wrong lens for an asset-rental business. FY26 EBITDA was ₹378.8 Cr, and ₹298 Cr of it disappeared into depreciation (₹204 Cr) and interest (₹94 Cr). The correct comparator is EV/EBITDA:

| Metric | Calculation | Value |
|---|---|---|
| Market cap | ₹5,752 Cr | — |
| Debt post-IPO | ~₹1,107 Cr (₹1,467 Cr less ₹360 Cr repaid) | — |
| Enterprise value | ~₹6,860 Cr | — |
| FY26 EBITDA | ₹378.8 Cr | **EV/EBITDA ~18.1x** |
| FY27E EBITDA (+20%) | ~₹455 Cr | **~15.1x** |
| Global comparable — Brambles/CHEP (ASX: BXB) | Pallet pooling, global leader | 15-18x EV/EBITDA |

So on the metric that actually fits the business, LEAP trades at the **top of the global comparable range, not outside it** — with far faster growth than Brambles but far worse returns on capital (ROE 8% vs Brambles' double digits) and a much weaker balance sheet.

**Return-on-capital red flag:** Screener computes FY26 ROCE at 8.34% and 3-year ROE at 6.32%. The company presents "ROCE 19%+" in its investor deck. That gap is too large to be rounding. Screener's figure is EBIT (PBT ₹81 Cr + interest ₹94 Cr = ₹175 Cr) over capital employed (~₹2,100 Cr) ≈ 8.3%; the company's 19%+ almost certainly uses a different definition (EBITDA-based, or a net-debt/revaluation-adjusted base). Until the company reconciles it, **use the 8% figure when comparing against the cost of capital** — a business earning 8% on capital while paying ~9-10% on debt is not yet compounding shareholder value.
