## Rubio in Mumbai: "As Close as You Can Be Without Having Executed"

The US Secretary of State arrived in Mumbai on Tuesday morning and met External Affairs Minister **S Jaishankar** at the Taj Mahal Palace at 3:50 pm, with US Ambassador **Sergio Gor** and Foreign Secretary **Vikram Misri** in the room. His programme, per the MEA advisory issued on October 19, runs **October 20-22** — Mumbai first, then the **Jamnagar refinery complex in Gujarat on Wednesday**, where he meets business leaders including **Mukesh Ambani**.

On the trade deal, Rubio's formulation to reporters was the most explicit any US official has given: **"I would say we're about as close as you can be without having executed the agreement."** Reuters reports a comprehensive deal is almost complete and could be signed during the visit; Commerce Secretary Rajesh Agarwal had said on September 9 that the first tranche was finalised and in legal drafting.

Two things changed the odds in the past week. Trump **waived** the Graham Act's Section 112 and 113 duties and secondary sanctions on Friday, removing the 100% tariff that would have applied to Indian goods as a consequence of Russian crude purchases. And the administration is now in a **60-day review** during which it decides which tariffs, if any, to impose.

The unresolved piece is the parallel **USTR Section 301 excess-capacity probe into India**. Nothing about the waiver touches it.

**The Jamnagar leg is the part with a number attached.** Rubio said he would press Reliance to refine more **Venezuelan** crude and to consider producing oil in Venezuela again:

> "We will talk about [refining more Venezuelan crude] tomorrow with Reliance... They have done so in the past, and I think as more and more of Venezuelan oil comes online, that is something they can do."

The mechanics are specific. Jamnagar's two refineries have combined capacity of about **1.4 million barrels per day** and are configured to run ultra-heavy sour grades, including Venezuelan crude with near-sludge consistency. Venezuelan output has risen since the US capture of **Nicolás Maduro** in January, and Reliance has already become a significant importer. What it is not is an operator inside Venezuela — that is the ask Rubio is carrying.

For an Indian refiner, this is a feedstock question, not a diplomatic one. India imports **more than 85% of its crude**, and the whole post-2022 refining margin story has been built on buying discounted barrels that others would not touch. A US-brokered Venezuelan slate is a substitute for Russian barrels that are now politically expensive to hold — and it comes with the same heavy-sour advantage Jamnagar was built for.

## The India-New Zealand FTA Is in Force Today

The agreement enters into force on **October 20, Vijayadashami**, with the **Customs Tariff (Determination of Origin of Goods under the Free Trade Agreement between India and New Zealand) Rules, 2026** notified on October 15 and the implementing duty notification under Section 25(1) of the Customs Act, 1962 taking effect the same day.

The terms:

- **Zero duty on 100% of India's exports from day one**, eliminating New Zealand peak tariffs of up to 10%.
- Coverage concentrated in **textiles and apparel, leather and footwear, gems and jewellery, engineering goods and processed foods**.
- **Duty-free access for wooden logs, coking coal and metal scrap** on the import side.
- **Dairy, meat (except sheep meat), sugar and edible oils are excluded** — the politically sensitive list stayed out.
- Tariff-rate quotas with minimum import price and seasonal windows for apples, kiwifruit and Manuka honey.
- **USD 20 billion** New Zealand investment facilitation commitment, MFN treatment in about **139 sub-sectors** across **118 sectors**, **5,000** temporary entry visas and **1,000** working-holiday visas.
- New Zealand will accept **FDA, EMA, MHRA and Health Canada** inspection approvals for pharma and medical devices.

The scale check matters more than the announcement. Bilateral merchandise trade was **USD 1.1 billion in 2025-26** against a stated NZD 7 billion 2030 goal. This is a template deal, not a volume deal — which is why the stock read is about specific tariff lines rather than sector-wide re-rating.

## The Market Was Shut, and Monday's Close Was Ugly

There was no trading on Tuesday — **NSE and BSE were closed for Dussehra**, the next holiday being **Diwali-Balipratipada on November 10**. So the reference point is Monday's close, and it was the fourth consecutive weak session:

- **Sensex 73,852.90, down 876.37 points or 1.17%**
- **Nifty 50 22,888.95, down 257.20 points or 1.11%**
- **Rupee 94.92 against the dollar, down 3 paise**
- **PSU Bank was the only green sectoral index, up 2.6%**; private banks and IT were down **1.3% each**

The gainers were **SBI +1.12%, Adani Ports +1.07%, L&T +0.61% and IndiGo +0.24%**; the laggards were **Axis Bank -3.85%, UltraTech -2.82%, Infosys -2.72%, Bajaj Finance -2.54% and Trent -2.43%**. UltraTech's Q2 revenue (₹23,300 crore) and EBITDA (₹3,720 crore) beat while net profit of ₹1,490 crore missed — a valuation reset dressed up as an earnings event.

For the trade story, the sequencing is what matters. A signed BTA before the November 3 midterms would be the single largest re-rating trigger available to Indian equities this quarter, and the market has spent October selling rather than positioning for it.
