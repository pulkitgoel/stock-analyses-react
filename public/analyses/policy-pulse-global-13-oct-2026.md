## China's 4F² DRAM Claim Takes 5.5% Off the KOSPI

The single largest cross-market move of October 13 came from a product roadmap announced in Shanghai over the weekend.

- **ChangXin Memory Technologies (CXMT)** plans to mass-produce **DDR5 RDIMM built on a 4F² DRAM cell architecture** by the end of 2026 — a structure that cuts cell area by roughly **30%**. President **Cao Kanyu** laid it out at an industry conference. The claim has **not been independently verified**.
- **South Korea's KOSPI fell 5.46% to close at 6,035.35**, its steepest decline since August 19. It opened 3.10% lower and plunged as much as **7.41% to an intraday low of 5,911.11**, trading below 6,000 for the first time in 47 sessions. The index is now **more than 13% down in five sessions** and over **35% below its June record**.
- The Korea Exchange triggered a **sell-side sidecar at 9:47 a.m. local time** after KOSPI 200 futures fell more than 5% — the first such activation since August 19 and the 26th this year.
- **Foreign investors net sold Won 4.53 trillion (about USD 3.4 billion)** of KOSPI shares, their heaviest selling since the July 28 rout. Retail bought Won 2.25 trillion and institutions Won 1.69 trillion, and it was not enough. Decliners outnumbered advancers 630 to 247.
- **Samsung Electronics closed 6.79% lower** and **SK hynix fell 9.47%** — a fifth consecutive session of losses for both. **SK Square tumbled about 12%** and Samsung Electro-Mechanics fell about 8%. The KOSDAQ fell 1.43% to 837.03.
- Contagion, and the tell in it: **Tokyo Electron -6.6%**, **SoftBank Group -8%+**, **TSMC -1.8%**, **ASML -1.8%**, STMicroelectronics, Soitec and Infineon down 1.4-2.3%, and an AI-token basket down 7.7%. Overnight in the US, the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index fell **2.67%**, with Micron -3.88% and SanDisk -5.97%.
- **The reverse leg is the part Indian readers should not miss.** The same shock that de-rates capital-intensive memory producers re-rates capital-light services: **India's Nifty IT index rose 5.22% on October 13**, its biggest single-day gain since May 2025.

## Five Days to the Graham Act Deadline, and Still No Notice to Congress

The **Lindsey O. Graham Sanctioning Russia and Iran Act**, signed on **September 18, 2026**, sets a hard date of **October 18** — with the thirtieth day falling on a Sunday, several law firms advising clients give the operative date as **Monday, October 19**.

- **Section 112** requires the president to raise duties on all goods imported from Russia, explicitly including petroleum products, to **up to 500%**. **Section 113** caps additional duties at **100%** on all goods from the five largest buyers of Russian crude or gas — the two numbers in circulation belong to different sections and different targets.
- **The trigger is narrow, and it matters for India.** A country qualifies only if it **knowingly makes new purchases on or after** the deadline. Purchases made before it do not count. India, the largest buyer at roughly **1.6 million barrels a day** (about 30% of its crude imports last financial year), accordingly has **no liability for anything already bought**.
- **Section 113(g)** required the president or USTR to send Congress a written justification **not later than 10 days before** imposing a duty — on or about **October 8** for an October 18 start. As of October 13 the administration had published **no target list, no duty and no waiver**, and the president retains the power to waive in the national interest.
- The live contradiction: a Treasury **General License 135 issued October 9** permits Russian diesel into the United States until **April 7, 2027**, while Section 112 says duties "shall" be raised on exactly those imports. **Trump's October 13 response** was to dismiss Republican critics as not "serious" and to float a **US diesel export cap**.
- On the Indian crude slate, the direction of travel is already visible: one trade-policy tracker reports Urals now pricing **above** Brent and India's Russian share falling to about 35%. ⚠️ **Single-outlet figure — treat as directional, not settled.**

## Brent Ends a Three-Session Winning Run as Washington Weighs a Diesel Export Cap

- **Brent settled at USD 106.65 a barrel on Monday, October 12**, the highest since September 15. On **October 13 it fell about 2% intraday**, trading in a **USD 104.6-106.0** range with **WTI near USD 91.9-93.3**. This edition was written while the US session was still open, so treat these as intraday levels.
- The supply risk has not cleared: attacks on Saudi energy sites continue, Houthi-Saudi tensions remain elevated, a tanker was struck near the Strait of Hormuz, and shipping and insurance costs have risen to levels that are themselves obstructing crude and product flows. Trump has said he is weighing whether to join Saudi strikes.
- The offsets are a **Treasury general licence permitting Russian diesel** into the US until April 2027 and the **October 11 Russia-Ukraine "energy ceasefire"**, which Zelenskyy says Russia has already breached with a strike on a Kharkiv power plant.

## Gold Holds Above USD 4,170 as the Dollar Slips From an 18-Month High

- **Spot gold traded around USD 4,178-4,187 an ounce** on October 13, up 0.2-0.4% on the session, with December futures at USD 4,202-4,211 and **spot silver near USD 60.2**. That is a mild recovery from Monday's 0.5% decline, as the dollar and oil both retreated.
- The **dollar index slipped to about 102.3** from an 18-month high, and the **10-year Treasury yield eased to about 5.19-5.24%** — its lowest since October 2 — while the **30-year held near 5.60%**.

## Europe Rises as Chemicals Lead and LVMH Drags Luxury

- The **STOXX 600 rose 0.61% to 639.54** by 0849 GMT, a one-week high. **London's FTSE 100 gained about 0.8-0.9%**, the best of the majors, **Paris' CAC 40 added about 0.3%** and **Frankfurt's DAX 40 about 0.2%**.
- The internals are the interesting part: **chemicals led with a 1.7% gain** and heavyweight **banks added 1%**, while **technology fell 0.9%** — Europe is tech-light, so the semiconductor rout that cost the KOSPI 5.5% and capped the Nasdaq was a relative advantage. **Euro-zone bond yields dipped** alongside Treasuries as the tech selloff pushed some flows into government debt.
- The drag was luxury: **LVMH fell 1.3%** on weaker-than-expected third-quarter sales as Chinese consumer sentiment deteriorated, pulling the luxury sector down about 1%.
- The **IMF lowered its 2026 euro-zone growth estimate to 1.1%**, after 1.2% in 2025, though that is 0.2 percentage points above its July projection.
