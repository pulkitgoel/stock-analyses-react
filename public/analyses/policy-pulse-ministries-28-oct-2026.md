India's factory data for September came out today and it is the most useful thing published this week — not because it was good, but because it shows precisely which part of the industrial economy is carrying the number and which part has quietly stopped.

The market fell on crude, not on this. That mismatch is the opportunity.

## First: 6.7% headline, 10.4% capital goods, and a pharma collapse

**The Index of Industrial Production rose 6.7% year-on-year in September 2026**, with the index at **124.3 against 116.5 a year earlier**. Growth slowed from **8.0% in August** and came in **below the 7.2% consensus**.

A data note, because it matters for anyone comparing month to month: one tracker reports August as downwardly revised to 7.8% while the primary release reports 8.0%. The direction of the revision is the same either way — September decelerated — but the precise base is not settled across sources, and I am not going to pick the more flattering one.

Inside the number:

- **Manufacturing +7.6%**, with 17 of 23 industry groups growing.
- **Electricity and gas supply +11.2%** — the strongest broad segment.
- **Mining -6.2%**, contracting, led by non-metallic minerals.
- **Intermediate goods +12.2%** and **capital goods +10.4%** — the two highest use-based categories, and both investment-linked.
- At industry level: **electrical equipment +33.9%**, rubber and plastics +19.9%, motor vehicles +19%, computers/electronics +7.3% (down from 19.3% in August), wood products -10.5%, **pharmaceuticals -8.9%** (from +1.6% in August).
- **Q2 FY27 IIP growth accelerated to 7.3%**, which is why consensus GDP expectations for the quarter sit above 7.0% even with today's deceleration.

The composition is the story. **Capital goods at 10.4% and intermediate goods at 12.2% say the investment cycle is running. Mining at -6.2% and pharma at -8.9% say two large employers are not.** A single headline number cannot hold both of those, which is why the sector tables below are split rather than uniform.

The pharma number deserves a caution rather than a trade. A single month can be a base effect, a plant shutdown or an inventory correction, and the US generics pricing environment is a separate variable entirely. **This is an output statistic, not a margin statement.**

## Second: a $4 billion shipyard where the shipyard is not the trade

**Union Minister for Ports, Shipping and Waterways Sarbananda Sonowal met HD Hyundai Chairman Chung Ki-sun in Seoul today** and the project to build a greenfield shipyard at Thoothukudi, Tamil Nadu moved from memorandum to structure:

- **HD Korea Shipbuilding & Offshore Engineering (HD KSOE)** reiterated its intent to participate as **primary strategic investor** in a **$4 billion** greenfield yard.
- A **joint working group** will now oversee development, and a **joint venture structure** is to be agreed.
- The **Detailed Project Report is expected within six months**.
- The base document is the MoU signed **20 April 2026** between HD KSOE and **National Shipbuilding & Heavy Industries Park, Tamil Nadu Limited (NSHIP-TN)**, alongside the India-Korea Comprehensive Framework for Partnership in Shipbuilding adopted the same month.
- Technology transfer and workforce skilling are central, with Indian industry and academia to be brought in.

The strategic frame is India-Korea, and the minister was explicit that the goal is to make good on the vision the chairman set out to Prime Minister Modi.

**The tradeable problem: the equity is Korean and the park is a Tamil Nadu state entity. Neither has an NSE ticker.** What a listed Indian shipyard owns is a capacity benchmark, and in the long run a competitor.

Six months to a DPR is the honest timeline. Nothing is contracted.

## Third: Japan, mine countermeasures and joint naval design

**Defence Minister Rajnath Singh met Japanese Defence Minister Shinjiro Koizumi on the margins of the ADMM-Plus in Cebu, Philippines today**, and the joint statement of progress contains two commercially specific lines:

- Discussions on **maritime security cooperation**, including **enhancing mine countermeasure capabilities** and **exploring the possibility of joint development in naval shipbuilding and design**, leveraging Japan's technology and India's production capacity.
- An **India-Japan Defence Industry Forum** at the Embassy of India in Tokyo with **approximately 40 companies** from both countries.

Both sides agreed to accelerate toward the **4th 2+2 Ministerial and a Defence Ministers' meeting in Tokyo later this year**.

## Fourth: the close — crude took the market down, breadth held up

**Sensex 73,246.03, down 352.64 points (-0.48%). Nifty 22,698.85, down 50.90 (-0.22%).** The Sensex recovered most of an intraday loss that had reached roughly 642 points at the morning low.

Sector performance: **Nifty IT -2.43%, Oil & Gas -2.27%**, with Realty, Energy and FMCG down about 0.5% each. Gainers were **Consumer Durables +1.71%, Media +1.58%, Auto +1.21%, Pharma +0.95%**. Broader market outperformed: **Midcap 100 +0.4%, Smallcap 100 +0.9-1.0%**. Breadth was positive at **2,178 advances, 1,957 declines, 185 unchanged**. India VIX eased to 16.27.

Top Nifty gainers: **Apollo Hospitals, L&T, Trent, SBI Life, InterGlobe Aviation**. Top losers: **Infosys, Reliance Industries, ONGC, HCL Tech, Bharti Airtel**.

The cause was external and specific: **Brent jumped about 1% to $114.5-115.5 a barrel** after Yemen's Houthis claimed a ballistic missile strike on Saudi Arabia's **Ras Tanura** refinery — the kingdom's largest, at 550,000 barrels a day, and a complex that includes a shipping terminal. That is an import-bill and margin event for India, not a growth event.

Flows: **FIIs were net buyers of ₹871.83 crore and DIIs net buyers of ₹643.35 crore** in the cash segment, provisional. The rupee traded around **95.02-95.22** to the dollar, with traders reporting RBI intervention.
