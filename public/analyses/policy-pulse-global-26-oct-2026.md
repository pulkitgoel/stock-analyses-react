Crude fell 1.6% on Monday from its highest close in more than four years, and the Asian session still could not hold a bid. That combination is the most informative thing on the tape today.

## First: oil came off the high, and the reason is mechanical

**Brent settled Friday at $116.72 a barrel — up 3.1% and its highest close since June 2022, and up from under $97 earlier in October. On Monday it traded down about 1.6% to roughly $114.80. West Texas Intermediate fell about 2% to $101.**

**A figure-discipline note before the analysis.** The Associated Press reported Brent "fell 1.7% to $109.86" on Monday. That number does not reconcile with AP's own statement two paragraphs later that Brent settled at $116.72 on Friday — a 1.7% decline from $116.72 is $114.74, not $109.86. BusinessLine printed $114.80 early Monday, down 1.6%, and TradingEconomics printed $114.87, down 1.58%. Three sources and the arithmetic all agree on roughly $114.8, so that is the figure used here and AP's $109.86 is discarded.

**What the move means.** Friday's spike was a war-premium event. Monday's reversal came without any de-escalation headline, which tells you the premium is being carried by positioning rather than by an actual interruption in supply. That is the most reversible kind of price, and it will keep taking $4-6 off in a session as easily as it added them last week.

The offsetting detail matters as much: Hormuz flows have been recovering toward pre-war levels, and the East-West pipeline is carrying volume that bypasses the strait entirely. The market is pricing probability, not barrels.

## Third: the "reverse AI trade" did not fire — and saying so is the point

This tracker has documented the July-August pattern where a chip rout in Asia rotated capital into Indian IT services. **It did not happen today, and the evidence is unambiguous.**

**Infosys fell about 4% after cutting FY27 constant-currency growth guidance. Coforge surged on its own Q2. Nifty IT did not rally against the Nikkei's fall — it traded on Indian earnings.**

That is the correct answer even though it is a less satisfying one. Indian IT in October 2026 is being repriced on deal wins and guidance, not on a global rotation, and the two big-cap results either side of today prove it. A template that worked in July should not be reused when the tape contradicts it.

## Fourth: this week is a Fed week with a proof problem attached

**The Federal Reserve decides Wednesday, October 28, with a hold almost fully priced and the market leaning toward a December hike.** The September core PCE print — the Fed's preferred measure — is expected to show inflation at **3.7%**, nearly double target.

The sequencing matters: **the Bank of Japan meets October 30 and the ECB meets October 29**, so all three decisions land within 48 hours of each other, on top of a Big Tech earnings cluster.

**Alphabet, Microsoft and Amazon all report this week.** That is the real event risk, not the Fed. As SPI Asset Management's Stephen Innes framed it: "This week is less about broad conviction and more about proof. Big Tech now has to show that the spending binge still deserves the market's faith."

The AI funding question is now explicit. Meta's Q3 last week showed a company generating $32.79 billion of operating cash flow and spending $34.91 billion on capex, turning free cash flow negative. With the 10-year Treasury yield around 5%, the cost of the debt funding that buildout is a live variable. That is why a chip selloff now shows up in Tokyo and Seoul but not in Chinese platform stocks.

**The US session is open at the time of writing, so nothing here is a close.** Friday's close: **S&P 500 -0.5%, Dow -1.2%, Nasdaq -0.2%**, a second consecutive weekly loss. Monday premarket pointed higher, with S&P futures +0.2% and Dow futures +0.4%.
