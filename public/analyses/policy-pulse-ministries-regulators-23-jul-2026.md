## 🏛️ Ministries & Regulators Watch — July 23, 2026

### 1. 📜 SEBI Proposes Sweeping PMS Overhaul — Foreign Securities, MF-only PMS, Higher Derivatives Flexibility

SEBI on July 23 issued a consultation paper proposing a comprehensive revamp of the Portfolio Management Services (PMS) regulations — the biggest change to PMS rules in years. **Key proposals:**

**🔓 PMS can now invest in foreign securities:** For the first time, SEBI has proposed allowing portfolio managers to invest client funds in foreign securities — including listed overseas equities, listed debt securities, and overseas mutual funds. This brings PMS at par with mutual funds, AIFs, and IFSC-based portfolio managers. Investments remain subject to FEMA and LRS limits. Explicit client consent required.

**📊 New MF-only PMS (MF-PMS) category:** A separate category that invests exclusively in direct plans of mutual funds, ETFs, and SIFs. Minimum investment threshold reduced to ₹25 lakh (from ₹50 lakh). Net-worth requirement for applicants halved to ₹2 crore (from ₹5 crore). Management fee capped at 2.5% of AUM, with performance-based fees allowed with client consent.

**📈 Greater derivatives flexibility:** Total exposure capped at 1.25x AUM; unhedged short exposure through equity derivatives limited to 50% of AUM. Option premium exposure capped at 10% of AUM.

**💼 Expanded investment universe:** Expressly permits investments in 'to-be-listed' securities (pre-IPO). Discretionary PMS can invest up to 10% of AUM in investment-grade unlisted debt.

**🔄 Ease of doing business:** Digital disclosure documents, longer filing timelines, simplified reporting, demat portability (eliminates re-KYC when switching PMS providers), relaxed dealing room requirements for smaller managers.

### 2. 🏛️ SEBI Proposes Online Dispute Resolution (ODR) Revamp

SEBI also issued a consultation paper proposing to revamp the online dispute resolution framework for investor grievances. **Key proposal:** Move investor dispute resolution to stock exchanges (NSE, BSE) as the primary forum. This would replace the current system where disputes are handled by individual market participants with limited escalation mechanisms.

### 3. 🏛️ SEBI Considering Reducing Upfront Margins for Cash Market Trades

Sources indicate SEBI is considering a plan to reduce upfront margins for cash market trades, aligning margins more closely with actual risk rather than fixed percentages. This is a pro-trading-volume move that would benefit exchanges and brokerages.
