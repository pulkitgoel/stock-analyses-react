The Defence Acquisition Council approved ₹2.12 lakh crore of acquisitions today. Not one company was named in the release. That gap is the entire story, and it is where most readers will lose money.

## First: ₹2.12 lakh crore of in-principle approval, and what "in-principle" means

**The DAC, chaired by Defence Minister Rajnath Singh on October 26, accorded Acceptance of Necessity — in-principle administrative approval — to capital acquisition proposals estimated at about ₹2,12,000 crore. Approximately 82% of the procurements will be made from Indian industry.**

The platforms, by service:

**Indian Army**
- High Mobility Vehicles (HMV)
- Self-Propelled Tracked Gun System
- Gun Shot Response System (GSRS)
- Light Multi Role Helicopters (LMRH) — shared with the Air Force
- Very Short-Range Air Defence System (VSHORADS)
- Platform-Based Counter Unmanned Aerial System (C-UAS)
- Unmanned Aerial System Brick Long Range
- Surveillance UAV
- Integrated Surveillance and Strike System
- Long Range Kamikaze Drone System

**Indian Navy**
- Active Offboard Decoy System (AODS)
- Other major naval platforms, indigenously designed and constructed

**Indian Air Force**
- Helicopters, air defence systems and weapons
- Special Utility Helicopters
- Mobile X-Band Doppler Weather Radar System

**Now the part that matters. An AoN is not an order.** It is administrative permission to go and negotiate. The money is not committed, the vendor is not selected, and the contract — the event that actually moves a revenue line — typically lands eighteen to thirty-six months later, sometimes never. Today's announcement moves sentiment. It does not move earnings.

## Second: the Defence Minister put rare earths on the record

**At SWAVLAMBAN 2026, the Navy's Naval Innovation and Indigenisation Seminar, Rajnath Singh called for "Aatmanirbharta 2.0" — a second phase of defence self-reliance oriented toward top-end technology, R&D and resilient supply chains.**

The substantive content, as distinct from the rhetoric:

- **"Supply chain dependencies arise from a limited domestic availability of certain raw materials, such as rare earth elements, and the absence of specific technologies within the country."** A serving Defence Minister naming rare earths as a defence supply-chain constraint is a procurement signal, not a talking point.
- A demand for a **time-bound roadmap** identifying, per item, the technology to be developed, the industry that will drive it, and the resources required.
- **A "Dual Technology Roadmap"** to identify technologies serving both civil and military shipping, plus priority technology lists **beyond the existing Positive Indigenisation Lists** — which implies further indigenisation lists are coming.
- Navy iDEX: challenges up from 20 in 2022 to **229**, roughly 35% of all iDEX challenges; **41 AoNs worth over ₹3,500 crore** and **25 contracts worth over ₹1,900 crore** signed.
- A candid admission that Indian R&D must tolerate failure, and an explicit question about funding mechanisms for high-risk, high-reward projects with private-sector participation.

**The rare-earth line is the one to trade.** India has defence-grade demand for rare-earth magnets and alloys and no scaled domestic separation capacity. Any roadmap naming rare earths as a critical dependency is a multi-year tailwind for the two listed names with actual critical-minerals exposure.
