## Indonesia Loses Its Emerging-Market Status and the Jakarta Index Falls 11%

**MSCI said on Friday October 16 that Indonesia's continued classification as an Emerging Market is "no longer appropriate", and opened a consultation on reclassifying it as a Standalone Market.** It is the largest market the index provider has ever proposed to place in that category.

The market's reaction was the sharpest of the global cycle:

- The **Jakarta Composite Index collapsed 8% at the open, triggering a trading halt**, and was **down 11.1% when trading resumed** — its heaviest single-day fall since **early 1998**, and its lowest level since **2020**. Reuters records the intraday low at **-11.4%**, the worst one-day drop in 28 years.
- The **rupiah slid 0.6% to 18,070 per US dollar**, its weakest since late July. The currency has lost about **8% this year**.
- Indonesian equities are already the **world's worst-performing market in 2026, down roughly 39-40% since January**. Foreign investors have net sold **$4.6-4.9 billion** of Indonesian stocks this year.
- **UBS estimates the reclassification could trigger about $4 billion of passive outflows**, with **active investors potentially adding a further $15 billion**.
- MSCI will consult until **April 30, 2027** and announce a final decision by **end-June 2027**, proceeding unless it finds "clear evidence of material improvements in market governance and functioning."

The reasons MSCI gave are about market integrity, not macro. It cited **"ongoing opacity in shareholding structures"**, **"continued suspected coordinated trading behaviour"** and **"lack of evidence of meaningful enforcement"**, adding that a downgrade to Frontier Markets "would not properly recognise the depth of the market problems."

The pushback is already in motion. The **Indonesia Stock Exchange** said it "respects MSCI's independence" but will request clarification on the basis of the assessment. Senior economic minister **Airlangga Hartarto** said there was "a big question mark" over why the issue was not raised at a meeting in Bangkok this week. **Bank Indonesia** committed to a market presence to defend the rupiah. **Citi's** base case is still that Indonesia retains its emerging-market classification, while flagging that active-fund outflows have become likelier.

If the downgrade proceeds, Indonesia would join **Argentina, Lebanon, Palestine and Ukraine** in the standalone category — the only Asia-Pacific market in that group, and by GDP the largest, at more than twice Argentina's.

## The Read-Across: What a Standalone Indonesia Means for the Rest of Emerging Markets

The second-order question is where the money goes. There is no listed Indian company whose revenue depends on Indonesia's index status, so the mappings below are portfolio-flow inferences rather than company fundamentals.

- MSCI's index architecture means a standalone market is **excluded from both the Emerging and Frontier product families**, which are the products passive funds track and active managers are benchmarked against. The sale is forced, not discretionary.
- India is the **largest remaining Emerging Market** in Asia after China, and Indian equities have **outperformed through the October global risk-off** — the Nifty has gained roughly 668 points over the last three sessions while the KOSPI fell 7.57% in a single day on October 15.
- The countervailing force is that this is a **governance-driven exit, not a valuation-driven one**. Fund allocators do not necessarily reallocate Indonesian money into Indian money; they may reduce emerging-market beta outright, which is the risk to the optimistic reading.

## Paris and Berlin Agree to Soften the EU's 2035 Combustion-Engine Ban

**Chancellor Friedrich Merz and President Emmanuel Macron** published a joint position paper on **October 16** titled "Securing the future of the European automotive industry," and it changes the arithmetic of the EU's 2035 phase-out.

- The **2035 fleet-average CO2 reduction would be limited to 90% rather than 100%**, and crucially **without any obligation to offset the residual 10%** — the point Germany had been fighting for.
- With flexibility for **renewable fuels**, and by counting renewable-fuel use across the existing fleet toward the target, the effective requirement could fall to around **80%**. The paper also proposes a **new vehicle category for cars running exclusively on renewable fuels**.
- Continued sales of combustion-engine vehicles would be permitted as long as the fleet average of about **11g of CO2 per kilometre** is achieved.
- In exchange, Germany supports **tighter "Made in Europe" eligibility**: **70% of car components, excluding batteries, produced in Europe**, and **10% of battery content from European sources**. That restriction would apply **from 2028** to purchase premiums, leasing subsidies and public contracts.
- **UK inclusion is conditional** on London aligning its tariffs on Chinese electric cars with the EU's.

Merz called it "a good day for motorists and for jobs in Germany, France and Europe… This is an investment signal to industry." The European Commission proposed a 90% target in December 2025; this Franco-German paper is a member-state position ahead of that negotiation, not yet EU law.

## Asia Rebounds From Black Thursday — but TSMC's Record Quarter Still Sold Off

Asia's bounce on October 16 was real and incomplete.

- **South Korea's KOSPI jumped 2.1% to 5,668.05**, a day after falling 7.6% and triggering circuit breakers on both boards. **Samsung Electronics rose 2.6%** and **SK hynix added 0.8%**.
- **Japan's Nikkei 225 rose 1.4% to 65,324.29** after a 4.9% fall. **SoftBank Group climbed 1.9%**, **Tokyo Electron 2.6%**, **Kioxia 3.8%**. **Toyota rose about 2.1%** and **Mitsubishi UFJ about 2%**, which is the detail that matters — demand spread into exporters and financials rather than staying confined to the AI names.
- **Taiwan's TAIEX was up 0.3% but TSMC fell 0.8%**, despite reporting record quarterly profits on Thursday and saying AI demand remained very strong.
- **Hang Seng +0.5% to 24,186.83**; **Shanghai Composite +0.2% to 3,716.28**; **S&P/ASX 200 +1% to 8,806.70**.
- In Europe, the **DAX rose 1% to 25,632.39**, the **CAC 40 added 0.2% to 7,947.74**, the **FTSE 100 fell less than 0.1% to 10,836.46**, and the **STOXX Europe 600 rose 0.3%**.
- **India's Sensex rose 0.4%** at the time of the AP wrap, and closed **0.56% higher**.

Two statements did the work. **Nvidia chief Jensen Huang publicly defended CoreWeave's financial position**, which is the specific name whose borrowing costs had become the symbol of AI-financing stress. And **Fed Chair Kevin Warsh**, speaking in Bangkok, called AI "probably the most consequential change to the U.S. and the global economy in my lifetime."
