The Ministry of Defence signed a contract today that is small in rupees and specific in what it buys — which is the useful combination.

## MoD Signs ₹770 Crore Radar Contract With Bharat Electronics

PIB announced at 3:30 PM today (Release ID 2325933) that the Ministry has signed a contract with **Bharat Electronics Limited** to procure the **Improved Low Level Light Weight Radar (LLLR-I) at approximately ₹770 crore**, in the presence of Defence Secretary **Rajesh Kumar Singh** in New Delhi on October 21, 2026.

The specification is the story. The LLLR-I **detects and tracks fighter-size targets and mini/micro drones**, has an **additional capability of prioritising aerial targets**, and is described as enabling the Army to "successfully counter the threat of the Unmanned Aerial System (UAS)." The release frames the buy around indigenisation of air-defence platforms and the MSME ecosystem.

BEL is named in the release, so the primary beneficiary is confirmed rather than inferred. Everything downstream of it is inference.

Scale check, because ₹770 crore is not a needle-mover on its own: the Ministry signed **193 contracts worth ₹2,09,050 crore in FY2024-25**. The signal here is the counter-drone line item appearing as a routine Army procurement, not the size of the order.

## The RBI Injected Another ₹50,000 Crore and Got Nearly Two Times the Bids

The Reserve Bank of India conducted an **overnight variable rate repo auction for ₹50,000 crore today**. It received **bids of ₹93,241 crore** — nearly twice the notified amount — and **accepted ₹50,007 crore**. Another **₹50,000 crore overnight VRR is scheduled for Thursday, October 22**.

This is the second infusion in three sessions. On Monday the RBI ran a **seven-day VRR for ₹1 lakh crore** — its first liquidity infusion since **July 29**, after an absorption cycle that had run since **August 6** — drawing bids of **₹1,50,735 crore** and accepting **₹1,00,014 crore at 5.52%**. The context for all of it: the **weighted average call rate was 5.67% on Monday, 17 basis points above the 5.50% repo**, and the market traded at the **marginal standing facility on Friday for the first time this year**.

Sequence matters. Average daily net absorption ran **₹1.2 lakh crore in July, ₹5.8 lakh crore in August and ₹7.8 lakh crore in September**; daily CRR tightening took effect **October 16**; the liquidity surplus was **₹1,85,873 crore as on October 18**. Flipping from absorption to infusion is the RBI restoring its operating target so that the 25 basis point hike actually transmits. For valuations that is neutral. For anything that earns a spread, it is the first genuinely supportive liquidity signal in ten weeks.

## Jio's IPO Was Fully Subscribed in Under Two Hours

The largest Indian public issue ever opened today and did not need the full three days. **Jio Platforms was subscribed 1.03 times on Day 1**, with the book fully covered **in under two hours** of the 10 AM open. The issue is a **₹30,213 crore fresh issue at a band of ₹1,065 to ₹1,119**, runs **October 21-23**, and is expected to list around **October 28** at a valuation near ₹10.3 lakh crore.

The anchor book did the early work: **₹8,110 crore of anchor allocation cleared more than 30 times**, with bids above **$25 billion** from BlackRock, GIC, Norges, SBI Mutual Fund and LIC. The rupee responded — it closed at **94.4460 (+0.5%)**, its strongest in six weeks.

For the market plumbing, the relevant number is not the ₹30,213 crore raised but the fee pool and the demat and transaction flow around it. India's largest listing is a one-off revenue event for the exchanges and a multi-year distribution event for the brokers and registrars.

## The Close: the IT Rally Did Not Hold

The session reversed twice. The Sensex opened up **527 points at 74,380**, ran to **74,675.54 (+822.64)** and the Nifty to **23,106.60 (+0.95%)** on IT buying and firm global cues; by 12:31 PM the Sensex was **down 428 points at 73,424.86** and the Nifty below 22,800. It ended at **Sensex 73,709.69, down 143.21 points (-0.19%)** and **Nifty 50 at 22,811.35, down 77.60 points (-0.34%)**.

Sectorally the damage was concentrated in financials and consumer discretionary: **Nifty PSU Bank closed 0.51% lower at 8,251.50**, and the biggest Sensex drags were **Bajaj Finance -3.32%, Eternal -3.20%, Trent -2.70%, Axis Bank -2.23%, Maruti Suzuki -2.20%, Bajaj Finserv -2.16%, IndiGo -2.14%, NTPC -2.06% and BEL -1.89%**. Bharti Airtel and L&T were the relative survivors at -0.04% and -0.22%.

Two things did the work. **Bajaj Finance reported a 28.7% year-on-year rise in Q2 net profit to ₹6,266 crore and still fell 3.32%** — the classic sell-the-print in a stock priced for perfection. And **Brent at $109.64** kept the import-bill arithmetic in front of every domestic buyer. Eternal, Adani Green, Indian Hotels and SRF reported today as well; the consumer discretionary reaction was the tell.

The rupee at **94.446** and the Nifty at 22,811 are telling the same story from opposite ends: foreign money is arriving for the IPO, not for the index.
