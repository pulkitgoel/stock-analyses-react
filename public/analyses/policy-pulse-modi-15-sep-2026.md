## PM Modi Opens SEMICON India 2026 on September 17 — Five Years of Chip Policy Meets Its Delivery Test

The Press Information Bureau confirmed on September 15 that **Prime Minister Narendra Modi will inaugurate SEMICON India 2026 on September 17 at Yashobhoomi, New Delhi**, kicking off three days (September 17-19) of the country's flagship semiconductor and electronics conference.

The scale is the story:

- **600-plus exhibitors**, **300 international companies and delegations from more than 40 countries**, and **six country pavilions — Japan, Korea, Netherlands, Singapore, Malaysia and Sweden**.
- Roughly **18,000 sqm of exhibition space** plus a dedicated **360 sqm workforce-development area**; participation from over **300 Indian companies** and representation from **nine-plus states**.
- Named participants in the published lineup include **ASML, Applied Materials, Lam Research, Tokyo Electron, Micron, NXP, Infineon, Teradyne, Advantest, Jabil, Ebara, IBM Research, Merck KGaA, Rapidus, Tata Electronics, CG Power and Uno Minda**.
- The event is organised by the **India Semiconductor Mission (ISM) under MeitY**, in partnership with SEMI.

The policy stack behind it is now substantial. Under the Semicon India Programme the government has **approved 12 semiconductor projects**; the Cabinet cleared **Semicon 2.0 with an outlay of ₹1,27,500 crore on July 15, 2026**, on top of the original programme; Union Minister Ashwini Vaishnaw said on September 11 that **four semiconductor plants begin commercial production during 2026**, with the Dholera fab on ASML tooling. On talent and design, India has now **trained about 68,000 students and taped out 211 chips**.

## Why This Matters for Positioning

The semiconductor complex has been the most policy-driven part of the Indian market since July: Semicon 2.0's ₹1.27 lakh crore, the 12 approved projects, four plants going commercial this year, and now a global summit with the PM opening it. The sector also has a **global tailwind from the US side** — President Trump's explicit rejection of an AI slowdown ("whoever wins AI wins") removes the single biggest demand-side risk to chip capex, which matters for an Indian ecosystem selling into the same supply chain. Conversely, the domestic electronics names are not immune to a market that fell 1.04% today on crude and yields; the IT-services complex (Nifty IT +2.19%) was the only sector that gained, and semiconductors are not part of that rotation.
