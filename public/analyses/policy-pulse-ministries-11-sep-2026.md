## The NSE IPO Is Priced: Band ₹1,700-1,785, ₹22,562 Crore, Opens September 17

The National Stock Exchange formally announced its IPO terms on September 11 — the single largest capital-markets event of the Indian year:

- **Price band: ₹1,700-1,785 per share**, face value ₹1.
- **Issue size: 100% offer for sale of 12.64 crore shares — about ₹22,562 crore at the upper band** (₹21,494 crore at the lower band), **trimmed nearly 15%** from the 14.89 crore shares in the June DRHP.
- **Timeline: anchor book September 16, subscription September 17-21, allotment September 22, listing around September 24.**
- **Lot size 8 shares — ₹14,280 per lot at the top of the band.** Retail gets 35%, QIBs 50%, NIIs the balance.
- **Implied market capitalisation around ₹4.4 lakh crore.** It will be India's second-largest issue behind Hyundai Motor India's ₹27,870 crore, surpassing LIC's ₹21,000 crore.
- **Sellers include SBI (the largest), CPPIB, Aranda Investments, MS Strategic, New India Assurance and Bank of Baroda**; Morgan Stanley cut its offer to 1.10 crore shares in the reduction. Twenty merchant bankers manage it.
- **Grey-market premium fell to about ₹191 from ₹285 a week ago** — the OFS cut and weak secondary sentiment have cooled the grey market.

## SEBI's Demat 2.0 Tokenised Bond Pilot Goes Live — REC, L&T and IIFL Finance Already In

SEBI launched the **"Demat 2.0" pilot for tokenised corporate bonds on September 10**: corporate bonds move onto a **permissioned distributed ledger**, with **settlement through the RBI's wholesale central bank digital rupee** and smart-contract servicing. **REC, Larsen & Toubro and IIFL Finance have already raised ₹1,025 crore under the framework**, and SEBI's stated ambition is to extend the architecture to other asset classes if it works. Investor protections are unchanged.

The strategic read: if Demat 2.0 scales, India is prototyping an integrated securities-settlement-servicing architecture — the same direction as the intra-BRICS payments discussion running in parallel at the summit.

## Brent Above USD 100 Turns Oil-Marketing Margins Deeply Negative

The most concrete earnings-calculation from today's India file: with Brent averaging about **USD 105 for September**, oil-marketing margins are running at roughly **₹(-5) per litre on petrol and ₹(-23) per litre on diesel**. That is the direct arithmetic consequence of the US-Iran war and Hormuz disruption passing into Indian fuel pricing, and the OMCs' discretion to reset prices is constrained because **diesel is politically and CPI-sensitive**.

Brent surged more than 8% this week to **USD 104-107** — its highest close since May 19 — before slipping about 1.5-3% on September 11.
