The President said the words Indian exporters have waited a year to hear, and then immediately qualified them.

Asked at the White House on Tuesday about the prospects of an agreement, **Donald Trump said India and the United States were "pretty close" to finalising a trade deal** and added: **"It's going to be a much different kind of deal than we've done."** The full quote was "They want to make a deal. We're going to make a deal. Pretty close, yeah."

## What "Pretty Close" Is Worth

Less than a signature, and more than the previous nine months of briefings. The negotiation has run more than a year. Commerce Secretary **Rajesh Agarwal said on September 9 that the BTA was "more or less finalised"**, with the last-mile issue being the **preferential tariff edge over Vietnam, Bangladesh and Thailand** — that is, whether Indian textiles and footwear get a better rate than their direct competitors, not merely a decent rate.

Three things sit between the quote and a signed text: the tariff schedule itself, the USTR's **Section 301 excess-capacity probe into India**, which remains open, and the fact that Trump's describe-it-as-different framing suggests a structure unlike the conventional BTA. There is no date, no product list and no rate in what he said.

The Graham Act backdrop matters here. The duties of up to 100% on the top five buyers of Russian crude were **waived by executive order on October 16** under Section 115 — but a waiver is discretion, not repeal, and the 60-day review window runs to roughly **December 15**. Trade policy toward India in this window is a negotiation lever, not a settled regime.

## Rubio Went to Jamnagar and Asked Reliance to Refine More Venezuelan Crude

The Secretary of State arrived in Gujarat on Wednesday to tour **Reliance's Jamnagar complex** — **1.4 million barrels per day** of crude processing, a complexity index of **21.1**, the largest and most complex single-site refinery in the world by the company's own description. Rubio called India a **"refining superpower"** and said he would discuss **refining more Venezuelan crude** with Mukesh Ambani, adding that the question of Reliance producing oil in Venezuela itself had come up before and would come up again.

The mechanics are the point. Jamnagar was built to run **ultra-heavy, high-sulphur crude** that most refineries cannot process economically, and Venezuelan grades have sludge-like consistency. Kpler estimates Reliance will take **350,000 to 370,000 barrels per day** of Venezuelan crude in October, against a sharp revival in those imports since Washington took over Venezuelan oil policy earlier this year.

For Reliance this is a feedstock-advantage story, not a volume story — discounted heavy sour barrels plus the highest complexity index in the industry equals a wider refining margin than a simple refinery can earn on the same crude. For the state refiners the read is narrower: supply diversification away from Hormuz risk, with Brent at **$109.64** and India importing more than 85% of its crude.

## The Rupee's Best Close in Six Weeks Is an Exporter Headwind

The currency did something it has not done in six weeks: it strengthened **0.5% to close at 94.4460 per dollar**, its strongest level since early September, after touching **94.43** intraday against a previous close of **94.91**. The driver was the Jio Platforms IPO's foreign inflows plus continued RBI management of dollar demand.

That is a good headline and a bad line item for anyone paid in dollars. Every 1% of rupee appreciation takes roughly 1% off the rupee value of IT and pharma export revenue, and it arrives in the same week that Indian IT had its best session in a month on global tech cues.

## What to Watch

- **The text, not the quote.** A signed BTA with a published tariff schedule is the tradeable event. "Pretty close" has been the language since September 9.
- **Rubio's remaining stops** on a visit the MEA advisory dates October 20-22. Energy cooperation was the stated theme; watch for anything concrete on Venezuelan offtake volumes.
- **October 26** — final US anti-dumping and countervailing duty orders on crystalline silicon solar cells from India, Indonesia and Laos, with a preliminary Indian margin of **123.04%**. This is the one duty the Russia waiver did not touch, and it hits WAAREEENER and VIKRAMSOLR directly.
- **November 9** — NPPA's cancer-drug price action.
- **December 15** — end of the 60-day Graham Act review. Sections 112 and 113 remain one determination away from returning.
- **December 31** — RoDTEP and RoSCTL export incentives expire. Commerce has asked for five years and ₹23,000 crore for FY27 against a ₹10,000 crore budget estimate.
