## What changed since our July note

We published on Zen on 27 July 2026 at ₹1,697.60 with a CAUTION verdict and the argument that Q1's weakness was an execution-timing issue rather than a demand issue. Three months later the stock is ₹1,466.80, 13.6% lower, and the picture has both clarified and complicated. This is that note updated, not a second article on the same company.

Four things have changed:

1. **A ₹295 crore simulator order from the Ministry of Defence** (October), with a one-year execution window. This is the largest single order since the ₹177.50 crore win in July.
2. **A ₹135.15 crore acquisition of 51.98% of Cogknit Semantics**, an AI battlefield-management firm, approved 17 October, completing by 13 December.
3. **The CMD formally walked back two guidance pillars** in a 12 October interview: the 35% EBIT / 25% PAT margin targets are under pressure, and management has stopped projecting a ₹4,000 crore cumulative order book.
4. **The stock has de-rated**, from 79x to 73x trailing, on a defence sector that is itself correcting 10-17% over the month.

The capability story has strengthened. The financial proof has not yet arrived. And the valuation still assumes it will.

## 1. The number the July note underplayed

Q2 FY27 is due within days — the trading window closed on 1 October and last year's Q2 results came on 25 October — and it is now the single most important data point in the story.

## 2. Capabilities: what Zen can actually do

This is where the company is genuinely differentiated, and it is the reason the stock has ever commanded a 70-80x multiple.

**Army simulation — the core franchise.** Combat training centres, gunnery simulators, tank and crew simulators, driver training, small-arms trainers. Zen is the incumbent supplier to the Indian Army and has been for two decades. The ₹177.50 crore July order was for upgradation and integration of tank and crew gunnery simulators; the ₹295 crore October order is for simulator supply. This is annuity-like: the installed base needs upgrades, and upgrades are higher-margin than new builds.

**Navy simulation — the second leg.** Marine solutions, submarine navigation simulators, deep submergence rescue vehicle training. Fewer competitors, longer cycles, better pricing.

**Air Force — the gap being closed.** R&D on Zen's first Indian Air Force product is complete, and management says it is in advanced negotiations with OEM partners to enter the air simulator segment, expected to be formalised during FY27. If it lands, Zen becomes the only Indian private company with simulation presence across all three services. That is the single largest capability upgrade available to it, because air simulation is the biggest and most technically demanding slice of the market and is currently dominated by imports.

**Counter-drone (C-UAS).** A high-altitude man-portable anti-drone system launched on Kargil Vijay Diwas, plus the Hyperstrike interceptor drone. The DAC has cleared ₹52,000 crore of acquisitions including the AKASH TARANG anti-UAV programme — the largest addressable pipeline in the company's history. Zen's claim is a layered detection-plus-interception stack rather than a single product.

**Unmanned ground vehicles and border systems.** The Zen Vrishab UGV and an integrated smart border suite (AI-powered surveillance). Early-stage revenue, strategically relevant.

**Propulsion.** Vector Technics, the drone-propulsion subsidiary, gives Zen a component position in the drone supply chain rather than only a systems position.

**AI battlefield management — the new leg.** The Cogknit acquisition adds AI-enabled battlefield management and situational-awareness software, which is the layer above simulation: not training the soldier, but informing the commander.

Read as a portfolio, this is the most complete indigenous defence-training and counter-drone stack in India. The strategic logic is not in doubt. The question is conversion.

## 3. Counter-drone: the ₹52,000 crore question

The DAC clearance of ₹52,000 crore including AKASH TARANG is the largest number in any Zen presentation, and it deserves scepticism as much as excitement.

The honest framing: a DAC acceptance-of-necessity is a budget authorisation category, not a contract. Defence procurement converts AoN into orders through RFP, trial, negotiation and contract signature, over periods measured in years. Zen's own management has now said as much publicly, having stopped projecting cumulative order book targets because, in the CMD's words, "astrologers are better than me."

What makes it credible rather than promotional:

- **Orders are actually arriving.** ₹177.50 crore in July, ₹295 crore in October, and over ₹400 crore of the ₹800 crore FY27 simulator target already booked by mid-October.
- **The government's procurement posture is genuinely favourable** — Buy Indian-IDDM preference, emergency procurement windows, and a stated counter-drone priority after recent border incidents.
- **Zen has a manufacturing licence** for arms, which removes a structural barrier that keeps pure-software players out.

What makes it fragile: the same pipeline is open to every defence electronics firm in India, and the conversion timeline is controlled entirely by the customer.

## 4. Exports: the highest-margin leg

The most under-appreciated part of the 12 October interview: **exports are already Zen's highest-margin segment**, ahead of simulators and counter-drone. Management expects exports to reach **30-40% of revenue within three to four years**.

Why that matters more than the percentage: a domestic simulator order is a competitive tender against BHEL-class pricing pressure; an export order to an allied military is a sole-source negotiation with a customer who cannot buy from China. The margin differential is structural.

The supporting evidence is real but early — the company lists presence across Saudi Arabia, Turkey, Australia, UAE, Malaysia, Indonesia and Morocco, and the counter-drone product is the most exportable thing in the portfolio because every military in the world now wants one.

The risk is that exports also carry the longest collection cycles and the most geopolitical risk. A 40% export mix would improve margins and worsen working capital at the same time.

## 5. Cogknit: ₹135 crore for an AI brain

The acquisition terms, from the 17 October filing:

| Term | Detail |
|---|---|
| Stake | 51.98% on a fully diluted basis |
| Consideration | Up to ₹135.15 crore, all cash |
| Implied enterprise value | ~₹260 crore |
| Instrument | Equity purchase plus Series A CCCPS at ₹27,015.71 per share |
| Target | Cogknit Semantics, Bengaluru, incorporated 2009 |
| Business | AI-enabled battlefield management and situational awareness |
| Completion | On or before 13 December 2026 |

The valuation needs to be stated plainly: **Cogknit's turnover was ₹2.24 crore in FY24, ₹0.78 crore in FY25 and ₹0.48 crore in FY26.** Zen is paying roughly **540 times the target's FY26 sales** and about ₹260 crore of enterprise value for a business with under ₹1 crore of revenue.

Two readings, both legitimate:

**The generous one.** This is an IP purchase, not an earnings purchase. Battlefield-management software is exactly the layer India lacks, it is the highest-value-per-line-of-code segment in defence, and a company with ₹1,889 crore of equity and ₹1,000+ crore of unspent QIP proceeds can afford to buy capability rather than build it. Zen has been explicit that its inorganic strategy targets R&D-heavy firms and a three-to-five year horizon.

**The sceptical one.** ₹260 crore is 20% of market cap committed to a company with declining, sub-₹1 crore revenue and no disclosed order book. The consideration includes preference shares, which protects downside somewhat, but the earnings contribution for FY27 and FY28 will be zero to negative. And it lands in the same quarter that margins are compressing — capital going into a loss-making adjacent business while the core is underdelivering.

The test is straightforward: by FY28, does Cogknit have a named army contract? If yes, the price was cheap. If it is still a ₹0.5 crore revenue company with a "technology platform", the capital was misallocated.

The apparent inconsistency — ₹1,239 crore in June plus ₹472 crore of wins is ₹1,711 crore, but the CMD cites ₹1,500 crore — is explained by Q2 execution: orders shipped and recognised reduce the book. The ₹1,500 crore figure is the more current one.

Management's stated ambitions:

- **₹800 crore of simulator orders in FY27**, of which over ₹400 crore was already booked by mid-October.
- **₹2,500 crore total order book by 31 March 2027**, explicitly conditional on Fast Track Procurement moving and the government signing before the fiscal year ends.
- Execution cycle of 12-18 months, so FY27 wins become FY28 revenue.

At ₹1,500 crore of order book against FY26 revenue of ₹688 crore, coverage is a little over two years — healthy, and the strongest single argument that the revenue trough is behind rather than ahead. But note what the CMD did *not* say: the earlier ₹4,000 crore cumulative aspiration was withdrawn.

## 7. Margins: the 35% EBIT era is ending

This is the most consequential change since July, and it was said plainly in the 12 October interview: the historical targets of **25% PAT and 35% EBIT "may face slight downward pressure"** as the portfolio diversifies, because entering commodity-like segments such as weapon systems dilutes margin. Management put the impact two to three years out and expects absolute profits to keep growing.

That is honest, and it is also a structural re-rating input. Zen's multiple was built on the idea that it was a software-like defence business earning 38-42% operating margins. The trend is already there:

| Period | Operating margin |
|---|---|
| FY25 | 39% |
| FY26 | 36% |
| TTM | 33% |
| Q4 FY26 | 28% |
| Q1 FY27 | 27% |

A 1,360 basis point compression from Q1 FY26 to Q1 FY27 (40.9% to 27.3%), with management now signalling that the destination is lower than the old target. If the steady state is 25-28% rather than 35%, then every forward earnings estimate built on the old margin is too high, and the stock's 73x multiple is being paid for a business model that no longer exists.

The counter-argument: absolute profit is what compounds, and a ₹2,000 crore revenue business at 25% margins earns more than a ₹700 crore business at 35%. That is true, and it is the right way to think about it — provided the revenue arrives.

The counterweight: this balance sheet means Zen can fund a ₹2,500 crore order book without debt, and can keep buying capability. The cash is optionality, not waste — but optionality has an expiry, and it is being carried at 7x book.

## 13. Future scope: three scenarios to FY28

The forward case rests on three levers, and it is worth being explicit about what each produces.

**Lever one — the order book converting.** ₹1,500 crore today, an aspiration of ₹2,500 crore by March 2027. At a 12-18 month execution cycle, the current book is FY27 and FY28 revenue.

**Lever two — the margin destination.** Management has guided down from 35% EBIT. The question is whether the steady state is 30% (diversified but still speciality) or 25% (commodity-adjacent).

**Lever three — the new legs.** Air Force simulation, exports at 30-40% of mix, and Cogknit's AI layer. None of these contribute meaningfully in FY27. All three could contribute in FY28-FY30.

Building the P&L explicitly: EBIT from operations, plus ₹50 crore of net other income (treasury income on the surplus, which shrinks as capital is deployed), taxed at 27%.

| Scenario | FY28 revenue | EBIT margin | EBIT | PBT | PAT | EPS | Value at 30-60x |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Bear | ₹800 Cr | 25% | ₹200 Cr | ₹250 Cr | ₹182 Cr | ₹20.2 | ₹606-1,212 |
| Base | ₹1,100 Cr | 30% | ₹330 Cr | ₹380 Cr | ₹277 Cr | ₹30.7 | ₹921-1,842 |
| Bull | ₹1,400 Cr | 32% | ₹448 Cr | ₹498 Cr | ₹364 Cr | ₹40.3 | ₹1,209-2,418 |

Read the base case carefully: **₹30.7 of FY28 EPS against a ₹1,467 price is 48x.** So the market is paying a full but not ridiculous multiple for a scenario that has not yet been demonstrated. At 45x the base case implies ₹1,382, about 6% below today; at 55x it implies ₹1,689, about 15% above.

The bull case — ₹1,400 crore of revenue, 32% margins, 55x — implies ₹2,216, a 51% return. It requires all three levers at once, and it is not the likely outcome.

The point for a buyer today is this: **there is no margin of safety in the base case, and the bear case is a 17% to 59% drawdown depending on the multiple the market retains.** That asymmetry is why the entry should wait for the Q2 print rather than anticipate it.

## 14. Key risks

**Execution dependency.** Revenue is back-ended by design — orders secured from October 2025 onward execute through Q2-Q3 FY27. Two consecutive quarters of disappointment would push the recovery into FY28 and break the base case.

**Margin normalisation.** Already happening: 41% → 27% over five quarters, with management guiding lower. Every estimate built on 35% EBIT is stale.

**Valuation with no cushion.** 73x trailing on a 29%-declining revenue base. The base case justifies the current price, not more.

**Acquisition risk.** ₹135 crore for a company with ₹0.48 crore of FY26 revenue. If Cogknit does not produce a named contract by FY28, that capital is impaired.

**Order conversion.** The ₹52,000 crore DAC pipeline is not a Zen order book. Government procurement timelines are outside the company's control, which management now concedes publicly.

**Working capital.** Defence contracts are lumpy; FY25 saw a ₹419 crore working capital build and negative operating cash flow of ₹146 crore. The cash position absorbs this today, but it is a real drag on returns.

**Concentration.** A handful of MoD orders drive the year. One deferral moves the whole P&L.

## 15. Verdict

**CAPABILITY IS REAL, TIMING IS NOT — WAIT FOR Q2 FY27.**

Two judgements, held apart.

On **capabilities and future scope**, Zen is the strongest indigenous franchise in defence simulation and counter-drone in India. Army and Navy leadership, a completed Air Force product awaiting an OEM partnership, a man-portable anti-drone system inside a ₹52,000 crore authorisation, an export business that is already the highest-margin segment, and now an AI battlefield-management capability bought with surplus capital. If any Indian company is going to be the domestic simulation prime over the next decade, this is the most likely one.

On **the next two quarters**, the numbers do not yet support the story. FY26 revenue fell 29%. Five quarters of flat-to-declining revenue. Margins down 1,360 basis points year-on-year. A formal walk-back of the 35% EBIT target. And Q2 FY27 — the quarter management said would prove the back-end loading — reports within days.

The stock at ₹1,467 prices the base case almost exactly. That makes it neither a bargain nor a bubble; it makes it a **show-me situation with a binary catalyst in the next week.**

**If Q2 FY27 shows revenue above ₹200 crore and operating margin back above 30%,** the base case is validated and the stock can re-rate toward ₹1,600-1,750. That is the entry trigger, not the current price.

**If Q2 disappoints again,** the FY27 recovery story is dead, the base case moves to FY29, and ₹1,223 becomes the target rather than the floor.
