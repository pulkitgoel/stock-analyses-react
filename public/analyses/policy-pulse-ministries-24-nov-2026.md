On 23 November, quietly, SEBI removed the number that retail investors have been using to decide whether to apply for an IPO.

The advisory is titled, in the regulator's own flat style, "Advisory regarding unregulated pre-listing premium often called as Grey Market Premium (GMP) for upcoming Initial Public Offers (IPOs)". It says four things. GMP figures are unofficial, unverified and unregulated. Their publication, display, circulation or dissemination should be discontinued through any mode or platform. SEBI-registered intermediaries and market participants should stop publishing, displaying or broadcasting them on websites, digital platforms, emails, SMS and social media. And investors should not treat them as indicators of listing price or expected return, should not participate in GMP-linked transactions, and should understand that such transactions carry no settlement guarantee, no price discovery, no investor-protection cover and no access to the exchange grievance machinery or the ODR portal.

A second advisory the same week warned about impersonation fraud — groups on WhatsApp and Telegram posing as registered intermediaries, offering "institutional trades" and recovery of portfolio losses through foreign markets, then routing investors to fake applications and accounts belonging to unregistered entities.

Neither advisory names a company, a platform or a transaction. Everything below that maps this to a listed stock is my inference from the business model, and I have marked it as such.

## Why this landed now

The timing is not random. The Mahanadi Coalfields offer for sale — ₹8,981.12 crore of Coal India stock, 100% OFS, priced at ₹129–136, about 8.42 times FY26 earnings on revenue of ₹33,899 crore and profit of ₹10,698 crore — closed on 21 November at 0.58 times.

The decomposition is the story. Qualified institutional buyers bid 1.45 times. Non-institutional investors bid 0.29 times. Retail bid 0.34 times. And the grey market premium, which had been ₹3.65 on 17 November, collapsed to ₹0.45.

That is a large, cheap, government-sold issue with an institutional bid and almost no retail bid. Allotment is on 25 November and listing on 27 November, which means the market gets its verdict on the pricing within the week.

For two months the working assumption in this column has been that a hot primary market is a revenue story for the exchanges and the brokers. The Mahanadi book is the footnote that assumption needed: the pipeline is real, the demand is not uniform, and the retail leg — the part that pays the highest fees and churns the most — is the part that did not show up.

Removing GMP does not fix that. It removes the mirror retail used to look into, which is defensible on its own terms and unhelpful if you were using GMP as a demand proxy. There is now no public, high-frequency read on retail IPO appetite other than the subscription numbers themselves.

## The other number that decides Wednesday

Indian markets are closed today for Guru Nanak Jayanti and reopen on 25 November at 9:15 a.m. When they do, the pricing question is the December 4 MPC.

The setup: the repo rate is 5.50% after the 25 basis point hike on 7 October — the first since February 2023 — with the stance moved to calibrated tightening. October CPI came in at 6.09%, above the 6% upper tolerance band for the first time in the new series, with food inflation at 8.94% and core at 4.6%. WPI ran at 12.3%, roughly double CPI, on a 32.8% rise in fuel and power costs. The MPC meets 2–4 December and announces on the 4th.

The consensus is 25 basis points, with a minority arguing for 50. On Tuesday, State Bank of India's research desk argued publicly for the smaller move, warning that a jumbo hike could trigger capital outflows, currency instability and growth concerns at a moment when global markets are already unstable — and pointing at US household debt and weakening housing demand as external fragilities. That is a lender with the most to lose from a credit event arguing for caution, which is worth weighing as an interest and not only as analysis.

## Twenty-five thousand crore borrowed before the decision

Corporates are not waiting for the answer. Indian companies are set to raise at least ₹25,000 crore — about $2.7 billion at 92.8850 to the dollar — through bond sales this week, ahead of the policy decision, according to Reuters.

The pipeline reported for Wednesday alone is up to ₹236.5 billion of issuance: SIDBI up to ₹80 billion across two series, Power Finance Corp ₹60 billion across two bonds, LIC Housing Finance ₹50 billion of 2031 paper, Torrent Power ₹20 billion across five maturities and Jio Credit ₹15 billion of ten-year money. Monday's primary market did ₹43.90 billion, including a ₹14.27 billion reissue from Bajaj Finance and ₹5 billion from Poonawalla Fincorp.

The economics are simple. AAA corporate spreads have tightened 20–30 basis points across the curve in November as oil fell, foreign investors returned to local debt and equities, and the rupee steadied. Roughly ₹900 billion has already been raised this month through private placements, and calendar-2026 issuance is likely to cross ₹10 trillion for the second consecutive year. Borrowing now locks a spread that a hawkish 4 December would take away.

## Flows and price have disconnected

Foreign investors have bought Indian equities for 15 consecutive sessions, with net purchases above ₹1.3 lakh crore in November alone. India moved to net overweight in the BofA Asia fund manager survey for the first time since January. The rotation is explicitly out of the global AI trade and into Indian large caps, banks in particular, which is why the banking index made successive record highs this month.

And yet Monday was the worst single-day fall since 8 July. The Sensex lost 1,483.72 points (-1.77%) to 82,435.99 and the Nifty 450.95 points (-1.75%) to 25,349.65, having touched 25,992.60 intraday — within eight points of 26,000 — before giving back 643 points. BSE-listed market capitalisation fell ₹5,19,829.82 crore to ₹5,08,98,444.87 crore. Profit booking in banks, financials, metals and energy, after four straight up weeks.

Both facts are true. The flow is real and the price had run. A market 2% below its all-time high on record foreign buying and a December hike pending is a market where the marginal buyer has already acted.
