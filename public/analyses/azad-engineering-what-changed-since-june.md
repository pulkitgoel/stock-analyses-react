## The Core Problem: Price Ran 44%, Earnings Did Not

Our June analysis worried that 98x earnings left no room for error. The market disagreed, and it was right about the price for the wrong reason — it paid more for the same earnings.

Look at where profit actually is:

- **TTM profit before tax is ₹184 Cr, below FY26's full-year ₹187 Cr.** Trailing twelve months are lower than the last completed year.
- **Q1 FY27 consolidated PAT was ₹35.2 Cr, below Q4 FY26's ₹37 Cr.**
- Trailing EPS is ₹21.52 against a ₹2,910.20 price — a **135x trailing P/E** versus an industry average of 33.7x.

Forward estimates do not rescue it. Working from the FY26 base of ₹603 Cr revenue with margins held at FY26/TTM levels:

| FY27E scenario | Revenue | EBITDA | PBT | PAT | EPS | Forward P/E |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Bear: 20% growth, 35% OPM | ₹724 Cr | ₹253 Cr | ₹144 Cr | ₹108 Cr | ₹16.62 | 175.1x |
| Base: 25% growth, 37% OPM | ₹754 Cr | ₹279 Cr | ₹171 Cr | ₹128 Cr | ₹19.68 | 147.8x |
| Bull: 30% growth, 38% OPM | ₹784 Cr | ₹298 Cr | ₹194 Cr | ₹145 Cr | ₹22.33 | **130.3x** |

**Even the bull case does not de-rate the stock.** At 30% revenue growth and a 38% margin — above the guided band — FY27 EPS is ₹22.33, which is 130x the current price.

The reason is that operating leverage is being spent before it reaches shareholders. Depreciation has climbed from ₹53 Cr (FY26) toward ₹80 Cr as four new plants capitalise. Finance cost is at ₹10.1 Cr a quarter and rising 80% year on year. Other income, which contributed ₹46 Cr in FY26, collapsed to ₹4 Cr in Q1 FY27 on forex normalisation. Those three lines absorb almost the entire EBITDA increase.

This is also why the bull target had to be pushed three years out. ICICI Securities raised its target to ₹3,200 on 29 September, valued at **55x FY29E EPS of ₹58.3**. Reverse-engineer that number and it implies FY29 net profit of roughly ₹380 Cr. On ICICI's own 33% revenue CAGR, FY29 revenue is about ₹1,419 Cr — which means an EBITDA margin near **48%**, against management's guided ceiling of 35%. The bull case is not a growth case. It is a margin case that requires 13 percentage points of expansion beyond anything the company has committed to.

## The June Quarter: Growth Held, Profit Stalled

Q1 FY27 (quarter ended 30 June 2026), reported 7 August, earnings call 8 August:

**Standalone** — revenue ₹170.5 Cr, up 26.8% year on year and 8.3% sequentially. EBITDA ₹64.07 Cr, up 32.1%, margin 37.6%. PAT ₹36.35 Cr, up 21.2% YoY, EPS ₹5.63.

**Consolidated** — revenue ₹172.6 Cr, up 25.9%. EBITDA ₹64.4 Cr, margin 37.3%. PAT ₹35.2 Cr, up 19.5%.

Two things sit underneath that revenue beat:

- **Profit before tax actually fell year on year**, to ₹40.1 Cr from ₹42.4 Cr. Other income dropped to ₹4.0 Cr from ₹9.1 Cr, depreciation rose to ₹17.9 Cr from ₹9.6 Cr, and finance cost rose to ₹10.1 Cr from ₹5.6 Cr.
- **Segment mix is unchanged**, with Energy and Oil & Gas at ₹138.4 Cr, or 81.2% of revenue, up 26.7%. Aerospace and Defence contributed ₹28.7 Cr, up 24.7%. Exports were 88% of revenue, up 21.2%, while domestic revenue more than doubled to ₹20.5 Cr.

Management guidance on the call: long-term revenue growth of 25%+ reiterated; EBITDA margin guided at 32-35%; substantive contribution from the four new dedicated plants expected only from H2 FY27, with the "major breakthrough" in Q3-Q4 FY27; working capital around 200 days in H1 FY27 easing to 160-180 days by H2; debtor days of 170-180 targeted down to about 90 through bill discounting; a full natural hedge of export receivables by Q4 FY27.

One detail in that guidance deserves emphasis. Bill discounting converts receivables to cash, so it improves the cash conversion cycle — but the discounting charge sits in finance cost. **Cash improves before profit does.** Anyone expecting the working capital fix to show up as a profit improvement will be disappointed.

## The Working Capital Question: Still Unanswered

Our June analysis named working capital normalisation as the single factor the entire thesis rested on. Three months later, no published statement shows it turning.

- **CARE Ratings** (press release, 22 June 2026) put the FY26 working capital cycle at **344 days, up from 287 days in FY25**. Inventory holding was 262 days against 209, collection period 163 against 157, creditor period 80 against 79.
- On Screener's consolidated data, **inventory days went from 1,085 to 2,067** and the cash conversion cycle from 804 to 1,720 days.
- **FY26 operating cash flow was -₹123 Cr**, against +₹63 Cr in FY25. No quarterly cash flow statement is published in India, and neither the Q1 FY27 filing nor the August investor presentation discloses one, so the FY26 figure remains the latest hard evidence.
- Q1 FY27 finance cost rose about 80% year on year to ₹10.1 Cr, which is the price of funding the cycle.
- ICICI Securities (29 September) models FY27E operating cash flow at +₹106 Cr but free cash flow still at **-₹119 Cr** — it expects the operating turn only in FY27, and it has not been reported yet.
- The roughly **₹100 Cr of accumulated GST credit** was reiterated on the call as realisable in H1 FY28 — unchanged, and not accelerated.
- On the ₹700 Cr QIP, **₹589.42 Cr had been utilised at 30 June 2026** with ₹110.58 Cr unutilised, and the project timeline has been flagged as extended to March 2027.

The honest summary: the turnaround is still a promise, now pushed further out, and the company is financing it with debt that carries a visible P&L cost.

## What Genuinely Improved

Being fair to the company, several things went right, and two of them are strategically significant.

**Two more dedicated facilities for GE Vernova (28 September 2026).** Azad inaugurated two 7,600 sqm lean manufacturing facilities for GE Vernova's Gas Power business at Tunikibollaram, taking GE Vernova to three dedicated plants and making it the company's largest single-customer footprint. One caution: these were built against the **existing** contract book — the USD 112 mn six-year aerofoil agreement and the USD 53.5 mn steam power contract — and the filing discloses no new order value or volume commitment. It is capacity, not a new order.

**India's first indigenous expendable turbojet engine delivered to DRDO and the Ministry of Defence (22 July 2026).** This is a milestone under the earlier GTRE programme. Management has been explicit that the programme is **not** part of the 25% revenue guidance, that volumes remain undisclosed pending DRDO weapon-integration testing, and that the first five to six engines are expected within three to four months. Strategically valuable, financially unquantified.

Also positive:

- **EBITDA margin of 37.6% in Q1 FY27**, at or above the top of the guided 32-35% band.
- **CARE Ratings reaffirmed CARE A / A2+ with a stable outlook** on 22 June 2026, while enhancing limits — long-term bank facilities raised to ₹336.55 Cr from ₹208.32 Cr.
- **Rolls-Royce's first qualification batch was pulled forward to Q2 FY27.**
- **Analyst sentiment reversed.** ICICI Securities upgraded from ADD to BUY and raised its target to ₹3,200 from ₹2,610 on 29 September; Goldman Sachs remains BUY at ₹3,315. Eight of nine covering analysts are positive.
- **The technical picture flipped.** In June the price sat below all short-term moving averages. It now trades above every major average from the 10-EMA to the 200-SMA.

## Updated Verdict: CAUTION — PRICED FOR PERFECTION

**Rating change: Favorable with Caution (June) → Caution.**

Azad Engineering is a genuinely good business executing a hard plan. Revenue grew 26.8% in Q1 FY27 on the guided trajectory, the EBITDA margin is above the top of guidance, the turbojet delivery to DRDO is a strategic milestone, and GE Vernova has just taken a third dedicated facility. The balance sheet is not stretched, promoter holding is stable and unpledged, and CARE reaffirmed its rating with enhanced limits.

None of that is the problem. The problem is what the market now pays for it:

- **135x trailing earnings on a TTM PBT that is lower than the last full year's.**
- **A 130x forward multiple even if FY27 delivers 30% revenue growth and a 38% margin.**
- **Institutions reducing while retail absorbs** — FIIs and DIIs both cut holdings in the June quarter, and aggregate institutional holding fell 2.84 percentage points.
- **Working capital still deteriorating**, with the operating cash flow turn now expected in FY27 rather than already delivered, and the QIP project timeline pushed to March 2027.
- **The rally to the all-time high came on the weakest delivery of the period.**
- **A bull target that requires a 48% EBITDA margin**, 13 points above the guided ceiling.

Where we were wrong, stated plainly: our June entry zone of ₹1,800-1,900 was never revisited. We expected a pullback to support that never came; the stock went the other way and added 44%. Our caution on the 98x multiple was correct about valuation and wrong about near-term price, because the market was willing to pay more for the same earnings. Readers who ignored our entry zone and held were right, and we should say so.

Where we were right: the working capital deterioration we flagged as the swing factor has not reversed. Every subsequent hard data point — CARE's 344-day cycle, Screener's 2,067 inventory days, the ₹123 Cr negative operating cash flow, the 80% jump in finance cost — reinforces it.

**What this means practically.** At ₹2,910 the stock is priced for flawless execution of a plan whose hardest part has not yet started working. The trend is up and the analysts are bullish, so this is not a call to short a strong stock. It is a call not to chase one that has already discounted three years of margin expansion that management has not promised.

**For existing holders:** the trend is intact and the news flow is positive, so a trailing stop below ₹2,716 (the 50-day EMA) protects the gain without fighting the tape.

**For anyone looking to buy:** wait for the working capital evidence, not the price. Either a pullback into the ₹2,636-2,716 support cluster, or Q2 FY27 results showing debtor days genuinely falling toward the guided 90, would improve the risk-reward materially. Buying strength here means paying 135x for earnings that have not yet grown.
