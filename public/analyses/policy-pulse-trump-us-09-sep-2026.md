## Oil Crosses USD 100 as the Tanker War Widens — Day 194

Crude breached the psychologically key USD 100 mark on Wednesday, September 9 — its first visit in roughly six weeks — after the United States and Iran escalated their tanker war in and beyond the Strait of Hormuz:

- **US Central Command said American forces destroyed five Iranian crude-oil tankers on September 7-8**, in reprisal for ballistic-missile attacks on a US Navy warship; at least seven tankers have been struck in the Gulf region over the past two days, according to CNN.
- **Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps is now threatening vessels near Bahrain and Kuwait and warning of attacks on Gulf port infrastructure** — widening the conflict beyond the chokepoint itself.
- **Brent touched ~USD 100 intraday (settling around USD 99.5, a fourth straight gain and highest settle since late June); WTI ~USD 94.6.** Front-month crude trades at a roughly USD 20 premium over late-2027 — record backwardation — and US gasoline prices are at record highs. Kpler estimates Hormuz crude flows averaged only ~4.3 mbpd in August, recovering to ~5 mbpd in early September, versus the multi-million-barrel daily throughput before the war.

President Trump continues to post that oil "will drop precipitously" once the US wins the war, but an Axios analysis (Aug 21) shows oil-market reactions to his Truth Social posts have been shrinking — traders now price the physical supply loss, not the rhetoric.
