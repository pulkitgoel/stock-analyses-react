The Cabinet briefed at 4:14 p.m. on Wednesday. The market closed at 3:30. Thursday was the first session that could price anything at all.

That sequence is the entire story of the ₹11,000 crore construction and infrastructure equipment scheme, and it is worth stating plainly because Wednesday's article in this series read the tape as a verdict when it was only a calendar.

## Thursday was the verdict

Five listed manufacturers, one scheme, and the market picked two of them.

The reason is the product list. The scheme's seven fully-or-largely-imported categories are tunnel boring machines, off-highway trucks, excavators, cranes, loaders, piling rigs and drilling machines. Action Construction Equipment makes cranes, and cranes are the category where the listed Indian universe is deepest and the import substitution is most direct. Sanghvi Movers is crawler cranes and heavy lifting — the same category, a different link in the chain, and it took 5.24%.

BEML makes earthmoving equipment, which is on the list, but it is a defence and rail PSU whose order book is set by its own procurement cycle and by a 100% government shareholder, not by a sales-linked incentive. Elecon is gears, transmission and material handling — a component supplier, which the scheme does cover, but not one of the seven named categories. Neither moved at all.

That is a clean piece of market evidence: an eight-year, ₹11,000 crore scheme was priced by the two names whose products appear by name in the eligibility list, and ignored by the two whose exposure is indirect. The market read the product list, not the payment calendar.

## The calendar has not changed, and it is still the constraint

The scheme runs eight years from FY2026-27. FY27 and FY28 are a gestation period for setting up manufacturing facilities. The incentive window is FY2028-29 to FY2032-33, and FY2033-34 exists to settle the incentive on FY33's incremental sales. Rates start at 13% of incremental sales and grade down to 8% across the five years. There is ₹375 crore of viability gap funding for a dedicated CIE testing facility.

So nothing in the scheme reaches a profit and loss account before the fiscal year beginning April 2028.

An 8.8% move on that calendar is a multiple, not an earnings revision — the market is capitalising a terminal market share, not discounting FY27. Both readings are defensible. What is not defensible is treating an 8.8% move as confirmation that the cash is near.

## The correction, stated plainly

Wednesday's note in this series said the sector's listed manufacturers barely moved and that the scheme's own schedule explained it. The schedule part was right. The tape part was wrong, and the reason is mechanical: the Cabinet briefing by Information and Broadcasting Minister Ashwini Vaishnaw began at 4:14 p.m., forty-four minutes after the close. The flat Wednesday session never had the information.

The first tradable session produced 8.79% in the most directly exposed name and 0.05% in the least. That is a much stronger statement about how this market prices a product-linked incentive than Wednesday's tape was, and it is the one to carry forward.

## A named rupee figure still moved nothing

Bharti Airtel signed a memorandum of understanding with the Assam government to invest ₹1,200 crore across three data centres, expected to be completed by 2028. Bharti Airtel closed at ₹2,102.50, down 0.71%, on a day the Sensex rose 0.46%.

Same lesson in a different key. A named counterparty, a named rupee amount and a named state is still not a policy and still not a capital commitment — a memorandum with a 2028 completion date carries no obligation that a shareholder can discount. Data-centre capex is a real theme; this announcement is not an event inside it.
