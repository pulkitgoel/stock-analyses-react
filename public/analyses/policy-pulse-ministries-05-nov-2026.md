Two regulators published today. Only one of them is about a number that got 95% smaller, and that number took nearly a month to travel from the RBI to the exchanges.

## First: the 95% cut has now reached the stock exchanges

SEBI issued **Circular No. HO/(561)2026-MRD-POD2/I/25177/2026** today, titled *"Revision of limits relating to requirement of underlying exposure for currency derivatives contracts."* It amends Clause 5.3.3 of the Master Circular for Stock Exchanges and Clearing Corporations to give effect to the **RBI's A.P. (DIR Series) Circular No. 25 dated 10 October 2026**.

The operative number:

> Domestic clients and FPIs may take long or short positions in currency derivatives contracts involving INR, without having to establish the existence of underlying exposure, **up to a single limit of USD 5 million equivalent of notional value (outstanding at any point), across all currency pairs involving INR, put together, and combined across all recognised stock exchanges.**

The threshold it replaces was **USD 100 million**. That is a **95% reduction**, and it is not a technicality — it is the difference between a market where a participant can run a directional INR view at institutional size without documentation, and one where they cannot.

The circular also imports RBI's new conditions on **cancellation and rebooking** of foreign exchange derivative contracts, and on documentation for hedging contracted exposures. For FPIs specifically, **short positions across all exchanges and all FCY-INR contracts are capped at the same USD 5 million**, and taking long positions above that requires demonstrated underlying exposure in Indian debt or equity securities, including mutual fund units.

**Why this is a bigger deal than the headline suggests.** The RBI announced the cut on 10 October; the facility took effect from 12 October; but the exchange-traded segment only becomes compliant with today's SEBI amendment. That gap matters because exchange-traded currency derivatives are where the retail and proprietary flow sits. Until today, the exchange rulebook still described a threshold that the central bank had already abandoned.

**The two-sided read, and it is genuinely two-sided:**

- **Lost volume, on the margin.** The unhedged speculative book in currency futures and options shrinks by an order of magnitude. That is negative for exchange-traded currency derivative turnover.
- **Shifted venue, not destroyed demand.** Corporates and FPIs still need to hedge. Business moves toward the **authorised dealer bank channel** — forwards and OTC — where the same RBI circular applies but where relationships and credit lines already exist. This is a transfer from exchange infrastructure to bank balance sheets.

The honest caveat: neither SEBI nor the RBI has published an estimate of how much exchange-traded currency derivative volume was unhedged speculative flow. The direction is unambiguous; the revenue magnitude is not.

## Second: TRAI built the satellite layer, and it is deliberately wholesale

The more substantial document today is TRAI's **Recommendations on the Framework for Satellite Communication Network Provider Authorisation, and Assignment of Spectrum to Satellite Communication Network Providers**, released at 5:00 pm.

This closes a consultation that began with DoT's reference of **3 July 2025**, ran a consultation paper on **8 April 2026**, drew comments from **31 stakeholders** and counter-comments from **12**, and an open house on **2 July 2026**. The recommendations:

- A new **Satellite Communication Network (SCN) Provider Authorisation** under Section 3(1)(b) of the Telecommunications Act, 2023 — **national area, maximum 20 years, renewable for up to another 20**.
- SCN providers may sell **Satellite Communication Network as a Service (SCNaaS)** to eligible service providers. They **may not provide telecommunication services directly to end users.** That single line is the architecture of the whole framework: satellites become infrastructure, telcos stay the customer-facing layer.
- Permitted services: **FSS, MSS, MSS-based direct-to-device, and IMT-based direct-to-device.**
- **Spectrum is assigned administratively, not auctioned** — feeder-link FSS spectrum on an administrative basis, user-link FSS/MSS on demonstration that the service falls in the First Schedule of the Act. Bands: **C, Ku, Ka and Q/V** for FSS; **L and S** for MSS.
- Spectrum assignment runs **up to five years, extendable by up to two**; terms and pricing locked for five years from notification, extendable by two. Any revision after five years applies to **existing** SCN providers too — that is the clause that decides long-run returns.
- Financials: **8% of Adjusted Gross Revenue** as authorisation fee with **no minimum**; **₹37.50 lakh** entry fee; **₹45 lakh** initial guarantee; **₹60 lakh** minimum equity and net worth; **₹10,000** processing fee; minimum spectrum charge **₹3,500 per MHz per year** for government-assigned FSS and MSS spectrum.
- **IMT-based D2D is permitted without waiting for WRC-27**, on a non-interference, non-protection basis under Article 4.4 of the ITU Radio Regulations, across **600, 700, 800, 900, 1800 and 2100 MHz** FDD bands — but only where access-service licensees holding the relevant channel across **all 22 licensed service areas** agree to let an SCN provider use it.
- **No additional spectrum charges** on partnering telecom operators for their IMT spectrum used for satellite D2D, and a recommendation that the government **encourage handset makers to build MSS-band modules** into future India-sold phones.

Read as a package, this is a deliberately concessionary regime: low entry costs, no auction, a five-year price lock, and the incumbent telcos positioned as the mandatory distribution layer. The constraint on IMT-based D2D — all 22 service areas must consent — is what stops a satellite operator from bypassing the terrestrial licensees entirely.

**And the tell is in the last item.** A recommendation asking government to nudge handset makers toward MSS modules is an admission that the demand side does not exist yet in India. The framework is being built ahead of the devices that would use it.

## Third: two quieter SEBI circulars, one with a hard date

**Mutual fund net settlement — effective 1 April 2027.** Circular **HO/24/13/(1)2026-IMD-POD-2/I/25242/2026** permits **net settlement of funds at the level of an individual mutual fund scheme** for outright cash-market transactions, while **securities settlement stays gross**. Netting is permitted only within one scheme and one settlement cycle; **no netting across schemes of the same fund**, no inter-scheme funding, and any security bought *and* sold in the same cycle is excluded from netting entirely. STT and stamp duty remain on delivery basis.

This extends to domestic mutual funds a facility SEBI already granted to **FPIs** by circular dated **24 April 2026**, for implementation by 31 December. The pattern is consistent: the institutional cash market is moving to **net funds, gross securities**, one participant class at a time. The benefit is working-capital — a scheme's temporary funding requirement falls to the netted figure — which is an operational gain for AMCs and custodians, not a revenue event.

**Accredited investors — effective immediately.** Circular **HO/19/34/11(6)2026-AFD-POD1/I/25244/2026** operationalises the AIF Regulations amendment notified on **15 October 2026**. Managers — of an AIF, an AMC of a Specialised Investment Fund, or a portfolio manager — may now **determine and record accredited status themselves**, valid **three years**, recognised across managers of the same corporate group. The Accreditation Agency route survives as an alternative. Thresholds: **₹5 crore** securities-market exposure for an individual, HUF, family trust or sole proprietorship; **₹20 crore** for a body corporate or non-family trust, counting demat holdings, MF folios, unlisted demat securities, overseas securities and NPS.

The clause worth flagging: **a Person Resident Outside India is deemed an accredited investor** outright, with no certificate and no manager-led determination. Combined with SEBI's 4 November circular making NRI/OCI onboarding digital from **3 December**, the sequencing is now clear — foreign private capital gets a paperwork-free path into AIFs and PMS at the same moment it gets a paperwork-free path into a brokerage account.

## The line to take away

The day's real policy content is a **95% cut** and a **wholesale-only satellite layer**. One shrinks a speculative market that was never disclosed in size. The other creates an industry in which the customer-facing telco is protected by design and the satellite operator is a supplier.

Both are unglamorous. Both will still matter in 2028. And both were published on a day when the index rose 2.4% and nobody was reading circulars.
