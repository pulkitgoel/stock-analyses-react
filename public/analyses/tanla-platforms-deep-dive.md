## 2. Business Overview

Tanla runs two businesses that share one enterprise customer base and one balance sheet.

**Enterprise Communications — 91.6% of Q1 FY27 revenue (₹11,233 Mn, +18.4% YoY).** This is high-volume application-to-person messaging: SMS, voice, and OTT channels (WhatsApp, RCS, Truecaller). Gross margin is around 20% because a large slice of every rupee passes through to the telecom operator or to Meta. The business is deeply embedded — Tanla claims presence with almost 80% of the top 250 enterprises in India — and pricing is set annually, so volumes grow and realisations fall.

**Digital Platforms — 8.4% of Q1 FY27 revenue (₹1,031 Mn, +12.2% YoY).** Trubloq (the blockchain-based DLT platform behind TRAI's anti-spam framework), Wisely (spam and fraud detection), Wisely ATP (anti-phishing, sold per subscriber per month to banks), and Wisely.ai (the AI-native communications platform). Gross margin here is 98.5% because Tanla owns the software. This is the business that could justify a SaaS multiple; it is also the business that has grown at single digits for three years.

**Where the revenue actually comes from.** Q1 FY27 was the fifth consecutive quarter of growth and the strongest in two years. Of the ₹1,857 Mn YoY increase, ₹1,195 Mn came from existing customers spending more and ₹662 Mn from customers onboarded in the trailing twelve months. Sequentially, existing customers added ₹458 Mn and new logos added ₹31 Mn. Annualised revenue from customers spending above ₹10 Mn a year reached ₹11,805 Mn, up 16.7% YoY, with the ₹100-500 Mn cohort up 43.9% YoY — the middle of the customer base is scaling fastest.

**The moat.** Indian A2P messaging is regulated through DLT registration, and Tanla operates the largest DLT implementation. Switching costs are real for a bank whose OTP traffic runs through a platform it has certified. Tanla was Meta's "Partner of the Year" for India and a Google Partner of the Year in FY26, which is distribution as much as it is technology: WhatsApp and RCS volumes reach Tanla's customers through those partnerships.

**The vulnerability.** The moat protects the traffic, not the price. In the Q4 FY26 call, Deepak Goyal described SMS pricing pressure from PSU banks and government customers directly, and described WhatsApp promotional traffic — the expensive kind — as something Meta itself wants to reduce. Tanla's market share claim has also drifted: the Screener company page still says 30% of the Indian market, while the July 2026 investor deck says approximately 35%.

**What the quarter actually shows — and it is operational, not treasury-led.** Other income rose ₹2 Cr YoY against a ₹34 Cr rise in PBT, so 94% of the pretax increase came from operations. Revenue grew 17.8%, EBITDA 22.7%, and PAT 20.1%. This is the opposite of the pattern where a quarter looks strong because of other income.

**But the operating leverage is still negative.** The investor update discloses indirect expenses of ₹1,250 Mn, up 29.3% YoY against 17.8% revenue growth, and now 10.2% of revenue against 9.3% a year earlier. EBITDA margin held at 16.4% only because gross margin improved 110 bps to 26.6%. Management is spending on go-to-market faster than revenue is arriving, which is a deliberate choice and also the reason a 17.8% revenue quarter produced 20% profit growth rather than 30%.

**D&A is the quiet drag.** Quarterly depreciation went from ₹23 Cr in FY24 to ₹33 Cr now, up 21.1% YoY, on ₹100-150 Cr of annual capex that management confirmed will continue. Every incremental rupee of EBITDA now carries a bigger depreciation charge than it did two years ago, which is why PBT grew 22.9% while EBITDA grew 22.7% but PAT grew only 20.1% after a 22% effective tax rate against 19.7% a year ago.

**Visibility, stated honestly.** The top cohort of 21 customers generates ₹5,264 Mn annualised — 44% of revenue — and 13 of last year's top 20 are still in the top 20, with the rest in the top 30. Retention at the top is not the problem. The problem is that the funnel is thinning: 71 new customers added in Q1 FY27 generated ₹31 Mn of revenue against 99 customers generating ₹77 Mn a year earlier. New-logo revenue is down 60% YoY while the sales and marketing line is up 29.3%. Growth is coming from extracting more wallet share from customers already won, and that is a finite well.

**Contracted platform revenue.** Wisely ATP is sold per subscriber per month on roughly five-year contracts; three Indian banks are live, the third being Bandhan Bank, which went live in March 2026 and billed one month in Q4 FY26. Wisely.ai's first international telco deployment, Indosat in Indonesia, covers a subscriber base of about 100 million on the same per-subscriber model. Management declined to quantify the per-subscriber rate, so the revenue cannot be modelled from public disclosure — the deck shows only the ₹1,031 Mn quarterly platform total.

## 8. Management Commentary

From the Q4 FY26 earnings call transcript (27 April 2026, filed 1 May 2026):

**On margins — CFO Anubhav Batra:** "We do have seen a decline, but honestly, we have become range bound... at EBITDA level, we are making some conscious investments in GTM... our investments are going to continue the GTM side, but they will bear fruits in the coming quarters."

**On the growth rate — Executive Director Deepak Goyal:** "We are seeing a growth of overall... CPaaS industry for about, you can say, 8% on an average, 8% to 10%. There's organic growth... we expect that our market share would also increase with the kind of investments we have made. And, it's going to be definitely more than 10% a year."

**On acquisitions — Founder and CEO Uday Kumar Reddy:** "We're pretty open, but we are not actively looking for any acquisition... we always believe in build versus buy... all these AI companies are, I mean, the expectations are skyrocketing. So, we need to be very mindful."

**On ValueFirst UAE — Uday Kumar Reddy:** "Effectively, we should be able to close the deal in this quarter. Let me commit that... it's around ₹150 crores to ₹170 crores top line, with around 22% gross margin is going to add to this financial year."

**On pricing — Deepak Goyal, asked whether SMS prices had bottomed:** "It is on the cards. When it will happen, I can't say."

**On regulated messaging risk — Anubhav Batra:** "Honestly, there would be an impact, but it's a single-digit percentage of our overall volumes that we do, which fall under strictly regulated kind of messages."

**Grading that call.** The ValueFirst commitment was met, one quarter late — the board approved the share purchase agreement on 28 July 2026, which is Q2 FY27, not the Q1 FY27 quarter he committed to on 27 April. The margin guidance ("range bound", investments continuing) has held exactly: 16.4%, 16.3%, 16.4% across the last three quarters. The growth guidance ("definitely more than 10% a year") is being beaten, with Q1 FY27 at 17.8%. Management is being conservative and accurate on margins, and slightly optimistic on timelines.

## 16. Bull vs Bear Case

### Bull case

**The discount is the opportunity.** At 14.1x trailing, 12.9x FY27E and 7.7x forward EBITDA, Tanla trades at half the ₹30 industry median P/E while earning a 26.3% ROCE against the peer median's 13.6%. In its own peer set — Oracle Financial Services at 24.3x, CE Info Systems at 32.9x, Capillary at 86.7x — it has the lowest multiple and the highest quarterly sales growth of the group at 17.9%.

**The margin decline has stopped, and revenue is accelerating.** Four quarters of 16%, 16%, 17%, 16% and one at 16.4% is a floor, not a slide. Meanwhile revenue growth accelerated for five straight quarters, from 3.8% to 17.8%. If revenue grows 15% and margin merely holds, EPS reaches ₹44 and grows 15% — and the multiple on that is 12.9x.

**The cash flow is real and the balance sheet is clean.** ₹2,539 Cr of operating cash against ₹2,551 Cr of PAT over five years, ₹477 Cr of free cash flow in FY26, ₹1,197 Cr of cash, no debt. That funds the ₹12 dividend and any platform investment without dilution.

**The platform business is the free option.** Digital Platforms is 8.4% of revenue at 98.5% gross margin, sold per subscriber per month on five-year contracts to three Indian banks and to Indosat's 100 million subscribers. It grows at 12% and barely moves the consolidated number today. If it ever scales, the mix shift would lift blended gross margin without any change in the enterprise business.

### Bear case

**Four years of zero earnings growth is a verdict, not an accident.** Revenue up 38% from FY22 to FY26, EBITDA up 3%, EPS down 1% a year. The market is not mispricing this; it is pricing exactly what happened. A company that converts revenue growth into nothing trading at 14x is not cheap — it is priced on the assumption that the conversion stays at zero.

**The growth that produced the last five quarters came from the existing customer base, and the new-logo engine has stalled.** New customer revenue fell 60% in a year. Wallet-share gains inside the top cohort have a ceiling, and the top cohort's growth decelerated to 5.4% YoY in Q1 while the ₹100-500 Mn cohort grew at 43.9% — meaning the growth is now coming from a narrower, smaller slice.

**Costs are growing faster than revenue by design.** Indirect expenses up 29.3% against 17.8% revenue, GTM headcount investment continuing "in the coming quarters" per the CFO. If the GTM spend does not convert to new-logo revenue, margin goes below 16%, not above it.

**Foreign money has been leaving for two years and the stock is 73% below its 2022 peak.** A 15.13% to 7.60% FII exit is a verdict from the only part of the register that does global relative-value work. Domestic institutions hold 0.73%. Retail holds 44.6%.

**The acquisition is a receivable dressed as growth.** ₹148.52 Cr of enterprise value for a business with negative net worth of ₹136.52 Cr, revenue that halved to ₹181.63 Cr in FY26, and ₹136.52 Cr of the "consideration" being liability assumption, including amounts payable to Tanla's own group entities. It adds top line; it does not obviously add value.
