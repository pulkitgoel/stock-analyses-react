## Two Days From the Graham Act Deadline, and Still No Notice to Congress

The **Lindsey O. Graham Sanctioning Russia and Iran Act**, signed on **September 18, 2026**, sets **October 18** as the date by which the president must make his initial country determinations and impose the required measures. That is Sunday. Two days out, no target list, no rate and no waiver has been published.

- **Section 112** requires additional duties of **up to 500%** on Russian-origin goods, including petroleum products, stacked on existing duties.
- **Section 113** authorises duties of **up to 100%** on goods from countries among the five largest importers of Russian crude or natural gas — but only where those countries **knowingly make new purchases on or after October 18**. Purchases made before that date do not trigger it. India's exposure attaches only to future buying.
- The statute **directs the president to raise duties but specifies no numerical minimum for the initial rate**, and expressly excludes tariff authority over countries outside the listed categories. In other words, October 18 can be satisfied by a determination without a maximum-rate tariff landing.
- The **Section 113(g) notice to Congress**, on a ten-day clock, expired around **October 8** with nothing published.

On **October 16**, House Foreign Affairs chair **Michael McCaul**, **Jim Costa** and colleagues wrote to the administration urging "full and faithful" implementation by the first deadline, asking which countries will be hit, what trade and maritime data was used, and how exceptions for allies reducing their Russian purchases will be applied.

The contradiction at the centre of this is the diesel waiver. **OFAC General License 135 (October 9)** authorises Russian-origin diesel into the United States until **April 2027** — roughly **300,000 tonnes initially, 500,000 tonnes in November and 1 million tonnes thereafter**, about 6 million barrels, or roughly 1.5 days of US demand. Treasury Secretary **Bessent** rejected the claim that this bars the secondary tariffs. Representative **Krishnamoorthi** called it "illegal, ineffective" on October 15; Representative **Levin** cited an estimate near **$4 billion**.

## Trump's Pharma Portfolio Grew to $56.2 Million as Drugmakers Won Medicare Exemptions

The **Joint Economic Committee's minority staff** released a report on **October 16** tracking the president's pharmaceutical holdings against the Medicare price-negotiation carve-out that Republicans' 2025 tax and spending law expanded.

- Trump's reported pharmaceutical stock holdings grew **from a maximum value of $1.7 million in 2024 to as much as $56.2 million at the end of 2025**.
- Of that, **up to $39.1 million sat in companies that benefited from exemptions** to Medicare's drug price negotiation programme — the expanded orphan-drug exclusion that delays or removes eligibility for negotiation.
- The portfolio **bought up to $2.55 million** of those beneficiaries' shares on **three dates before the legislation was signed in July 2025**, including **up to $950,000 on July 1, 2025** — the day the Senate passed the bill. It then bought **up to $1 million of AbbVie** in August 2025, after enactment.
- Through the **first eight months of 2026**, the portfolio continued trading pharma, adding **up to $3.5 million** of sector value.
- The report estimates Medicare beneficiaries on **Merck's Keytruda** and **Bristol Myers Squibb's Opdivo** could pay roughly **$5,000 more a year** than under negotiated pricing. Trump's portfolio holds both.

White House spokesperson **Davis Ingle** said the portfolio is independently managed by third-party institutions and denied any conflict of interest: "Neither President Trump nor any member of his family has any ability to direct, influence, or provide input regarding how the portfolio is invested." **Senator Maggie Hassan**, who leads the committee's minority arm, called the findings "outrageous and yet typical from this president."

This sits on top of the **August disclosure released on October 8**: **517 transactions** worth between **$74.3 million and $273.3 million**, including **up to $25 million of Meta bought on August 21**, **up to $5 million of SpaceX debt on August 18 — two days before the national security memorandum on commercial space transportation** — and same-day purchases of up to $5 million each of AT&T, ConocoPhillips, Abbott, Netflix and Chevron.

## The US-Mexico Tariff Deal Stalls on Autos and Metals

The one tariff file that had been moving is now the one that has stopped. The **Wall Street Journal** reported, and Reuters carried on **October 15**, that progress toward a US-Mexico deal that would cut US levies on **steel, aluminium and automobiles** in exchange for Mexican concessions has stalled.

- The blocker is **Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick**, who opposed significant reductions in the **25% tariff on Mexican automobiles** and the **50% levies on steel and aluminium**, and objected to cuts on finished products made from them.
- Mexico's deputy trade minister **Luis Rosendo Gutierrez** nonetheless told the Journal there was progress in the negotiations, and a Commerce Department spokesperson said the department was in constant dialogue with Mexico.
- The practical read is that the metal and vehicle tariffs are now **policy objectives rather than negotiating chips**, which changes the arithmetic for every North American supply chain that had been waiting for relief.

## Warsh Calls AI the Most Consequential Change of His Lifetime — and the Hike Trade Unwinds

Speaking at the **IMF and World Bank gathering in Bangkok on October 16**, Fed Chair **Kevin Warsh** gave the AI complex an explicit endorsement: "I'd say the advent of this new technology is probably the most consequential change to the U.S. and the global economy in my lifetime. There really is, I think, a huge benefit to the global economy, the U.S. economy, over the horizon."

It is a striking intervention from a chair who has deliberately refused to signal policy, and it lands as the market has repriced the other way on rates.

- **October 27-28 hike odds have collapsed to roughly 13%** — CME FedWatch shows **87.3% hold against 12.7% hike**, and Kalshi prices 88% hold — down from the **~50%** priced after the hot September CPI on October 14.
- The **10-year Treasury yield has eased to about 5.07%**, and US equity funds drew renewed inflows as yields fell.
