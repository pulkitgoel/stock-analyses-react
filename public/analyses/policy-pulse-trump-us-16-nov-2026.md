A president's disclosure became a filing about one semiconductor company, and it lands on the morning the whole AI complex is being sold.

The Washington Post reported on Monday that Donald Trump held between $13 million and $62 million of Nvidia stock at the end of August, based on its review of his financial disclosures. At the end of 2024 the disclosed range was $615,000 to $1.3 million. Reuters, which carried the report, said it could not immediately verify it, and that neither the White House nor Nvidia had responded to requests for comment.

The filing behind that number is not new. The periodic transaction report covering August was signed on 17 September and received by the Office of Government Ethics on 25 September, then posted publicly on 8 October. What is new is the arithmetic done on top of it, on a day when Nvidia opens down 2.10% at $159.43 and the stock has shed roughly $1.9 trillion in market value since its 6 October peak.

## What the filing says, line by line

The August report lists six Nvidia lines: five purchases and one sale.

The sale is dated 21 August, in the $500,001 to $1,000,000 band. The purchases are dated 3, 10, 17 and 26 August plus a small line on 24 August in the $1,001 to $15,000 band, totalling between $766,004 and $1.565 million. The Nvidia close on 21 August was $214.72. It reached $239.24 on 6 October. It closed at $166.19 on 12 November.

So the sale preceded an 11.4% rally and then a 22.6% decline within eleven weeks. That is the sort of sequence that invites a story, and the story is not supported by the document. A transaction report shows a range and a date. It shows no intent, no order instruction and no profit.

The White House's explanation has been consistent since October. Spokesperson Davis Ingle told Reuters that third-party institutions manage the portfolio and largely replicate indexes such as the Schwab 1000, that all investment decisions are made by independent managers, and that there are no conflicts of interest. Presidents are exempt from the main federal conflict-of-interest statute, 18 U.S.C. 208, but must report transactions. Across the term, more than 31,000 securities trades have been disclosed between January 2025 and August 2026.

The honest reading is narrower than either side wants. The position grew from a sub-$1.3 million holding to one worth up to $62 million in twenty months while the administration was making policy that moved the stock. That is a real governance question and it belongs to Congress, which convenes on 3 January under Democratic control. It is not a trade you can copy, because the disclosure trails the transaction by up to 45 days and by the time you read it the position may not exist.

## The policy channel is the part that repriced

Two administration actions sit directly on the revenue line, and both are documented.

In December the President said the United States would allow Nvidia to export its H200 processors — its second-best AI chip — to China and collect a 25% fee on those sales. The Post's reporting adds that Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick held off restricting a company suspected of diverting Nvidia chips to China, and that Jensen Huang travelled on Air Force One on the China trip.

Then, on Sunday, the Wall Street Journal reported that the administration plans to designate Nvidia and more than two dozen other artificial-intelligence companies as "national champions". No executive order or list has been published. A designation with no instrument attached is a signal about intent, not a cost or a subsidy, and the market treated it as neither.

Against that, the leadership question is unresolved. Huang resigned as chief executive last week amid reporting on his relationship with Chinese businesswoman Alice Huang, whom US authorities suspect of intelligence ties to Beijing. The Nvidia board has said it has "no reason to believe that either national security or NVIDIA secrets were compromised". On Monday, Silicon Valley figures began publicly backing a call for his return, and the Financial Times reported he is considering it. Earnings are on 17 November — the first print without its founder as chief executive — and options are pricing an 8% move.

## The other half of the week is in Shenzhen

The President is in Asia from 16 to 19 November: a US-ASEAN summit in Manila on Tuesday, then the APEC leaders' meeting in Shenzhen, with a Trump-Xi meeting on 19 November that reporting suggests may include Vladimir Putin. None of that is confirmed by the White House in a document, and the truce expires on 10 January 2027 regardless.

The reason the Shenzhen meeting matters more than the tariff rhetoric is that America's negotiating position on minerals has not improved. Reuters reported on 15 November that China's capacity for neodymium-iron-boron magnets is roughly 210,000 metric tons a year against about 4,500 tons in the United States. The projects meant to close that gap are dated in the next decade: Thacker Pass targets mechanical completion late in 2027, Perpetua's EXIM loan is expected to close in the first quarter of 2027, a $7.4 billion Korea Zinc-backed refinery in Tennessee is not expected to open until 2030, and Trilogy Metals — in which the administration took an equity stake — plans to produce copper in Alaska from 2031.

There is one genuine green shoot. Vulcan Elements has begun making rare-earth magnets in North Carolina. Its customers have not yet approved them for use, which is the difference between a factory and a supply chain. "Right now there are more PowerPoint decks than permits," Samir Kapadia of the Vogel Group told Reuters.

Beijing, for its part, extended the suspension of its October 2025 extraterritorial rare-earth export controls until January. That is a renewal, not a repeal, and it runs out one day before the wider truce does.
