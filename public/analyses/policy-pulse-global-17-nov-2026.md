The largest technology listing ever attempted prices on Wednesday evening, and the market that has spent a month selling artificial intelligence is being asked to buy it at $1.4 trillion.

Anthropic is offering 825 million Class A shares at $70 to $78, of which 801.35 million are primary and 23.65 million come from selling stockholders. At the top of the range that is about $64.4 billion of gross proceeds. Seven companies — Nvidia, Alphabet, Microsoft, AMD, Broadcom, Micron and TSMC — have separately agreed to buy another $26 billion of stock at the IPO price, taking the total to $90.4 billion, above the record $75 billion SpaceX raised in June. Fully diluted, the top of the range values Anthropic at $1.405 trillion.

Orders are reported at roughly six to seven times the shares on offer. Up to 20% of the deal is earmarked for retail, against a 5-10% norm, through Fidelity, Charles Schwab, SoFi, E*Trade and Robinhood. Goldman Sachs and Morgan Stanley lead.

## The terms carry the argument

The structure tells you what the sellers think. A single Class F share held by the seven co-founders carries 50.1% of the vote, so public shareholders get one vote each and no practical say. Each founder holds 237-241 million shares, worth roughly $18 billion each near the top of the range. The lock-up is 180 days for most holders and 365 for founders and anyone above 0.5% of the diluted share count, with up to 10% of non-founder locked stock sellable from day one and about 3.6 billion shares becoming eligible before the 180 days expire.

The financials explain why the range was cut. Anthropic reported $17 billion of third-quarter revenue and $895.4 billion of purchase commitments in the revised S-1, against roughly $80 billion of first-half losses. The ambition was $2 trillion as recently as September. The Nasdaq has fallen about 5% in the past month, and it fell another 2.49% on Monday to 24,509.76 — a third straight losing session — before trading at 24,343.78 at 10:30 ET on Tuesday, down 0.68%.

That is the actual test being run this week, and it is being run twice. Nvidia reports after tonight's close against $108 billion of guidance and a $109.1 billion consensus with an 8% implied move. Anthropic prices the following evening. Neither is a referendum on whether AI works. Both are referendums on whether the equity market will keep funding it at current multiples while the operating losses are still $80 billion a half.

The demand is real and it is also not a valuation. Six times covered on a $64 billion book means the allocators want the exposure; it says nothing about what the stock does on 19 November. The 2026 cohort is the warning: the ten largest US IPOs of the year are trading about 20% below their offer prices on average, according to a Financial Times analysis. A hot book has been a poor predictor of a good first quarter all year.

## The other half of the trade is in Mumbai

While the AI complex de-rated, India became the destination. Foreign portfolio investors have bought ₹84,166 crore — about $9.5 billion — of Indian equities in November, the largest monthly inflow in six years, after pulling ₹70,704 crore in September and October combined. The rupee closed at 93.48 to the dollar on Tuesday, its strongest in seven months. The Nifty is up about 11% this month, its best in six years, and the Nifty Bank printed an all-time intraday high of 62,379.15.

The shape of Tuesday's session is the important part. The Sensex rose 427.40 points or 0.52% to 82,532.19 and the Nifty 96.30 points or 0.38% to 25,387.40 while the Nasdaq was falling, with Realty and PSU Bank up nearly 2% each and Nifty IT down 0.9%. HCL Technologies fell 2.09% and Infosys 1.32%.

That is the reverse of the July pattern. In July, when Korean and Taiwanese chip stocks crashed, capital rotated into Indian IT services as the "reverse AI trade." On Tuesday it rotated into Indian domestic cyclicals and out of Indian IT, because the buyers were foreign and what they came for was a market with no AI capex exposure at all. The Nifty IT index is the one part of the Indian market that still trades on American technology sentiment, and it is being sold by exactly the flow that is buying everything else.

Two numbers keep this honest. First, 2026 is still a net outflow year for foreign money in India — roughly ₹2.1 lakh crore sold between January and mid-November, of which November has offset a little under 30%. Second, the domestic institutions are on the other side: they sold ₹3,519.05 crore on 16 November while foreigners bought ₹4,864.68 crore. A rotation that requires DIIs to keep selling into FPI buying is a rotation with a natural end.
