The Defence Minister set two targets today: ₹3 lakh crore of defence production and ₹50,000 crore of defence exports by 2029. One of them is a stretch. The other is close to a formality. The market will probably treat them as equally ambitious, and that is the error to avoid.

## First: the two targets are nowhere near equally hard

**Rajnath Singh set the targets twice today** — once chairing the **Consultative Committee of the Ministry of Defence on "Modernisation of Indian Defence Forces" in Jaisalmer**, and again at the **third edition of the Delhi Defence Dialogue at MP-IDSA** in Delhi, a two-day event running 3-4 November.

The base numbers, from PIB:

- **FY27 defence budget: ₹7.85 lakh crore**, a record, including a **capital outlay of ₹2.19 lakh crore** for modernisation and capital procurement. That capital share is **27.9%** of the total.
- **Defence production: ₹46,429 crore in FY 2014-15 → ₹1.78 lakh crore in FY 2025-26.** A **3.83x** increase, or **13.0% CAGR over eleven years**.
- **Defence exports: under ₹2,000 crore in 2014 → ₹38,424 crore.** A **19.2x** increase. Indian defence products now reach **more than 80 countries**.

Now run the targets against that base. From FY26 to FY29 is three years.

| Target | FY26 base | FY29 target | Required CAGR | Delivered CAGR (FY15-FY26) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Defence production | ₹1.78 lakh crore | ₹3.00 lakh crore | **19.0%** | 13.0% |
| Defence exports | ₹38,424 crore | ₹50,000 crore | **9.2%** | ~28% |

**Production needs 19% a year against a demonstrated 13%. Exports need 9.2% against a demonstrated pace far above it.** The export target is the easier commitment in the entire defence narrative, and it is the one that gets quoted.

There is a second implication buried in the same arithmetic, and it is the more interesting one. **Exports are 21.6% of production in FY26. On the 2029 targets they would be 16.7%.** The government's own numbers describe a **domestic-demand-led** defence build-out, not an export-led one — exports grow in absolute terms while shrinking as a share of output. Any thesis that treats Indian defence as an export story is running against the plan it is citing.

## Second: the five doors being opened to private industry

The specific part of the speech that is actionable is short and worth quoting exactly. Rajnath Singh named the programmes where private participation is being invited:

> "Opportunities for private participation in programmes such as the **Advanced Medium Combat Aircraft**, **propulsion systems**, **ATAGS**, **artillery ammunition** and **shipbuilding** will accelerate indigenous development, expand manufacturing capacity and strengthen India's position in the global defence market."

That is a list of five, and each has a different competitive structure. AMCA is a development programme with a single prime and a long tail of subsystem suppliers. Propulsion is the hardest — India has no flight-rated indigenous fighter engine, which is why it is named. ATAGS is already a produced system with two contenders. Artillery ammunition is a volume business with low barriers. Shipbuilding is the most mature.

The Delhi Defence Dialogue framing sharpened the intent. The Minister argued that self-reliance "is not a retreat from the world" but a repositioning for **co-development, co-production and shared innovation**, with "appropriate sharing of intellectual property" as the condition. That language is directed at foreign OEMs, and it is a materially higher bar than the licensed-manufacture model of the last two decades.

He also noted the structural reform backdrop: **Defence Industrial Corridors**, and the **corporatisation of the Ordnance Factory Board into seven DPSUs**. And a point about asymmetric warfare worth recording — that "relatively inexpensive drones and autonomous systems are challenging sophisticated and costly military platforms," and that the ability to "innovate rapidly, manufacture at scale and replenish capabilities" now matters as much as possessing advanced systems. Munitions and attritable systems, not platforms, is the read.

## Regulators: SEBI moved twice, and neither moves a stock

Two SEBI items today, both structural and both small:

- **The cooling-off period for offers for sale by privately listed, illiquid InvITs has been cut from 12 weeks to 8 weeks.** The stated rationale is ease of doing business — illiquid InvITs trade in large lots with thin volumes, and a 12-week lockout made exits impractical.
- **REITs and InvITs can now issue foreign-currency denominated depository receipts** on international exchanges at GIFT City, subject to unitholder approval by majority.

Both are real improvements in capital access for the trust structure and neither has a clean listed-equity read. **The direct beneficiaries are the trusts themselves** — India Grid Trust, Powergrid InvIT and IRB InvIT on the InvIT side; Embassy, Mindspace and Brookfield India on the REIT side. Those units are not in the NSE EQ master file that this tracker validates tickers against, so they are named here in text and deliberately kept out of the ticker field rather than risk a dead company page.

**Also worth noting as background, not as today's news:** SEBI passed a final order on **28 October** censuring **Axis Capital**, a wholly owned Axis Bank subsidiary, in a matter dating to September 2024 — a guarantee to NCDs of Sojo Infotel treated as unauthorised credit enhancement. SEBI recorded no investor harm and no fraudulent default, and Axis Capital's FY24 PAT was about 0.70% of Axis Bank's consolidated PAT. Immaterial, and four days old. I am flagging it only because it will surface in searches as current.

## What to watch

- **AMCA** — the prototype bid deadline passed on 30 October. The development contract award is the first hard evidence on the 19% production CAGR, and it has not happened yet.
- **Any ATAGS or artillery-ammunition RFP** — these are the two named programmes closest to a signed order.
- **Q2 season residue** — SBI, Bajaj Finserv, Lupin, MCX and Emcure reported today. Earnings, not policy, is what moved individual names this session and will continue to.
- **The December GST Council and the Feb 2027 Budget** — the defence capital outlay is a Budget line, so the 19% target gets its first real test in February.

No buy or sell recommendations here. Impact direction and confidence only.
