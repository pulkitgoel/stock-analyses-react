## The RBI Flips From Absorption to Infusion

For ten weeks the Reserve Bank of India has been draining the banking system. On Monday it reversed.

The central bank conducted a **seven-day Variable Rate Repo auction for ₹1 lakh crore** — its first liquidity **infusion** operation since **July 29**. It received bids of **₹1,50,735 crore** against the notified amount and accepted **₹1,00,014 crore at a weighted average rate of 5.52%**. It will run another VRR for **₹50,000 crore on Wednesday, October 21**.

The trigger was the overnight market, not the policy stance. The **weighted average call rate was 5.67% on Monday, 17 basis points above the 5.50% repo rate** — and it had touched the **Marginal Standing Facility** on Friday, the first time this year. When the operating target (call rate at repo) is breached on the upside for two consecutive sessions, the liquidity desk acts; the Monetary Policy Committee is not involved.

What changed underneath:

- The RBI had been in **absorption mode since August 6**, running Variable Rate Reverse Repo auctions to mop up the liquidity created by banks mobilising **FCNR(B) deposits** under the limited-period swap window that ran **June 8 to August 31**.
- Average daily net absorption under the LAF rose from **₹1.2 lakh crore in July to ₹5.8 lakh crore in August and ₹7.8 lakh crore in September** — an extraordinary amount of money pulled out of the system in two months.
- A tightening of the **daily cash reserve requirement took effect on October 16**, adding to the squeeze just as monthly tax outflows began.
- Surplus liquidity has now fallen to **₹1,85,873 crore as on October 18**, from about **₹1.99 lakh crore on October 16**.

The RBI's own framing is that this is not a change of direction but a completion of one: as the surplus comes down toward a small deficit, it helps transmit the **recent 25 basis point repo hike** into deposit and lending rates. That is the right reading — the central bank is trying to keep the call rate anchored at 5.50% while the festive-season currency demand and tax outflows do their work, not to ease.

For equities the distinction is everything. A liquidity infusion that merely restores the operating target is neutral for valuations. A liquidity infusion that signals the absorption cycle is over would be a genuine turn for rate-sensitives. Wednesday's ₹50,000 crore operation, and the call rate after it, will tell you which one this is.

## Sitharaman Says the Problem Is Too Much Liquidity

In a CNBC interview published on Sunday, Finance Minister **Nirmala Sitharaman** argued that excess liquidity has itself been a cause of Indian equity underperformance: **"Because of the ease of liquidity, ease of availability, people are even exiting easily"** from equities, she said, while insisting the fundamentals of the Indian economy are fine.

It is an unusual diagnosis from a finance minister three weeks before a US election whose outcome drives foreign flows, and it sits oddly against her own central bank's problem — a system where the call rate has been pinned at the MSF because liquidity was too *tight* at the margin. The politically useful part of the statement is the second clause: the fundamentals are fine.

What the market has actually been discounting is narrower. October foreign portfolio outflows had already crossed **₹52,100 crore by October 16**, more than all of September's ₹35,861 crore, on a **5.13% US 10-year yield**, Brent above **$100** and a five-year annualised Nifty 100 dollar return of roughly **1.2%**. That is a relative-return problem, and no amount of domestic liquidity fixes it.

## The Export Support Cliff Nobody Has Priced

Two schemes that quietly underwrite a large share of India's labour-intensive exports expire on **December 31**, and the two ministries that own them disagree about whether they should survive.

- **RoDTEP** (Remission of Duties and Taxes on Exported Products) refunds embedded central, state and local duties not rebated elsewhere, and **RoSCTL** does the same specifically for apparel, garments and made-ups.
- Their latest extension runs only **three months**, after a six-month extension granted in April — a shortening pattern exporters read as an exit sign.
- The **Commerce Department** has asked for a **five-year extension** and an increase in the RoDTEP allocation to **₹23,000 crore for 2026-27** against a Budget Estimate of **₹10,000 crore**, citing export growth.
- The **Finance Ministry** is not convinced the schemes work as intended and has not granted the long extension.

The macro numbers argue Commerce's case: merchandise exports rose **18.51% year on year to USD 259.89 billion in April-September 2026-27**, from USD 219.30 billion a year earlier. The fiscal numbers argue Finance's: a ₹13,000 crore top-up on a scheme whose effectiveness is contested, in a year when wholesale inflation is running at a series-high **11.4%**.

For listed exporters this is a cash-flow risk, not a demand risk — the remission is booked into realisations, and a lapse would be absorbed in margins rather than volumes.

## US Holiday Orders Are Down 25-30% for Garment Exporters

The demand side of the same sector got worse this week. Indian ready-made garment exporters report a **25-30% decline in US orders for the Christmas and New Year season**, with American buyers holding back or renegotiating on inflation and logistics uncertainty.

The divergence inside the data is the story:

- **September 2026 RMG exports were USD 1.1 billion, up 13.7% year on year** — in rupee terms ₹10,824.6 crore, up 22.9%.
- **April-September 2026-27 cumulatively fell 6.2% to USD 7.2 billion** from USD 7.7 billion, per the Apparel Export Promotion Council.
- In rupee terms the first half rose **3.4% to ₹69,300 crore** — a pure currency effect flattering the reported numbers.

The relief is coming from Europe, where UK and EU FTAs have improved order traction, and it comes with its own cost: freight rates have more than **doubled** on Cape of Good Hope routing, with Suez transits only now gradually recovering.

## Jio's Anchor Book Went 30 Times, and Subscriptions Open Tomorrow

The market-structure item of the week is not a policy at all. **Jio Platforms' ₹8,110 crore anchor tranche was subscribed more than 30 times**, with total anchor bids above **USD 25 billion** from BlackRock, GIC, Norges, SBI Mutual Fund and LIC.

The offer is **₹30,213 crore of fresh shares — 27 crore shares at ₹1,065-1,119** — a **100% fresh issue** with proceeds going to prepay debt, valuing the company at roughly **₹10.3 lakh crore**. **Subscription runs October 21-23**, with listing expected around **October 28**. It is the largest IPO in Indian history.

Two mechanical effects matter for listed names. An issue of that size drains secondary-market liquidity during the subscription window — the reason the RBI's ₹50,000 crore VRR on Wednesday lands at a convenient moment. And the fee pool and subsequent trading volumes accrue to the exchange, depository and brokerage complex regardless of what the stock does after listing.
