# Policy Pulse — Global — 11 Aug 2026

## 🌍 Hormuz Honeymoon Is Over: Crude Rallies Toward $90 as U.S.-Iran Deal Hopes Fade — Markets Camp at Highs Ahead of Aug 12 US CPI

The **dominant global thread** this week is the **crude-oil reversal**. After Brent slid ~7% last week on hints of an imminent U.S.-Iran deal to reopen the Strait of Hormuz, the "honeymoon" has soured: **no agreement materialized**, Tehran is demanding conditions, and President Trump told Axios he is "**low-keying it**" — shifting from fresh military strikes to **mounting economic pressure**. **Brent crude rebounded toward $88–90 (a one-plus-week high)**, up from ~$83 at end of last week. Analysts warn the market, which has priced a benign "fudge" deal, is **time-sensitive**: if the deadlock rumbles through this week into next, traders will re-rate the odds of a **prolonged closure** — with Capital Economics flagging a potential **$120–140/bbl "tipping point" around the start of Q4** if OECD inventories deplete.

**Why the rebound could have legs:** Chinese crude imports are recovering (China "singlehandedly balanced the market in May" with import cutbacks, now reversing), and **Houthi strikes on Saudi infrastructure** are a fresh supply-side risk. The Strait remains effectively closed, and the market is pricing a deal that isn't signed — a fragile setup.
