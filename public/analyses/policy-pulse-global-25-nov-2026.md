Three sessions, two directions, and almost nothing to show for it. Korea's KOSPI closed at 4,697.51, down 308.52 points or 6.16% — which puts it 0.2% below Monday's 4,708.54 close and 8.6% below where it started last week. Monday's 7.5% rally is fully repaid.

Taiwan's TAIEX fell 1,926.92 points (-4.80%) to 38,195.43, having risen 9.1% on Monday. Japan's Nikkei 225 lost 1,633.01 points (-2.78%) to 57,011.62 after gaining about 5%. The MSCI Asia-Pacific ex-Japan index fell 2.3%. Hang Seng slipped 253.64 points (-1.14%) to 21,966.01, Shanghai 71.90 points (-2.03%) to 3,476.47, and Europe's Stoxx 600 was down 0.61% at 628.89.

This is the fourth time in eight sessions that the same complex has swung more than 5% in a day in one direction or the other. A market that does that is not repricing to information. It is repricing to positioning.

## The AI trade has a funding problem, not a leadership problem

Nvidia is down 7.7% at $143.35, back below Friday's close — the 31.5% gain it booked on Monday when Jensen Huang was reinstated as chief executive is entirely gone, then some. Micron is off 10.6%, AMD 9.1%, TSMC's ADR 6.8%, Oracle 8.5%, Broadcom 6.0%. In Tokyo, SoftBank — the largest single holder of OpenAI among listed companies — fell 15%.

Anthropic closed at $79.58, below its $83 offer price on the fifth trading day and 28.6% under the $111.50 first trade. It touched a $2 trillion valuation on debut and now sits near $1.5 trillion, roughly 40 times trailing sales. Cathie Wood's Ark bought another 751,710 shares on the way down, about $61.6 million.

The constraint being priced is the one BofA put a number on earlier this month: hyperscaler capital expenditure reaching roughly 120% of operating cash flow by mid-2027. A capex programme funded out of operating cash flow is a margin decision. A programme funded beyond it is a credit decision — and October's core PCE, at 3.3% against 3.2% expected, with a December Fed hike roughly 96% priced, raises the cost of the credit.

That is why the same 14 economies being hit with tariffs did not move the tape and the bond market did. The US 10-year fell eight basis points to 4.822% while the S&P 500 fell 2.6%. Risk assets were sold and duration was bought. Tariffs are a goods story; this was a discount-rate story.

## The tariff wave, and who it actually reaches

USTR published determinations against 14 economies today: proposed rates of up to 40% on China, 35% on Vietnam, 25% on Cambodia, Indonesia, Korea, Malaysia, Taiwan, Thailand and India, and 20% on Bangladesh, the EU, Japan, Switzerland and Singapore.

The caps do most of the work. For Korea, Taiwan, the EU, Japan and Switzerland the action is capped at 15% net of the MFN duty — the framework deals are honoured. Semiconductors, pharmaceuticals, energy and food that cannot be grown in the US are excluded, and the duties do not stack on Section 232 or on the 10%-12.5% forced-labour tariffs. Comments close 5 January 2027, hearings open 25 January, and implementation is "probably January at the earliest," per officials.

Which is why Seoul fell 6.16% on the same day it was handed a 25% band that is capped at 15%: the tariff was not the reason. The chip cycle was.

## India's read, and the distinction that matters

India's proposed rate is up to 25% on all goods, net of existing Section 301 duties, with exemptions — and 17% if India signs an Agreement on Reciprocal Trade that addresses the findings. USTR's India report names five unreasonable practices: industrial plans to expand manufacturing, inadequate labour protections, financial repression, sustained market access barriers, and a failure to address global excess capacity including through imports and FDI from non-market economies. It cites Make in India, the PLI schemes, Atmanirbhar Bharat and the India Brand Equity Foundation by name, and says the harm is especially acute in pharmaceuticals, medical devices, steel, solar and ICT.

Note the tension inside that: pharmaceuticals appear in the India finding as an affected sector and also in the exclusions list as a carve-out. A sector can be named in a determination and left outside the duty, and that gap is where the January hearing will be fought.

The structural point is worth keeping from yesterday's edition. A Section 301 duty is a goods duty. It cannot reach IT services, so TCS and Infosys sit outside it whatever rate India is assigned — their exposure is US client capex and the rupee, and the rupee closed at 92.23 today, an eight-month high. Goods exposure sits in steel, solar modules, electronics-for-export, auto components, chemicals, textiles and marine.

And the sequencing: USTR published at 6:41 p.m. IST, three hours after the Indian close. India's 1.85% rally today — led by FMCG at 3.79%, pharma at 2.38% and banks at 1.79%, with metals down 2.04% — did not price this. Tomorrow's open does.

## The read

The honest summary of today is that two large policy events — a 14-economy tariff determination and a hot core PCE — produced one market move, and the market move was the second one. The duties are capped for the allies, carved out for chips and drugs, and not collectable until January at the earliest. The inflation print is not capped, not excluded and not deferred, and it lands nine days before a Fed meeting that is almost fully priced for a hike.

For India the question is simpler and arrives at 9:15 tomorrow: does a market that just paid up for FMCG, pharma and banks at an eight-month-high rupee want to discount a 25% goods-tariff band, or does it treat the 17% reciprocal-trade clause as the number that will actually apply? The 5 January comment deadline and the December G20 in Miami are where that gets answered.
