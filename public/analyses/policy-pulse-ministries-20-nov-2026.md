Two numbers from the same week describe India's capital-market problem better than any policy paper.

Equity derivatives turn over 227 times the cash market in India. In the United States the ratio is four. In Germany it is 29.

## Fifty per cent off the tax on buying, more on the tax on betting

Bloomberg reported on Wednesday that Indian revenue officials are reviewing proposals to halve the securities transaction tax on the purchase and sale of shares while raising taxes on equity derivatives, with a shortlist to go to Finance Minister Nirmala Sitharaman before the February Budget. One of the proposals under review is a 50% cut in cash-market STT, alongside a reduction in long-term capital gains tax on securities.

The problem being solved is documented. Nearly 88% of individual equity-derivatives traders lost money in the year through March 2026, according to SEBI, with combined net losses of about ₹91,700 crore — roughly $10 billion — of which options accounted for 92%.

The current rates: STT is 0.10% each side on delivery, 0.025% on intraday sales, 0.05% on futures and 0.15% on options premium. Long-term gains on listed equity above ₹1.25 lakh are taxed at 12.5%, short-term at 20%. The February 2026 Budget already raised futures STT from 0.02% to 0.05% and options premium from 0.10% to 0.15%, and exercised options from 0.125% to 0.15%. CBDT chairman Ravi Agrawal said on Monday that the government is examining stakeholder proposals on both LTCG and STT, with the decision due in the Union Budget for FY28.

⚠️ This is a proposal inside a pre-Budget process, reported by Bloomberg from officials who were not named, and the Finance Ministry did not respond to a request for comment. Nothing is enacted. The February Budget is the decision point, and the most uncertain part is the direction on derivatives: raising that levy is revenue-positive and volume-negative at the same time, which is precisely why it keeps getting studied and not done.

## What a halved delivery STT actually moves

Halving delivery STT halves the friction on every purchase and sale of shares. That is the cheapest possible intervention to revive cash-market participation, and it does nothing for any company's earnings.

For the exchange and the brokers the exposure runs two ways, and this is the part the headline skips. BSE earns more from the derivatives franchise than from cash, so a derivatives levy that cuts option volumes costs it more than a halved cash STT gives back. ANGELONE and MOTILALOFS have the same shape of revenue. An investor reading "50% STT cut" as unambiguously good for exchange stocks is reading half the document.

The depositories and registrars sit further downstream. CDSL, KFINTECH and CAMS earn on holdings and on flows rather than on turnover, so a cash-volume recovery is second-order for them; the LTCG line matters more, because a lower long-term rate is the sort of change that shifts asset allocation rather than a single session's volumes.

One timing detail worth flagging: Indian equities are heading for their first annual decline in eleven years, and the November rebound is what has pulled them back. A tax cut is easier to promise into that than to deliver into a rising market.

## SEBI is already rewriting the primary-market rulebook

Separately, and with more certainty attached, SEBI has constituted a sub-group of its Primary Market Advisory Committee to review the ICDR Regulations, 2018, announced on Monday. It is chaired by NSE managing director and chief executive Ashish Chauhan.

The scope covers Chapters II, IIA, IV, V and VI — eligibility conditions for IPOs, follow-on public offers, preferential issues and qualified institutional placements; promoter and pre-issue capital lock-in; pricing and price discovery including price bands and floor prices; application, allocation and allotment procedures; lead-manager due diligence; and the consolidation of the related circulars, advisories and general orders.

The membership tells you the intent. BSE's executive director Gopalan S Raghavan sits on it alongside the presidents of ICAI and ICSI, the co-founder of Stakeholder Empowerment Services, Kotak Investment Banking's vice-chairman Ramesh Srinivasan and ICICI Securities' executive director Ajay Saraf. This is the exchanges and the sell side reviewing the sell side's paperwork, under an NSE chief.

The read-through is straightforward and slow: easier eligibility and lighter lock-ins mean more issues, shorter timelines and more listing fees. The merchant-banking revenue is real but no longer separately tradeable, because ICICI Securities merged into ICICI Bank and delisted — that exposure now sits inside a bank's other-income line rather than in a ticker.

## Tokyo signed a list of sectors, not a list of projects

The fourth India-Japan 2+2 Foreign and Defence Ministerial Meeting was held in Tokyo on 19 November: Rajnath Singh and S. Jaishankar for India, Toshimitsu Motegi and Shinjiro Koizumi for Japan.

The joint statement is worth reading for what it does not contain. It names five sectors for "concrete, project-based cooperation" — semiconductors, critical minerals, information and communication technology, clean energy and pharmaceuticals — building on the July 2026 economic-security declaration. It warns against "economic coercion, including through arbitrary export restrictions" and "non-market policies and practices that result in overcapacity," without naming China. It commits both sides to trusted, redundant high-speed undersea cables and to AI-enabled cybersecurity, and makes JAI a strategic R&D partnership in AI.

On hardware, the specific item is the UNICORN stealth mast programme, with both sides reportedly targeting India's Acceptance of Necessity approval by February 2027. Reciprocal logistics run under the 2020 Acquisition and Cross-Servicing Agreement, and Japanese forces have been given live-fire range access in India.

## The production number is the more interesting one

Rajnath, addressing the Indian community in Tokyo on 18 November, put domestic defence production at ₹1.80 lakh crore and exports at an all-time high of ₹38,000 crore.

PIB's own FY26 print from 4 February 2026 is more precise: exports of ₹38,424 crore, a 62.66% increase on the previous year. Set against ₹1.80 lakh crore of production, exports are about 2.1% of output. That ratio is the whole argument: this industry is built on the domestic order book, and any policy that slows domestic procurement hurts it more than any export incentive helps it.

## Two smaller items

The RBI clarified foreign-exchange reporting rules for service imports and exports on Friday. Compliance clarification, not a market event — but it lands in the same week that the rupee had its largest single-day gain in over a month.

Cyclone Akvan made landfall near Gopalpur between 07:30 and 08:30 IST on Friday with sustained winds of 70-80 kmph gusting to 90, weakening into a deep depression within hours. Odisha evacuated more than 3.72 lakh people, deployed 423 rescue teams and cancelled 47 trains; Ganjam's Patrapur recorded 209 mm of rain. The state's disaster management minister reported no deaths and no major public-infrastructure damage in the initial assessment, and Andhra Pradesh reported minimal impact. For insurers and coastal utilities this is a nil event. After three cyclones in a month, the nil is the news.
