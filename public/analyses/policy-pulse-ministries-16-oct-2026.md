## Trade Ministry: the India-US BTA First Tranche Is Finalised, Waiting on the Tariff Advantage

The first tranche of the **India-US Bilateral Trade Agreement has been finalised and is in legal drafting**, Commerce and Industry Minister **Piyush Goyal** said on **October 15** at the SRCC Centenary Conclave in New Delhi. Finance Minister **Nirmala Sitharaman** reinforced it the next day, telling the **Atlantic Council on October 16** that she hopes the agreement is concluded "at the earliest."

The conditionality is the part that matters for exporters.

- Goyal: the deal will be operationalised **"once we get comparable advantage or competitive advantage over our peer countries"** — a reference to securing tariff terms no worse than those facing **Bangladesh, Vietnam and Indonesia** in the US market.
- US Ambassador to India **Sergio Gor** said at Bloomberg's New Economy Forum that "the big picture, the big framework, the big items are done," with negotiations moving at an "incredible pace."
- **Trade Secretary Rajesh Agrawal**: "We anticipate closing the deal and signing the deal at the earliest possible time," noting parallel US work to reset its tariff structure and that outcomes under **Section 301** will shape the signing date.
- **Wheat, rice, maize and soymeal remain excluded** from tariff concessions, as do sensitive agriculture and dairy lines.
- Goyal also confirmed the **India-New Zealand FTA enters into force on October 20**, after ratification by both sides.

The base facts: India's goods exports to the US rose **0.92% to $87.3 billion** in FY 2025-26, imports from the US rose **15.95% to $52.9 billion**, and the bilateral surplus **narrowed to $34.4 billion** from $40.89 billion. In April-August, exports to the US were **$42.79 billion against $40.39 billion** a year earlier.

The overhang is the **Graham Act**, signed September 18, which could carry duties of up to 100% on the five largest buyers of Russian energy. India's exposure attaches only to purchases knowingly made **on or after October 18**.

## Defence: ₹4,106 Crore of Contracts, Led by a ₹2,563 Crore Dedicated IAF Satellite

The **Ministry of Defence signed contracts worth ₹4,106 crore on October 15**, reported on October 16, in a two-part package.

- **The ₹2,563 crore contract for GSAT-7C**, a dedicated communications satellite for the Indian Air Force, was signed in New Delhi on October 15 with **NewSpace India Limited**, the commercial arm of ISRO. It was witnessed by **A. Anbarasu**, Additional Secretary and Director General (Acquisition).
- The IAF has until now depended on **shared transponder capacity**. A dedicated satellite is intended to carry data and video between aircraft in flight and ground command centres, with encryption layers to reduce interception risk. It is being indigenously designed, developed and manufactured.
- The **Defence Acquisition Council granted in-principle approval in 2021**, so the project has been in the pipeline for several years. The Navy's equivalent, **CMS-03 / GSAT-7R**, was launched on November 2, 2025.
- Separately, the MoD signed a **₹586 crore contract with Pune-based Accurate Industrial Controls** for auxiliary power units for **T-72 and T-90 tanks**. The **Varunastra** heavyweight torpedo programme is the second component of the October 15 package.

## SEBI Rewrites the Close: a 10-Minute Auction Session From December 7

The capital-markets regulator has revised the **Closing Auction Session framework**, with the circular issued on **October 15** and reported on **October 16**. The changes take effect **December 7, 2026**.

- The **CAS runs from 3:30 pm to 3:40 pm** on all trading days.
- The **order-entry window closes randomly between 3:38 pm and 3:40 pm**, which is the structural defence against last-second manipulation of the closing print.
- The **post-close session in the cash market runs from 3:50 pm to 4:00 pm**, with trades executed at the closing price of the securities.
- The framework also changes **equity derivatives trading cut-offs** and the **expiry-day settlement methodology**.

The design intent is a closing price that is auction-derived rather than VWAP-derived. The reference price for a stock in CAS is determined from the **VWAP of trades executed between 3:00 pm and 3:15 pm**; where no trade occurs in that window, the last traded price is used.

## RBI and the Rupee: a Sixth Straight Gain, and Reserves Down $8.58 Billion

The rupee closed **94.92 against the US dollar on October 16, up 7 paise** — a sixth consecutive session of gains and its strongest weekly performance in seven months. It opened at 94.75, touched a high of 94.71 and a low of 95.06. On October 15 it had appreciated 18 paise to 94.99.

The supporting detail is the cost of that defence:

- **India's foreign exchange reserves fell $8.578 billion to $726.027 billion** in the week ended **October 9**, per RBI data released on October 16.
- The RBI has deployed approximately **$75-80 billion** through spot dollar sales and dollar-rupee swaps since its special measures took effect on **June 8**.
- The measures announced on **October 10** include a **special dollar window for three state-owned oil marketing companies**, tighter rules on rupee-linked foreign exchange derivatives, and a **20% Foreign Exchange Risk Reserve** requirement on eligible transactions. The RBI clarified the scope of those rules on October 15 after they disrupted trading, and forward premiums have surged to multi-year highs.
- Traders report **unwinding of long USD/INR positions** in the non-deliverable forward market, where the offshore rate has traded at a discount to onshore — unusual for a currency under pressure.

The counter-view is worth stating plainly: S&P Global Market Intelligence sees the rupee around **97 by end-2026**, Mizuho sees **98** by late 2026 or early 2027, and neither rules out **100**. Ambit Capital notes reserves are down **$51 billion** as of October 2 and that June's swap measures created forward dollar commitments of about **$200 billion**.
