## 2. Business Overview

Yatharth Hospital & Trauma Care Services Ltd, founded in 2008 by Dr. Ajay Kumar Tyagi and Dr. Kapil Kumar, is a Noida-based super-specialty hospital chain focused on Delhi NCR and contiguous markets. It listed on the exchanges in July 2024.

**Network (nine hospitals, announced capacity ~3,250 beds):**

| Hospital | Location |
|---|---|
| Greater Noida (Sector Omega-01) | Uttar Pradesh |
| Greater Noida West (Sector-01, "Noida Extension") | Uttar Pradesh |
| Noida (Sector-110) | Uttar Pradesh |
| Jhansi-Mauranipur Highway, Orchha | Madhya Pradesh |
| Faridabad (Sector-88, "Greater Faridabad") | Haryana |
| Model Town 3, New Delhi | Delhi |
| Faridabad (Sector 20B) | Haryana |
| Sikandra, Agra | Uttar Pradesh |
| Gurugram (Sector 40) | Haryana — under construction, live Q1 FY28 |

Accreditation matters in this business: the Greater Noida super-specialty hospital holds **Joint Commission International (JCI)** accreditation and the group carries NABH accreditation — a genuine differentiator for attracting international and insurance-funded patients. The group employs 7,440 people.

**How the company makes money:** inpatient and outpatient tertiary and quaternary care across cardiac sciences, neurosciences, oncology, orthopaedics, nephrology, critical care and trauma. The economics turn on three levers — **beds**, **occupancy**, and **ARPOB** (average revenue per occupied bed).

**The strategy is buy-and-turnaround plus greenfield:** acquire underperforming or under-construction assets in dense, high-income catchments, integrate them quickly, and lift occupancy and ARPOB. The evidence that this works is real — see Section 7.

**One structural feature to understand:** the group carries a hospital in Orchha, Madhya Pradesh alongside its NCR cluster. NCR hospitals achieve far higher ARPOB than the Orchha facility (₹50,000+ at the premium end vs a group average of ₹34,758), so **the reported group ARPOB understates the quality of the NCR assets and overstates the maturity of the portfolio**.

*Disclosure note: the company's own Q1 FY27 call (August 2026) cited a network of 2,555 beds. By September-October 2026, press reports and the company's EGM materials describe nine hospitals with ~2,800 beds and announced capacity of ~3,250 beds. The difference reflects capacity added during the intervening weeks plus the acquisition of the Gurugram asset.*

### 3.3 Key takeaways — the central anomaly

**Revenue grew 52% year-on-year in Q1 FY27 (₹258 Cr → ₹393 Cr) and the reported net profit was identical at ₹45 Cr — flat for five straight quarters.**

This is not an accounting trick or a one-off. It is the arithmetic of the expansion:

- **Depreciation has nearly doubled**, from ₹15 Cr to ₹28 Cr a quarter, as acquisitions and completed capex arrive on the balance sheet (annual depreciation: ₹57 Cr → ₹88 Cr → ₹101 Cr TTM)
- **Interest has gone from zero to ₹7 Cr a quarter** as the company borrowed to fund hospital acquisitions — consolidated borrowings rose from ₹12 Cr to ₹264 Cr during FY26
- **Operating margin has been stuck at 23%** for four quarters, held there by the drag from immature hospitals plus a worsening payer mix (Section 7)

The company is right to point at cash profit, which rose 32% year-on-year (PAT plus depreciation). But the honest reading is that **the P&L the market is capitalising at 54x earnings has not grown in over a year**, and the gap between revenue growth and profit growth is the single most important thing to understand about this stock.

**This is what the Advent capital is meant to fix** — by funding the expansion off the balance sheet rather than through debt, it should eventually relieve the interest burden. It does not relieve the depreciation burden, which is the larger drag.

**The good news is the quality of earnings:** operating cash flow rose to ₹205 Cr in FY26, which is **94% of operating profit** — up from a dismal 22% in FY24. This is a business that converts its reported profit into cash. For a hospital company carrying 113 debtor days (largely insurer and government receivables), a 94% conversion ratio is genuinely strong.

**The awkward news is the shape of the whole statement:** CFO of ₹205 Cr against investing outflow of ₹598 Cr. The company has been spending roughly three rupees for every one it generates to buy and build hospitals, funding the gap with debt and equity (₹254 Cr from financing in FY26, ₹509 Cr in FY25 — that was the QIP).

Free cash flow has been negative for three consecutive years. That is defensible for a company in a land-grab phase — hospital capacity, once built, generates cash for decades — **but it means every growth rupee has so far been funded by someone else's money**, and it explains precisely why the Advent deal matters.

Alongside them sat high-frequency desks (Share India Securities, Graviton Research, Mansi Share & Stock Broking) running matched same-day buy/sell arbitrage.

**How to read it:** a promoter choosing to sell 5.8% in a single day at ₹717 — while the company was publicly guiding to a doubling of capacity and record growth — is the kind of signal that deserves to be weighed rather than explained away. It does not invalidate the business, and the promoter group as a whole remains the largest shareholder with 55.80%, but it is a real data point and the market punished the stock on the day for good reason.

**FII/DII participation is modest and volatile** (FIIs 5.62%, DIIs 10.84%) — this is a domestically-owned story where institutions have rotated in and out rather than accumulating steadily. ## 7. Capacity Pipeline & Revenue Visibility

Hospitals have no order book, so revenue visibility comes from beds, occupancy, ARPOB and the maturity curve of new assets. Here is the actual operating detail from the Q1 FY27 call (11 August 2026).

### 7.1 Segment behaviour — mature vs new

| Cohort | Q1 FY27 revenue | YoY | Occupancy |
|---|---|---|---|
| Existing 3 (Noida, Greater Noida, Jhansi-Orchha) | ₹286.2 Cr | **+22%** | ~75% |
| Newer 4 (Greater Faridabad, New Delhi, Faridabad Sec-20, Agra) | ₹106.7 Cr | ramp-up | mixed |

The newer hospitals contributed **27% of group revenue** this quarter — up sharply, and the clearest evidence that the acquisition machine is producing.

### 7.2 Hospital-level operating metrics

| Hospital | Occupancy | ARPOB | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| Noida | — | ₹35,000 | >90% occupancy |
| Jhansi-Orchha | — | — | >90% occupancy |
| Greater Noida | 74% | ₹43,000 | — |
| Noida Extension | 56% | **>₹50,000** | Premium NCR |
| New Delhi (Model Town) | **29%** | **>₹50,000** | Breakeven expected Q3-Q4 FY27 |
| Faridabad Sec-20 | — | ~₹40,000 | **EBITDA breakeven in 9 months** |
| Greater Faridabad | — | ₹35,000 | — |
| Agra | — | ~₹30,000 | **20%+ EBITDA in first full quarter** |
| **Group** | ~75% | **₹34,758** (+7% YoY) | All-time high |

**The turnaround playbook is demonstrably working.** Faridabad Sector 20 reached EBITDA breakeven in nine months — unusually fast for a hospital acquisition — and now runs a monthly revenue rate of ₹12-13 Cr with ARPOB approaching ₹40,000 and a path to ₹45,000-50,000. Agra delivered 20%+ EBITDA in its very first full quarter of integration. Both New Delhi and Noida Extension have crossed ₹50,000 ARPOB, placing them among the premium hospitals in NCR, with 90%+ of the revenue mix at those two units coming from cash and private-insurance patients.

The clearest single number supporting the thesis: **adjusted EBITDA margin excluding the two loss-making newer units (New Delhi and Faridabad Sector 20) was 28.1%** in Q1 FY27. The core business is a 28%-margin business being reported at 23.3% because it is carrying its own expansion.

### 7.3 The two things holding margins down

1. **Government payer mix rose to ~40%** against a stated target of 25%, driven by a CGHS rate revision. Government business carries structurally lower realisations — management has explicitly said the mix should decline toward 25%, and margin expansion is tied to that.
2. **New Delhi's occupancy is only 29%** on an expanded census bed base, and breakeven is not expected until Q3-Q4 FY27. This single hospital is a meaningful drag on reported margin.

### 7.4 The growth pipeline

| Project | Scale | Timeline |
|---|---|---|
| Gurugram Sector 40 (acquired from RNY Healthcare for ₹100 Cr cash, completed 12 June 2026) | 250 beds, potential ARPOB ₹50,000+ | Live Q1 FY28 |
| Brownfield expansion, Noida Extension & Greater Noida | part of route to 3,250 beds | 15-18 months (delayed by monsoon) |
| Route to announced capacity | ~3,250 beds | near term |
| **Long-term target** | **5,000 beds** | management now says ~2.5 years vs the original 3-year plan |
| Oncology capability | second and third LINAC machines (Faridabad Sec-20, then New Delhi) | phased; oncology already ~10% of revenue with a single machine |

**Watch the cost line, not just the bed count.** Management disclosed that **capex per bed has risen to ₹61.4 lakh, from ₹30.7 lakh three years ago**, and guided **₹75-80 lakh per bed for future expansions**. The cost of each incremental bed has roughly doubled. That materially changes the return on the 5,000-bed plan — and it is exactly why the company needs ₹3,150 crore rather than ₹1,500 crore to get there.

## 8. Management Commentary

From the Q1 FY27 earnings call (11 August 2026), Mr. Yatharth Tyagi, Whole-Time Director:

> "I'm pleased to report a strong start of FY27. We delivered our highest ever quarterly revenue and profits this quarter. More encouragingly, we achieved a record revenue growth of 51% year-on-year and an EBITDA growth of 39% year-on-year."

> "Our newer hospitals at Greater Faridabad, New Delhi Faridabad Sector 20 and Agra collectively contributed 27% to the mix this quarter... A key highlight was our Faridabad Sector 20 hospital turning EBITDA breakeven within a record period of 9 months."

> "The opportunity now is to fill these beds; improve utilization and allow earnings to catch up with the capacity we have created."

That last sentence is the most honest and important thing management has said. **Earnings catching up to capacity is the entire investment case** — and it is also an admission that the capacity has run ahead of the profits.

On the DME-style capital discipline question, the CFO, Mr. Pankaj Prabhakar, and Group Financial Controller Mr. Sonu Goyal gave specific guidance:

- Depreciation: **₹20-30 Cr per quarter**
- Interest: **₹6.6 Cr in Q1**, with **no fresh loans planned**
- CapEx per bed: **₹61.4 lakh achieved; ₹75-80 lakh guided for future expansions**

**Formal guidance issued for FY27:**
- **Revenue growth to exceed the prior year's 37%**
- **Consolidated EBITDA margin above 24%**
- Capacity to reach **3,200+ beds**, with the 5,000-bed target pulled forward to ~2.5 years

On the interim dividend of 5% of face value and the first ESOP grant under a new ESOP Scheme 2026, the company framed both as signals of confidence. It also opened a Yatharth Information Center in Uzbekistan and ran OPD initiatives across Asia, Africa and the Middle East to build international patient flow.

**Data limitations**

- **The primary valuation input — the share count — is a moving target until the EGM and the warrant exercise.** This report models 9.635 Cr shares currently, 10.938 Cr post-equity-issue and 12.833 Cr fully diluted.
- **Depreciation and interest guidance is company-stated** (₹20-30 Cr and ₹6.6 Cr per quarter) and has been trending upward; a material step-up would further compress EPS.
- **The consensus EPS figures may already assume a different share count than I have modelled.** I could not verify the share count underlying the Markets Insider FY27 EPS of ₹26.58, which is why I present the restated figures explicitly rather than asserting a single number.
- **ARPOB, occupancy and hospital-level data are company-reported** and not independently audited; the Q1 FY27 call cited 2,555 beds while later press reports cite ~2,800.
- **The promoter profit-share arrangement has not been finalised.** Its terms — including the minimum return threshold and the graded share — are not fully public, so its economic impact on public shareholders cannot yet be quantified.
