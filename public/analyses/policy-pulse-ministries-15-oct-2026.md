## Finance Ministry: the ₹52,878 Crore Strategic Sale of IDBI Bank to Fairfax

The government has approved the strategic disinvestment of IDBI Bank, ending a process that has run since 2022.

- The Cabinet Committee on Economic Affairs approved the sale of a **60.72% stake to Fairfax Financial Holdings for ₹52,878 crore, or ₹81 per share.** The stake comprises **30.48% held by the Government of India and 30.24% held by LIC.**
- The CCEA has delegated powers to the Alternative Mechanism to undertake **minority stake sales of the government's residual 15% shareholding through the open market**, and LIC will manage its own residual holding under its internal procedures.
- The bank was placed under the **RBI's Prompt Corrective Action framework on May 5, 2017** for non-performing assets and losses, and the restrictions were lifted on **March 10, 2021** subject to conditions and continued monitoring.
- **IDBI Bank shares fell more than 7% on October 15**, because the previous market price was significantly above the ₹81 offer price. The open offer, RBI licensing and lock-in conditions have not been addressed in the two PIB releases.

The precedent matters more than the price. This is the first large bank privatisation of the cycle, and it establishes a valuation anchor for the government's residual financial-sector holdings.

## The Supreme Court Quashes Yes Bank's ₹8,415 Crore AT1 Write-Off

The single largest single-stock move in the Nifty 500 on October 15 was a Supreme Court ruling, not an earnings release.

- The Supreme Court **upheld the Bombay High Court's decision cancelling the write-off of Yes Bank's ₹8,415 crore of Additional Tier-1 bonds**, restoring the liability to the bank.
- **Yes Bank shares fell 12.68% to ₹18.52** on volumes more than five times the five-day average — **the stock's worst day since June 2025.** It trades 28% below its 52-week high.
- The bank was on an "only sellers" footing on the BSE during the session.

The read-through is about the instrument rather than the issuer. AT1 bonds were written down to zero in the Yes Bank reconstruction of March 2020, and a final judicial finding that the write-off was invalid changes the perceived risk of every AT1 tranche issued since.

## RBI's 20% Foreign Exchange Risk Reserve: the FAQs Land

The Reserve Bank of India published **detailed FAQs on October 15** clarifying the **20% Foreign Exchange Risk Reserve (FERR)** it introduced on **October 10-12**, together with the rupee-defence package.

- The FAQs set out a **five-condition scope test** for which rupee derivative contracts attract the reserve, a **capital-account carve-out**, the computation methodology, and **daily CIMS reporting** obligations.
- **RBI Circular 27, dated October 12**, limits **INR non-deliverable derivative contracts for non-residents to hedging purposes** and requires Authorised Dealer banks to keep a **20% cash FERR** on contracts hedging their own assets and liabilities.
- The package also includes a **special dollar window for IOC, HPCL and BPCL** running from October 12, providing direct dollar supply to the state refiners rather than routing their demand through the spot market.
- The rupee has responded: it closed **10 paise higher at 95.05 on October 15, a fifth consecutive session of gains**, after appreciating roughly 2% across three sessions earlier in the week, when it had traded as strong as 94.96 intraday. The RBI Governor, **Sanjay Malhotra**, has publicly pushed back on what he called overly pessimistic market positioning on the currency.

The market-structure consequence is a cost increase on speculative FX positioning by banks. That reduces the volume of purely directional rupee derivative activity while leaving genuine hedging untouched — the intended design.

## Commerce: September's Record Exports Against a Widening Deficit

Commerce Ministry data released on **October 15** shows both sides of the ledger moving in the wrong proportion.

- **Merchandise exports jumped 22% year on year to $44.4 billion in September — the highest on record**, per the Commerce Ministry data as reported by Trading Economics.
- **The merchandise trade deficit widened to $30.2 billion** for the month, from $26.86 billion in August, driven by imports of gold, silver, fertilisers, electronics and crude.
- The previous month's read was a narrowing deficit on a **26.1% merchandise export surge** to $43.81 billion, so September's widening is an import-side event rather than an export slowdown.

Record exports with a widening deficit is the shape of an economy importing capital goods and precious metals faster than it is exporting goods. The equity read is unambiguously positive for the exporters and neutral-to-negative for the macro.

## NPCI: the UPI Merchant Fee Did Not Go Live

The date that had been in every calendar this week came and passed without the change taking effect.

- **NPCI notified the framework on September 15**: an **MDR of 0.4% on eligible person-to-merchant UPI transactions above ₹2,000**, capped at **₹300 per transaction** for payments of ₹75,000 and above, with **person-to-person transactions free at all values**. NPCI's own FAQ states that around **96% of P2M transactions remain unaffected**.
- The **scheduled October 15 go-live has been deferred**, with reporting indicating the rollout will move to **after Diwali** following pushback from retailer bodies and at least one large broker.
- Reuters reported as early as October 8 that the government was considering a delay, and payment stocks had already slid on that report.

The deferral preserves the zero-cost consumer experience through the festive quarter, which is good for UPI volumes and therefore for the acquirers, and it delays the revenue line that the framework was designed to create for banks and payment platforms.

The **lot size is 12 shares, or ₹13,428** at the top of the band. The RHP was filed on October 14, **subscription opens October 21 and closes October 23**, and listing is expected on **October 28**.

One lot is 43 shares, a minimum of **₹14,792** at the upper band. The entire proceeds go to selling shareholders, so no primary capital is raised.
