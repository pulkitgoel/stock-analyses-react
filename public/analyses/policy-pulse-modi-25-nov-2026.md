The Union Cabinet approved ₹11,000 crore for construction and infrastructure equipment manufacturing today. The sector's listed manufacturers barely moved, and the reason is in the fine print of the scheme's own calendar.

The Scheme for Enhancement of Construction and Infrastructure Equipment (CIE) Manufacturing Ecosystem, cleared by the Cabinet chaired by the Prime Minister and to be implemented by the Ministry of Heavy Industries, runs for eight years from FY2026-27. The first two years — FY27 and FY28 — are a gestation period for setting up manufacturing facilities. The five-year incentive window is FY2028-29 to FY2032-33. FY2033-34 exists only to settle the incentive on FY33's incremental sales.

So the money does not reach a profit and loss account until the fiscal year that begins in April 2028. Nothing in the scheme changes FY27 or FY28 earnings, which is exactly how the market traded it.

## What is actually in it

Two components. A sales-linked incentive on the incremental sales value of eligible equipment and components, paid to two categories of approved applicant — eligible original equipment manufacturers and component manufacturers — at a graded rate starting at 13% and declining to 8% across the five years. And a grant of up to ₹375 crore, structured as viability gap funding, for a dedicated CIE testing facility.

The sector numbers the government is using: India's CIE industry is the world's third largest after China and the United States, worth about ₹97,500 crore in FY2024-25, and projected to more than double to roughly ₹2,28,100 crore by FY2029-30.

The seven categories the scheme targets are the ones that are fully or largely imported — tunnel boring machines, off-highway trucks, excavators, cranes, loaders, piling rigs and drilling machines. Information and Broadcasting Minister Ashwini Vaishnaw, briefing after the meeting, put import dependence for the sector at about 45%.

## The timing is not about incentives

China's export controls on key heavy machinery, tunnel boring machines among them, took effect on 18 November. A week later the Cabinet has funded domestic capacity in precisely those categories. The government did not draw the line explicitly, and Business Standard noted that it declined to, but the sequence is the sequence.

The tunnel boring machine is the emblem because it is the least substitutable input in the infrastructure pipeline. India's metro and tunnel programme is being built with imported machines, and the export lever sits in Beijing. An incentive that pays 13% of incremental sales from FY29 does not solve a supply constraint that bites in FY27. What solves it, or does not, is whether an OEM can get a machine certified — which is why the ₹375 crore testing facility may matter more in the near term than the ₹10,625 crore of incentives.

## The other three approvals, briefly

The Cabinet Committee on Economic Affairs approved a greenfield airport at Doloo in Cachar district, Assam, to be developed by the Airports Authority of India at an estimated cost of ₹2,014 crore. It replaces the existing Silchar airport, which operates as a civil enclave inside Air Force Station Kumbhirgram and has been constrained by that. The terminal is 12,351 square metres for 800 passengers at peak hour, on a 2,705 metre by 45 metre Code-4C runway sized for A320 aircraft, with an apron for six.

The Cabinet also cleared the continuation of two Department of Posts schemes through the 16th Finance Commission cycle: the Estates Management Scheme at ₹1,491 crore for FY27 to FY31, and Postal Operations at ₹1,141 crore over the same period. Neither has a listed-equity read — the Department of Posts is not a listed entity and neither scheme creates third-party order flow that a listed company has been named in.

## The read

A ₹11,000 crore scheme whose first rupee of incentive lands in FY29 is a capacity signal, not an earnings event, and the tape said so within an hour of the announcement: the six names most exposed to it moved between -0.5% and +1.7% on a day the Sensex rose 1.82%.

What it does change is the shape of the FY29-FY33 capital expenditure decision for any OEM thinking about a new line. And the 13%-to-8% taper is designed to reward the company that moves early — the first year of incremental sales earns nearly double what the fifth does.
