## The Justice Department Opens an Antitrust Probe Into the Television Pool

The Department of Justice's Antitrust Division has opened a formal investigation into whether the five networks that make up the television White House pool — **ABC, CBS, NBC, Fox News and CNN** — violated antitrust law by suspending shared pool coverage. The pool stopped covering the president on **September 21**, its chair saying the decision followed "the White House's position preventing CNN from fulfilling its assigned pool duties," after Trump banned CNN and two other outlets from the White House. Coverage has since resumed. The New York Times reported the probe on **October 10**; NPR carried it on **October 12**.

The same day, a **federal judge ordered the administration to cancel any remaining taxpayer-funded promotional television advertisements**, and a White House official said there were no immediate plans to reimburse money already spent. Axios reported the White House's position that Trump would pay for the advertisements himself.

- The five networks share the cost of covering the president, take turns organising coverage and syndicate the footage to outlets nationwide. That cost-sharing structure is what the Antitrust Division is examining.
- The cancelled advertising is immaterial to network revenue. The regulatory-risk premium attached to presidential coverage decisions is not, and both parties to it are now the same government.

## Foreign Military Sales Reform Takes Effect Today

The State Department's Directorate of Defense Trade Controls rule implementing **Executive Order 14268, "Reforming Foreign Defense Sales to Improve Speed and Accountability," takes effect on October 12, 2026**. The order targets the approval pipeline for government-to-government foreign military sales, which has been the binding constraint on delivering already-funded allied orders.

- Foreign military sales are a multi-year, backlog-driven book for the US primes. Shortening the approval step accelerates revenue recognition on orders that are already signed, rather than creating new demand.
- The order does not change budgets, quantities or export-eligibility rules, so this is a throughput item, not a demand shock.

## Importers Get a Class-Action Route to Tariff Refunds

A US trade court has cleared a class-action route for importers seeking repayment of duties, warning that billions of dollars in unlawful duties could otherwise remain with the government because smaller importers cannot individually fund litigation. The ruling widens access to refunds beyond the largest filers.

- Refunds of already-paid duties are a one-off cash recovery and a reduction in landed cost for importers, not a change to the tariff schedule itself.
- The administration can still appeal, so timing and realisation are uncertain.

## Trump's Russia Diesel Deal Cuts Both Ways

Trump said on **October 9** that Russia would release **300,000 tonnes of diesel immediately, 500,000 tonnes in November and a further 1 million tonnes after that**, and on **October 11** announced a Russia-Ukraine **"energy ceasefire" effective immediately**. Zelenskyy later said Russia struck a power plant in Kharkiv. Senator **Thom Tillis** said the arrangement may breach a 2022 law banning Russian oil imports absent conditions including a full Russian withdrawal; the White House referred to the ceasefire announcement. Details remain unclear and the plan faces opposition in Washington.

Moscow's planned 500,000-tonne export compares with **28.6 million tonnes last year**, so this is a marginal supply addition. The **IEA has urged governments to accelerate pledged diesel releases ahead of its October 15 meeting**, and the US Energy Department approved an emergency exchange of up to **4 million barrels** from the Strategic Petroleum Reserve to offset Hurricane Isaias disruptions.
