India approved its first **copper foil, cathode active material and specialty ceramics** projects today. That sentence is the story, and it is deliberately unexciting.

Six years into an electronics-manufacturing push that has mostly been measured in assembled phones, the Ministry of Electronics and IT has begun approving the inputs underneath the assembly — the films, foils and powders that go into a battery or a circuit board. It is a smaller number than the headline outlay suggests, and it is the first tranche that looks like an industrial policy rather than a subsidy.

## First: what was actually announced

PIB release **2333716**, dated **6 November 2026 at 6:05 p.m.**, Ministry of Electronics & IT. Two things happened at the same event.

**One — the Semicon 2.0 Web Application Portal went live for Pillars 2, 3 and 4.** Those are **Machines and Materials**, **Setting up More Fabs**, and **Further Strengthening the ATMP/OSAT Industry**. Applications are now open. Pillars 1 (design), 5 (R&D) and 6 (talent) are not part of this window.

**Two — twelve more applications were approved under the Electronics Components Manufacturing Scheme (ECMS):**

- **Projected investment: ₹7,503 crore.**
- **Projected production: ₹39,045 crore.**
- **Direct employment: 7,127.**
- **Cumulative to date: 118 applications, ₹69,548 crore approved earlier plus today's ₹7,503 crore, or roughly ₹77,051 crore.**

The twelve cover **ten target-segment products**:

- **Components:** multi-layer PCB, relays, heat sinks, specialty ceramics.
- **Supply-chain products:** copper foil, anode material, cathode active material, polypropylene film, camera rim.
- **Capital goods** and their parts.

And they are spread across **seven states** — Gujarat, Karnataka, Madhya Pradesh, Maharashtra, Punjab, Tamil Nadu and Uttar Pradesh.

## Second: the three firsts, and why they matter more than the amount

**Copper foil** is the current collector inside a lithium-ion cell and the base layer for copper-clad laminate, which is the substrate of every PCB. **Cathode active material** is the single largest cost line in a battery cell. **Specialty ceramics** sit inside capacitors, substrates and sensors.

None of the three had ever been approved under ECMS before today. All three are upstream of things India already assembles in volume. That is the difference between an assembly economy and a components economy, and it is exactly where the value has been leaking out.

## Third: the selection language has hardened

Read the minister's words as a correction, not a celebration. Ashwini Vaishnaw called for **"rigorous selection of projects"** and said **"quality, technological depth, R&D and global competitiveness must guide the country's semiconductor ambitions."**

That is a different register from the volume-of-approvals framing of the last two years, and it lands three months after he publicly warned component makers that **non-performers would be "weeded out"** and threatened to halt disbursements to applicants who had not built the design capabilities and standards they committed to.

Two admissions are buried in the same release and are worth more than the ₹7,503 crore:

- **Nearly 50% of the units approved under ECMS are MSMEs.** Good for geographic spread and employment. Bad for the balance sheets needed to survive a five-year qualification cycle with a global customer.
- **The target for Semicon 2.0 is to attract at least three major equipment manufacturers and five chemicals and gases companies.** Stated as an ambition, which is an acknowledgment that India currently has close to zero of either. Machines and materials are the two hardest pillars, and they are the two that were just opened for applications.

## Fourth: the base it is building on

The Semicon 1.0 scorecard, from the same release:

- **12 projects approved across six states**, cumulative investment **over ₹1.64 lakh crore**.
- **5 of those 12 are in commercial production as of October 2026.**
- **105 startups** given access to EDA tools; **24** supported through funding; **15** have raised venture capital.
- **332 institutions** provided EDA tools under the Chips to Startup programme, with **approximately 70,000 students trained**.

Five of twelve in production after four years is the honest number. The design pipeline — 105 startups on EDA tools, 250-plus chips designed by Tier-II and Tier-III students — is the strongest part of the programme precisely because it needs the least capital.

Semicon 2.0 itself carries a **₹1,27,500 crore** fiscal outlay across six pillars, which is support and not capex; the ₹77,051 crore figure above is applicant investment and the two should never be added together.

## The line to take away

₹7,503 crore of private investment for 7,127 jobs is not a transformation. But the **product mix** changed today: for the first time the scheme is funding the foil, the cathode powder and the ceramic, not the box they go into.

The test is not the approval. It is whether the copper foil line is running in 2028. Vaishnaw's own language — rigorous selection, quality over quantity, weeding out — suggests the ministry knows that most of the last 106 approvals have not yet been stress-tested.
