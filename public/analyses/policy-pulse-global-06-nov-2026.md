Two indices moved more than 2% today in opposite directions on the same piece of news. India closed at its best level since the Iran war began. Korea closed 6% lower, barely above 5,000, after an intraday plunge that halted trading.

The temptation is to call this decoupling. It is not. It is the same trade arriving at two different points in its life cycle.

## First: the settled India close

**Sensex 81,028.02 (+1,718.01, +2.17%). Nifty 50 25,030.35 (+493.75, +2.01%).**

- **Seventh consecutive gain**, and the **first close above 25,000 since 27 February 2026**.
- On the week the **Nifty gained about 8.58%, roughly 1,977 points** — the strongest weekly performance in about five years.
- **Bank Nifty 59,086.00 (+1,312.35, +2.27%)** — a clear outperformance against the frontline index.
- **Nifty IT 32,977.90 (+140.05, +0.43%).** The sector that led July's "reverse AI trade" did almost nothing today.

The composition is the whole argument:

- **Nifty Private Bank outperformed; Nifty Media fell the most.**
- **Nifty Midcap 100 60,606.65 (-0.02%)** and **Nifty Smallcap 100 9,651.60 (-0.50%)** both ended lower.
- Only **four Nifty 50 stocks closed in the red**. Top gainers: **M&M +5.1%, ICICI Bank +4.8%, HDFC Life +4%.**

A 2.17% Sensex day in which midcaps and smallcaps fell is not a broad re-rating. It is a large-cap, bank-weighted, foreign-flow day, and the flow data confirms it: **FIIs net bought ₹10,769.84 crore** in the NSE cash segment against **DII net sales of ₹6,208.91 crore**. Provisional figures, and the two agencies do not agree in scope — Trendlyne's read of the same session puts FII buying nearer ₹14,975 crore — but the sign is not in dispute. Foreigners bought; domestic institutions sold into them.

The **rupee closed at 93.91, up 56 paise or 0.60%** — a six-month high and the biggest single-day gain since 12 October — and the driver was **crude, not rate differentials**: Brent has come down from the mid-September spike to **$97.87**, with WTI at **$91.10**.

- **Samsung Electronics -7.99%, SK Hynix -9.97%.**
- **Foreign investors were net sellers of roughly 2.74 trillion won**, a **sixth consecutive session** of selling, taking the six-day total to about **11.99 trillion won**.
- **669 KOSPI and KOSDAQ stocks closed at their lowest price of the year.**

**Nikkei 225 57,434.77 (-5.96%). Hang Seng 24,481.86 (-1.27%). Shanghai -1.3%, ASX 200 -1.0%, TAIEX -4.2% at its worst.** In Europe, intraday: **DAX -2.25%, FTSE 100 -1.48%, CAC 40 -1.24%, STOXX 50 -1.27%.**

The proximate cause was Wednesday's US session — the Democrats won Texas and Georgia on data-centre moratorium platforms, and the AI complex sold off hard enough that Thursday became Wall Street's worst day since April 2025. Korea is the purest listed expression of that trade: two memory makers, one index.

## Third: why India rose

The mechanism is not that India is insulated from AI. It is that India has almost nothing in the AI capex chain to be short of, and three things that were being repriced in its favour on the same morning.

**One: the input cost.** Brent at $97.87 against a September peak above $115 is a direct margin transfer to importers, refiners, paint makers and airlines. India imports roughly 85% of its crude.

**Two: the currency.** A stronger rupee on cheaper oil lowers the landed cost of everything else too.

**Three: the venue.** Money leaving Korean memory and US data-centre names has to go somewhere, and the largest liquid equity market in Asia that owns no memory capacity is India. Same rotation as July, larger scale.

What India does **not** have is a moat. Indian IT services companies are paid by the same clients cutting AI capex. A rotation into banks is a rotation into domestic credit growth, and domestic credit growth is the one thing in this index that the AI cycle cannot take away — which is precisely why it led.

## Fourth: the American number that lands on all of this

The US added **165,000 jobs in October** against roughly 59,000-65,000 expected, with **unemployment at 4.1%** and wage growth of **0.4% m/m, 3.1% y/y**. **December Fed hike odds moved to about 73-80%**, the **10-year Treasury yield rose to 4.87-4.88%** and the **dollar index rose 0.64% to 102.20**.

For India that is a genuine two-way pull, and today only one arm of it won. A hawkish Fed and a rising dollar argue for a weaker rupee. A $40 fall in Brent argues for a stronger one. The rupee followed oil. If Brent turns, it will follow the rate differential instead — which points the other way.

## The line to take away

India's 2.17% and Korea's -6.02% are the same trade. One market is being sold because it owns the AI capex cycle. The other is being bought because it does not — and because a falling oil price, a stronger rupee and returning foreign flows all arrived on the same morning.

The rotation is real. The moat is not. If Brent turns back toward $110, the rupee follows the rate differential rather than the oil price, and the three tailwinds that produced today become one headwind and two neutrals.
