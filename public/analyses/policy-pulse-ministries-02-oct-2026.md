## Project-75(I) Nears ₹80,000 Crore: India Set to Build Six German-Designed Submarines

**Defence Secretary Rajesh Kumar Singh said on Thursday, October 1 that India is in the final stages of approving a deal worth more than ₹80,000 crore to build six advanced conventional submarines domestically in partnership with Germany.** The programme, **Project-75(I)**, awaits a final decision from the competent authority — the Cabinet Committee on Security — before contract formalities can begin.

The structure, as stated:

- **Mazagon Dock Shipbuilders (MDL) will build the submarines in Mumbai**, with **ThyssenKrupp Marine Systems (TKMS)** as the German technology partner. Indian private-sector companies are expected to play a significant role in the production effort.
- **Air-independent propulsion (AIP)** is specified, allowing the boats to stay submerged for weeks and making them substantially harder to detect.
- Germany has **completed all its domestic approvals** and is waiting for India to sign. Germany's Ambassador, **Jasper Wieck**, said Berlin can finalise the deal at short notice once the CCS clears it.
- **Separately, a proposal for three additional Scorpene-class submarines remains under consideration** — but Singh confirmed it is **less advanced** than Project-75(I).
- The deal follows the **defence industrial roadmap India and Germany signed during Rajnath Singh's visit to Berlin in August**.

The strategic context is India's ageing conventional submarine fleet against sustained regional competition. The approval timeline — advanced, but not yet cleared — is the fact that matters most for positioning.

## MoD Inks the ₹810.79 Crore SAT-SAAW Contract With Bharat Dynamics

**The Ministry of Defence signed a contract worth ₹810.79 crore with Bharat Dynamics Limited (BDL) for 160 Satellite Smart Anti-Airfield Weapons (SAT-SAAW) and associated equipment for the Indian Air Force.** The deal sits alongside a separate **₹586 crore contract with Accurate Industrial Controls Pvt Ltd (AICPL) for 3,257 auxiliary power units** for the Army's T-72 and T-90 tank fleet.

SAT-SAAW is a **DRDO-designed smart anti-airfield glide bomb**, first tested in 2016, giving the IAF a stand-off capability against hardened airfield targets without putting aircraft inside modern air-defence envelopes. For BDL, it is incremental, repeat-order revenue in the munitions line rather than a programme-level award.

## India's Power Shortage Hits a Three-Year High — and the Section 11 Order Is Now Live

**India's power shortages rose to 560 million kilowatt-hours in September 2026, the highest since August 2023**, despite coal-fired plants operating at higher capacity for a **sixth straight month**. The drivers are an **El Niño-weakened monsoon** (weak hydro generation, and the lowest June-September rainfall in more than a decade at **12.6% below the long-term average**), plus surging industrial demand.

The policy response was already in place before the September data landed: **on September 25, 2026 the government issued an order under Section 11 of the Electricity Act, 2003, compelling 112 privately owned captive coal-fired plants with at least 50 MW of installed capacity to operate at maximum output and sell surplus electricity into public power exchanges from October 1 to December 31, 2026.**

That is a **forced-supply order with a four-month clock**, and it cuts two ways: it is unambiguously positive for system availability heading into winter, and it is a cost-imposition on captive generators who must now run and sell rather than optimise for their own load.

Coal India's September operating data underlines the load: **provisional production of 53.5 million tonnes, up 9.2% year-on-year, and off-take of 61.2 million tonnes, up 12.5%**, led by MCL and SECL.

## GST Posts a Third Straight ₹2 Lakh Crore Month — But the Import Share Is Now Record-High

**September 2026 gross GST collections rose 14.7% year-on-year to ₹2,03,521 crore**, against ₹1,77,365 crore in September 2025. Net GST revenue after refunds rose **18.1% to about ₹1.77 lakh crore**, from ₹1.50 lakh crore a year earlier — helped by refunds falling 3% to ₹27,001 crore.

The composition is the story, and it is not an unambiguous positive:

- **Import-linked GST revenue jumped 25.9% to ₹65,525 crore**, from ₹52,031 crore.
- **Domestic GST revenue grew just 10.1% to about ₹1.38 lakh crore**, from ₹1.25 lakh crore.
- The **share of revenue from imports is now the highest on record, and the share from domestic transactions the lowest on record.**
- **H1 FY27 gross GST: ₹12.46 lakh crore, up 11.6%**; net **₹10.66 lakh crore, up 10.4%**. Domestic GST grew just **6.1% to ₹8.74 lakh crore** while import collections jumped **27.1% to ₹3.72 lakh crore**.
- This is the **third month of FY27 above ₹2 lakh crore**, after April (~₹2.43 lakh crore) and July (over ₹2.11 lakh crore).
- **The GST Council meets on October 7, 2026** — rescheduled from September 12 — with the ITC/supplier-default issue, corporate-guarantee valuation under Rule 28(2), registration simplification, cess and GSTAT on the agenda.

The honest read: a headline third straight ₹2 lakh crore month, built on a tax handle — import duties collected at a single chokepoint — whose base moves with **world prices and the exchange rate**, not with the domestic economy. Domestic collections growing at 6.1% in H1 is the number that describes the home economy.

## RBI Opens the Door for Passive Money in Banks

**The Reserve Bank of India issued Amendment Directions on October 1, 2026 establishing a "simplified approval process for subsequent acquisitions of major shareholding in a banking company by mutual funds, insurance companies and pension funds."**

The mechanism: a **one-time approval for up to a 10% stake**, replacing the earlier requirement to seek fresh regulatory clearance for each incremental acquisition. The intent is to remove repetitive regulatory friction for long-horizon, passive institutional holders in bank equity.

This is a **structural accessibility change, not a capital injection.** It matters most for index-tracking and long-duration institutional flows into bank equities — the same group of investors whose buying has been the counterweight to record foreign selling.

## SEBI: Investor-Awareness Mandate, Voluntary Director Workshops, and a ₹1 Lakh Penalty

Three SEBI actions this week, in descending order of materiality for listed intermediaries:

**One — the broker display mandate.** In a **circular dated October 1, 2026**, SEBI directed stock exchanges, depositories and stock brokers to display specified **investor awareness messages** on their websites and trading applications under **Project Jagrook**, ahead of World Investor Week 2026. From **October 5 to 31**, brokers must display the messages alongside existing risk disclosures on their websites; from **November 1**, the messages must appear on the **landing page**, and display on trading apps becomes mandatory. This is a compliance-cost item for the brokerage industry, not a revenue item.

**Two — independent director workshops look set to stay voluntary.** SEBI is likely to keep its proposed training workshops for independent directors **voluntary**, a shift from the earlier plan to make participation mandatory and potentially link it to reappointment. The first workshop, expected in September-October, is now likely **after Diwali**, with the Bombay Chartered Accountants Society, NISM and the NSE working on the programme. The softening removes a governance-compliance cost that listed companies had been bracing for.

**Three — a ₹1 lakh penalty on SMC Global Securities** for failing to maintain appropriate pre-order placement records for certain clients and for supervision failures relating to an authorised person.

Separately, **SEBI has cleared the draft IPO papers of four companies, including Danish brewer Carlsberg India's ₹6,600 crore issue.**

## RoDTEP Extended to December 31, 2026

**The government extended the Remission of Duties and Taxes on Exported Products (RoDTEP) scheme up to December 31, 2026**, announced by PIB on October 2, 2026. The extension maintains the existing duty-remission structure for exporters through the year-end, alongside the parallel extension of **RoSCTL** — together covering more than **15,400 exporters, predominantly MSMEs**.

Combined with the **second consecutive fortnightly cut in export levies on diesel (to ₹16 per litre) and ATF (to ₹10.5 per litre) effective October 1** — a ₹9 per litre diesel and ₹8.50 per litre ATF reduction in a single fortnight — the government is running a deliberate **export-cost-relief package** through the peak festive shipping window.

## Delhi's Winter Pollution Plan: "No PUC, No Fuel" Goes NCR-Wide

**From October 1, 2026, the "No PUC, No Fuel" rule is enforced across Delhi and all eight NCR regions under CAQM Direction No. 101**, allowing petrol pumps to refuse fuel to any vehicle without a valid Pollution Under Control Certificate. Roughly **500 petrol pumps in Delhi and 1,041 across the NCR** are being fitted with ANPR cameras.

The wider **Winter Action Plan 2026-27**, notified on June 23 and running annually from November 1 to February 28, includes:

- **Dust-generating demolition and open civil construction prohibited from November 1 to January 31**, except essential public infrastructure, with mandatory 100% CCTV web coverage and 'Malba App' registration required for building-plan approvals.
- **A complete construction-and-demolition ban from December 10 to January 20**, barring urgent government and emergency works. Vehicles carrying sand, stone, bricks and cement cannot enter the city in that window.
- **50% work-from-home, doubled parking charges and staggered office hours.**
- A ban from **November 1 to January 31 on out-of-Delhi vehicles that do not meet BS-VI** standards.
- **MCD is deploying 70 new mechanised road sweepers — 55 electric and 15 CNG — by mid-November**, imported from Italy and London with camera and brush-sensor equipment.

## The Rest of the Regulatory Tape

**RBI MPC meets October 5-7, decision October 7.** Repo sits at **5.25%**. A majority of economists polled expect a **25 basis point hike to 5.50% — the first increase since February 2023** — with another hike likely in December, taking the repo to 6% by year-end. SBI economists expect the MPC to "begin the rate hike cycle" pre-emptively. The drivers are August CPI at **4.82%**, crude near $100-107, a rupee near 96, record FPI outflows, and the $133bn FCNR(B) deposit deluge. ⚠️ **Do not pre-position on this — the decision lands after Monday's open.** Banks, NBFCs and rate-sensitive sectors carry the risk.

**Record primary market.** Indian companies raised a record **₹94,205 crore across 78 mainboard IPOs in H1 FY27** — 35% above the previous record — and roughly **$25 billion of equity in the first half**, even as the Nifty fell. September alone saw **34 companies raise over ₹39,300 crore**. **NSE listed on September 24 at ₹1,800, a 0.84% premium to its ₹1,785 issue price**, closing 0.94% higher at ₹1,817 with a market capitalisation of about **₹4.45 lakh crore** — a flat debut for India's largest-ever issue.

**N-pipeline.** **NPCIL and GSECL signed an MoU on October 2 to identify and assess sites for new nuclear power projects in Gujarat.**

**Monsoon.** India recorded its **lowest June-September rainfall in more than a decade — 12.6% below the long-term average, the sharpest shortfall since 2015** — prompting **ICRA to cut its FY27 farm-growth forecast**. This is a genuine negative for rural demand and a positive for irrigation, rural-employment spending and pump-set demand.
