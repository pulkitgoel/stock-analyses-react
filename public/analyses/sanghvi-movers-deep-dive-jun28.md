## Business overview

Sanghvi Movers Ltd (SML) is India's largest and Asia's largest crane rental company, ranked 5th globally (IC Index 2025). Headquartered in Pune, Maharashtra, the company was incorporated in 1989 and went public in 1994. It provides medium-to-heavy duty crane rental services to private and public sector undertakings across India and internationally.

**Core business segments:**

### Crane rental (core — roughly 65–70% of revenue)

- Fleet of **485+ cranes** with lifting capacity from **20 MT to 1,000 MT**
- **15 depots** across India, **175 acres** of freehold land for crane parking
- **65% market share** in the high-end crane segment (above 400 MT)
- Clients include Suzlon, Renew, JSW, L&T, BHEL, Adani and NTPC
- **Average capacity utilisation:** 87% in Q4 FY26, 79% for FY26 as a whole
- **Average blended yield (ABY):** 2.24% per month in Q4 FY26, 2.12% for FY26

### Wind EPC (growth segment — about 27% of Q3 FY26 revenue)

- Turnkey wind energy services via subsidiary **Sangreen Future Renewables**
- End-to-end, from conceptualisation to commissioning
- Track record of erecting **15+ GW** of wind turbine generators in India
- Asset-light model with **10–12% EBITDA margins** when normalised

### International operations (KSA, Botswana, Qatar)

- **Middle East:** Sanghvi Movers Middle East Ltd — utilisation of 80–90%, yield of about 4.5% per month
- **Botswana:** subsidiary received a $4.3M work order from Jindal Energy Botswana
- **KSA:** inquiry pipeline of $50M for the next 24 months
- Targeted at the $1T+ GCC construction market

### Logistics

- End-to-end logistics solutions for wind energy assets across India

**Key strengths:**

- **Network moat:** 15 depots across India — cranes are expensive to mobilise over long distances, which gives SML a structural advantage
- **OEM relationships:** long-standing ties with Liebherr and Manitowoc, which bring preferential delivery timelines
- **Cyclical recovery play:** crane rental is asset-heavy operating leverage — small changes in utilisation or yield produce outsized EBITDA effects
- **Elevate 2030 vision:** from pure crane rental to integrated heavy-lift and EPC solutions

Two data points since disclosed belong with this section: at the Q1 FY27 stage the company reported 86% utilisation in both India and the GCC, a 4.10% monthly GCC yield, and KSA operations cumulatively EBITDA positive in their first year at a 23% margin.

## Why it fell: no company announcement explains October

I looked for a disclosed trigger and could not find one. There is no profit warning, no order cancellation, no auditor resignation and no exchange-reported bulk or block deal on the most recent session — the [NSE bulk and block deal files](https://archives.nseindia.com/content/equities/bulk.csv) for 23 October 2026 carry no SANGHVIMOV row. The last hard news was the Q1 result on 31 July.

The proximate cause is the small-cap complex. In the week to 23 October the Nifty fell 2.3%, mid-caps 5.6% and small-caps 7.1%, with the small-cap index down roughly 8% in four sessions as margin-funded positions were unwound — borrowed positions stood at ₹1.56 trillion on 19 October against a record ₹1.6 trillion in September ([Economic Times](https://economictimes.indiatimes.com/markets/stocks/news/d-streets-margin-funded-stock-bets-fall-as-smallcap-rally-falters/articleshow/135113682.cms), [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-23/margin-trade-liquidation-speeds-indian-small-cap-stock-decline)).

Sanghvi is a 1.33-beta small-cap with a 5.7% average daily range that had run from ₹226 in March to ₹528 in August. That combination — high beta, high recent gain, high daily volatility — is precisely what forced deleveraging hits hardest, and it explains a 28% monthly fall against a 7% index fall without needing a company-specific cause.

Mark the reasoning honestly: **the attribution to deleveraging is inferred, not disclosed.** What is verified is that Nifty Smallcap 250 fell 7.1% that week and this stock fell 28% that month. What no source establishes is a Sanghvi-specific event in October.

## Where five percentage points of crane-rental margin went

The company's own margin bridge from FY26 to Q1 FY27 is unusually candid, and it is the most important table in this update:

| Core crane rental EBITDA margin | Impact | ₹ Cr |
|---|---|---|
| FY26 | 53% | — |
| Expected credit loss provision on aged receivables | −2 pp | 6.2 |
| Revenue mix: ancillary equipment and cross-rental instead of owned capex | −2 pp | — |
| One-time employee incentive (for crossing ₹1,000 Cr revenue) | −1 pp | — |
| Forex mark-to-market on foreign currency loan | −1 pp | 1.4 |
| **Q1 FY27** | **47%** | — |

Two of those four are one-offs and management says so: the ECL provision should reverse as collections come in, and the incentive was a one-time payment. Excluding forex and the incentive, the company puts the underlying core margin at about 49% and expects a return toward 51%.

Two are not one-offs. The mix shift is deliberate — management chose to serve incremental demand through hired ancillary equipment and cross-rental cranes, which carries a lower margin but consumes no capital, which is accretive to ROCE. That is a defensible capital-allocation decision that permanently lowers the reported margin percentage. Investors who bought Sanghvi for its 60% margin crane business in FY23 are now owners of a 35% blended-margin group, and the market is repricing that.

The ECL provision is the one to watch, because it is not really a one-off. It is the statistical consequence of a receivable book that has been ageing for two years.

The GCC number is the one that matters. A 201-day collection cycle in Saudi Arabia against a 4.10% monthly yield means the yield is being earned on paper for two-thirds of a year before it becomes cash. Management's answer on the Q1 call was that there is zero working-capital draw in Saudi Arabia, that significant collections happened in July, and that the number improves in Q2. That is a promise, not a result — and it is exactly the kind of promise that needs one more quarter before it is believed.

In India, 124 days for crane rental is roughly 30 days worse than the FY23 figure of 92 days on Screener's basis. Receivables, not demand, are what turn a 47% margin into a 35% margin and what funds a ₹652 Cr capex programme out of borrowings rather than operations.

## Guidance is unchanged, and that is now the entire thesis

This is the part the market may be mispricing.

| Guidance (consolidated) | FY26 actual | FY27 projection | FY28 target |
|---|---|---|---|
| Total income | ₹1,100 Cr | ₹1,400–1,500 Cr (+30–40%) | ₹1,800–1,900 Cr |
| EBITDA | ₹429 Cr | ₹525–575 Cr (+20–30%) | ₹650–700 Cr |
| Blended ROCE | 16% | 16.25–16.5% | 16.5–17.0% |
| D/E ceiling | 0.47x | 0.72x | 0.76–0.80x |

Management reiterated rather than cut this guidance on the Q1 call, after a quarter that missed on margin. That is the single most bullish fact available and it deserves to be stated plainly.

Now do the arithmetic on what it requires, because nobody on the call did.

FY26 EBITDA was ₹429 Cr: ₹188 Cr in H1 and ₹241 Cr in H2 on the company's quarterly EBITDA prints (Q1 ₹107 Cr, Q4 ₹143 Cr). Guidance of ₹550 Cr at the mid-point, less the ₹139 Cr already delivered in Q1, leaves **₹411 Cr for Q2, Q3 and Q4 — roughly 1.7x the ₹241 Cr those three quarters produced last year.**

That is not impossible. The ₹560 Cr of capex lands between Q3 and Q4 and starts generating revenue, Q4 is always the strongest quarter by a wide margin (₹143 Cr against ₹85 Cr in the preceding Q3), and utilisation is running at 86% in both India and the GCC against 79% for FY26. But it is a demanding second half, and it is the specific thing Q2 results on the November deadline have to start validating.

## Verdict: WAIT — the discount is real, the floor is not

**What is genuinely better than in June.** The price. 13.9x trailing and roughly 11x guidance-implied FY27 versus 19.3x four months ago. The order book is 19% larger, the Gulf business has crossed into cumulative EBITDA positive, utilisation is 86% in both geographies, guidance has been reiterated rather than cut, and Q1 revenue growth of 39% is the best the company has ever printed.

**What is worse.** The margin. 33% blended OPM is a three-year low, the core crane business is at 47% against 53%, receivables at 116 days are 24 days worse than FY26's 92-day debtor position, and 86% of the FY27 capex is still unspent while free cash flow is negative. The technical picture is a clean downtrend below every moving average with no base, in a market where small-caps are being de-leveraged.

**The tension, stated plainly.** At 11x forward you are being paid to wait for a margin normalisation that management says is already underway but has not yet shown up in a printed quarter. If Q2 confirms 49% core margins and DSO below 110, the stock is materially undervalued and the re-rating could be violent, because there is no analyst coverage to smooth it. If Q2 confirms 45–46%, then the mix shift is structural, 11x is not cheap for a 35%-margin rental business, and ₹270–285 becomes the level.

**For a long-term holder already in the position:** the thesis is intact and this is not a sell on fundamentals. The measured fall is a multiple compressing toward a lower-margin business, and the business is still growing 30%+.

**For a new entry, including a swing trade:** wait. The trigger is not the price, it is the print.
