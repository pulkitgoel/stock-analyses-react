## Business Overview

Azad Engineering is a global Tier-1 supplier of highly-engineered, mission and life-critical precision forged and machined components for **Aerospace & Defence**, **Energy** (gas, steam and nuclear turbines), and **Oil & Gas**. Headquartered in Hyderabad and incorporated in 1983.

**Customers:** 6 major aerospace OEMs (GE, Honeywell, Rolls-Royce, Pratt & Whitney, Eaton, Safran) and 5 of the world's largest turbine manufacturers (GE Vernova, Siemens Energy, Mitsubishi Heavy Industries, Baker Hughes, MAN Energy Solutions).

**Key stats:**
- Around 88-93% export revenue
- Portfolio of 1,700+ qualified parts across 45+ manufacturing processes
- Tier-1 supplier status for critical engine components
- Four dedicated lean manufacturing facilities commissioned in FY26; eight planned in total

## Q1 FY27 in Detail (Quarter Ended June 2026)

Reported 7 August 2026, earnings call 8 August 2026.

**Standalone** — revenue ₹170.5 Cr, up 26.8% year on year and 8.3% sequentially. EBITDA ₹64.07 Cr, up 32.1%, margin 37.6%. PAT ₹36.35 Cr, up 21.2%, EPS ₹5.63.

**Consolidated** — revenue ₹172.6 Cr, up 25.9%. EBITDA ₹64.4 Cr, margin 37.3%. PAT ₹35.2 Cr, up 19.5%.

Two items sit underneath that revenue beat:

- **Profit before tax actually fell year on year**, to ₹40.1 Cr from ₹42.4 Cr. Other income collapsed to ₹4.0 Cr from ₹9.1 Cr on forex normalisation, depreciation rose to ₹17.9 Cr from ₹9.6 Cr, and finance cost rose to ₹10.1 Cr from ₹5.6 Cr.
- **Segment mix is unchanged.** Energy and Oil & Gas contributed ₹138.4 Cr, or 81.2% of revenue, up 26.7%. Aerospace and Defence contributed ₹28.7 Cr, up 24.7%. Exports were 88% of revenue, up 21.2%, while domestic revenue more than doubled to ₹20.5 Cr.

**Guidance from the call:** long-term revenue growth of 25%+ reiterated. EBITDA margin guided at 32-35%. Substantive contribution from the four new dedicated plants expected only from H2 FY27, with the "major breakthrough" in Q3-Q4 FY27. Working capital around 200 days in H1 FY27 easing to 160-180 days by H2. Debtor days of 170-180 targeted down to about 90 through bill discounting. A full natural hedge of export receivables by Q4 FY27.

One detail in that guidance deserves emphasis. Bill discounting converts receivables to cash, so it improves the cash conversion cycle — but the discounting charge sits in finance cost. **Cash improves before profit does.** Anyone expecting the working capital fix to show up as a profit improvement will be disappointed.

No quarterly cash-flow statement is published in India, and neither the Q1 FY27 filing nor the August investor presentation discloses one, so **FY26 remains the latest hard evidence** and it is negative.

**Working capital, from the credit rating agency's own numbers:**

- CARE Ratings (press release, 22 June 2026) put the FY26 working capital cycle at **344 days, up from 287 days in FY25**.
- Inventory holding: **262 days**, up from 209.
- Collection period: **163 days**, up from 157.
- Creditor period: 80 days, against 79.
- On Screener's consolidated data, inventory days went from 1,085 to **2,067**, and the cash conversion cycle from 804 to **1,720 days**.

**Supporting evidence:**

- Q1 FY27 finance cost rose about 80% year on year to ₹10.1 Cr — the price of funding the cycle.
- ICICI Securities (29 September) models FY27E operating cash flow at +₹106 Cr but free cash flow still at **-₹119 Cr**. It expects the operating turn only in FY27, and it has not been reported.
- The roughly **₹100 Cr of accumulated GST credit** was reiterated on the call as realisable in H1 FY28 — unchanged, and not accelerated.

The June analysis named working capital normalisation as the single factor the entire thesis rested on. Three months later, no published statement shows it turning. The turnaround remains a promise, now pushed further out, and it is being financed with debt that carries a visible P&L cost.

## What Went Right

Being fair to the company, several things improved, and two are strategically significant.

**Two more dedicated facilities for GE Vernova (28 September 2026).** Azad inaugurated two 7,600 sqm lean manufacturing facilities for GE Vernova's Gas Power business at Tunikibollaram, taking GE Vernova to three dedicated plants and making it the company's largest single-customer footprint. One caution: these were built against the **existing** contract book and the filing discloses no new order value or volume commitment. It is capacity, not a new order.

**India's first indigenous expendable turbojet engine delivered to DRDO and the Ministry of Defence (22 July 2026).** A milestone under the GTRE programme. Management is explicit that the programme is not part of the 25% revenue guidance, that volumes remain undisclosed pending DRDO weapon-integration testing, and that the first five to six engines are expected within three to four months. Strategically valuable, financially unquantified.

Also positive:

- **EBITDA margin of 37.6% in Q1 FY27**, at or above the top of the guided 32-35% band.
- **CARE Ratings reaffirmed CARE A / A2+ with a stable outlook** on 22 June 2026, while enhancing limits — long-term bank facilities raised to ₹336.55 Cr from ₹208.32 Cr.
- **Analyst sentiment reversed.** ICICI Securities upgraded from ADD to BUY and raised its target to ₹3,200 from ₹2,610 on 29 September; Goldman Sachs remains BUY at ₹3,315. Eight of nine covering analysts are positive.
- **The technical picture flipped.** In June the price sat below all short-term moving averages. It now trades above every major average from the 10-EMA to the 200-SMA.

**Even the bull case does not de-rate the stock.** At 30% revenue growth and a 38% margin — above the guided band — FY27 EPS is ₹22.33, which is 130x the current price.

The reason operating leverage is not reaching shareholders: depreciation has climbed from ₹53 Cr toward ₹80 Cr as four plants capitalise; finance cost is running at ₹10.1 Cr a quarter and up 80% year on year; and other income, which contributed ₹46 Cr in FY26, collapsed to ₹4 Cr in Q1 FY27 on forex normalisation. Those three lines absorb almost the entire EBITDA increase.

### What the Bull Target Actually Requires

ICICI Securities raised its target to **₹3,200** on 29 September, valued at **55x FY29E EPS of ₹58.3**. Reverse-engineer that number:

- FY29E EPS of ₹58.3 implies net profit of roughly **₹380 Cr**
- Which implies PBT of roughly ₹506 Cr
- On ICICI's own 33% revenue CAGR, FY29 revenue is about **₹1,419 Cr**
- Which implies an EBITDA margin near **48%**

Management guides EBITDA margins at **32-35%**. The bull case is not a growth case — it is a margin case requiring roughly 13 percentage points of expansion beyond anything the company has committed to.

At the current price, the target implies **+10.0%** and Goldman's ₹3,315 implies **+13.9%** — for which the holder must underwrite either execution of that margin expansion or continued multiple expansion. Our June note made the same point about ICICI's then-target of ₹2,060 (60x FY28E); the target moved up, the reasoning did not change.

## Final Verdict: CAUTION — PRICED FOR PERFECTION

**Rating change: Favorable with Caution (June) → Caution.**

Azad Engineering is a genuinely good business executing a hard plan. Revenue grew 26.8% in Q1 FY27 on the guided trajectory, the EBITDA margin is above the top of guidance, the turbojet delivery to DRDO is a strategic milestone, and GE Vernova has just taken a third dedicated facility. The balance sheet is not stretched, promoter holding is stable and unpledged, and CARE reaffirmed its rating with enhanced limits.

None of that is the problem. The problem is what the market now pays for it:

- **135x trailing earnings on a TTM PBT that is lower than the last full year's**
- **A 130x forward multiple even if FY27 delivers 30% revenue growth and a 38% margin**
- **Institutions reducing while retail absorbs** — aggregate institutional holding fell 2.84 percentage points
- **Working capital still deteriorating**, with the operating cash turn now expected in FY27 rather than already delivered
- **The rally to the all-time high came on the weakest delivery of the period**
- **A bull target requiring a 48% EBITDA margin**, 13 points above the guided ceiling

Where this note was wrong, stated plainly: the entry zone of ₹1,800-1,900 published on 27 June was never revisited. We expected a pullback to support that never came; the stock went the other way and added 44%. Our caution on the 98x multiple was right about valuation and wrong about near-term price, because the market was willing to pay more for the same earnings. Readers who ignored the entry zone and held were right, and we should say so.

Where this note was right: the working capital deterioration flagged as the swing factor has not reversed. Every subsequent hard data point — CARE's 344-day cycle, 2,067 inventory days, the ₹119 Cr negative operating cash flow, the 80% jump in finance cost — reinforces it.

### Suitable For

- Investors with high risk appetite and a 3-5 year horizon
- Not suitable for anyone expecting a margin of safety at this price

### For Existing Holders

### For Anyone Looking to Buy

Either a pullback into the **₹2,636-2,716** support cluster, or Q2 FY27 results showing debtor days genuinely falling toward the guided 90, would improve the risk-reward materially.
