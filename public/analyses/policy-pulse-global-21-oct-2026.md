## Oil Is Back Near $110 and the Waiver Is Doing the Work

**Brent traded at $109.64, up 0.78%, and WTI at $98.58** after a session in which Brent settled at **$108.79** having touched **$111.99**, a five-month high. The bid is geopolitical: reports of **attacks on vessels and on Saudi oil infrastructure** have revived Red Sea and Gulf supply fears.

The offset is policy. Trump's **October 16 executive order waived Sections 112 and 113 duties under the Graham Act** — the up-to-500% tariff on Russian goods and the up-to-100% tariff on the five largest buyers of Russian crude — and OFAC's **General License 135 authorises transactions related to imports of Russian diesel into the US until April 7, 2027**. Zelensky called the waiver "wrong." The market's reaction was to shave about 2% off Brent before the shipping attacks put it back.

Two-way, and unresolved. A signed Ukraine peace framework re-prices this violently lower; another tanker strike re-prices it higher.

## Europe's Carmakers Got Their 2035 Reprieve

Three mainstream party groups in the European Parliament — EPP, S&D and Renew — reached a deal to soften the 2035 combustion-engine phase-out. Instead of a **100% CO2 cut**, carmakers would need an **87.5% reduction by 2035 against 2021 levels, with a further 7.5% top-up from production using low-carbon steel made in the EU**. Vans get **80%**. The 2030 targets drop to **55% for cars and 35% for vans** from 40%, **2029-31 averaging** is permitted, renewable fuels count toward compliance as zero-emission, and **super-credits** apply to small, affordable and efficient EVs. The deal still needs group sign-off and then Council agreement.

The low-carbon-steel top-up is the clause nobody is pricing: it converts a vehicle-emissions rule into a European steel demand mandate.
