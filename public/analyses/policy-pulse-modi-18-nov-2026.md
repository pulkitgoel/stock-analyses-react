The Prime Minister flew home on Tuesday night with nine announcements from the ASEAN-India Summit, four from the East Asia Summit, and three signed documents. One of those documents matters for Indian equities. None of them name a company.

## Two minerals frameworks in forty-eight hours

In Manila on Tuesday, Trump announced that the United States, Australia, India and Japan had agreed several new deals on critical minerals and rare earths, framed as insurance against "coercion by any single foreign supplier." He did not name China. He said the US would catch China in rare-earth production "within 10 months."

There is no published text, no tonnage, no offtake and no price floor. InsideTrade reported that he offered no details. What exists institutionally is a first Quad Business Leaders' Forum scheduled for 2027, joint military exercises, and Quad export credit agencies meeting in Tokyo today. That sits inside a framework agreed in May 2026: a $20 billion government-and-private mobilisation target, the Quad Critical Minerals Framework, a separate India-US framework on critical minerals and rare earths, and $10 million for recycling announced on 31 October.

The same week, the ASEAN-India Summit produced a formal line item of its own: "ASEAN-India cooperation on mining and critical minerals," listed among nine outcomes in the Prime Minister's Office release. No value, no counterparty, no timeline.

That is two frameworks in forty-eight hours and zero tonnes. The Indian Express put the strategic problem better than any official document: the harder task is building processing capacity, and much of that capability lies in Southeast Asia. Beijing has extended its pause on wider rare-earth controls by only two months, to 10 January. The IEA estimates that, fully applied, those controls would put $6.5 trillion a year of downstream production outside China at risk. A pause renewed every two months is a truce in which both sides buy time.

## What India actually took home

The full list, because the shape of it is the story — it is almost entirely capacity-building rather than commerce:

At the 23rd ASEAN-India Summit: a long-term ASEAN-India Maritime Cooperation Plan covering connectivity, scientific research, disaster management, Coast Guard cooperation, maritime domain awareness and seafarers' security; a Joint Cyber Forensics Training Programme; ASEAN-India Nuclear Energy Technical Exchanges; an ASEAN-India Space Hackathon; cooperation on mining and critical minerals; a youth summit plus young-parliamentarian exchanges and start-up hackathons; expanded scholarships for ASEAN students at Nalanda University; an ASEAN-India Creative Partnership Pavilion at WAVES 2027; and free e-visas for tourists from ASEAN countries.

At the 21st East Asia Summit: a Seafarers' Safety Initiative for joint training and emergency response; an EAS Cyber Crime conference hosted at the National Forensic Science University; an EAS Maritime Heritage Festival at Lothal; and an EAS University Conclave at Nalanda.

The three documents signed were the ASEAN-India Joint Statement on Cooperation in Disaster Management, the EAS Leaders' Statement on Strengthening Cooperation Against Online Scams and Related Offences, and the EAS Declaration on Navigating our Future Together.

Two-way trade with ASEAN was $128.37 billion. India's exports to the bloc rose 58% in April-September against 21.96% growth in total merchandise exports for April-October — a genuine outperformance, and the strongest argument for the AITIGA review that India actually wants. But nothing announced this week changes a tariff line, an offtake contract or a factory.

## What the PIB does not say

Neither the PMO outcomes release nor the ASEAN statements name a single Indian company. Every stock mapping below is my inference from historical vendor relationships and sector position, not from the text. Read the confidence scores accordingly.

The listed Indian exposure to a critical-minerals framework is real but narrow, and it is upstream. NMDC and MOIL are the iron-ore and manganese-ore miners; GMDCLTD holds lignite and bauxite; HINDCOPPER is the only listed copper producer of scale; MIDHANI melts and forges the specialty alloys and titanium that defence and aerospace supply chains need. None of them is a separation or refining business, which is the actual chokepoint — that capacity is Chinese, and the Indian answer to it is largely unlisted or state-owned outside the exchanges.

On the maritime and shipbuilding side, the Cooperation Plan and the Seafarers' Safety Initiative are the most substantive items on the list, because India has listed shipyards that can physically execute them and a seafaring workforce that is one of the world's largest. COCHINSHIP, MAZDOCK and GRSE build; SCI and GESHIP own and operate; the seafarer training and certification pipeline runs through the marine institutes rather than the listed universe.

The nuclear technical exchanges and the space hackathon are the weakest links to a ticker. Nuclear technical exchange building with ASEAN does not move NPCIL-adjacent listed names in any measurable way, and a hackathon is not a procurement.

## The tape disagreed, and it usually does

Sensex closed at 82,031.81, down 500.38 points or 0.61%, and the Nifty at 25,227.95, down 159.45 or 0.63%, ending a two-day rally. The Nifty Metal index fell 2.5% and the Nifty Consumer Durables index 3%, the two worst sectors.

Hindalco lost 2.99% to ₹900.00. National Aluminium and SAIL were among the weakest midcap names, SAIL down 3.64%. So the day a critical-minerals framework was announced in Manila and a mining cooperation line was published in Delhi, Indian metals fell two and a half per cent — on global commodity weakness, a firmer dollar and rising expectations of a Federal Reserve hike at the December FOMC.

This is worth stating plainly because it is the recurring lesson of every minerals announcement: the policy headline and the sector tape are not correlated at a one-day horizon. The framework is a two-to-five-year story about processing capacity. The metal index is a same-day story about the dollar. Anyone who bought Hindalco on the Quad communiqué lost money by the close.

Breadth was decisively negative: 2,206 declines against 969 advances on the NSE, with 109 unchanged. Midcaps fell harder than the benchmark, the Nifty Midcap 100 down 1.22% and the Smallcap 100 down 0.35%. The India VIX rose about 1% to near 16.

## What to watch

- **Today in Tokyo** — Quad export credit agencies meet. This is the first venue where the Manila minerals announcement could acquire loan terms, project names or offtake volumes. Without one of those, the framework remains a statement.
- **10 January 2027** — China's rare-earth export-control pause expires, one day before the US-China tariff truce. The two-month renewal pattern is the signal to watch, not the communiqué.
- **The AITIGA review** — the actual Indian ask with ASEAN, and the reason the 58% export growth number matters more than any of the nine announcements.
- **HAL's AMCA decision**, expected within roughly a month per the company's own 16 November statement, with bids received from all three shortlisted bidders.
- **Domestic processing capacity** — the missing piece in every Indian critical-minerals announcement. Until a separation or refining plant with a listed owner is sanctioned, the theme is upstream-only.
