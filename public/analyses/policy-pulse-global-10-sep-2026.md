## ECB Hikes for the Second Time in 2026 as Middle East Energy Costs Keep Inflation Above Target

The European Central Bank **raised all three key rates by 25bp on September 10**, effective September 16: the **deposit facility to 2.50%**, the main refinancing rate to **2.65%** and the marginal lending facility to **2.90%**. This is the **second hike of 2026** (after June, with a deliberate pause in July) and it takes the deposit rate to the top of the ECB's estimated neutral range.

The ECB's own words are explicit about the cause: "the conflict in the Middle East continues to generate inflation pressures, and inflation is set to remain well above target for an extended period." Euro-area headline inflation was **3.3% in August** — roughly two-thirds above target. Staff projections put **2026 inflation at 3.0%, 2027 at 2.5% and 2028 at 2.1%**, meaning a return to 2% is a late-decade story; growth projections were raised slightly to **0.9% for 2026** (from 0.8%). Lagarde kept the guidance data-dependent and meeting-by-meeting.

## Brent Holds Above USD 100 as Tanker Attacks Deepen Supply Fear

Brent **broke back above USD 100 on September 9 for the first time since July** — a 3.4% jump to settle at **USD 101.21** — and extended to **USD 101.34-102.37 on September 10**, with WTI at USD 96.55-97.49. This is the **largest attack on shipping since the six-month conflict began**:

- The **US sank five Iranian oil tankers** on September 8-9 (Kharg Island and the Gulf of Oman) after Iranian missile attacks on a US warship.
- **Iran said it attacked 10 vessels** near the Strait of Hormuz and its Revolutionary Guard warned of escalation; Tehran also **expanded a no-go zone outside the strait**.
- **Hormuz oil flows have collapsed to below 2 million barrels a day**, from 8-9 mbpd before fighting resumed on August 30 — the strait normally carries about a fifth of global oil and gas.
- The **EIA raised its 2026 and 2027 oil price forecasts** as global stockpiles drain; **US diesel hit an all-time high** and gasoline averages above USD 4.20/gallon.
- Brent is now **more than 40% above the early-July low near USD 72**.

## The Political Overhang: Trump Says the War Ends After the November Midterms

President Trump said at the Republican convention that he believes the Iran war will end **immediately after the November 3 midterm elections**, and that US oil and gasoline prices will not fall before then. For a market that has repeatedly tried to price a de-escalation (the August 3-6 window), this resets the base case: **the conflict runs into November, and the crude risk premium is a fourth-quarter feature, not a fading one**. Two-way tracking still applies — only a signed deal re-triggers the sell-energy/buy-airlines trade — but the political calendar has pushed that back.

## Japan Risk Factor: A BoJ Hike and the Yen Carry Trade

Indian market commentary flagged a second-order global risk: **expectations of a Bank of Japan rate hike (BoJ meets September 17-18) and a stronger yen** are reviving concern about a **yen carry-trade unwind**, which would pull capital out of emerging markets precisely as global bond yields rise. Combined with the ECB and a hawkish Fed, the synchronized-tightening narrative is the main structural headwind for EM flows into the fourth quarter.
