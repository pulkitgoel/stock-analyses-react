## 2. What Kernex Actually Does

Kernex Microsystems was incorporated in 1991 and has spent most of its listed life (since 2005) making railway safety electronics from a 10-acre, 265,000 sq ft campus at Hardware Park in Hyderabad. It is an ISO 9001-certified 100% export-oriented unit with roughly 740 staff across R&D, engineering, verification and validation, production and projects.

Three product lines matter:

- **Kavach / TCAS** (Train Collision Avoidance System) — both on-board locomotive equipment and trackside installation, including the station-level infrastructure
- **Automatic block signalling and level-crossing control** — the older core business
- **EPC works** for related railway infrastructure

It is not a pure Kavach play. But Kavach is what the earnings now depend on, and the company's own investor material says so. Its earlier anti-collision device was discontinued in 2017 when Indian Railways moved to the TCAS standard, and the long product-approval cycle that followed is why revenue stayed near ₹1–9 Cr a quarter until FY24 and why the company lost money through FY22.

**The structural point for valuation:** this is a single-customer business. Indian Railways production units — Chittaranjan Locomotive Works, Banaras Locomotive Works, Patiala Locomotive Works, the Integral Coach Factory — award the orders, set the specifications and pay the bills. There is no diversification story here, only an execution story.

The margin is the part that surprised the market. Small numbers against a ₹504 Cr quarter, but they are the kind of item that should be read alongside the 268-day receivable figure in section 7.

## 4. The Order Book: Big, Shrinking, and 2028-Shaped

This is where the bear case lives, and it is arithmetic rather than opinion.

| Date | Order book (incl. GST) |
|---|---|
| 29 May 2026 | ₹4,150 Cr |
| 13 Aug 2026 (Q1 FY27) | **₹3,641 Cr** |
| Change | **-₹509 Cr (-12%)** |

The company has won ₹5,174 Cr of orders in total and executed ₹1,687 Cr, leaving the ₹3,487 Cr balance shown in its September presentation. A further ₹2,100 Cr of that is scheduled for FY27 execution — **4.9x FY26 revenue** — which is why the growth is not in doubt for the next four quarters.

But a shrinking order book during a quarter when revenue multiplied eightfold is a signal, not a glitch. Three things explain it:

- **Execution is running ahead of intake.** That is a good problem in the short term and a bad one in the medium term.
- **The big orders are already won.** The CLW contract for 3,024 locomotives was awarded in December 2024 at ₹2,041 Cr, and the largest incremental wins since have been small — ₹475 Cr in May 2026, ₹66.62 Cr from ICF in August 2026.
- **The tender pipeline is not converting at the rate the market assumed.** Kavach is being rolled out across roughly 12,000 remaining locomotives, but awards are lumpy and competitive, and the three approved vendors bid against each other.

**The 2028 question.** HBL Engineering — the largest listed Kavach supplier, and the closest comparable — has told its own shareholders that Kavach sales should be steady at ₹1,300–1,500 Cr a year through FY26-FY28 and then **dip**. Its FY25 annual report says so explicitly, and its management repeated it at the 2025 AGM. Kernex has not given a similar warning, but it sells into the same programme, to the same customer, on the same equipment cycle. If HBL's read is right, Kernex's FY27-FY28 earnings are a peak, not a base.

That is precisely why a stock growing at 800% trades at 15x.

## 5. Execution: The CLW Clock

The CLW order is the ballgame: 3,024 locomotives, of which **1,360 units (45%) had been supplied as of 13 August 2026**, with 175 ready for dispatch and 1,025 "almost completed". The company guides the remaining dispatches to be finished by **November 2026**, with installation continuing into **February 2027**.

Three implications:

- **Q2 and Q3 FY27 will carry the bulk of the remaining CLW revenue.** If dispatch completes by November, the revenue recognition tail runs into Q4 and the installation revenue into Q1 FY28.
- **The FY27 revenue number is therefore front-loaded and knowable.** ₹2,100 Cr of in-hand FY27 order book against a company that has just demonstrated ₹504 Cr in a quarter is not a stretch — it is roughly four more quarters like Q1.
- **What happens after February 2027 is the open question.** With ₹3,641 Cr of order book and ₹1,541 Cr of it beyond FY27, the company needs fresh awards in FY27-FY28 to keep the run-rate alive. That is what the market is watching, and it is why the stock fell on a quarter that beat everything.

One more asset worth noting: every locomotive fitted generates an **annual maintenance contract** — the company's own framing is roughly 3% of contract value a year for 11 years. On 3,024 locomotives that is a meaningful annuity layer, but it starts small and compounds slowly; it will not fill a post-2028 hole on its own.

## 6. Margins, Warranty, and Earnings Quality

The 32.3% operating margin invites the obvious question: is it real, and does it last?

Evidence for:
- The mix has shifted to on-board kits, which carry more software and less civil work
- Fixed costs (740 staff, 265,000 sq ft, in-house test labs) are now spread over 8x the revenue
- The company has pricing power in the short term because only three vendors are approved and demand is mandated

Evidence against:
- The ₹30 Cr warranty provision is a direct charge against future margin
- Railway EPC pricing is tender-driven; the next round of awards will be bid against two competitors who can see the same margin
- The CARE report flags "execution and technology risk" and the "tender-based nature of business" as rating constraints, not footnotes

**The earnings-quality note:** Q1's PAT of ₹110 Cr was earned with ₹13 Cr of interest cost, up from ₹3 Cr a year earlier. That is the cost of financing a working-capital-heavy model. Profit is real, but it is being funded by debt, and the debt is growing faster than equity.

## 7. Cash: 268 Receivable Days and a Tripled Borrowing Line

This is the least comfortable part of the story, and it is why the P/E of 15 should not be taken at face value.

| Balance sheet item (consolidated) | Mar 2025 | Mar 2026 |
|---|---|---|
| Borrowings | ₹40 Cr | **₹162 Cr** |
| Other liabilities | ₹38 Cr | **₹509 Cr** |
| Total assets | ₹236 Cr | **₹919 Cr** |
| Reserves | ₹141 Cr | ₹231 Cr |

**Receivable days: 268.** For a business selling to Indian Railways, long receivables are normal — but 268 days means three-quarters of a year of revenue is sitting in the working-capital cycle. Against FY26 revenue of ₹431 Cr, that is roughly ₹316 Cr tied up. The company has sanctioned working-capital limits of ₹490 Cr, with ₹281 Cr undrawn and a further ₹165 Cr under enhancement.

**Why it matters for the equity:** a business that earns ₹110 Cr in a quarter but needs ₹300+ Cr of working capital to do it is not self-funding at this growth rate. Every incremental rupee of revenue consumes cash before it produces it. That is sustainable while Indian Railways pays and the banks renew limits — the CARE upgrade says they will — but it caps how much of the profit can ever reach shareholders, and it is the reason there is no dividend.

**The cash-conversion test to watch in Q2:** operating cash flow against PAT. If Q2 shows ₹100+ Cr of profit and negative operating cash flow, the model is consuming capital faster than it earns it.

Two things in that table matter more than the rating itself. First, **the rated facilities were tripled** — ₹175 Cr to ₹525 Cr — which is the bank's acknowledgement that the working-capital requirement has structurally changed, not temporarily spiked. Second, CARE's negative sensitivities are explicit: **working capital cycle beyond 150 days**, or **revenue/profit declining more than 30%**. The company is currently at 268 receivable days, well past the first threshold, and the second is the FY28 risk in rating-agency language.

Being one notch above investment grade with a stable outlook is a fair description of this business today: credible, growing, and dependent on a single customer's payment cycle.

## 11. What the Market Is Pricing

The stock's three-month performance (-18.3%) against its six-month (+46.4%) tells the story: the market re-rated it into the Q1 result and then de-rated it out of it, despite the numbers. Three things did that:

1. **Order book degrowth (-12% QoQ)** on a quarter that otherwise beat everything.
2. **HBL's explicit guidance** that Kavach revenue peaks around FY28 and dips thereafter — read across to the smaller, more concentrated player.
3. **The warranty provision**, which says the installed base is not yet fully proven in the field.

None of those is a governance event. All three are about the durability of the earnings, which is exactly what a 15x multiple is debating.

## 12. Risks

- **Customer concentration.** Indian Railways production units are effectively the entire customer base.
- **Post-FY28 cliff.** If Kavach ordering peaks, revenue normalises sharply and the trailing P/E of 15 becomes a forward P/E of 15 on a shrinking base.
- **Working capital.** 268 receivable days, ₹162 Cr of borrowings and ₹509 Cr of other liabilities. Growth consumes cash.
- **Execution risk.** The CLW installation tail runs to February 2027; slippage moves revenue between quarters and can trigger penalties.
- **Low promoter holding (28.73%).** No controlling bloc, no pledge, but limited skin in the game relative to peers.
- **Thin liquidity.** ₹31 Cr average daily turnover and a 1.68 crore share count; exits are harder than entries.
- **Competition.** Three approved vendors bidding for the same tenders; the 32% margin is not protected by a moat, only by an approval.
- **Warranty.** ₹30 Cr provisioned in one quarter; if field failures rise, so does the provision.
