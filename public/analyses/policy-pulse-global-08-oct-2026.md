## Brent Above USD 104 as the Pentagon Is Told to Prepare for a Possible Resumption of Strikes on Iran

**Brent crude rose 4.25% to USD 104.5 a barrel on Thursday, October 8** — live trackers showed USD 104.73 and, on a contract-for-difference basis, USD 105.58 — as two supply shocks converged.

- **Axios reported on October 8 that the Pentagon instructed US Central Command several days ago to conclude preparations for a possible resumption of major combat operations against Iran**, with officials saying renewed strikes could target nuclear and infrastructure sites. Trump is weighing timing, and the reporting points to a decision window set against the **November midterm elections**. Iran has said it is preparing a broader and more forceful response if large-scale attacks resume.
- **Tropical Storm Isaias forced Gulf of Mexico producers to halt more than 5.10 lakh barrels per day of crude output** — approximately **one-fourth of the region's production**.
- Attacks on commercial vessels in the Strait of Hormuz continued, and the **Saudi-Houthi war intensified**, with Houthi strikes having damaged two Saudi airports this month. Middle Eastern exports have gradually recovered towards pre-war levels, which is why the price has so far been contained rather than spiking.
- On Indian exchanges, **MCX crude closed at ₹8,990, up ₹438**.

## India Joined the US-Led 15-Country Push Against "Excess Capacity" — While a Section 301 Probe Into India Is Live

**India signed a joint ministerial statement with the United States and 13 other economies on structural excess manufacturing capacity**, committing to **meet at the technical level before December 2026** to develop terms of reference and share non-confidential data. The initiative covers **automobiles and electric vehicles, batteries, chemicals, semiconductors and solar panels**.

- Participating economies include **Argentina, Australia, Canada, the European Union** and others, alongside the US and India.
- **The timing is awkward: the USTR launched a Section 301 investigation into structural excess manufacturing capacity across 16 economies on March 11, 2026 — and India is one of them.** Any US remedies arising from those investigations follow a separate process. India is, in effect, an ally and a target of the same investigation.
- The joint statement is a commitment to share data and develop a framework. It imposes **no tariff, no cap and no immediate obligation**.
