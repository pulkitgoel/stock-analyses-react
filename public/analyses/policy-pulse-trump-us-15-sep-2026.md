## Trump Rejects the AI Slowdown — "Whoever Wins AI, Wins"

President Trump spent Monday firing off at least five Truth Social posts on artificial intelligence, and the message was unambiguous: **no slowdown, no new guardrails, keep building.**

The trigger was Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei's weekend essay, *"We Must Pace the Frontier,"* which called on frontier labs to slow model development because capabilities are improving faster than anyone can understand or control them. OpenAI's Sam Altman agreed, Elon Musk replied *"Dario is right,"* Microsoft's Satya Nadella wrote in support of "deliberate pacing," and Google DeepMind chair Demis Hassabis said the essay "points towards the right path forward."

Trump's response, per CNBC and AP:

- *"The only control or 'guardrails' that AI needs is a STRONG AND SMART (High IQ!) PRESIDENT, and the U.S.A. has that, in spades!"*
- *"AI taking over the World, destroying Humanity, and all other things bad, is a HOAX,"* comparing the warnings to climate change — he called himself the "Hoax Buster."
- *"There is a SICK conspiracy going on against AI and Data Centers, and the only one that is happy about it is China."*
- *"We're leading China in AI, and, frankly, I want to keep it that way, because whoever wins AI wins."*
- *"We already have tremendous CRIMINAL and REGULATORY power over these companies!"*

House Speaker Mike Johnson echoed the position, warning that an emergency congressional session on AI regulation would lose the race to China. Trump also phoned **Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang live during the All-In Summit on September 14** — a public CEO-level alignment with the AI-infrastructure buildout.

Beijing's response matters for the trade. China's Foreign Ministry said "engaging in confrontation and malicious competition" on AI is "not in the interests of any party," and state-run Global Times called Amodei's proposal a "Cold War playbook." So the two governments that matter most for AI policy **both rejected the slowdown** — from opposite motives. A **Trump-Xi meeting is scheduled for September 24, with AI governance expected to feature**, but Trump's dismissal of catastrophic risk makes an agreed pacing framework unlikely.

For markets, the near-term signal is a **capex licence to keep spending**. The AI-infrastructure complex sold off hard on Monday *before* Trump's posts — Nvidia -3%, Marvell -7.6%, Micron -5%, Lam Research -8%, Corning -9.2% on a USD 2 billion equity raise, AMD and Intel -4% to -5% — but the policy read-through from a president who says "don't kill the Golden Goose" is that the buildout is not about to be regulated into a pause.

## Canada Section 338 Product Changes Take Effect Today — ATVs and Dairy In, Road Salt and Cement Out

The revised product lists under the **Section 338 Canada tariffs are effective September 15, 2026**, following the five proclamations Trump signed on September 8 in response to Canada's counter-tariffs on about C$27.6 billion (USD 20 billion) of US goods.

- **Added to the 50% duty list:** certain **all-terrain vehicles**, additional **dairy products**, **motorboats**, additional wood products and furniture.
- **Removed:** **rock salt** and **cement** — both exempted after lobbying by senators from northern border states (Senator Susan Collins said one Maine town faced USD 10,000 in extra road-salt costs and a ready-mix operator USD 150,000 per month).
- **Import bans** on designated Canadian **alcoholic beverages, dairy products and motor vehicles** take effect **September 29**.
- **Section 338 duties stack on top of Section 232** and give **no USMCA exemption** — the White House says combined rates reach **up to 100% on certain items**.
- Separately, **USTR and the GSA are removing Canadian-origin products from the GSA Multiple Award Schedules**, which administer more than USD 50 billion of annual federal procurement.

## CLARITY Act Test Vote Today — Crypto's 60-Vote Moment, With an Ethics Clause Aimed at the President

The Senate holds a **procedural (cloture) vote on the Digital Asset Market CLARITY Act today at 2:15pm ET**. It is not final passage: backers need **60 votes** to open debate, meaning **at least seven Democrats** with all 53 Republicans. The revised Republican text released over the weekend added an **ethics provision giving state attorneys general enforcement authority and requiring covered officials — including the President — to place digital-asset holdings in a qualified blind trust or divest**. Trump has called for CLARITY to pass; CFTC chair Selig calls it "plan A." Banks including **Bank of America and Citi have lobbied against it**, warning of deposit flight through stablecoin interest payments, while community banks want the stablecoin yield loophole closed.
