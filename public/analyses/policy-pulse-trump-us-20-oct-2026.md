## The October Skip Is Now Consensus, and December Is the Live Meeting

The most useful number published today was not a market print. It was a poll.

Reuters surveyed 100 economists between **October 16 and 20**, and **94 of them expect the FOMC to hold the federal funds rate at 3.75%-4.00%** when it meets on **October 27-28** — the meeting that lands six days before the midterms. That part is priced. The part that is not fully priced is the second line: **all 100 respondents now expect a rate hike in December**, against fewer than half saying so in last month's poll. **Roughly 60% see the peak at 4.00%-4.25%**, but **38 of 92 forecasters expect at least two further increases**, up from one-fifth in September.

The inflation arithmetic behind that shift is unforgiving. The poll's median has the Fed's preferred **PCE gauge more than 100 basis points above the 2% target over coming quarters and not back to target until at least 2028**. Inflation has now been above target for nearly six years. Third-quarter GDP is forecast at **3.2%**, slowing to 2.2% in October-December, with annual growth of 2.3% this year and 2.1% in 2027 — above the 2.0% the Fed considers non-inflationary.

Chair **Kevin Warsh** has given no guidance, and the recent messaging has whipsawed the curve: long-dated Treasury yields hit multi-decade highs on **October 7** on inflation and near-term hike expectations, then retreated nearly **25 basis points** as October hike odds collapsed.

Two quotes capture the split. **Alex Pelle of Mizuho**: "Them not going in October is the wrong call from a policy point of view. They'll go 25 in December, but the risk is greater they end up going 50 than they don't go at all." **Michael Gapen of Morgan Stanley**: "The risks are in the direction of a Fed that ultimately needs to hike rates more than we expect."

The political overlay matters for how tariffs get handled between now and November 3. Inflation has been driven by energy costs from the Iran conflict and by tariffs on imported goods, and that combination is the Republicans' weakest issue in an election being fought on affordability.

The transmission is already visible in housing, and today's data was bad. **US single-family housing starts plunged in September and building permits hit a 3-1/2-year low**; separately, **pending home sales tumbled to a record low** for the month. A 30-year mortgage priced off a 5%-plus 10-year Treasury does that.

## Homeland Security Orders DeepSeek Out of Civilian Agency IT

The **Department of Homeland Security** published a notice on the Federal Register on Monday ordering the removal of **DeepSeek** products, and of third-party services built using DeepSeek models, from **US civilian executive-branch agency IT systems**. It also bars those agencies from **acquiring** such products going forward. Department of Defense components are excluded from the order.

The rationale in the attached document is that DeepSeek's models "present multiple threat vectors" and that use by government employees or vendors "may result in the transfer of sensitive data" to China, whose legal system gives the government extensive authority over company infrastructure. Commerce and the Navy had already banned DeepSeek on government devices; this is the first civilian-wide removal-and-procurement order.

Beijing answered within a day. China's commerce ministry said on Tuesday that the order **"disregards important consensus" reached between Xi Jinping and Trump, "seriously hurts Chinese firms' interests"** and interferes with normal commercial cooperation, and urged Washington to "immediately correct wrong practices... and revoke relevant discriminatory restrictions."

This is the second concrete AI-decoupling act in a fortnight, and it lands with the **January 10, 2027** tariff-truce expiry ahead and a leaders' summit in Shenzhen expected next month. The practical read for equities is narrower than the headlines: it is a procurement signal, not a demand signal.

## Trump Eases Off Bombardier in Wichita

At a rally in Park City, Kansas on Monday night for Senator **Roger Marshall**, Trump walked back his September threat to bar **Bombardier** from the US market: "I'm going to be helping a certain air company get themselves straightened away... But now I find out they have a presence here, and we're not hurting them if they're in Kansas. So we'll take care of those people and the jobs."

The company employs **3,500 people in the US, more than 40% of them in Wichita**, which hosts Bombardier Group's US headquarters and Bombardier Defense; it announced a further **$50 million** Wichita investment last week. **Bombardier shares rose 2.1%** on Tuesday.

The context is the Canada file, which is the most legally fragile part of the tariff programme. Washington imposed duties on about **$20 billion of Canadian goods in August**, Canada retaliated in early September, and the US banned a broad swath of **Canadian alcoholic beverages, motorcycles and dairy products** from **September 29** — but took no action against Bombardier. The February threat to decertify Bombardier jets over a certification spat involving **Gulfstream** never materialised either.

## A Rare-Earth Plant in Rochester, and the November 10 Clock

New York Governor **Kathy Hochul** announced Tuesday that **Phoenix Tailings** will build a **$375 million**, 500,000-square-foot rare-earth processing facility at Rochester's Eastman Business Park, processing about **18,000 tons** of rare-earth material a year, with partial operations next year and full production in **2028**. The state is providing **$3 million** in performance-based tax credits. The Pentagon had already conditionally committed **$500 million** in long-term debt financing to the company for a US midstream plant.

The strategic point is timing. Most US rare-earth capacity does not come online until 2027 or later, and **China's export-control pause expires on November 10** — six weeks before the US-China truce deadline of **January 10, 2027**. Every plant announced in this window is a hedge against a date, not a response to a price.

Note the listed exposure is thin and early-stage: the plant is privately held, so the trade is in the processors and miners that re-rate on each announcement rather than in the project itself.
