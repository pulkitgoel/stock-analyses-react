## Trump Takes a 40 Percent Government Stake in a New Submarine Shipyard

President Trump and Secretary of War Pete Hegseth unveiled **"Arsenal-2"** at **Sparrows Point, Baltimore County, Maryland on Tuesday, October 6** — a new 2-million-square-foot shipyard on the former **Bethlehem Steel** site at Tradepoint Atlantic, to be built and operated by **Anduril Industries**.

- **Anduril is committing USD 3.7 billion of its own capital**; the **US Navy has awarded a contract worth up to USD 2.9 billion** for what the yard produces. Combined value: **USD 6.6 billion**.
- Trump announced that **"American taxpayers will receive a 40 percent stake in the new shipyard."** Per Anduril, the stake sits in a **new subsidiary created to operate Arsenal-2**, not in Anduril itself; the rights, obligations and return mechanics have not been published.
- **Maryland and Baltimore County have committed about USD 481 million** in matching support. The Department of War calls it the largest facility of its kind in the United States.
- Initial scope is **Virginia-class submarine components starting with torpedo tubes** — described by a senior administration official as a current **"gating component"** — before expanding to larger modules, hull sections and **autonomous underwater vessels**. Production is expected to begin **around 2030**.
- Jobs: **3,100 direct**, roughly **11,000 indirect** (one account says 14,000 total including indirect).
- Capacity maths: US yards deliver about **1.3 Virginia-class attack submarines a year** against a Navy goal of two, which it does not expect to reach until about **2032**. Arsenal-2 is expected to add roughly **9 million labour hours a year**, lifting Virginia-class production capacity by about **15%**. China accounts for **more than half of global commercial shipbuilding output**; the US, a fraction of 1%.

## The August Trade Deficit Was the Widest Since Liberation Day

The Census Bureau and BEA reported on **Tuesday, October 6** that the **US goods and services trade deficit widened to USD 105.6 billion in August**, up **USD 12.7 billion (13.7%)** from a revised **USD 92.8 billion** in July and **above the USD 102 billion consensus**. It is the widest monthly gap **since March 2025**, the month before the "Liberation Day" reciprocal tariff announcement.

- **Imports hit a record USD 420.8 billion (+4.3%)**; exports rose 1.4% to **USD 315.2 billion**.
- The goods deficit rose **USD 12.8 billion to USD 136.6 billion**; the services surplus was almost unchanged at **USD 31.0 billion**.
- The drivers were **industrial supplies (+USD 9.1 billion), with crude oil +USD 3.3 billion and nonmonetary gold +USD 3.1 billion**, and **capital goods (+USD 6.2 billion), led by semiconductors**.
- Largest bilateral goods deficits: **Mexico USD 27.7 billion, Vietnam USD 24.0 billion, Taiwan USD 18.3 billion, China USD 16.4 billion**; the **Canada gap widened USD 4.1 billion to USD 7.1 billion**.
- The scorecard caveat: the **cumulative deficit through August is still about 20% (USD 138.2 billion) narrower** than the same period of 2025, on USD 267.7 billion of export growth.

## Also Today: an IRS Safe Harbour for Staking, and a Congressional Report on Webull

**The IRS released Revenue Procedure 2026-20 on October 6**, setting out a safe harbour for trusts that stake digital assets without losing investment-trust or grantor-trust status. Existing trusts get **six months from October 6, 2026 (into April 2027)** to amend governing documents; funds must **distribute staking rewards within 60 days after each calendar quarter** and may hold a liquidity reserve. Losing grantor-trust status raises total federal tax on staking rewards by about **49% (from USD 275 to USD 411** in the worked example), with IRA holders unprotected.

Separately, a **congressional report alleged that investment platform Webull downplayed ties to the Chinese government** — a governance headline for the listed US brokerage complex.
