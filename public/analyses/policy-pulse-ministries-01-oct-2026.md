## The Windfall Tax Comes Down Again: Diesel to ₹16 and ATF to ₹10.50 from October 1

**The Finance Ministry cut the special additional excise duty on exported diesel and aviation turbine fuel for the second consecutive fortnight, effective October 1.** The notification, issued on Wednesday, September 30:

- **Diesel export levy: ₹16 per litre, down from ₹20.** This is the SAED together with the road and infrastructure cess.
- **ATF export levy: ₹10.50 per litre, down from ₹15.**
- **Petrol export levy: unchanged at ₹0.50 per litre.** It had already been cut from ₹1.50 to ₹0.50 in the September review.
- **Domestic fuel duties are untouched** — the ministry confirmed no change in the existing rates on petrol and diesel cleared for domestic consumption.

The path matters: the levies were imposed on **March 27, 2026** as the West Asia conflict began, extended to petrol from **May 16**, and have been revised every fortnight against average international prices. The September 16 revision took diesel from ₹25 to ₹20 and ATF from ₹19 to ₹15. So in a single fortnight the diesel levy has fallen **₹9 a litre** and the ATF levy **₹8.50** — a clean, quantifiable measure of how far product cracks have moved off their war peaks, and a direct margin transfer to refiners that export into the product market.

The irony is the timing: this cut landed on the morning that **Brent's December contract traded back above $100, up 2.1-2.5%, after China's refiners suspended most October fuel exports**, and with US–Iran diplomacy stalled. So the government is cutting the export levy on the day the global product market tightened again. That is the fortnightly formula working mechanically rather than a policy judgement — and it means the next revision, in mid-October, is the one that reflects the China export halt.

## GST Crosses ₹2 Lakh Crore for a Third Month, and Factories Post a Seven-Month High

**Two of the government's own high-frequency indicators printed strongly on October 1, and the market ignored both.**

- **Gross GST collections rose 14.7% year on year to ₹2,03,521 crore in September**, up from **₹1,77,365 crore** a year earlier — the **third consecutive month above the ₹2 lakh crore mark**. **Net collections rose 18.1% to more than ₹1.76 lakh crore.** The composition is the important qualifier: **domestic GST growth ran slower than the headline pace while import-linked collections grew faster**, which is a statement about trade volumes and import prices as much as about domestic demand.
- **The HSBC India Manufacturing PMI rose to 55.1 in September from 52.8 in August** — a **seven-month high**, though **revised down from the 55.7 preliminary reading**. Underneath it: faster growth in new orders and output, **hiring resumed at its fastest pace since May**, buying activity accelerated, **finished-goods inventories posted their second-largest increase in nearly 12 years**, and business confidence reached a **four-month high**. Pranjul Bhandari, HSBC's chief India economist, framed it as the factory sector ending the quarter "on a firmer footing," with the inventory build signalling "a clear shift from leaner stock levels."
- **The offsetting line is price.** There were **quicker increases in both input costs and output charges**, which is exactly the variable the RBI is watching into its October 5-7 meeting.

The market's response was to sell. The Nifty was already down 0.28% within an hour of the PMI release and finished **0.88% lower**, extending the losing streak to an **eighth consecutive week — its longest run in 25 years** — as **foreign investors sold about $1.06 billion of Indian equities on September 30 and roughly $3.6 billion over five sessions**, taking 2026 outflows to a record **$27.8 billion** on Reuters' measure. Strong domestic data is not, on its own, a market catalyst when the binding constraint is the rate and flow picture.

## SEBI Clears Corporate Bond Index Futures for the NSE

**The National Stock Exchange has received SEBI's no-objection for the introduction of corporate bond index futures** — a market-structure approval that deepens the Indian interest-rate derivatives menu beyond sovereign and equity products.

This is the latest in a run of regulator-driven structural changes that this tracker has been logging, alongside the **Closing Auction Session** consultation (comments close **October 3**), the derivatives settlement-price rework, the **ETF trading framework** that went live September 7, the **PMS regulation rewrite** and the **BRSR Core value-chain assurance mandate**. The pattern is consistent: SEBI is expanding the listed product set and the disclosure burden simultaneously, which is a volume-and-compliance tailwind for exchanges, depositories, registrars and brokers, and a cost headwind for issuers and listed entities.

The commercial question is whether corporate bond index futures actually trade. India's corporate bond market has repeatedly produced well-designed products with thin volume because the underlying cash market remains illiquid and held-to-maturity. That is why the read-through should be treated as structural rather than as a revenue event.

## RoSCTL and RoDTEP Extended to December 31 — the Textile Export Safety Net Continues

**Two export-incentive schemes due to lapse on September 30 were extended into the new fiscal period:**

- **The Ministry of Textiles extended the Rebate of State and Central Taxes and Levies (RoSCTL) scheme for exports of apparel, garments and made-ups by three months, from October 1 to December 31, 2026**, at the **existing rates and under the prevailing guidelines**. RoSCTL has operated since **March 7, 2019** and remits eligible embedded state and central taxes and levies not refunded through other mechanisms. The ministry says the scheme **benefits over 15,400 exporters, predominantly MSMEs**, across many districts.
- **The Directorate General of Foreign Trade extended RoDTEP to December 31, 2026** for eligible DTA, advance-authorisation, SEZ and EOU exports, with **existing rates and value caps unchanged**.

The extension is policy continuity rather than new money, and it comes with an unresolved question the exporter groups have been raising: how long the support continues, and whether the budget allocation behind it grows. A three-month rollover is a signal that the government intends to keep the scheme but has not settled its medium-term design — the same pattern as the September 1 textile tariff-rate-quota date passing without claim rules while the 10% Section 301 duty continued.

## Pharma's PRIP Gets a Discovery Track — up to ₹50 Crore per Project

**The Department of Pharmaceuticals opened a new "Discovery" track under the Promotion of Research and Innovation in Pharma & MedTech (PRIP) Scheme, and the PRIP portal is now accepting applications under the second call.**

- **Financial assistance of up to ₹50 crore per company, project or portfolio of projects**, subject to **minimum co-funding of 25% of project cost from bona fide institutional investors**.
- **Eligibility: start-ups and MSMEs undertaking New Chemical Entity or New Biological Entity projects at technology readiness levels 1, 2 or 3**, with support to progress them to no further than TRL 6.
- **Scheme outlay ₹5,000 crore**, across three priority areas in the second call.
- **Separately, 41 pharma and MedTech innovation projects have been approved under PRIP.**

The design is deliberate: the track exists to plug the **early-stage equity gap** in Indian drug discovery, where capital typically arrives only after a molecule has de-risked. The 25% institutional co-funding requirement is the mechanism meant to force private capital in early rather than substituting for it.

The honest mapping problem here is that PRIP's beneficiaries are, by construction, **unlisted start-ups and MSMEs**. The listed read-through is about the ecosystem — contract research, CDMO capacity and the domestic innovation pipeline — rather than about revenue arriving at any large-cap balance sheet.

## Defence: the AMCA RFP Closes October 30, and HAL Is Not in the Race

**This is the most consequential capital-allocation signal in Indian defence this week, and it is a Ministry of Defence decision.**

- **Three entities are shortlisted and hold Requests for Proposal to build the Advanced Medium Combat Aircraft prototypes:** **Tata Advanced Systems** (unlisted), **an L&T–BEL–Dynamatic consortium**, and **a Bharat Forge–BEML–Data Patterns grouping**.
- **The bid deadline has been extended to October 30, 2026**, with bid opening scheduled around **October 31**.
- **HAL did not qualify**, on what Defence Secretary **Rajesh Kumar Singh** described as "fairly routine technical grounds." He rejected the suggestion that HAL was deliberately excluded and said the **policy objective is to create a second fighter production line outside HAL.** ANI reported that HAL could still announce a joint venture with DRDO and a private company to progress the AMCA programme.
- **What the winner must deliver:** **five flying prototypes and one structural test article**, with the first prototype required to complete **taxi trials and first flight within 30 months of contract award**, and an **84-month, roughly 1,800-test-sortie** programme before series production. Prototypes will initially use **GE F414 engines**, the same family specified for the LCA Mk-2.
- **The programme was cleared at Full Scale Engineering Development for roughly ₹15,000 crore.**
- **A dedicated Aircraft Integration and Flight Testing Centre at Puttaparthi, Andhra Pradesh** is being developed to support integration, testing and certification.
- **Timelines:** AMCA first flight targeted **September 2028**; **LCA Mk-2 first flight September 2027**. The Defence Secretary expressed the hope the AMCA would fly within two years.

The strategic arithmetic behind it is in the same week's doctrine speech. **Defence Minister Rajnath Singh told News18's DefCon 2026** that India has moved beyond "strategic containment" and "deterrence by denial" to **"deterrence by punishment"**, and warned against the **"perimeter fallacy"** — treating national security as the defence of geographic boundaries alone when threats now run through **drones and unmanned systems, cyberattacks, lone-wolf attacks, disinformation, the economy and energy supply chains.**

The funding context he gave: the **defence budget has risen from roughly ₹2.50 lakh crore in FY2013-14 to ₹7.85 lakh crore in FY2026-27 — a near three-fold increase** — while **defence production has reached approximately ₹1.80 lakh crore**, ordnance factories have been restructured and defence PSUs are registering profits.

## The Rules That Turned On Today

**October 1 is a compliance date for a cluster of central-bank and departmental changes**, and they are the kind of operational detail that reprices deposits and fee income quietly:

- **Bulk fixed-deposit rate disclosure.** The RBI's new framework requires banks to **announce bulk deposit interest rates in advance and disclose the applicable rates daily at 10 am**, offering an **identical rate for similar bulk deposits** except where the **Liquidity Coverage Ratio treatment differs**. This is a transparency measure aimed at the deposit-price competition that has accompanied the rate cycle.
- **Bankers' Books Evidence Act, 2026** — a digital-era framework for the admissibility of banking records takes effect.
- **LPG Aadhaar-KYC** requirements bite.
- **SBI's free ATM transaction limit falls to five per month**, and **new NPS charges** apply.
- **UPI MDR** on person-to-merchant transactions above ₹2,000 — **0.4%, capped at ₹300 above ₹75,000, flat ₹5 for railways, telecom, insurance and fuel** — is notified and goes live on **October 15**, with person-to-person transfers free at all values.
