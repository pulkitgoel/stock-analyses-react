Three cost-of-living actions in two days, all of them signed twelve days before the midterms, and each one landing on a different set of listed companies. The housing order is the largest by market impact because it attacks the persistency of two insurance revenue streams at once. The refining directive is the smallest by dollars and the largest by precedent. The wind proclamation is a punt dressed as a finding.

## First: the housing order goes after mortgage-insurance persistency

**On Thursday the President signed an executive order titled "Decreasing Housing Costs for the American People."** It has four operative parts, and the text is written conditionally throughout — the FHFA director "shall, if he determines" that a measure serves Fannie Mae, Freddie Mac, the agency and the public, direct the two companies to act. No date is set for that determination.

**Section 2 — proactive cancellation of mortgage insurance.** Fannie and Freddie would run a pilot that periodically re-values single-family loans they own, guarantee or securitise using automated valuation models, and cancel mortgage insurance without the borrower asking when current market value gives them the required equity. Two limits matter more than the headline: the pilot applies **only to mortgages made after it is established**, and it names **no equity percentage** — the threshold is an open number.

This is the substantive change. Under the Homeowners Protection Act a borrower can request cancellation at 80% of the home's *original* value, and servicers must terminate automatically at 78%. Original value means the price or appraisal at closing, so house-price inflation does not count. An AVM looking at *current* value does count it. That converts a decade-long premium stream into a shorter one for every loan written after the pilot starts.

**Section 4 — title insurance.** The existing title acceptance pilot, which since 2024 has let some lenders skip a lender's title policy on low-risk refinances under 80% loan-to-value in selected areas, is to be expanded into an ongoing nationwide programme. The order tells the FHFA to consider raising the LTV ceiling above 80%, removing geographic limits, certifying more title-assessment providers, waiving appraisals and income re-verification for limited cash-out refinances that reduce the monthly payment, and cutting loan-level price adjustments on those refinances. Savings must be passed to borrowers and disclosed by lenders.

The remaining parts create a task force on manufactured housing with a 180-day reporting window, and direct the Departments of Agriculture and Veterans Affairs to reduce rates on their direct single-family loan programmes.

**What it does not do: change a mortgage rate.** The 30-year fixed averaged **7.32%** this week, per Freddie Mac's survey. The order reaches costs *around* the loan, not the loan's price.

## Second: the Defense Production Act, $150 million, and a named refinery

**A presidential determination under Section 303 of the Defense Production Act, signed Thursday, designates domestic petroleum production, refining and logistics capacity as essential to national defence.** The same day the Department of Energy announced plans to provide **up to $150 million** in DPA Title III funding to expand petroleum production, storage and logistics at refineries, expected to add "millions of gallons per day" of gasoline, diesel and jet fuel. Trump put the number on it himself at a rally in Sioux City, Iowa.

The money is small. A single refinery expansion runs into the billions. What matters is the mechanism: the memo says that without action the US cannot provide those energy services in a timely manner "due to constrained financing, long lead times, permitting and infrastructure bottlenecks, and supply chain limitations," and it opens the door to direct funding or purchase agreements for production sites, refineries, pipelines and storage. It sits on top of **$1 billion** already appropriated for energy projects deemed critical to national security.

One company has been named in this thread, and not in the memo. **Last week the Vice President said the administration would use the DPA to expand capacity at a Texas refinery owned by Delek US Holdings.**

The pressure behind it is retail, not strategic. The EIA puts average US diesel at **$6.28 a gallon** in the week to October 19, against **$3.81** immediately before the war began, with gasoline at **$4.28** from **$2.94**.

## Third: wind turbines declared a threat, and then nothing

**A proclamation signed Thursday concurred with a Commerce Department finding that imported wind turbines and their components "threaten to impair the national security of the United States,"** on four grounds: cybersecurity vulnerabilities in foreign-made networked inverters, radar interference affecting air traffic control, weather forecasting and military systems, insufficient US blade manufacturing capacity, and the argument that wind's intermittency itself burdens the grid.

It imposed no duty. Instead the Commerce Secretary and the USTR are directed to negotiate agreements with trading partners and report to the President at least once within **180 days**; "depending on the status or outcome of such negotiations, I may consider alternative remedies in the future," the order says. Existing Section 232 duties on aluminium, steel and copper continue to apply to turbine components — turbines were added to 407 derivative steel and aluminium categories in August 2025 at a 50% rate on the metal content — and the proclamation does not touch them. The underlying investigation opened on August 13, 2025.

Separately, **Trump said on Truth Social that Bombardier will invest $270 million in the United States**, creating jobs in Kansas, Texas and Indiana, days after he dropped a threat to decertify the Canadian planemaker's aircraft. Bombardier employs about 1,500 people in Wichita, and the February decertification threat arose from a certification dispute involving **Gulfstream**, a General Dynamics subsidiary. No US-listed company is a direct beneficiary; the pattern is the point — tariff and certification threats are being converted into investment announcements ahead of November 3.

## Fourth: the Iran option stays open

**"The Iran War, and we've knocked the hell out of them, and we may do it a little bit further,"** Trump told reporters before departing Washington on Friday, adding that there is "a price to pay, which I knew immediately when I did it." He gave no target, no timetable and no indication that an operation had been ordered — it was an option, restated, not an announcement.

Brent did the rest of the work, trading at **$116.48** late in London, up 2.9% on Thursday's $113.17 settle and at its highest since early May.
