Three dates did the work today, and only one of them arrived as an announcement.

## 1 December: the margin cut that is already in the price

SEBI's circular of 17 November — HO/47/16/14(3)2026-MRD-POD1/I/25885/2026 — takes effect on 1 December and runs for a year from issuance. It rewrites paragraph 9.1.3.I.a(iv) of Chapter 9 of the Master Circular for Commodity Derivatives dated 4 August 2023, and it sets one margin table for everything.

Minimum initial margin of 6%, 8% and 10% for low, medium and high volatility commodities, with a minimum margin period of risk of 2, 2 and 3 days. The same numbers for agricultural and non-agricultural contracts.

For non-agricultural commodities that is unchanged. For agricultural commodities it is a cut, and the cut is the point. The framework it replaces dates to January 2020, when agricultural contracts carried a minimum initial margin of 8% in the low-volatility band against 6% for non-agricultural ones, and a margin period of risk of 3 days against 2 — four days against three in the high-volatility band. The revision equalises by lowering the agricultural side, on recommendations from the working group on agricultural commodity derivatives and the Risk Management Review Committee.

Two definitions matter for what happens next. Initial margin is the collateral locked per position. The margin period of risk is the number of days a clearing corporation assumes it would take to close out a defaulted position, and it sets how much collateral has to sit behind each unit of exposure. Together they are the carrying cost of a hedge.

That is why the exchange is the instrument. A lower minimum initial margin per lot raises the notional that a given rupee of margin can support, and an exchange earns on volume, not on the margin rate.

Wednesday's close was ₹3,565. Thursday's was ₹3,835, up 7.57%, a record, and the largest move in the midcap complex. BSE closed at ₹3,990.40 (+5.70%), KFin Technologies at ₹995 (+6.53%) and Computer Age Management Services at ₹745.90 (+3.96%) — the same capital-market-activity trade running through the registry layer.

One honest caveat: an 8% day is not margin arithmetic. Gold futures rose 1.85% to $3,601 and commodities rallied broadly, and agricultural margins are a modest slice of the exchange's revenue mix. But a rule that takes effect on the first session of next month and directly lowers the cost of carrying a position is a dated tailwind, and it is the reason this sits in the policy column rather than in a market wrap.

## The minimum public shareholding rule did the selling

LG Electronics Inc is selling 2% of LG Electronics India — 13,575,447 shares — in the open market, taking its holding from 85% to 83%, to meet the minimum public shareholding requirement. At Wednesday's close of ₹1,819.70 that is about ₹2,470 crore. The stock closed at ₹1,756.90, down 3.4%.

This is the cleanest example available of a rule generating a trade. The supply is not a view on the appliance cycle, a reaction to results, or a promoter losing confidence. It is a compliance obligation with a rupee value attached, and the market discounts the float rather than the thesis.

Expect more of it. Any Indian subsidiary of a foreign parent that listed through a pure offer for sale starts at 85% and has a deadline to reach 75%, and the sale is mechanical each time.

## NSE got the second nod on corporate bond futures

The National Stock Exchange has an RBI no-objection certificate for futures on corporate bond indices, following an equivalent nod from SEBI. Contract specifications and a start date have not been announced.

There is no listed beneficiary to point at — NSE is unlisted. The listed read is BSE, whose competitive position in the interest-rate complex is what changes if corporate bond futures become a liquid exchange-traded product. An index future on corporate bonds gives holders of debt ETFs and passive debt portfolios a hedge that currently only exists over the counter, and that is a genuine market-structure gain. It is not revenue this quarter.

## RBI put out draft priority sector norms

The draft rules, out for comment, put a ₹100 crore per-borrower cap on agricultural infrastructure lending across the entire banking system, and ₹4 crore per borrowing entity on crop loans, medium and long-term farm loans, and pre- and post-harvest loans. Education loans to individuals up to ₹25 lakh would count. Renewable energy gets ₹35 crore for generators, public utilities, street lighting and remote village electrification, and ₹10 lakh for individual households. The 5% cap on NBFC on-lending survives.

Separately, RBI rejected the microfinance industry's request to exclude principal repayments from the 50% cap on a household's total loan repayments as a share of monthly income, and said it is open to reviewing the ₹3 lakh annual household income eligibility limit.

HDFC Bank closed at ₹862.00 (+1.87%) and State Bank of India at ₹1,136.65 (-0.13%). No bank moved on the draft, and that is correct. These are caps on what qualifies for the target, not changes to the 40% target itself.

The Finance Minister's 13th and final pre-Budget consultation — with youth — closed a cycle that ran from 16 to 26 November with more than 200 stakeholders across 13 sectors. PIB posted the release at 7:43 p.m., four hours after the close.

Budget 2027-28 is the event. A consultation calendar is not a decision, and the February document is where any of it becomes a number.
