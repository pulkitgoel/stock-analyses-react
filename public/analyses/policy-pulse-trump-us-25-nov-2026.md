The determination landed at 8:11 in the morning. By half past ten the market had decided it was not a tariff story.

US Trade Representative Jamieson Greer signed the findings of the Section 301 investigation into structural excess capacity today, against 14 economies: China, the European Union, Singapore, Switzerland, Indonesia, Malaysia, Cambodia, Thailand, Korea, Vietnam, Taiwan, Bangladesh, Japan and India. Mexico and Norway were investigated and left out of the determination for now.

The proposed rates, as published: up to 40% on China, 35% on Vietnam, 25% on Cambodia, Indonesia, Korea, Malaysia, Taiwan, Thailand and India, and 20% on Bangladesh, the EU, Japan, Switzerland and Singapore.

Three design features decide what that headline number actually means.

First, the caps. For Korea, Taiwan, the EU, Japan and Switzerland the action is capped at 15% net of the most-favoured-nation duty. For China it is capped at 20%, stacked on the first-term duties. Greer, on CNBC: "when it all nets out, it really will just top up a few percentage points." A tariff that tops up is a tariff the counterparty has already negotiated.

Second, the exclusions. Energy, food that cannot easily be grown in the United States, certain semiconductors and pharmaceuticals are carved out. The duties do not stack on the Section 232 tariffs on automobiles, steel and aluminium, and they do not stack on the 10%-12.5% forced-labour duties that have been live since 24 July.

Third, the clock. Comments close on 5 January 2027, hearings open on 25 January, and officials briefing reporters put implementation at "probably January at the earliest." Nothing is being collected tonight. The rates CBP assesses tomorrow morning are the rates it assessed yesterday.

## The AI complex took the whole day

This is the third session in four in which the same names were repriced, and the direction has now round-tripped twice: Nvidia fell hard on Friday, rose 31.5% on Monday when Jensen Huang was reinstated as chief executive, gave back 5.9% on Tuesday and is down another 7.7% today at $143.35. Micron is off 10.6%, AMD 9.1%, Oracle 8.5%, TSMC's ADR 6.8%, Broadcom 6.0%, Ambarella 20.7% after guiding the current quarter below expectations.

Three separate instruments landed on Nvidia inside five days, and only one of them is a tariff. On 16 November, Commerce published an interim final rule requiring licences for advanced AI chips and servers shipped to Singapore, Malaysia, Indonesia, Thailand and Brazil. On 23 November, the Bureau of Industry and Security added Megaspeed International and six affiliates — Swiftdata and Apex Enterprise Solutions in Singapore, MSFusion, PT Megaspeed and Speedmatrix in Malaysia, and Megaspeed Thailand — to the Entity List under a presumption of denial; that rule reaches formal Federal Register publication today. Separately, the Atlantic Council reports that the Securities and Exchange Commission has opened an inquiry into Nvidia's accounting practices, and congressional hearings continue with investigations resuming from 1 December.

President Trump, asked about the reinstatement, told CNBC: "I spoke to 'em. Let me just put it that way, and I convinced 'em... I made the deal." A chief executive restored by a phone call from the president is a governance fact that the multiple has to carry.

Anthropic closed at $79.58, 2.8% lower, on its fifth trading day — below the $83 offer price and 28.6% below the $111.50 first trade on 19 November. Cathie Wood's Ark bought another 751,710 shares on Tuesday, about $61.6 million at the close.

## The trades are a disclosure, not a signal

The September periodic transaction report, signed 1 November and released by the Office of Government Ethics on 19 November, was 751 purchases and sales totalling between roughly $40.9 million and $148.1 million — purchases of at least $29.2 million, sales of at least $11.7 million.

The Washington Post's analysis, published on 24 November, counts 44 Nvidia trades between January and September: 29 purchases totalling $6 million to $17 million, 15 sales totalling $4 million to $12 million. At the start of 2025 the holding was between $13 million and $58 million; as of 23 September it was between $6 million and $71 million. September alone included purchases of up to $265,000 and sales of up to $15,000 of Nvidia, and up to $315,000 of SpaceX.

The White House position, restated by spokesperson Davis Ingle, is that third-party institutions manage the portfolio independently and replicate "recognized indexes, such as the Schwab 1000." That is the correct frame. An index-replication account that bought Nvidia 29 times is not expressing a view on the chip cycle — and treating it as one is how a disclosure becomes a rumour.

## What to watch

Thanksgiving closes US markets tomorrow, and Friday is a half session ending at 1:00 p.m. ET. The 5 January comment deadline is the real decision point for these rates, and the 25 January hearing is where the product lists get argued. Between now and then the operative question is not whether the duties are high but whether the exclusions survive contact with the comment file — because a 25% rate with a pharmaceutical carve-out is a different instrument from a 25% rate without one.
