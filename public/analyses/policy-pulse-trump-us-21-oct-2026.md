The White House put two things on the table today, and only one of them is a stock story.

The first is process. President Trump and Vice President JD Vance announced a **$25 million Fraud Whistleblower Incentive Program** on Tuesday — anyone whose tip leads to a fraud conviction or a significant federal enforcement action can collect **up to $30,000**, with Trump saying payments will amount to a **1% commission on the fraud the tip helps uncover**. The Justice Department administers it, and Trump told reporters he will personally decide who gets paid when more than one person claims a tip, calling it "a binding arbitration."

That is enforcement capacity, not a subsidy. It matters for how quickly fraud cases convert into headlines, not for anyone's revenue line, and it is not a reason to own or sell anything.

The second item is the one with tickers attached.

## Trump Is Selling the Crypto Scam Crackdown as Policy

At a White House cybercrime event today, Trump and cabinet members are highlighting the government's **seizure of fraudulent crypto investment websites** and its work against online impersonation scams run by foreign criminal networks. The numbers behind it are ugly enough to be a demand-side story: the FBI logged **181,565 crypto-fraud complaints and $11.37 billion of losses in 2025**, and the interagency **Scam Center Strike Force** says it has recovered more than **$400 million** in crypto earmarked for victims.

The operational detail matters more than the podium. **Coinbase said its investigators traced transactions and shared intelligence with law enforcement during the October 6-9 operation** — the exchange is inside the enforcement loop, not outside it. Separately, blockchain trackers flagged roughly **$470 million of seized crypto moving to wallets likely tied to Coinbase Prime**, which revives the question of whether government-held coins get liquidated, custody-shifted, or left alone. Bitcoin was around **$83,545, up 1.2%**, so the market is not treating seizures as supply risk today.

## Netflix Cut Its Ad Forecast and Lost $17 Billion

Netflix reported Tuesday after the close and the headline numbers were fine: **revenue $12.87 billion, up 12%**, net income **$3.66 billion or $0.87 per share** against **$0.82 expected**, operating margin **34.6%** versus 28.2% a year earlier.
