## The Inflation Double-Whammy: 3.7% CPI, Then a 6.3% PPI

The week's macro sequence, not any single headline, is what repriced US assets. Two inflation prints landed back to back and both surprised to the upside.

- **September CPI, released October 14: headline +0.6% month on month and 3.7% year on year**, above the 3.6% consensus and up from 3.4% in August. Core came in at **2.6% year on year** against 2.5% expected.
- **September PPI, released October 15: +1.3% month on month — the second-largest monthly increase on record — and 6.3% year on year, the biggest jump since 2022.** The driver is fuel. Wholesale energy costs tied to the Middle East war have kept Brent above $100.
- The Fed's **Beige Book**, out the same afternoon as CPI, was described by Rosenberg Research's Robert Embree as carrying a **"stagflationary flavour"** that "strongly undercuts the narrative of a solid economy."
- Repricing followed within minutes. Fed funds futures moved from roughly **22% to about 50%** for a hike at the **October 27-28** meeting — the highest since the soft PCE print two weeks earlier. The market now prices a **96% chance of at least one more hike before year-end and a 50% chance of two**.
- The **10-year Treasury yield** touched about **5.3%** intraday on October 14, testing its 24-year high, before bond prices recovered and yields closed lower.
- Trump's own position has not moved: he has called for rates capped at 1% and said in a late-September TIME interview that the Fed should not raise again this year.

**The tape.** October 14 was the worst session since July — **S&P 500 -86.51 (-1.11%) to 7,718.94**, its fifth loss in six sessions since setting its all-time high; **Nasdaq Composite -511.51 (-1.88%) to 26,692.87**; **Dow -48.62 (-0.09%) to 51,621.48**; **Russell 2000 -1.89% to 2,729.53**. Eight of the S&P 500's eleven sectors fell, led by information technology at -2.6%.

On October 15 the shape inverted rather than recovered. Mid-session the **Dow was up 1.10% at 52,190.08** and the **S&P 500 up 0.16% at 7,731.05**, while the **Nasdaq 100 was down 0.54% at 29,789.79** — its sixth decline in seven sessions. That is rotation, not a rally: **Walmart +3-4%, Nike +3.85%, Sherwin-Williams +3.62% and Eli Lilly +2%** against **Caterpillar -4.72%** and **Nvidia -1.90%**. A rally in Treasuries is what the defensive bid is being funded from.

## The AI Trade's Real Crack Is Financing, Not Demand

The sell-off in AI-linked equities this week has a different character from the summer's chip routs. The trigger is a combination of decelerating revenue at the largest private lab and stress in the debt that funds data-centre construction.

- **Anthropic** disclosed third-quarter revenue of **$16.8 billion** at a pre-IPO meeting with institutional investors. That is up **45% sequentially** from $11.6 billion in the second quarter and $4.7 billion in the first — but it represents a **deceleration** from earlier in the year and came in below most estimates. The company has reportedly pushed its IPO from October to **November**.
- MarketWatch framed it directly: **"Anthropic's slowing growth could be the AI trade's latest red flag."** The logic is that if the reference customer's revenue curve is flattening, the case for the sector's capex curve steepening weakens.
- **CoreWeave** fell **15.5% on October 14 — its worst day since February — and another 8% on October 15, with its bonds declining sharply.** CEO Michael Intrator's defence was specific: "we are confident in our backlog which more than supports all of our debt obligation." The market is pricing the liability, not the backlog.
- The read-through hit memory and storage hardest on October 15: **Micron, Intel, AMD and Marvell each dropped more than 6%, while Seagate and Western Digital tumbled 9%.** The **iShares Semiconductor ETF fell for a seventh consecutive session.**
- Total capital expenditure commitments are the exposure. Broadcom's Hock Tan said in Singapore on October 12 that "the biggest constraint in AI expansion is capital," and Intel's Lip-Bu Tan said the same about the cost of capital. On October 15 the market began charging for it.

## TSMC Posts a Record Quarter and the Stock Falls Anyway

The counter-example to the demand story arrived on October 15 and the market's reaction is the instructive part.

- **Third-quarter revenue of NT$1,494.24 billion (about $46.72 billion), up 41.2% year on year**, beating the NT$1,460.39 billion consensus. **Net profit rose 68.7% to a record NT$763.06 billion ($23.6 billion)** — a sixth consecutive record quarter.
- **Earnings per ADR of $4.60** against $4.45 expected. **Gross margin reached 66.4%.** High-performance computing was **68% of revenue** and advanced nodes (7nm and below) were **79% of wafer revenue**.
- Guidance: **fourth-quarter revenue of $52.3-54.3 billion**, full-year 2026 revenue growth of about **44%**, and the **$60-64 billion capital budget maintained**, with "significantly higher" AI spending planned through 2028. Management is considering a **Texas fab**.
- And yet **the TSM ADR fell about 1.5% to $423.30 in premarket trade and the Taiwan-listed shares closed 2% lower.** The reason is in the guidance detail: management remains cautious on the 2027 macro backdrop and warned that **overseas capacity expansion will continue to weigh on margins**.
- Goldman Sachs' Sean Johnstone wrote that the results should reassure investors that "AI deployment remains on track" — but added that bulls had hoped for another **capex increase**, and did not get one.

The signal is that a company reporting its best quarter ever, with margins at 66%, is being marked down. That is a multiple event, not an earnings event.

## Five Days to the Graham Act: No Notice to Congress, and a Diesel Contradiction

The tariff deadline that matters for November's prices is now inside the week.

- The **Lindsey O. Graham Sanctioning Russia and Iran Act** was signed on **September 18, 2026** (Senate 86-11, House 262-159).
- **Section 112** requires duties of **up to 500%** on goods imported from Russia by **October 18**, explicitly including petroleum products. That is a Sunday, so several law firms give the operative date as **Monday, October 19**.
- **Section 113** requires duties of **up to 100%** on all goods from the five largest buyers of Russian crude or gas — but only where those buyers **knowingly make new purchases on or after the deadline**. Purchases already made do not count.
- **Section 113(g)** requires a written justification to Congress **not later than 10 days before imposing a duty** — around **October 8** for an October 18 start. As of October 12 there was **no published target list, no rate and no waiver**. The Act also lets the president waive any duty by certifying a national-interest rationale.
- The live contradiction is the diesel deal. **OFAC General License 135, issued October 9, authorises transactions in Russian-origin diesel until April 2027.** Trump said Russia will initially supply more than **300,000 tonnes**, then **500,000 tonnes in November** and **1 million tonnes thereafter** — roughly six million barrels, about a day and a half of US demand.
- Treasury Secretary **Scott Bessent** rejected the claim that the law bars those purchases and accused Europe of hypocrisy, saying Europeans had given Russia more money since the war began than they had given Ukraine. Representative **Raja Krishnamoorthi** called the arrangement "illegal, ineffective" on October 15; Representative **Mike Levin** cited a New York Times estimate that about **$4 billion** of American money could reach Russia's economy.
- Trump's public position has two halves that conflict. He wrote on Truth Social that major oil companies are not cutting pump prices in line with falling oil costs, complaining that "customers" are not seeing the benefit — while pressing an arrangement whose stated purpose is to lower fuel costs.

## Leader Trades: the August Disclosure, and Why the Copy-Trade Effect Matters

The largest single disclosure in the August filing was a technology purchase, and the sequencing against policy is the part worth tracking.

- Trump's August disclosure, released **October 8**, reported **517 transactions worth between roughly $74 million and $273 million** — a slower pace than earlier months, when his accounts often cleared more than 1,000 transactions.
- **The biggest buy was up to $25 million of Meta (META) on August 21.** The same day he bought up to **$5 million each of AT&T, ConocoPhillips, Abbott Laboratories, Netflix and Chevron**, and sold up to **$5 million each of AMD and Church & Dwight**, while trimming **Nvidia, Boeing, Home Depot and Dell**.
- Separately, the filing shows a purchase of up to **$5 million in SpaceX debt two days before the administration signed a national space transportation policy** on August 20 — the sequencing that drew the most attention.
- Volume: **more than 9,200 transactions disclosed in the first eight months of 2026**, and **21,573 purchases across his first 633 days in office** — more than 34 trades a day, and more than all of Congress combined. Common Cause puts his total traded since January 2025 at **$3.2 billion**, against **$1.2 billion for Congress** over the same period. Biden made 13 trades in his entire term.
- The broker is his own company. The Trump Organization says the trades are executed by third-party institutions with no input from Trump or his family, and that "neither President Trump, his family, nor The Trump Organization plays any role in selecting, directing, or approving specific investments."
- **Forward event:** a **Melania Trump-led executive order signing ceremony is scheduled for Monday, October 19 at 11:00 a.m. ET in the Rose Garden** — the media advisory was issued on October 15. The First Lady has separately launched the **2027 Presidential Super Intelligence Challenge** for K-12 students, open for registration through December 18, and it follows the September 29 executive order that renamed artificial intelligence as "super intelligence" across executive-branch documents.
