## What you are actually buying

Three things get confused in the same ticker, and separating them is most of the work:

**First, a services business that has genuinely turned.** FY26 revenue ₹1,269 crore, operating profit ₹269 crore, net profit ₹253 crore. Three years ago the same business lost ₹441 crore. Debt is down to ₹18 crore. Operating cash flow was ₹469 crore. This is not a story about a recovery that might happen; the recovery is in the reported numbers.

**Second, a demerger that is smaller than the market thinks.** The Durgapur factory is going to JSW Energy, and GVPIL shareholders receive 10 JSW Energy shares for every 139 GVPIL shares they hold. At JSW Energy's ₹463.90, that entitlement is worth **₹33.37 per GVPIL share** — about **5.5%** of the current price. It is a real entitlement, but it is not the reason to own the stock.

**Third, a chart that has broken.** From a June high of ₹1,084 the stock has fallen to ₹608.90, down 43.8%. It trades below every moving average from the 9-day EMA to the 200-day. Every monthly high since June has been lower than the one before.

The business passes. The chart does not. That gap is the entire investment question, and it resolves in one of two ways: either the price bases and reclaims ₹630-675 and the turnaround gets re-rated, or the de-rating continues toward the ₹476 area where the stock broke out in April.

## 1. What GE Power India is now

GE Power India is the Indian listed arm of GE Vernova, and it is no longer a manufacturer. After two years of portfolio surgery it is a **services and upgrades business for thermal power plants**, with a small equipment leg and a growing international order book.

The four revenue lines:

**Core services.** Maintenance, spares, retrofits and upgrades for boilers, turbines and generators. The installed base is the moat: GE equipment across NTPC, state utilities and private generators, plus — and this is the growth part — *other OEM* fleets. Servicing a BHEL or Siemens turbine is a larger addressable market than servicing only GE machines, and management has been deliberately pushing into it.

**Air quality control systems (FGD).** Flue-gas desulphurisation retrofits for thermal plants, driven by the regulatory deadline structure rather than by anyone's capex optimism.

**Other OEM (oOEM).** Third-party fleet service. Order intake went from ₹162 crore to ₹322 crore over the period management describes.

**Durgapur parts — being demerged.** Boiler pressure parts, piping, mills and pressure vessels, made at the Durgapur facility in West Bengal: an industrial unit plus a residential township on roughly **661 acres of leasehold land**. This is the piece leaving the company.

What has already left: the hydro and gas businesses, sold by slump sale, and a long-running BHEL commercial dispute, settled. What is left is asset-light: fixed assets of ₹40 crore on a ₹2,138 crore balance sheet.

## 3. FY25 was other income; FY26 was the business

This distinction matters because it is where the "24% profit CAGR" claims come from.

In FY25 GEPIL reported ₹203 crore of net profit on ₹1,047 crore of revenue with an operating profit of ₹9 crore. The profit came from **₹252 crore of other income** — the BHEL settlement. Strip it out and FY25 was a break-even year.

FY26 is different. Other income fell to ₹34 crore and the company still made ₹259 crore of pre-tax profit, because operating profit reached ₹269 crore. The profit is now coming from the business.

That is the cleanest single sentence about this company: **FY25 was a legal settlement; FY26 was the first year the operations paid for themselves in a decade.**

The qualification: the tax line. FY26 tax was 2% of pre-tax profit — ₹5 crore on ₹259 crore. Two years of heavy losses left carried-forward losses to absorb, which is legitimate, but it means the reported EPS of ₹37.58 is not a taxed EPS. At a normalised 25% rate, FY26 profit would be about ₹194 crore and the trailing multiple at today's price would be **21x, not 15x**. The tax shield runs out as the losses are used up; that is a headwind arriving quietly over the next two to three years.

## 4. The Durgapur demerger: the arithmetic

The scheme, sanctioned by the NCLT Mumbai Bench on **1 October 2026**, demerges the Durgapur undertaking into **JSW Energy** on a going-concern, as-is-where-is basis, with an appointed date of **1 July 2025**.

The entitlement: **10 fully paid JSW Energy shares of ₹10 each for every 139 fully paid GVPIL shares of ₹10 each.**

Here is what that is worth, worked through:

- JSW Energy closed at **₹463.90**
- 10 ÷ 139 = 0.07194 JSW shares per GVPIL share
- 0.07194 × ₹463.90 = **₹33.37 per GVPIL share**
- Against ₹608.90, that is **5.5% of the current price**
- Across 6.72 crore GVPIL shares, JSW Energy is issuing roughly **48.4 lakh shares, about ₹224 crore of stock**

For that, JSW Energy takes on a facility that management says ran at an **average loss of ₹27 crore a year** during 2023-2025, with utilisation limited to service work and non-coal activity.

Two conclusions follow, and both are uncomfortable for the popular version of this trade.

**The entitlement is not the thesis.** Social media framed this as "buy GEPIL, get JSW Energy shares free." Free, yes, but worth 5.5%. Anyone who bought GEPIL at ₹1,000 in June on that reasoning has lost 39% net of a ₹33 entitlement.

**The demerger is worth more to GEPIL than to JSW.** Removing a ₹27 crore annual loss is a direct uplift to the retained company's earnings — roughly 10% of TTM profit — while JSW Energy is paying ₹224 crore of stock for a loss-making asset with 661 acres attached. JSW presumably wants the land, the fabrication capacity and a thermal supply chain; that is a strategic call, not an earnings one. The asymmetry is real and it favours GVPIL's remaining shareholders.

What protects the retained business: a **five-year manufacturing services agreement** with JSW Energy giving GEPIL reserved capacity at Durgapur on pre-agreed schedules and pricing, while it builds an independent supply chain. That removes the obvious execution risk from the demerger, at the cost of a related-party dependency that will need watching at each renewal.

## 5. What happens on the record date

The scheme is sanctioned but **not yet effective**. The company told the exchanges on 1 October that it will intimate the Effective Date and Record Date "in due course"; the certified copy of the order was received in mid-October. As of 22 October, no record date has been announced.

That matters mechanically. You receive the JSW Energy shares only if you hold GVPIL on the record date. Between now and then the entitlement is embedded in the price, and it is only 5.5% of it, so this is not a merger-arbitrage spread worth trading on its own.

For anyone already holding: nothing needs doing. The 139 shares stay, and 10 JSW Energy shares arrive. For anyone buying now: you are buying a services business at 15x with a small, time-limited option attached.

The order book at 31 March 2026 was **1.28x FY26 revenue** — adequate, not spectacular, and the reason the market cap is ₹4,093 crore rather than something larger.

The **₹550 crore Dar Al Balad contract** is the most important thing to happen to the order book this year. Two notices of award for the **Shoaiba fuel conversion project** in Saudi Arabia: supply of boiler modification equipment plus technical field advisory services, executed over about **2.5 years**. That is 43% of FY26 revenue, spread across ten quarters, and it is international work at what should be better pricing than domestic utility tenders.

It is also a concentration risk. One client, one project, one country, for two and a half years.

The structural case is India's thermal fleet. Coal capacity is still being added, the FGD retrofit programme runs on regulatory deadlines, and the existing installed base needs servicing regardless of what is built next. A services business attached to the installed base earns through the cycle in a way an equipment order book does not. That is the right business model for this company, and FY26 is the first year it showed in the numbers.

## 7. Margins: repeatable or not

This is the crux of the valuation.

Operating margin went 0.9% (FY25) → 21% (FY26) → 24% (TTM), and the quarterly path within FY26 was 0% → 10% → 32% → 34%, then 14% in Q1 FY27.

An eight-point-per-quarter expansion does not happen because a business gets 20 points better at servicing boilers. It happens because of mix, pricing on a thin order book, and completion of legacy low-margin contracts rolling off.

The optimistic reading: services is structurally higher-margin than equipment, oOEM work carries better pricing than OEM work because the customer has no alternative, and the BHEL settlement removed a drag that had been distorting project margins for years. On that reading, high-teens to low-twenties is the new normal.

The sceptical reading: Q1 FY27 at 14.6% is the first evidence of normalisation, and 34% in Q4 FY26 was a completion-quarter effect. If the run-rate settles at 14-16%, then FY27 operating profit on ₹1,300 crore of revenue is ₹185-210 crore, not ₹300 crore, and the trailing multiple stops looking cheap.

Q1 FY27 is only one quarter, but it is the quarter that leans against the bull case, not for it. This is the number to watch on 7-8 November when Q2 FY27 is reported — not the revenue, the margin.

Debt is effectively gone: ₹18 crore of borrowings against ₹582 crore of net worth. Fixed assets are down to ₹40 crore, which is what an asset-light services business should look like.

The liability side is the interesting part. ₹1,538 crore of "other liabilities" against ₹2,138 crore of total assets is a working-capital structure funded by customer advances and payables — the company gets paid before it pays suppliers. That is why operating cash flow (₹469 crore) exceeded EBITDA (₹269 crore): customers are funding the business.

Cash conversion is genuinely strong: free cash flow of ₹467 crore in FY26, ₹376 crore in FY25, ₹180 crore in FY24. Cumulative three-year free cash flow of **₹1,023 crore** against a ₹4,093 crore market cap is a real number, not a narrative.

The caution is receivables: **219 debtor days**. Better than 409 in FY25 and 597 in FY24, but still slow, and characteristic of government and state-utility customers. If the order mix shifts toward international work, this should improve; if it shifts back to state utilities, it will not.

## 12. Risks

**Margin normalisation.** The single largest risk. Q1 FY27's 14.6% against FY26's 21% is either noise or the beginning of the real run-rate. If it is the latter, the earnings base is 25-30% lower than the trailing number implies.

**Order concentration.** The ₹550 crore Saudi contract is 43% of annual revenue in one project for one client. Slippage or a payment dispute would be material.

**Tax normalisation.** A 2% effective tax rate is flattering EPS by roughly 20%. As carried-forward losses exhaust, the multiple mechanically rises.

**Working capital.** 219 debtor days plus a ₹1,538 crore other-liabilities line is a structure that works while orders grow and can invert quickly if they do not.

**Demerger execution.** The five-year manufacturing agreement with JSW Energy keeps GEPIL dependent on a related party for fabrication capacity. Supply-chain independence is "targeted soon" with no date.

**Regulatory/legacy.** A **₹31.95 crore GST penalty** disclosed in the FY26 BRSR is under appeal. It is 2.5% of market cap if it lands.

**Liquidity and float.** ₹9.3 crore of daily traded value. Fine for a retail position, not fine for an institution — which is also why no institution owns it.

**Record date uncertainty.** Until the Effective Date is notified, the ₹33 entitlement is an unquantified timing item for anyone modelling the transaction.

The turnaround is real and it is in the reported numbers, not in a press release. What is not established is whether FY26's margin is the new level or a peak. Q1 FY27's 14.6% is the first data point against the bull case, and Q2 FY27 — due by mid-November — is the one that settles it.

For an investor with a two-to-three year horizon, ₹609 for a debt-free, cash-generative services business at 15x with a ₹1,628 crore order book is defensible, provided you accept that the next two quarters may be ugly and that the chart offers no support until ₹555.

For a swing trader, the checklist says no. 15.0/25 with four critical fails is a pass, and the honest action is to wait for the market to prove the base rather than to buy the story into a falling EMA stack.

**If buying as an investment, three tranches:**

- **₹575-600 (30%)** — around current price, only if the 22 October low holds
- **₹490-520 (40%)** — the April breakout area, if the drift continues
- **Above ₹660 (30%)** — on a weekly close that reclaims the 50-day EMA, adding to a position that has proved itself

**Invalidation:** a weekly close below **₹540** breaks the 22 October low and opens ₹476. That is the level at which the base has failed and the thesis needs rebuilding, not averaging.

**Targets:** ₹675 (50-day EMA), ₹739 (October high), ₹800-850 (the June-July breakdown zone). The bull case above that is a re-rating to 18-20x pro-forma earnings, which is ₹800-890.

**Stop discipline for a trading position:** below ₹555 on a closing basis, because that is the level the reversal was built on.
