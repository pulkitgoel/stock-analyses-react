The index is up. The largest company in it is down.

At 10:30 a.m. ET, an hour into a session that closes at 1 p.m., the S&P 500 was at 7,248.19 against Wednesday's 7,182.78 settle, the Nasdaq Composite at 23,874.78 against 23,585.80, and the Dow at 50,549.42 against 50,182.95. Nvidia was at $146.74, down 1.71% from $149.30.

Every price in this article is intraday. US cash equities close three hours early for Black Friday and the session is still open at the time of writing, so none of these is a close and none should be read as one.

## The rebound left the biggest name behind

The overnight move was led by futures — S&P up roughly 1.8%, Nasdaq-100 up roughly 2.7% — and the cash market has held about half of it. Six of the eight S&P sectors were positive in the first hour.

Nvidia did not join. It is the only mega-cap in the AI complex that is lower on a day when the complex is higher, and it is now about 27% below its November high.

The reason is not demand and it is not a tariff. It is a licence.

## A licence, not a tariff, is what stopped the servers

Nvidia has told manufacturing partners, including Dell and Super Micro, to stop shipping servers containing its chips to Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand and Indonesia, per Financial Times reporting on 26 November that Reuters could not independently verify and that none of the three companies commented on.

That is the compliance landing of the Bureau of Industry and Security interim final rule issued on 16 November and published in the Federal Register on 19 November, which requires a licence to export, re-export or transfer in-country advanced semiconductors to Indonesia, Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand and Brazil — five markets the rule describes as presenting "a higher-than-normal risk of diversion." It also reaches remote access, which is the part that matters commercially: it closes the route where a Chinese customer rents compute from a South-East Asian data-centre operator instead of buying the hardware.

Three details make the size of this legible.

Nvidia recognised **$104 billion** of revenue through its Singapore subsidiary in 2025, up 72% in twelve months. Some of that is Taiwan and China revenue booked through the Singapore branch, so it overstates local demand — but it is the number the halt runs through.

Cloud providers in the affected markets have **90 days** to obtain licences, which puts the operative deadline around mid-February 2027.

And the secondary market is already clearing the inventory: CNBC reported dealers listing an unusually large number of Nvidia GPUs for sale since the rule, including thousands of B300 chips originally intended for data centres with Chinese rental customers. Banks have halted roughly **$500 million** of loans to Singapore-based Aolani for a planned ByteDance supply deal.

A tariff is a price, and a price can be paid. A licence is a refusal, and a refusal cannot be negotiated by the importer. Four instruments in eleven days now sit in that category — the 16 November rule, the 23 November Entity List addition of Megaspeed and six affiliates, the 26 November shipment halt, and yesterday's ratings action.

## The ratings agency priced the governance, not the cycle

S&P Global Ratings put Nvidia's AA issuer credit rating on CreditWatch negative on Wednesday, citing board independence and governance. Moody's held its Aa1.

This is the first time a third party has said the management is the risk rather than the order book. An order book cannot answer that, which is why the stock is falling while the sector rises, and why the rebound from Monday's reinstatement rally has not held.

## Forty-four trades, and the disclosure stops in September

BBC Verify, working with The Washington Post, counted **44 Nvidia trades** in the president's investment portfolios between January and September 2026 — 29 purchases and 15 sales, with purchases of $5.2 million to $17 million and sales of $4.4 million to $12 million. The portfolios held between $12.5 million and $58 million of Nvidia at the end of 2025.

CNBC's longer window, from January 2025 through the August disclosures, counted at least 103 trades worth $27 million to $97.5 million. The September filing alone — signed 1 November, received by the ethics office 5 November, released 19 November — lists 751 transactions worth roughly $40.9 million to $148.1 million, of which purchases were at least $29.2 million and sales at least $11.7 million.

The September entries that carry information:

- Four Nvidia purchases totalling $152,004 to $380,000, including two on 2 September worth as much as $350,000 combined.
- One Nvidia sale of $1,001 to $15,000 on **24 September** — the day Huang attended the state dinner with Xi Jinping.
- On **23 September**, the largest single transaction in the report: a $5 million to $25 million purchase of Vanguard Dividend Appreciation ETF. The same day, sales of $1 million to $5 million each in Comcast, McDonald's, Home Depot and Fidelity National Information Services, and $500,001 to $1 million each in Nike and Constellation Brands.

Earlier in the year, CNBC flagged a $500,001 to $1 million Nvidia **sale** on 18 May — five days after Secretary of State Marco Rubio reportedly briefed the president on Huang's ties to a suspected Chinese spy, a New York Times report the White House disputes.

Two things are true at once and both should be said.

The White House position is that the accounts are discretionary and run by computer-based model portfolios replicating recognised indexes such as the Schwab 1000. On that reading, 29 purchases of one stock in nine months is a rebalancing artefact, and the September sales of Comcast, McDonald's and Home Depot are index composition, not conviction — all three in fact led Wednesday's tape, which is the opposite of what a copy-trade reading predicts.

And separately, the timing overlap is real and the disclosure stops in September. It covers nothing of the crash, nothing of the reinstatement, and nothing of the 30% day. Senator Ruben Gallego has called for an investigation into positions taken before the reinstatement was announced, and the honest answer today is that October and November filings do not exist yet.

The information is in the calendar, not the direction.

## The rate is still proposed; one comment clock opened today

Nothing in the Section 301 slate of 25 November has taken effect. Comments close **5 January 2027**, hearings open 25 January, and officials have put implementation at "January at the earliest." The rates are "net of existing Section 301 tariffs," which means the headline is the total and not an increment.

One procedural thing happened today: USTR's Brazil comment period opened on 27 November for the proposed modification adding a 10% duty on Embraer commercial aircraft and higher duties on raw and refined sugar above quota — a hearing in December, announcements in January.

For an equity market, a proposed rate with a comment window is not an event. The licence is.
