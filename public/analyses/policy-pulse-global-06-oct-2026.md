## Brent Falls Back Below USD 100 as Hormuz Flows Recover

Crude moved the global tape in both directions on Tuesday, and the net direction was down. **Brent crude fell USD 1.99 to USD 98.33 a barrel** in early trading, with **US WTI giving up USD 1.83 to USD 87.60**. WSJ put December Brent down 0.4% at **USD 99.88** and December WTI down 0.8% at **USD 87.54**. Later in the session Brent recovered slightly to **USD 100.59 (+0.3%)** with WTI at **USD 89.73**, and the front month printed **USD 98.17 (−2.15)** intraday against a USD 100.23 high.

The drivers, in order of importance:

1. **Hormuz flows are recovering.** Analysts cite larger volumes crossing the Strait and a recovering **Saudi East-West pipeline**, which was out of service for several weeks after drone attacks. ING's Warren Patterson and Ewa Manthey: *"While there are growing signs of a recovery in oil flows from the Persian Gulf, the market remains nervous about potential supply disruptions from the region. This is keeping prices well-supported for now."*
2. **The G7/IEA 100-million-barrel coordinated release**, agreed October 2, is now flowing — including a frontloaded diesel tranche within 20 days.
3. **Offsetting escalation risk is still live**: fresh shipping incidents around Hormuz, renewed fighting near **Bab al-Mandeb**, and **Houthi claims of missile strikes on Abha airport** in Saudi Arabia with no Saudi confirmation. A **Panama-flagged vessel was attacked off the Oman coast**, injuring 11 Indian nationals — India has condemned the attack.
4. **OPEC+ held November production quotas unchanged.**

**The curve is the signal.** Front-month Brent near USD 98 against **February 2027 at USD 93.62** and **August 2027 at USD 85.51** is steep backwardation — the physical market is pricing near-term disruption and long-run normalisation simultaneously.

## Rates Near Multi-Decade Highs, Yet US Equities Print Records

The macro puzzle of the week: **the S&P 500 and Nasdaq hit record levels on Tuesday** even with Treasury yields near their highest since 2002. The resolution is that yields **eased off the peak**.

- The **10-year Treasury yield fell back to around 5.26%** after briefly crossing the **5.35%** mark — its highest since 2002. Yields had been near multi-decade highs on Monday.
- The Nasdaq had already closed at a **record high on Monday**, led by heavyweight technology and by stocks expected to benefit from the AI buildout, including materials and industrials; the S&P 500 rose 0.66% and the Dow 0.18% on Monday.
- Tuesday premarket futures pointed higher again (Dow +180, Nasdaq +169, S&P +32). The proximate cause of the relief: **softer-than-expected US jobs data reduced expectations of a Federal Reserve rate hike** at the October meeting. The September FOMC minutes land **October 7**.
- The **euro hit a 17-month low** on French fiscal concerns and a French government bond sell-off, with French OAT yields at their highest since 2002. The dollar is near an 18-month high.
- Separately, the **EU and China meet for trade talks in Beijing this week**, with several member states — France prominent among them — advocating a **European version of Section 301**. Beijing has warned of a strong response to "discriminatory restrictive measures," having previously retaliated with duties on European cognac and investigations into pork and dairy.

## Forecast Revisions: BMI Sees Brent Averaging USD 107 This Quarter

Two sell-side revisions published today, both upward and both premised on the Iran standoff continuing:

- **BMI Research (a Fitch Solutions unit)** raised its Brent forecasts and pushed back its expected preliminary US–Iran deal and Hormuz easing to **early 2027**. It now expects Brent to average **USD 107 a barrel in Q4 2026 and USD 112 in Q1 2027**, assuming rising demand and supply constraints erode physical market buffers. *"This assumes that rising demand for crude and continued constraints on the supply side combine to erode physical market buffers, leaving Brent increasingly exposed to renewed bouts of geopolitical escalation and unplanned production outages."*
- **Kenanga Research** raised its Brent forecasts to **USD 91 for 2026 and USD 85 for 2027**, from USD 80 and USD 74, attaching a **USD 5/barrel geopolitical premium** and expecting at least a partial US–Iran deal in 2027.

The spread between the two houses — roughly USD 16 a barrel this quarter — is itself the tradeable information: nobody has conviction on the Hormuz timeline, which is why the crude complex is trading event-driven rather than on fundamentals.
