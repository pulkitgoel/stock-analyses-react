Five things landed today and only one of them was a decision. The defence ministry asked for more money, the market regulator said it had decided nothing, the commodity margin circular takes effect in thirteen days, the central bank argued its own rescue in court, and the commerce ministry pushed the trade deal into next year.

## Twenty per cent on top of twenty-two

At FICCI's annual convention, Defence Secretary Rajesh Kumar Singh said the ministry has sought a further 20% increase in capital expenditure for the coming year, compounding a 22% rise received last year. The FY27 budget allocated ₹2.19 lakh crore for defence capital spending, against a Budget estimate of ₹1.8 lakh crore for FY26.

Then he said the thing that matters more than the ask: "End of the day, the issue is not getting budgetary outlays. You need to spend it." Contracts must be signed and delivered before the ministry takes on new commitments. The ministry has signed around ₹80,000 crore of contracts so far this year against more than ₹2 lakh crore in each of the past two years.

That gap is the single most important number in Indian defence right now. A ₹2.19 lakh crore capex line against ₹80,000 crore of contracts signed with two months left in the fiscal year is either a December compression of extraordinary size or a genuine underspend. The secretary's own framing — that the constraint is execution, not allocation — points at the second.

The structural items are more interesting than the budget ask. The draft Defence Acquisition Procedure 2026 is in the final stages of approval, and it proposes indigenous content requirements of up to 60%. Twenty-four DRDO laboratories have been opened to Indian industry, conducting around 4,000 tests a month, largely for private firms. DRDO is preparing to transfer technologies related to conventional missile systems to qualified Indian companies. Twenty-five per cent of the defence R&D budget is open to industry, start-ups and academia.

On the Advanced Medium Combat Aircraft, bids have been received from all three shortlisted bidders and the winner could be announced within a month — a private-sector company or a private-led consortium, potentially including a public-sector firm. That winner will operate the country's first private-sector-led fighter production line, initially a prototype line. On Tejas Mk2, the platform remains in the IAF plan and an Acceptance of Necessity would be considered after it flies in the middle of next year.

One target is slipping. The ₹50,000 crore defence export goal may be missed by about a year, Singh said, with exports currently going to around 80 countries.

## The regulator said nothing and the brokers fell two per cent

SEBI chairman Tuhin Kanta Pandey, speaking at the World Federation of Exchanges annual gathering in Mumbai, said the regulator continues to consider proposals to strengthen the margin trading facility but has taken no decision. On the specific question of linking leveraged positions to a stock's free float: "The thought process is always there in our workings, in different ways but whenever there is a concretised issue, we bring it up. No such decision has been taken yet."

That is a non-announcement, and it moved stocks anyway. Angel One and BSE traded up to 2% lower in the afternoon; BSE closed down 2.72%, one of the worst Nifty 50 performers. Reuters had reported on Tuesday that SEBI is weighing restrictions on fresh margin-funded purchases once leverage in a stock crosses a threshold tied to free float, with 5-10% under discussion.

The Jefferies arithmetic is why the fear is larger than the exposure. Only 4% of the MTF book sits in stocks where exposure exceeds 10% of free float. Lower the threshold to 5% and that rises to 23%. The MTF book itself has roughly doubled in two years to ₹1.5 lakh crore, and sits at about 1.2 times cash average daily turnover — comparable to China and below developed markets.

So the rule, if it comes, would bite on concentrated small and mid-cap leverage rather than on the market's aggregate. The brokers fell because their revenue is a function of the book, and any threshold is a cap on growth. The regulator's own language — "no such decision has been taken" — is the only fact available.

Pandey also closed three other doors. Market hours will not be extended: growing cyber risk means there would have to be a strong business case, and he does not see the feasibility. Securities lending and borrowing will continue on exchange platforms. And full rupee convertibility remains the hurdle to allowing dual listings in India.

## A two-point cut that only reaches agriculture

SEBI's commodity derivatives circular of 17 November takes effect on 1 December, and it is narrower than most of the coverage suggests.

Minimum initial margin for agricultural commodities falls from 8%, 10% and 12% for low, medium and high volatility to 6%, 8% and 10%. Minimum margin period of risk falls from three, three and four days to two, two and three. Non-agricultural commodities are unchanged at 6%, 8% and 10% and two, two and three days. The effect is to align agriculture with non-agriculture, and the framework runs for one year unless extended.

One outlet reported the change as a cut "to 2 per cent from 6 per cent." That is wrong. Five separate reports — Livemint, Moneycontrol, Tribune, ET Legal and BusinessLine's own later piece — plus the circular itself give 6%, 8% and 10% against the old 8%, 10% and 12%. The correct read is a two-percentage-point cut, one MPOR day, agriculture only. Anyone trading off the 2% number would have sized a position on a figure that does not exist.

The commercial logic is a deliberate revival attempt. The cut follows representations from stakeholders and recommendations from the working group reviewing agri-commodity norms and the Risk Management Review Committee. Lower margin means lower hedging cost and more participation in a segment that has been shrinking. MCX is the direct beneficiary as the dominant commodity derivatives venue; the impact is on volumes rather than on any single contract.

## The central bank argues its own rescue

In a court filing reviewed by Reuters, the RBI warned that a Supreme Court ruling reversing the 2020 write-off of Yes Bank's additional tier-1 bonds would undermine bank rescue rules. Restoring ₹8,414 crore of liabilities would dilute capital injected by SBI and other rescue investors and weaken depositor protections, the central bank said.

The RBI also rejected the court's characterisation of it as a "mere bystander" during the collapse, saying it had repeatedly pushed the lender to raise capital, appointed an additional director and intervened when the bank became insolvent.

This is a structural question rather than a Yes Bank question. AT1 bonds are designed to absorb losses precisely when a bank is in trouble; if a write-off executed under that design can be reversed by a court years later, the loss-absorption feature is not a feature, and every future resolution gets more expensive for the taxpayer and the surviving banks. The listed read sits with SBI as the largest rescue investor, and with the AT1 market generally rather than with Yes Bank's own equity.

## The trade deal that will not be signed this year

Commerce Secretary Rajesh Agrawal said on Wednesday that the actual signing of the India-US trade deal will still "take some time," although the negotiation and the legal text will be completed soon.

The reason he gave is the important part. Signing can happen only after the United States concludes its Section 301 investigations into its trade partners and finalises its tariff structure. The framework was announced against an executive tariff architecture that no longer exists: the tariff on India was 50%, and in February the Supreme Court struck down the legal foundation of those tariffs. A new architecture is being built, and India's deal has to land inside it.

Piyush Goyal said a few days ago that the US has acknowledged India will receive lower tariffs than its competitors. That is a commitment about relativity, not a number, and it cannot be priced until the Section 301 outcomes are published.
