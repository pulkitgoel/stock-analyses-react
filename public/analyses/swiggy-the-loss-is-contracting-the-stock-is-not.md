## The short answer

The premise worth testing first: **is the loss contracting?** The answer depends entirely on which comparison you make, and the three give different verdicts.

| Comparison | Q2 FY27 | Base | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Year on year | -₹922 Cr | -₹1,092 Cr | **improved 15.6%** |
| Quarter on quarter | -₹922 Cr | -₹791 Cr | **worsened 16.6%** |
| FY27 H1 vs FY26 H1 | -₹1,713 Cr | -₹2,289 Cr | **improved 25.2%** |
| Full-year FY26 vs FY25 | -₹4,154 Cr | -₹3,117 Cr | **worsened 33%** |

So the honest statement is this: **the annual loss grew every year through FY26 as Instamart scaled, and the contraction only began in FY27 — where the half-year loss is 25% lower than a year ago, but the latest quarter went backwards.**

That is the whole argument in one line. The direction of travel over six months is right. The direction of travel over three months is wrong. And a stock does not bottom on a six-month trend when the three-month trend is accelerating the wrong way.

**One segment generates 97% of the group's losses.** That is the company in a sentence.

## 2. Where the loss comes from

Reconstructing the consolidated adjusted EBITDA from the segments:

| Segment | Adj. EBITDA | % of GOV/NOV |
|---|---|---|
| Food delivery | +₹341 Cr | +3.4% |
| Instamart (quick commerce) | -₹895 Cr | -9.7% of GOV |
| Out-of-home | +₹8 Cr | +0.5% |
| Corporate and unallocated | ~-₹214 Cr | — |
| **Consolidated** | **-₹760 Cr** | — |

Two things follow.

The good news is structural: **food delivery is now a genuinely profitable business.** 3.4% adjusted EBITDA margin on ₹10,054 crore of quarterly GOV, with management's long-term target at 5%. At the current GOV, every 100 basis points of margin is ₹100 crore a quarter. Going from 3.4% to 5% is worth roughly ₹650 crore a year, and that alone would not be enough to offset Instamart.

The bad news is arithmetic: Instamart's ₹895 crore quarterly loss is larger than food delivery's ₹341 crore profit by a factor of 2.6. Even if food delivery reaches its 5% target tomorrow, Swiggy still loses money.

## 3. Instamart: has the loss actually peaked?

Management's central claim, from the Q2 shareholder letter: **"We believe the adjusted EBITDA losses to have peaked out in Q2FY27."** The guidance attached to it:

- ~100 basis points of sequential margin improvement in Q3 FY27
- Contribution margin between 0 and -100 bps of GOV in Q3, improving after
- An inventory-led operating model worth ~80 bps once fully implemented
- Completion of that transition over two to three quarters

If that guidance is delivered exactly, Instamart's path looks like this:

The margin is quoted on **GOV (₹9,230 crore)**, not on NOV. So the same guidance, applied to a GOV base growing about 5% a quarter:

| Quarter | Adj. EBITDA margin on GOV | Guided loss |
|---|---|---|
| Q2 FY27 (actual) | -9.7% | -₹895 Cr |
| Q3 FY27 (guided) | ~-8.7% | ~-₹845 Cr |
| Q4 FY27 | ~-7.7% | ~-₹785 Cr |
| Q2 FY28 | ~-5.7% | ~-₹645 Cr |
| Q4 FY28 | ~-3.7% | ~-₹465 Cr |

That is a credible glide path, and it is why the sell-side is still 30-100% above the current price. But three caveats matter more than the guidance itself.

**The guidance is a rate, not a destination.** "100 bps a quarter" is management's own framing, and it implies roughly two years before Instamart reaches contribution break-even on a consolidated basis. Anything that interrupts the rate — a competitive price war, a store-expansion step-up, a festive-season discount cycle — pushes break-even out by quarters, and the market discounts the endpoint, not the direction.

**Contribution margin is already near zero.** At -1.0% of GOV in Q2, the *contribution* line is almost break-even; the ₹895 crore loss sits in fixed costs, dark-store overheads and growth investment below contribution. That is a better position than it looks, but it also means the next leg of improvement has to come from operating leverage — more orders per store — rather than from pricing.

**Q2 was supposed to be the peak, and Q2 was worse than Q1.** Instamart's loss went from ₹849 crore to ₹895 crore year-on-year. The "peaked" claim is untested until Q3 prints.

## 4. The store-count gap is the strategic problem

This is the number that decides the terminal economics, and it is not in the earnings release narrative.

| | Instamart (Swiggy) | Blinkit (Eternal) |
|---|---|---|
| Dark stores, Sep 2026 | **1,240** | — |
| Store target, Mar 2027 | not guided publicly | **3,500** |
| Store target, Mar 2028 | — | **~5,000** |
| Area | 5.2 million sq ft | — |
| Cities | 133 | — |

Blinkit is targeting roughly **three times** Instamart's current store count within eighteen months. Swiggy added 69 stores in Q2 — equal to its entire addition over the previous three quarters — which is acceleration, but from a base that is a third the size.

Quick commerce economics are density economics. A dark store becomes contribution-positive when orders per store cross a threshold; below it, every order carries a fixed-cost share that pricing cannot recover. Swiggy is therefore in the position of needing to *add* stores to compete on speed and selection while each new store dilutes near-term margin. That is the opposite of what the "losses have peaked" narrative implies, unless the cohort of mature stores is carrying the P&L.

Eternal's Q2 is the proof of what maturity looks like: **₹125 crore of consolidated profit, up 92% year-on-year, on ₹24,138 crore of revenue, up 78%.** The market now has a profitable comparable in the same industry, in the same quarter, and it is repricing Swiggy against it. That, more than the ₹922 crore loss itself, is why the stock fell 13.6% today.

## 5. The inventory-led switch

Instamart is moving from a marketplace model to an **inventory-led (1P) model**, beginning in mid-Q3 FY27 and completing over two to three quarters. Swiggy has the approvals to qualify as an Indian-owned and controlled company, which is the precondition for holding inventory directly under FDI rules.

Why this matters for the future P&L:

- **Margin:** guided +80 bps to contribution once fully implemented, from better sourcing, private-label control and fewer intermediary layers.
- **Working capital:** it inverts the model. A marketplace collects from customers and pays brands later; an inventory-led business buys stock before it sells it. Working capital days, currently 66 and rising, will get worse before they get better.
- **Capital intensity:** dark stores, cold chain and inventory become Swiggy's balance-sheet risk rather than a partner's.

The strategic logic is sound — private label is the only durable margin lever in quick commerce, and private label requires control of assortment. But it is a transition that consumes cash at exactly the point when the company is asking investors to believe the losses have peaked. **Watch working capital and inventory in the Q3 and Q4 balance sheets; that is where a botched transition shows up first.**

## 6. Toing: the option nobody is pricing

Toing, the standalone app for affordable meals, is now in 50+ cities. The disclosure worth noting: **two out of three new Toing users were new to the food delivery category.** Management believes Toing can lift combined Swiggy-plus-Toing marketplace growth to 25-30%.

If that is right, it is the most interesting thing in the release, because it expands the market rather than taking share in it. Indian food delivery has been stuck at roughly 20% growth for the addressable urban cohort; a lower-ticket format reaches a customer who was never going to pay ₹400 for a delivery order.

The risk is that low-ticket delivery is structurally low-margin — smaller order values, similar delivery cost. Toing is being run as a separate app with separate economics for exactly that reason. Treat it as a free option, not as the base case.

## 7. Building the FY28 P&L: three scenarios

This is the part that matters for anyone buying today, because at ₹183 you are not buying FY27 — you are buying the FY28 shape.

**Assumptions common to all three:** food delivery GOV grows 15% to ~₹46,000 crore for FY28, margin reaches 4.0% (between the current 3.4% and the 5% target), giving ~₹1,850 crore of EBITDA; out-of-home ₹7,500 crore at 0.7%; corporate costs ₹900 crore; Instamart GOV grows 25% to ~₹46,000 crore, with the margin measured on GOV as the company reports it.

| Scenario | Instamart margin on GOV | FD EBITDA | Instamart EBITDA | Consolidated EBITDA |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Bull | -1.0% | ₹1,850 Cr | -₹460 Cr | **+₹543 Cr** |
| Base | -3.0% | ₹1,850 Cr | -₹1,385 Cr | **-₹382 Cr** |
| Bear | -6.0% | ₹1,850 Cr | -₹2,760 Cr | **-₹1,757 Cr** |

Three conclusions:

**Consolidated breakeven needs Instamart at roughly -2% of GOV**, given the food delivery profit and the corporate cost base. Management's own glide path reaches about -1.7% by Q4 FY28 — so the *exit* run-rate in FY28 can be around breakeven while the *full year* is still negative. On management's own schedule, the first genuinely profitable full year is FY29, not FY28.

**The company is not one decision away from profit; it is one segment away.** Food delivery's ₹1,840 crore of EBITDA is already sufficient to carry the corporate cost base and out-of-home. Everything depends on how deep Instamart's structural loss is at scale.

**The bear case is not catastrophic, but it is expensive.** At -6%, Swiggy loses ₹1,757 crore in FY28 against ~₹14,000 crore of cash — survivable, and with food delivery still compounding. This is not a balance-sheet solvency story. It is a "how long does the market wait" story.

## 8. Cash and runway

- **Cash balance: ~₹14,000 crore as of Q1 FY27** (brokerage estimate; the company raised ₹10,000 crore through a QIP to fund Instamart).
- **Borrowings: ₹2,514 crore** (Sep 2026), so net cash is roughly **₹11,500 crore**.
- **Equity capital ₹262 crore, reserves ₹16,708 crore** — net worth ₹16,970 crore, book value ~₹65 a share.
- **FY26 operating cash flow: -₹2,898 crore**, with free cash flow -₹3,809 crore.

At the current burn of roughly ₹800-900 crore a quarter, that is **three to four years of runway** without another raise. Even the bear scenario in section 7 does not threaten solvency. This is the strongest single argument against the panic: the company cannot run out of money before FY29 on any plausible path.

What it *can* run out of is patience. The QIP was raised at a much higher price; the stock at ₹183 is 58% below its 52-week high and 70% below its all-time high of ₹617. Any future equity raise would be heavily dilutive, which is precisely why management will push the "losses peaked" line hard.

## 13. Bull and bear

**Bull case**

- The loss is contracting year-on-year: H1 FY27 ₹1,713 Cr against ₹2,289 Cr, a 25% improvement.
- Food delivery is genuinely profitable — record ₹341 Cr at 3.4% of GOV, heading to a 5% target worth ~₹2,000 Cr a year.
- Instamart's contribution margin is already -1.0% of GOV, and its adjusted EBITDA margin is -9.7% of GOV; the gap is fixed cost, which shrinks with density.
- Management guides losses peaked, with ~100 bps of improvement per quarter on a GOV base.
- ~₹14,000 Cr of cash and ~₹11,500 Cr net cash — no solvency risk before FY29.
- At 1.2x EV/revenue against Eternal's ~5x, the market has already priced a lot of failure.
- Toing is expanding the category, not just taking share: two of three new users were new to food delivery.

**Bear case**

- The loss widened sequentially, and Instamart's loss widened year-on-year. "Peaked" is a claim, not a result.
- Blinkit is targeting 3,500 stores by March 2027 against Instamart's 1,240 — a structural scale gap in a density business.
- Eternal made ₹125 Cr of profit in the same quarter; the profitable comparable now exists and repricing against it is rational.
- The inventory-led switch consumes working capital during a loss-making transition.
- Consolidated breakeven needs Instamart at roughly -2% of GOV — a 770 bps improvement — and on management's own glide path that is an FY29 outcome, not FY28.
- FII holding has fallen from 16.07% to 8.86% in three quarters; institutional money is leaving.

## 14. Verdict

**AVOID THE ENTRY. THE THESIS IS REAL; THE TIMING IS NOT.**

Two separate judgements, deliberately kept apart.

As a *business trajectory*, the future prospects are better than the price implies. Food delivery is a profitable, cash-generating franchise. Instamart's contribution margin is near breakeven, the fixed-cost base is the problem rather than the unit economics, and there is enough cash to see the plan through to FY29 without distress. A company that reaches consolidated breakeven in FY28 and grows revenue 30% along the way is worth materially more than ₹47,900 crore.

As a *trade*, the stock is at an all-time low, in a strong downtrend, with 12 checklist fails and no base. Buying a stock at ₹175 when its own all-time high is ₹617 is not value investing; it is catching a fall and calling it conviction.

**What to watch before committing capital:**

1. **Q3 FY27 Instamart adjusted EBITDA.** If it comes in at or better than -₹800 crore (roughly -8.7% of NOV), management's "peaked" claim has one supporting data point. If it is flat or worse, the guidance is a promise that has already failed once.
2. **Contribution margin at or above 0%.** The single cleanest indicator that the unit economics work.
3. **A weekly close above ₹236** (the 20-day SMA) with above-average volume — the first objective evidence that the downtrend has stopped.
4. **Working capital and inventory** in the Q3 balance sheet, as the inventory-led model begins.
5. **Blinkit's store count at March 2027** against Instamart's — the competitive gap either narrows or it does not.

**Levels for the patient:**

- **First real accumulation zone: ₹150-175** — only with a confirmed base, not on the way down
- **Confirmation entry: above ₹236 on a weekly close with volume**
- **Do not add** while price makes lower highs; the current sequence has no higher low since October 2025
- **Bear extension: below ₹175**, the stock enters price discovery with no technical support; the SOTP bear case at ₹143 is the reference

**For an investor with a three-year horizon who can tolerate a further 30% drawdown**, the risk/reward at ₹183 is genuinely asymmetric: the SOTP midpoint is ~₹220 and the bull case ~₹292 against a bear case ~₹143. But the honest sequencing is to wait for the Q3 print on Instamart, because that single number decides which of those three scenarios you are in.

## 15. What would change this view

**Upgrade to constructive:** Q3 Instamart adj. EBITDA loss at or below ₹800 Cr, contribution margin at or above 0%, and a weekly close above ₹236.
