## Day 193: Tanker War Escalation + Saudi Facility Attacks Push Brent Toward $100

The Hormuz conflict re-escalated over the weekend and crude is again the dominant global macro driver. **US forces struck three Iranian oil tankers on Sep 5** (two disabled, one destroyed — M/T Downy near Kharg Island) after Iran fired ballistic missiles at two US Navy warships; Tehran called it a "war crime." Iran-aligned **Houthi forces then claimed attacks on Saudi energy facilities** (Sep 7-8), forcing temporary operating halts, and US Defense Secretary Hegseth warned Washington will "destroy (and sink)" Iranian tankers that fire on US vessels while Iran's Parliament Speaker warned "strike our assets and you get struck."

**Brent crude: ~$97.2-98.4 (+1.4% Tuesday), third straight gain, six-week high; WTI ~$92.5-94.** The front-month contract trades at roughly a **$20 premium over late-2027** — record backwardation — and US gasoline prices hit record highs over the Labor Day weekend. **Goldman Sachs raised Brent/WTI forecasts by $5 to $85/$80 (Dec-2026)** and now expects Gulf shipping disruptions to persist into 2027 (Brent $80 / WTI $75 in 2027), with production recovering only in H2 2027. President Trump posted that oil "will drop precipitously ... when we WIN the war with Iran." An Iran-Oman arrangement to manage Hormuz shipping is reportedly under discussion but has failed to ease supply fears.

## India: Crude-Import Vulnerability Bites — Rupee's Sharpest Fall in a Month

India imports ~85% of its crude requirement, so every dollar of Brent above ~$90 is a direct macro tax. Tuesday's reaction: the **rupee slid 0.35% to 94.81/USD** (sharpest single-day fall in over a month) and oil-linked sectors dragged the Nifty to its lowest close since mid-June (Sensex -555 to 75,577.58, Nifty -144 to 23,635.10). Defensive and domestic-demand sectors outperformed (pharma +0.7%, FMCG +0.35%, media +1.3%), while banks, O&G and IT fell. FIIs were net buyers Monday (~Rs 280 cr cash) after three straight sessions of selling — a fragile stabilization if crude keeps climbing.
