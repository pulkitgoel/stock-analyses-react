The market was shut. The news was not.

Thursday was Thanksgiving, so US cash equities and Treasury cash trading were both closed and Friday is a half session to 1 p.m. ET. Every price in this article is Wednesday's settle, and it is labelled as such.

## Nothing was collected, and the instrument that bit was a licence

The Section 301 determinations published on Wednesday morning against 14 economies are now in their comment window — comments close 5 January 2027, hearings open 25 January, and officials put implementation at "probably January at the earliest." China's foreign ministry called excess capacity a "false narrative" and a pretext for political manipulation. The European Union and China both criticised the slate. Brazil picked up the only genuine escalation: a new 10% duty on Embraer commercial aircraft and higher duties on raw and refined sugar above quota, after Trade Representative Jamieson Greer said months of talks had made "no meaningful progress."

None of that is a trade yet. The thing that actually bound on Thursday was a licence.

Nvidia has halted shipments of AI chips into South-East Asia, as reported in trade press today — the compliance landing of the 16 November interim final rule that put licences on advanced AI chips and servers going to Singapore, Malaysia, Indonesia, Thailand and Brazil. A tariff is a price. A licence is a refusal, and a refusal cannot be negotiated down by an importer.

That difference is the whole of this week's AI de-rating. Three instruments in eight days: the 16 November remote-access rule, the 23 November Entity List addition of Megaspeed and six affiliates, and now a shipment halt. None of them is about demand.

S&P Global Ratings put Nvidia's AA issuer credit rating on CreditWatch negative, citing management and governance. Moody's held its Aa1.

Nvidia closed Wednesday at $149.30, down 3.91%, and is 9.5% below Monday's $165.05.

Every other item on the list this week is a distribution question or a disclosure question. ## The disclosure says he sold the three names that then led

The 25-page periodic transaction report — signed 1 November, received by the ethics office 5 November, released 19 November — has now been read line by line, and the September entries are the ones that carry information.

On 23 September the accounts sold $1 million to $5 million each of McDonald's, Home Depot and Comcast, plus one other stock: $4 million to $20 million across the four. On 24 September they bought SpaceX, between $16,002 and $65,000. The largest single purchase in the report was $5 million to $25 million of the Vanguard Dividend Appreciation ETF.

Those three sales are the interesting line, because on Wednesday — the session where AI fell and the Dow rose — McDonald's closed +1.00%, Home Depot +2.14% and Comcast +1.38%. The manager rotated out of exactly the cash-generative defensives that then outperformed. That is a fact about index replication, not about conviction, and it cuts against the copy-trade reading rather than for it.

Nvidia remains the position. Two purchases on 2 September, up to $350,000 combined, landed on the same day Nvidia signed its agreement to acquire Hugging Face for about $12.9 billion, which was announced the following day. Reuters counts the September Nvidia activity at up to $265,000 bought and up to $15,000 sold; Newsweek counts up to $380,000 bought. The range-based reporting format is why the two numbers differ, and neither is large enough to be a thesis.

The wider counts: the Washington Post finds 44 Nvidia trades between January and September — 29 buys, 15 sells, $6-17 million bought, $4-12 million sold, and $6-71 million held as of 23 September. CNBC's count across the whole second term is 103 trades worth $27-97.5 million. The Financial Times puts the gain from the Huang reinstatement at $1.3-15 million.

Two process points, neither of which is a signal. The STOCK Act requires covered transactions to be reported within 30 days of the filer learning of them and no later than 45 days after execution; transactions from 1 September, signed on 1 November and released on 19 November sit outside that window. And the White House position is unchanged — third-party discretionary accounts replicating the Schwab 1000, with no ability for the president or his family to direct, influence or provide input.

An index account that buys the same stock 29 times in nine months is a rebalancing artefact. That is precisely why it should not be traded on, and it is also why the disclosure keeps producing headlines that the tape ignores.

## Crypto: the bill reached the floor and the money left the funds

Two things moved on the crypto file and they point opposite ways.

The American Reserve Modernization Act, H.R. 8957, cleared the House Financial Services Committee 28-21 on 24 November and was placed on the House calendar the same day. It would write the Strategic Bitcoin Reserve into law, lock the government's 329,693 coins for at least 20 years, require proof-of-reserve attestations and independent audits, and bar the coins from being pledged as collateral. The vote was on party lines, the Democrats take both chambers in January, and Polymarket has under 5% on a statutory reserve before 2027. A bill that removes a seller without adding a buyer is not a bid.

The money: US spot bitcoin ETFs have lost a record $5.39 billion net in November through the 25th, more than any month since the funds launched in January 2024. Bitcoin is down 21.7% in November and was trading near $60,900 on Thursday.

On the policy side, Senators Elizabeth Warren and Chris Van Hollen wrote to SEC chair Paul Atkins on 25 November asking him to withdraw the Regulation Crypto Assets framework and the tokenized-securities initiative. Maxine Waters, who takes the Financial Services gavel in January, marked Trump's latest $TRUMP holders' dinner with the Stop TRUMP in Crypto Act, which would bar the president, vice president, members of Congress and their immediate families from issuing, promoting or profiting from crypto. She put the memecoin's peak at "around $74", its current value at "less than $1", and the president's royalties at $635 million in the past year.

Two dated items sit on Trump Media itself: holders of the May 2028 convertibles can put the notes on 30 November, and the shareholder vote on the TAE Technologies merger is 14 December.
