The Prime Minister is in Manila for two summits. The trade half of the visit has no date attached. The defence half already has a number attached to it, and the number is sixty.

Modi left on Monday for the 23rd ASEAN-India Summit and the 21st East Asia Summit, both on 17 November, at the invitation of Philippine President Ferdinand Marcos Jr. The departure statement is a single paragraph and names no company and no deal. What gives the visit a stock read is the briefing that preceded it.

## What was actually said

On the eve of the visit, the Ministry of External Affairs' Secretary (East), Rudrendra Tandon, told reporters that India is committed to supporting Manila's military modernisation and coastal defence networks, and that the BrahMos supersonic cruise missile partnership is a central pillar of that support. He also set out India's position on a South China Sea code of conduct: it must comply with international law, including UNCLOS, and protect freedom of navigation.

The Philippines was the first foreign buyer of BrahMos, signing a $375 million agreement in 2022 for three shore-based anti-ship missile batteries. Defence reporting in November 2026 indicates Manila is exploring a follow-on order for up to sixty additional missiles — a materially larger requirement than the original purchase.

That follow-on order is the story. A summit is a photograph; a second order is a revenue line.

## Why the listed exposure is indirect, and smaller than it looks

Here is the part that has to be stated plainly: **BrahMos Aerospace is not listed.** It is a joint venture between the DRDO and Russia's NPO Mashinostroyeniya. Neither the MEA briefing nor any PIB release names a listed supplier for this order, and no contract has been signed.

So the tickers below are my inference from the industrial chain around the missile programme, not from any disclosure. Treat them as such:

- **Bharat Dynamics** is the listed missile house with the closest read-across to Indian missile order flow, and the Sep 2026 Rs 810.79 crore SAT-SAAW order for the IAF shows the procurement channel is live.
- **Bharat Electronics** supplies seekers and electronics into Indian missile programmes, and states a capacity of over 1,000 missile seekers a year.
- **Solar Industries** is the listed propellant and explosives supplier to the defence sector, which is the input stage of any missile order.
- **HAL** is the integration platform for several of the air-launched programmes, and separately told reporters on 16 November that an AMCA prototype winner would be announced within a month and a Tejas squadron stood up by March 2027 — a nearer-term catalyst than the Philippines order.

The order itself, if signed, would sit inside an unlisted JV. The listed beneficiaries would be subsystem suppliers on a much smaller revenue base than the headline $375 million implies.

## The trade half is where the numbers are, and where the dates are not

India's trade with ASEAN is around $122 billion. The stated agenda for the summit is a review of the trade agreement, deeper connectivity and maritime cooperation.

That is a real agenda and it has produced nothing with a date on it this week. An FTA review that has been running for years is not a policy event until a notification issues, and no notification has. The part of the visit that can move a stock is the missile line, and the part that can move a stock has no signature yet.

One item to flag without over-reading: a Quad leaders' meeting in Manila involving Modi, Trump, Japan's Sanae Takaichi and Australia's Anthony Albanese has been discussed in Indian coverage but is **not formally confirmed**. If it happens, the read-across is diplomatic rather than sectoral, and I would not build a position on a meeting that has not been scheduled.

## What would change the read

Three things, in order of importance:

1. **A signed contract.** A repeat order of up to sixty missiles would be a named procurement with a value, and would convert this article from inference to fact. Watch PIB's defence releases and the MoD's contract announcements, not the summit communiqué.
2. **A production line announcement in the Philippines.** New Delhi's stated offer includes support for Manila's defence infrastructure. Local assembly or a maintenance facility would be a larger and longer-lived revenue stream than the missiles themselves.
3. **Anything on the FTA review with a notification number.** Until then, the $122 billion figure is context, not a catalyst.
