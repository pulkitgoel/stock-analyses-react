Every trade remedy does two things at once: it raises a wall in one direction and invites one in the other. Today both happened, in two capitals, using the same instrument.

## India: a recommendation, which is not yet a duty

The Commerce Ministry's investigation arm recommended definitive anti-dumping duties of up to **$565 per one lakh pieces** on medical examination rubber gloves from Thailand and Malaysia, for five years. The recommendation follows a dumping probe in Case No. AD (OI)-38/2025 and takes effect only when the Central Government issues the notification. Until that notification, it is a finding, not a cost.

Four days earlier the same authority recommended a five-year extension of the anti-dumping duty on untreated fumed silica from China, at unchanged rates, in final findings dated 6 November on an application from Cabot Sanmar. Chinese exporters face dumping margins of up to 160%. That recommendation also waits on a notification.

Both findings protect producers who are largely not investable through the exchange. India's examination-glove capacity is fragmented and mostly unlisted; Cabot Sanmar, the sole domestic fumed-silica producer, is a joint venture. There is no clean listed beneficiary, and pretending otherwise is exactly the failure mode this series was built to avoid. What is investable is the other side of the ledger: every user of fumed silica — silicones, adhesives, paints, tyres — now faces a protected input price.

The honest read of the gloves duty is narrower still. It is a hospital-supply-cost measure wearing industrial policy's clothes.

## The United States: orders, which are a cost today

At the other end of the same instrument, the US Department of Commerce published anti-dumping duty orders on crystalline silicon photovoltaic cells from India, Indonesia and Laos, applicable 9 November.

The Indian numbers are not marginal:

- A **dumping margin of 123.04%** for every Indian producer, set on facts available with adverse inferences.
- A **cash deposit rate of 107.17%** after subsidy offsets.
- A **countervailing duty of 126.09%**.
- A combined wall near **249%**, which stacks on existing executive tariffs and Section 232 minimum-import-price floors rather than replacing them.

The order names Mundra Solar PV Limited, Mundra Solar Energy Limited, Kowa Company and Premier Energies Photovoltaic Private Limited, all at the same 123.04%. The "All Others" rate is also 123.04% — which means not being named is not protection. The case ran from an August 2025 petition by the Alliance for American Solar Manufacturing and Trade, through a September 2026 final determination, to the ITC's affirmative injury finding notified on 2 November.

Separately, Waaree Energies received a final CBP evasion determination on 29 October in EAPA Consolidated Case 8163, affirming a June finding on historical entries routed through Vietnam, with the exposure already provided for under Ind AS 37. Different case, same consequence: the US door is closing from two directions at once.

The second US action published today is earlier in its life. Commerce initiated less-than-fair-value investigations into seamless carbon and alloy steel standard, line and pressure pipe from **India and Georgia**, on petitions filed 15 October by Tenaris USA, Vallourec Star and the United Steelworkers, with a countervailing petition on India attached. The period of investigation for India is 1 October 2025 to 30 September 2026. Scope comments are due 24 November, rebuttals 4 December. No duty exists yet; the cost so far is legal, and the risk is a preliminary margin in the first half of 2027.

## Why the two halves belong in one article

India is simultaneously complainant and respondent. Its own remedies protect small, unlisted, import-competing producers. The remedies aimed at it hit its most export-visible manufacturers — solar cells and seamless pipe — which are listed, capital-intensive, and priced off US realisations.

A domestic market can absorb redirected solar volume. It cannot absorb it at US prices. That is the second-order trade worth writing down: if Indian cells and modules stay home, module prices soften, and the beneficiaries are the developers buying them rather than the factories making them.

## What to watch

- **24 November and 4 December** — scope and rebuttal comment deadlines in the seamless pipe case.
- **First half of 2027** — preliminary margins in the pipe case. Until then, US revenue is a litigation variable for Jindal Saw and Maharashtra Seamless.
- The solar order is already live: cash deposits apply from today on entries, and no appeal changes the deposit.
