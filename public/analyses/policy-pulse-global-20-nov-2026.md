The index that was up about 25% for the year is down 17.5% for the week.

Taiwan's TAIEX fell 2,393.60 points, or 6.23%, to 36,033.29 on Friday — its third-largest one-day point drop on record and a seven-month low. Over the week it lost 7,641.83 points, or 17.5%, its steepest five-day decline since 1990, and it now sits roughly 28% below its record near 50,000 in early October. Taiwan's exchange alone lost $1.12 trillion of market value from that peak, according to LSEG data.

Inside those indices: TSMC closed down 4.76% at NT$2,000 and was heading for its worst week since October 1997; MediaTek fell 9.95% and Foxconn 5.4%. SoftBank dropped 14.5% in Tokyo, its steepest daily fall in more than two years, with Advantest and Kioxia in double digits. Samsung Electronics fell 7.1% and SK Hynix 6.6%, and the Korea Exchange briefly halted trading.

The trigger was Anthropic's listing, in the sense that the listing was supposed to end the argument and did the opposite. A record $81.3 billion raise at $83 a share, a 34% opening pop to $111.50, a first-day close at $97.17 — and by Friday morning the stock was $86.43, down 11.0% on the day and about 21% below its first trade, though still 4.1% above the IPO price. Retail bought $18.6 million net in the opening half hour against SpaceX's $25.6 million, on $255 million of turnover, more than double. The listing succeeded. The sector did not.

## The reason is funding, not models

Nvidia reported a strong quarter this week and the stock fell anyway. NVDA traded at $138.54 on Friday, down 5.09%, below every regular-session close since 5 June 2025, and down about 28% in November. AMD lost 7.72% on Thursday to $489.19 and a further 8.09% on Friday; Micron fell 9.92%, Broadcom 7.89%, TSMC's ADR 6.00% and ASML 4.40%. CoreWeave is down 18% this month after its bond yields traded above 16% earlier in November.

Capital Economics put the argument in one line: the boom is becoming "increasingly unsustainable", with valuations and investment plans resting on profit expectations that may not arrive, and it projects a roughly 30% US peak-to-trough decline by end-2027, a similar fall in Taiwan and 50% in Korea. That is a forecast and should be labelled as one. The observable fact underneath it is that the AI buildout still needs enormous funding while financing costs are rising and credit stress is appearing at the leveraged end of the complex.

## Two policy instruments could turn a de-rate into an order cut

This is where a valuation story becomes a policy story.

Washington tightened scrutiny of data-centre operators in Singapore and four other countries in an effort to curb illegal AI chip exports to China, and separately is weighing forcing US companies to disclose their use of Chinese AI. Both are administrative measures rather than statutes, which makes them fast.

The distinction matters for anyone holding the Indian IT complex. A multiple compression in semiconductors does not reach Indian services revenue. A licensing regime that restricts where compute can be deployed does, because it caps the demand that hyperscalers are allowed to serve.

## India was the counterparty on Friday

The Nifty rose 1.12% to 25,800.60 and the Sensex 1.08% to 83,919.71, both at nine-month closing highs, with fourth consecutive weekly gains of about 2.9% and 2.6%. Nifty IT rose 1.30% to 31,248.65 while the Nasdaq fell 2%. Nifty Bank made a fresh high at 64,579.50, up 2.39%.

Foreign portfolio investors have put more than $12 billion into Indian equities in November — ₹84,291 crore in the first half of the month, more than half of it into banks and financials. The rupee closed at 92.67 against 93.29, its strongest since 20 April and Asia's best-performing currency this month at +1.75%, even with the dollar near a 19-month high.

The honest caveat is in the breadth. On the BSE, 2,456 stocks fell against 1,582 that rose. The Nifty Midcap 100 added 0.66% while the Smallcap 100 lost 0.13% and the Microcap 250 fell 0.87%. India VIX rose 7.52% to 17.32. An index at a nine-month high on negative breadth, carried by four banks and two hospital chains, is a flow trade. It is a good flow trade — the money is real and it is rotating out of the most crowded trade in the world — but it is not evidence that Indian earnings have decoupled from global compute spending.

## The other assets

Gold futures fell 3.9% to $3,542 an ounce, extending a slide that has taken bullion to one-year lows on hardening rate-hike bets; silver fell close to 5%. Bitcoin traded around $60,245, its lowest since early July. The 10-year Treasury yield slipped back below 5% as money sought safety, and the two-year fell 8.7 basis points to 4.690%.

A gold-and-bitcoin selloff alongside a Treasury rally is not a liquidity crisis. It is a rotation into duration and out of everything that had been bought as an inflation hedge.
