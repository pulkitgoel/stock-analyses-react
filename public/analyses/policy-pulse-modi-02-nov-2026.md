The Prime Minister inaugurated a renewable energy summit today and the headlines will be about 310 gigawatts. The number that actually matters is 45.

India now has **225 GW of solar module manufacturing capacity and 45 GW of solar cell capacity**. Modules are no longer the constraint. Cells are. That is the difference between a subsidy story and a supply-chain story, and it is the difference between owning a solar stock on capacity and owning it on margin.

## First: the summit, and the two schemes inside it

**Prime Minister Narendra Modi inaugurated the Bharat Renewable Energy Summit & Expo 2026 at Bharat Mandapam today.** The event runs **2-5 November** and is unusually broad this year: it coincides with the **Ninth Assembly of the International Solar Alliance** and includes delegates from the **Fourth Conference on Green Hydrogen**.

The PM's own remarks were directional rather than fiscal. He argued that recent global energy shocks have demonstrated a direct link between energy security and economic security, warned that continued over-reliance on fossil fuels will keep disrupting the world economy because reserves are "finite and concentrated in a few regions," and said plainly: **"the future of Energy Security lies in Clean Energy."** His specific ask was to startups, IITs and entrepreneurs — build **round-the-clock, all-weather solar models** that cover the maximum energy needs of a household, including rooftop solar cooking to cut imported gas and home solar charging for electric two- and four-wheelers.

The money came from Union Minister **Pralhad Joshi**, and two items are new enough to matter:

| Scheme | Outlay | What it builds |
|---|---|---|
| **Pradhan Mantri Surya Sarovar Yojana** | **more than ₹5,000 crore** | **5,000 MW of floating solar** capacity |
| **Green Energy Corridor Phase-III** | **₹1.86 lakh crore** investment | transmission to evacuate the renewable build-out |

The rest of Joshi's data set is the cumulative record, and it is worth quoting because the ratios are the argument:

- **Non-fossil installed capacity at 310 GW**, up from **200 GW in September 2024** and **80.3 GW twelve years ago** — a rise of 280%.
- **Solar 2.8 GW → 171 GW**, up 5,800%. **Wind 21 GW → 60 GW**, up 176%.
- **Solar module manufacturing 2 GW → 225 GW** (up 11,150%). **Solar cell manufacturing 0 → 45 GW.** **Wind turbine manufacturing 10 GW → 24 GW.**
- **Solar tariffs fell from ₹10-11 per unit in 2011-12 to ₹2.15 per unit in 2024-25.**
- **Green hydrogen at ₹279/kg and green ammonia at ₹49.75/kg — both claimed as the lowest prices globally.** India is first in the world in green hydrogen offtake commitments.
- **Total RE investment above $67 billion in the last two years**, the largest of any sector, plus **$6.2 billion of FDI**.
- **PM Surya Ghar past 65 lakh installations**, at more than one lakh installations every six days.
- The **IMF has revised India's FY27 growth forecast to 7%.**

## The read: 225 GW of modules against 45 GW of cells

Do the arithmetic the minister did not do out loud. **Module capacity is five times cell capacity.** A solar module without a cell is glass and a frame. So the binding constraint in India's solar manufacturing chain has moved one step upstream — from assembly, which the PLI schemes solved, to the wafer-to-cell stage, which they have not.

A media report today (Urban Acres, carried by Now.Solar) makes the same point independently, describing India's solar manufacturing capacity boom as facing a cell supply gap. That is consistent with the 5:1 ratio in the official numbers, and it is the thing that determines whether the 225 GW figure converts into domestic value-add or into imported cells assembled under a Made-in-India label.

Two consequences, and they point in opposite directions for the listed complex:

**For module makers, the bottleneck is a margin risk, not a volume risk.** A cell shortage raises input cost and hands pricing power to whoever has cell lines — which is why the integrated players are the ones worth owning on this story, and why pure assemblers are not.

**For grid and transmission, the ₹1.86 lakh crore is the larger and more certain number.** Green Energy Corridor Phase-III is a defined capital programme with a defined set of listed contractors, and unlike a manufacturing subsidy it does not depend on a global price cycle.

## The Rule 7 screen, run and stated

Three names a reader would expect in a renewable-energy table are **excluded**, and I would rather say why than leave a gap:

- **SUZLON** — **Excluded.** SEBI imposed a **₹28.95 crore penalty** on the company and its promoters in a misleading-financial-statements matter covering FY15-FY21 (**₹15.95 crore on the company, ₹5.75 crore on Vinod Tanti, ₹5.45 crore on Girish Tanti**), and the company is moving the Securities Appellate Tribunal against it. A policy inference about wind capacity does not need to carry a live regulator order against the company and both promoters. No rating.
- **INOXWIND** — **Excluded.** Promoter entity Devansh Trademart LLP has **4.58% of equity pledged**, the company received a **SEBI warning letter** over a trading-window compliance lapse, and most materially **SEBI has put the group's Inox Clean Energy ₹10,000 crore IPO on hold** with a separate investigation touching promoter Devansh Jain. Governance overhang; no rating.
- **BORORENEW** — **Excluded.** Subject to a **customs adjudication order** disclosed on **17 October 2026** over tariff classification of imported plant and machinery (₹60 lakh total impact, being paid under protest), and the stock is **down ~36% over twelve months**. The penalty is immaterial; the combination is a flag, and solar glass has enough other things to prove.

**A policy read is not a company rating.** Excluding three names costs the article nothing and is the only honest way to publish the rest.

## What to watch

The **Bharat RE Summit runs through 5 November**, and the useful signal will not be the speeches — it will be any announcement on cell and wafer capacity, because that is the piece of the chain the 225 GW number has outrun. Watch also for the first tenders under Surya Sarovar; 5,000 MW of floating solar is a defined procurement with a small universe of bidders, and the award list will name them.
