## The Global Rates Trade Flips: a 29,000 Payroll Print Removes the October Hike

**The September US jobs report landed at 29,000 nonfarm payrolls against a 84,000-90,000 consensus, with unemployment rising to 4.2% from 4.1% and July revised into a LOSS of 10,000.** Combined revisions cut July and August by **60,000**. Average hourly earnings rose just **0.1% month-on-month, 3.0% year-on-year**.

Global markets repriced immediately. **Treasury yields tumbled** — the 10-year had closed October 1 at **5.233%** after touching an intraday **5.344%, its highest since April 2002** — and the CME-implied odds of an October hike collapsed. Charles Schwab's Joseph Brusuelas: the report "will take an October rate hike off." The FOMC meets **October 27-28**; December is now the market's live meeting.

This is the single most important global macro development of the week because it **reverses the dominant transmission channel of the past three weeks**. Through September, the story was multi-decade-high Treasury yields compressing equity multiples, draining emerging-market flows and forcing a global bond selloff. A 29,000 payroll print interrupts that chain at its source.

For India specifically, the channel is direct and material: **record FPI outflows (₹34,970 crore sold last week alone), a rupee near 96, and an RBI MPC on October 7 where a majority of economists expect a 25 basis point hike.** A softer US rates path eases the pressure on all three.

## Oil Reverses Hard: Brent Falls Below $100 on a Diesel-Reserve Release

**President Trump announced on Truth Social that Europe has agreed to release emergency diesel stocks.** *"Europe has just agreed to release a massive amount of their heavily stocked Diesel Oil. The process will begin immediately."* It followed a Reuters report that EU nations were weighing tapping diesel reserves to relieve soaring fuel prices and to head off Trump's threat to **ban US diesel exports**. EU trade chief **Maros Sefcovic** said he had discussed tight diesel supplies and price spikes with USTR Jamieson Greer.

**Brent fell more than 3%, below $100 a barrel**, after settling at **$102.31 (+4.4%)** on October 1 and WTI at **$92.87 (+2.7%)**. Early Friday quotes put Brent around **$99.25** and WTI near **$89.28**, down $3.60 on the session.

**The key structural fact is that this is a refined-product shock, not a crude one.** JPMorgan estimates Middle East crude exports at **17.5 million bpd — 98% of pre-war levels** — a "remarkable recovery for a region still at war," with producers relying on pipelines, shuttle tankers and ship-to-ship transfers. **India's West Asian crude imports rose 74% month-on-month to 2.10 mbd in September**, and roughly **70% of India's crude now arrives by routes outside Hormuz**, up from about 55%.

What has *not* recovered is the product complex — refined-product supplies have rebounded far more slowly, which is exactly why diesel is at record highs and why a stock release moves the price.

**India's own framing is worth quoting:** New Delhi has emphasised that **shipping chaos, not supply scarcity, is driving high oil prices.** About **60% of India's LPG imports pass through Hormuz**, and **47.4 MMSCMD of India's ~189 MMSCMD gas consumption was hit by force majeure** during the disruption — which is the channel through which this conflict reaches Indian kitchens and fertiliser plants even as crude diversification insulates the refinery side.

## Milwaukee: A Steel Framework, No China Timetable, and a $900 Billion Price Tag

**The G20 Trade Ministerial closed in Milwaukee with three outcomes worth tracking.**

**One — the "Milwaukee Framework" on steel was endorsed.** Four themes from USTR Jamieson Greer: **eliminating forced labour, "updating" the MFN principle, denouncing food-trade weaponisation, and addressing structural excess capacity** — with the steel framework covering excess capacity, trade remedies, supply-chain monitoring and transshipment. Alongside it, the **Global Forum on Steel Excess Capacity** met in the morning.

**Two — there is no timetable for the $60 billion of US-China tariff cuts** recommended by the US-China Board of Trade. *"We have to stick to our legal processes,"* Greer said, flagging that his **ongoing excess-capacity investigation** could generate China remedies that feed into "managed trade" through the board.

**Three — the price of tariff peace is being paid in investment commitments.** Bloomberg: *"Tariff Peace Comes With a $900 Billion Price Tag From Japan and South Korea."*

Bilaterals: **Goyal–Greer on the interim India-US BTA** ("short strokes," but "nothing imminent"), **Canada's Sidhu targeting a deal by year-end**, and **Brazil's Marcio Elias Rosa seeking "full tariff reduction."**

⚠️ **The India-specific risk to keep separate:** the USTR's **Section 301 overcapacity investigation, opened in March 2026, covers 16 economies INCLUDING INDIA and has not yet been imposed.** It is a different instrument from the forced-labour tariffs (10%/12.5% on ~60 economies). India also already carries a **10% forced-labour tariff** for insufficient action on forced-labour imports, and the US has passed a law that could impose tariffs of **up to 100% on India over Russian oil imports**. All three remain unresolved roadblocks.

## Europe's Bond Market: the French Spread Is the Widest Since 2012

**A global bond selloff flipped in volatile trading, and the composition of the move is the story.** The 10-year Treasury yield retreated sharply after hitting a new 24-year high near **5.34%**, finishing October 1 at **5.233%**. But rather than a uniform rally, investors **separated perceived winners from losers among developed-market debt** — piling into **US and German** bonds while **French, Italian and Greek yields rose sharply.**

The **French 10-year spread over German bunds is near its widest since 2012**. Traders attributed the moves partly to **hedge funds forced to unwind crowded bullish bets on French bonds**, with France's 2027 budget plan — designed to shrink the deficit — raising political and market stakes.

The 30-year Treasury reached **5.677%**, a 24-year high. The euro extended losses against a firmer dollar index.
