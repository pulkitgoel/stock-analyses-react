That is the shape of a positioning rebound, not an information event. A market that opens up 2% on overnight futures and finishes up 0.3% has been sold into on the way.

## What actually caused the bounce

Kioxia closed up 12.57% after a GPU-memory proof of concept with Nvidia. SoftBank Group rose 7.32%. Tokyo Electron added about 1.5%. TSMC, the largest and most obvious expression of the trade, closed down 0.23%.

A proof of concept is a validation of a specification, not an order. It is enough to reprice the second-derivative memory name by 12% in a tape where the first-derivative name is under a ratings cloud, and that is a statement about positioning rather than about demand.

The cloud is real. S&P Global Ratings has Nvidia's AA issuer rating on CreditWatch negative over management and governance, while Moody's holds Aa1. This is the third regulatory or analytical action against the AI complex in eight days, after the 16 November licensing rule and the 23 November Entity List addition, and it is the first one that is about the management rather than the distribution.

## Two central banks moved hawkish on the same day

The Bank of Korea raised its policy rate 25 basis points to 3.25% with two dissents, and 15 of 21 board projections now sit above 3.25%. That is a hiking bias, not a pause, and it lands on the economy with the highest memory concentration in the world.

The Bank of Japan's hawkish dissenter Tamura said rates should reach a neutral level of around 2% "soon", and the two-year JGB yield rose about 4 basis points in response. Australia's private capital expenditure jumped 12.2% against a 4.4% forecast, and the Australian dollar firmed to about 0.6646.

Two of Asia's three main central banks telling the market that the next move is up, into a chip-led equity bounce, is an unusual combination. It caps the rebound even if the AI bid returns.

## India's first session with a 25% band on the table

The Section 301 determinations were published at 6:41 p.m. IST on Wednesday — three hours after the close. Thursday was the first session in which any Indian stock could price a proposed 25% duty, or 17% with a trade agreement.

The answer was a 0.46% gain on the Sensex and a 0.25% gain on the Nifty, led by information technology.

The sector is now up 7.98% over four sessions, from 30,919.45 on 19 November, and has added roughly ₹1.70 lakh crore of market capitalisation.

The structural reason is not sentiment. Section 301 is a duty on goods. It cannot reach a service delivered from Bengaluru to a client in Texas at any rate, in any tranche, under any of the 14 determinations. Indian IT sits outside this instrument completely, and its variables are US client capex and the rupee — one of which is moving the right way. The rupee has firmed to about 92.4 to the dollar, an eight-month high, which is a headwind to reported revenue rather than a tailwind, and the market ignored it.

## The two-sided view is unusually well documented

Bernstein initiated coverage on the sector with a positive call, putting the addressable IT market at $2.1 trillion by FY30 with India's global share rising to about 15%, and flagging that leaders and laggards will diverge. It puts earnings sensitivity to the rupee at the lowest — 2% — for TCS and Infosys, and highest for the smaller names.

Against that: Jefferies has an Underperform rating on TCS, HCL Tech, Wipro and Tech Mahindra. Its own numbers show aggregate operating margins expanded 25 basis points sequentially to 19.9%, led by 130 basis points at HCL Tech, but it flags a steady rise in subcontracting costs while employee costs as a share of sales fall — which it reads as low confidence about the growth outlook. Choice Institutional says a broad recovery in IT services demand remains elusive, notes that Infosys and Cognizant have both cut organic growth guidance, and expects FY27 to be a transition year.

So the sector is rallying into a downgrade cycle on a flows argument. Foreign investors have put $14.15 billion into Indian equities in November and are explicitly rotating out of the concentrated AI trade in Korea and Taiwan — which means the marginal buyer is not buying Indian IT for its growth, it is buying it to not own semiconductors. That distinction is the whole risk in the trade.
