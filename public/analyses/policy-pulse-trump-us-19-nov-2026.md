He sold the consumer and bought the compute. That is the direction of the September filing, not a verdict on anything, and it is the only genuinely new information in it.

## What the report actually says

A periodic transaction report, signed 1 November and made public on Thursday, covers September. The largest single lines are sales of between $1 million and $5 million each in Comcast, McDonald's and Home Depot. Beneath those sit hundreds of smaller transactions, including sales of Nike, Tesla, Boeing, Eli Lilly and Amazon.

On the buy side: SpaceX stock in multiple tranches of $1,000 to $50,000, plus a $100,001 to $250,000 purchase of SpaceX 5.35% senior unsecured notes. Shares of Nvidia, Microsoft, Alphabet, Apple and Amazon. CoreWeave stock and bonds. Dozens of tax-exempt municipal bonds. Corporate debt in Boeing, Occidental Petroleum, Macy's, Victoria's Secret, Sirius XM, General Motors and Coty. And perpetual subordinated debt from JPMorgan Chase, Capital One, M&T Bank, Citizens Financial, Morgan Stanley, Charles Schwab and State Street.

The brackets describe transaction values, not holdings, so none of this establishes a position size. Forbes puts the president's net worth at $6.3 billion.

## Why the SpaceX line is different this time

Except for one detail, none of this would be worth a column. Trump's portfolio is run by outside advisers, he disclosed more than 21,000 trades in 2025 alone against roughly 500 across his entire first term, and at that volume coincidence is guaranteed rather than suggestive. The White House has said consistently that he gives no direction and that the accounts track recognised indexes.

The detail is that SpaceX is now a listed company. It listed on the Nasdaq in June as SPCX. Every earlier disclosure of "SpaceX purchases" referred to private paper that an outside investor could not own; the September report is the first in which the line sits in a security anyone can buy. SPCX closed at $143.50 on Wednesday and traded at $141.83 at 10:32 a.m. ET, down 1.16%.

The same is true, less cleanly, of CoreWeave. CRWV traded at $48.84, up 2.11%. It is the single line in the report least explained by the "recognised index" description, and it is the line a reader will fixate on. There is no way to test whether it is a rebalance artefact or an opinion, and the disclosure system does not require the distinction.

## The position and the policy, again

The president's Nvidia holdings are the recurring question and the answer has not changed. CNBC counted at least 103 Nvidia transactions since January 2025 worth a combined $27 million to $97.5 million, and reported that at the end of August the stake was between $13 million and $62 million against $615,000 to $1.3 million at the end of 2024.

The tradeable half of that story is not the position. It is that Nvidia is not currently shipping Hopper-generation data-centre products to China, against $2.4 billion sold there in the prior quarter, and that this was said on the earnings call rather than inferred from a filing. Nvidia closed at $150.96 on Wednesday, down 2.9% despite beating, and traded at $152.49, up 1.01%.

On Thursday the president also said he would be open to Jensen Huang returning as chief executive and played down the national security questions around Huang's departure. That is a statement about a company's management, made by the person who sets its export policy.

## Rare earths: the pledge has no instrument

At the Quad meeting in Manila on Tuesday, the president said the United States is producing rare earths at a level it has never reached and will "catch" China within ten months. He did not say whether he meant mining, refining or magnets, and no contract, budget or company accompanied the claim. He also said the four Quad members had reached agreements to secure critical-mineral supply chains, without naming a deal, a company or a dollar figure.

China controls roughly 91% of rare-earth refining and 94% of magnet production. Matching its ore output would change very little. The market read it that way: MP Materials closed at $34.86 on Tuesday, down 3.41%, after USA Rare Earth fell 6.94% to $6.64, its lowest close since April 2025. Both bounced on Thursday, MP to $36.47 and USAR to $6.79, with no new information behind the move.

The one substantive item in the complex remains the July 2025 Pentagon arrangement: $400 million of convertible preferred, about 15% on conversion, and a ten-year neodymium-praseodymium floor of $110 per kilogram. That floor is a real support. It does not fix the refining gap, and no policy announced this week closed it.

## Shenzhen produced a communiqué

The bilateral with Xi Jinping at Kylin Villa, followed by the first trilateral with Xi and Vladimir Putin, ran about ninety minutes and ended with no apparent agreement to extend the trade truce that expires on 10 January, according to Associated Press reporting from the ground. One Korean outlet reported an agreement in principle; no instrument was published, and the two readouts do not name the semiconductor rules that are the actual dispute.

What the meeting did centre on was a new Board of Trade intended to shape a 30-for-30 tariff framework, lowering duties on roughly $30 billion of annual imports in each direction. That is a negotiating structure, not a signed thing. The joint artefact of the week is the APEC Shenzhen Outlook, a declaration with three headings — trade, connectivity, innovation — and no tariff schedule.

The honest summary is that the rare-earth stalemate and the chip stalemate are where they were on Wednesday. For a magnet or semiconductor position, nothing changed except the date on the calendar getting closer to 10 January.

## What to watch

- **10 January 2027** — the US-China truce expiry and China's rare-earth export-control pause, both on the same day. No extension was signed in Shenzhen; the Board of Trade framework is the vehicle, and it has produced no text.
- **The Section 232 polysilicon measures on 4 December** — 15% ad valorem on ingots, wafers, cells and modules plus minimum import prices. That is the next dated instrument in the solar complex, and it stacks on the India/Indonesia/Laos anti-dumping findings.
- **The Huang question** — the president has now said publicly he is open to the former chief executive returning. Nvidia's board has said it will not reinstate him. The gap between those two positions is a governance event with an export-policy edge.
- **28 November** — October IIP. September printed 6.7%, a four-month low, with mining at -6.2%.
- **8-9 December** — the FOMC. Wednesday's minutes said almost all participants saw further increases as likely appropriate, and a couple floated more than 25 basis points.
