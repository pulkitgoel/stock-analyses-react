## The EIA Turns More Bullish on Crude — Brent at USD 105 for the Fourth Quarter

The **US Energy Information Administration**, in its **Short-Term Energy Outlook released October 6**, raised its oil price forecasts sharply as **global inventories fall and diesel markets stay tight** through the Iran war:

- **Brent is now forecast to average about USD 98 a barrel in 2026, up 8%** from the prior estimate, and **about USD 105 in Q4 2026 — a USD 14 upgrade**.
- **2027 Brent was lifted USD 10 to about USD 84.**
- **US retail diesel is expected to stay above USD 6/gallon in October** before averaging about **USD 4.50 in 2027** — the number that explains the political pressure behind the dyed-diesel executive order.
- The **EIA also flagged the supply response**: Middle East **shut-in production is projected to fall from 4.5 million barrels per day in Q4 2026 to 2.7 million bpd in Q1 2027**, with regional shut-ins in September already at their **lowest level since hostilities began**, and flows recovering as Hormuz transits improve and producers use alternative routes and ship-to-ship transfers.
- Structural context: **Hormuz carried about 20% of global oil supplies before the war**, and attacks on **Saudi Arabia's East-West pipeline** (since restarted) are the EIA's stated risk case.

**Where prices actually are:** Brent traded around **USD 101.2–102.0 (+1.3% to +1.6%)** and WTI around **USD 89.9–90.1**; the **front month printed USD 98.17 against February 2027 at USD 93.62 and August 2027 at USD 85.51** — steep backwardation, the market pricing near-term disruption and long-run normalisation simultaneously. Drivers today: **Houthi attacks that damaged two Saudi airports (Jazan and Najran)**, a **Gulf of Mexico hurricane supply risk**, and a Middle East conflict with no de-escalation path — **Secretary of State Marco Rubio said Hormuz is open and Iran has "lost complete control," while Iran insists it will not fully reopen the strait until Washington meets seven conditions**. Offsetting: **Gulf exports have recovered to pre-war levels**, and **Aramco cut its November Arab Light OSP to Asia to a six-year low**.
