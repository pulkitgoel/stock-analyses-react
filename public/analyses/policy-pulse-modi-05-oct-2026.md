## Modi and Swiss President Parmelin Sign Five Agreements: Market Access, a $100 Billion EFTA Investment Track

**Prime Minister Narendra Modi and President of the Swiss Confederation Guy Parmelin held talks at Hyderabad House in New Delhi on Monday, October 5, and witnessed the exchange of five agreements** covering trade and investment, defence production, nuclear energy, infrastructure and mobility, and science, technology and innovation. President Parmelin is on a **state visit to India from October 5 to 7, 2026** at the Prime Minister's invitation. Foreign Secretary **Vikram Misri** and Swiss Ambassador to India **Maya Tissafi** were present for the exchange.

The operative content, in the Prime Minister's own words:

- **India has implemented the Trade and Economic Partnership Agreement (TEPA) with the EFTA states** — Switzerland, Norway, Iceland and Liechtenstein — and Modi described it as **"India's first FTA with any economic bloc in Europe."**
- **The stated goal is $100 billion of EFTA investment into India.** Modi framed Monday's decisions as expanding **market access for Indian exports to Switzerland** and simplifying **investment between the two countries**.
- **Sector-specific on the export side:** the Prime Minister named **agriculture, pharma, textiles and engineering goods** as the categories that stand to gain Swiss market access.
- **Sector-specific on the inbound side:** he named **biotech, life sciences, banking, insurance, food processing and sustainability** as the areas where India will seek fresh Swiss investment.
- Beyond trade, the agenda covered **defence production, nuclear energy, infrastructure and mobility, science and technology, health, clean energy, space, Yoga and Ayurveda**.

Why this is more than a diplomatic readout: Switzerland is one of India's largest sources of direct inward investment per capita of source country, and it is a **pharma and life-sciences capital**. A bilateral frame that pairs market access for Indian generics with investment in biotech and life sciences is a two-way structural arrangement rather than a one-way export push. The defensive leg matters as much as the offensive one — with the US Section 301 overcapacity investigation covering India and the October 18 secondary-sanctions decision pending (see the Global article), **widening the non-US export corridor is the policy response**.

## Migration and Mobility Plus a Young Professionals Agreement: The Services and Human-Capital Leg

Beyond the commercial pacts, **India and Switzerland signed a Migration and Mobility Partnership Agreement and a Young Professionals Agreement.** Modi's framing was that these open "new opportunities in Switzerland for India's students, researchers, professionals and young people."

This is the leg of Monday's package with the longest economic half-life, and the least quantifiable in the near term. Switzerland is a high-wage, low-unemployment market with structural shortages in engineering, life sciences and financial services — precisely the three areas the investment agenda names. A Young Professionals Agreement that lowers the friction on temporary professional mobility does three things simultaneously: it eases the remittance channel, it deepens the corporate-level pipeline between Swiss employers and Indian talent, and it addresses the workforce gap that constrains the very biotech and life-sciences investment the two governments want.

The read-across to listed Indian equities is genuinely thin, and the honest position is that this is a **services-and-human-capital signal rather than a stock driver**. The named sectors — students, researchers, professionals — have only indirect listed proxies, and marking up an IT services name on a mobility agreement would be exactly the kind of fabricated linkage this tracker is supposed to avoid.
