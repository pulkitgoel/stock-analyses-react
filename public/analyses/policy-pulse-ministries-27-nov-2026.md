Two regulators moved on Friday and only one of them moved a stock index.

SEBI's chairman described four priorities at a CII summit in Mumbai. The Supreme Court, a day earlier, ordered the food regulator to redesign every packaged-food label in the country. The market priced the second and ignored the first, which in both cases was the right call for the session and the wrong call for the year.

## Five days to register a foreign fund, three to list a REIT

Tuhin Kanta Pandey set out four priorities on Friday: simplify the rulebook, deepen the markets that finance growth, ease access for global investors, and use technology with safeguards.

The concrete items:

- **FPI onboarding cut to five business days**, from a process that has taken months, via digital workflows and system integration. About **740 FPIs** have registered under the SWAGAT-FI trusted-investor route, holding roughly **17% of FPI assets under custody** as of 31 October.
- **REIT and InvIT listing cut from six working days to three.**
- A **full review of the LODR framework** to strip disclosures that duplicate Ministry of Corporate Affairs filings, and a **single penalty** for a lapse that currently attracts fines from more than one exchange.
- A **review of the ICDR framework** — IPOs, follow-on offers, preferential issues and QIPs — plus the SME IPO framework and the Innovators Growth Platform.
- A **review of short selling and securities lending and borrowing** to improve liquidity and price discovery.
- **AIF simplification** covering investor consent, related-party transactions, co-investment and exits.
- A stated ambition of **about 2,000 additional listings** in the next few years, substantially from the small and medium segment.

He also said a consultation paper will shortly propose removing FPI constraints that "other countries don't have, but we do." That follows a separate consultation paper published on **20 November** proposing to drop the Additional Disclosure Framework for certain FPIs, with comments open to **11 December 2026**.

The context that makes this a policy rather than a preference: foreign portfolio investors pulled a net **₹5.61 lakh crore** out of Indian equities between October 2024 and October 2026. November has reversed part of that — FPIs have bought over **$15 billion** net this month, and Friday's provisional numbers had foreign investors net buyers of **₹5,843.33 crore** on 26 November. So the regulator is simplifying an access channel at the moment the channel is filling on its own.

Not one of these is a rule yet. They are priorities and reviews, and the operative documents are consultation papers with comment windows. That distinction goes in the confidence score, not the narrative.

## The order that actually changed a product

The Supreme Court's FOPNL judgment, delivered on Constitution Day, is 131 pages and it does not defer.

The Food Safety and Standards Authority had proposed a single red hexagon carrying all three warnings. The court rejected it as something that would "undermine the very objective," and directed:

- **A separate hexagon for each nutrient** — one, two or three of them — each carrying a standard pictogram and the words "High Fat," "High Sugar" or "High Salt."
- **Total sugars and saturated fat**, not added sugar and added fat. The court's reasoning is technical and hard to argue with: a laboratory cannot distinguish added sugar from natural sugar in a finished product, and a maker could sweeten with fruit concentrate and escape the label entirely. "Added fat" as a label metric, it noted, does not exist anywhere in the world.
- **Each warning covering 4 to 5% of the principal display panel**, in a prescribed font size.
- **Thresholds reformulated on scientific evidence** — with an explicit warning against setting them too low. The judgment cites a study finding that **94.1%** of 44,109 packaged-food records would trip at least one warning under the proposed values, which the court said would breed "label fatigue."
- Rules to be in force within the regulator's own **four-month** timeline, then a **365-day** transition for companies.

The court will review compliance after ten weeks. It also directed nutrition education in schools, and said that if consensus among stakeholders cannot be reached, consumer protection takes priority.

The market read the first-order effect, not the second-order one. Nifty FMCG fell 0.93% and the sector was among Friday's worst. The 4-5% panel requirement and the shift to total sugars are the two provisions that change a product rather than a package: India's biscuit, namkeen and sweetened-beverage portfolios are built on formulations that will now carry warnings, and reformulating them is a cost and a taste problem, not a printing problem.

The counter-argument, made by at least one brokerage, is that the transition is a year long and the thresholds are still unwritten — and the threshold numbers, not the hexagon shape, decide how many products are caught. Both readings are defensible. The one thing that is not defensible is treating this as a packaging cost.

## The listing that did not pay

Mahanadi Coalfields, Coal India's largest producing subsidiary, listed on Friday at **₹134** against an issue price of **₹136** — a discount debut on a **₹8,981.12 crore** offer that was subscribed **1.52 times**.

It was a pure offer for sale of 66.18 crore shares, 10% of pre-offer capital, so Coal India's stake goes from 100% to about 90% and Mahanadi received nothing. At the upper band the company is valued at roughly **₹90,000 crore** on FY26 net profit of ₹10,698 crore. This is the third Coal India subsidiary to list in 2026, after Bharat Coking Coal and Central Mine Planning & Design Institute.

A 1.5% discount on a 1.52x book is the market's answer to a ₹90,000 crore valuation for a coal producer, and it is worth noting because the next subsidiary in the queue will price against it.
