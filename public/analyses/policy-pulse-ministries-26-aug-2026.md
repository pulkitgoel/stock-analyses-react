### 🇮🇳 Story 1: RBI MPC Holds Repo Rate at 5.25% — GDP Forecast Raised

The Reserve Bank of India's Monetary Policy Committee (MPC) maintained the **repo rate at 5.25%** in its August 2026 meeting, citing resilient domestic demand amid global uncertainties. The decision was unanimous, with the neutral policy stance retained.

**Key Policy Decisions:**
- **Repo Rate:** Unchanged at 5.25%
- **Stance:** Neutral maintained
- **FY27 GDP Forecast:** Raised to **6.7%** (from 6.6%)
  - Q1 FY27: 7.0%
  - Q2 FY27: 6.4%
  - Q3 FY27: 6.5%
  - Q4 FY27: 6.8%
- **FY27 CPI Inflation:** Lowered to **5.0%** (from 5.1%)
  - Core inflation: 4.3%
  - June CPI: 4.45% (up from 4.38% in May)

**New Policy Proposals:**
1. **UCB (Urban Cooperative Banks) Licensing Reform:** Proposed changes to licensing norms and interest-rate regulation for cooperative banks
2. **Polymer Currency Notes:** Rollout target set for early FY28
3. **FCNR(B) Scheme:** No early withdrawal permitted to preserve FX reserves

### 🇮🇳 Story 2: Piyush Goyal to Visit US for G20 Trade Meet & USTR Talks

Commerce Minister **Piyush Goyal will visit the United States in September 2026** for the G20 Trade Ministerial Meeting and bilateral talks with USTR Jamieson Greer. The visit comes as US-India trade negotiations reach a critical phase.

**Key Agenda Items:**
1. **Bilateral Trade Agreement (BTA):** Framework finalized in February 2026 needs recalibration post-Supreme Court IEEPA ruling
2. **Tariff Advantage:** India seeking competitive edge over Asian competitors (Sri Lanka, Bangladesh, Thailand, Vietnam, Indonesia, Malaysia)
3. **G20 Priorities:** Forced labor, MFN principle updates, food trade weaponization, structural excess capacity

**Trade Context:**
- **Current US Tariff on India:** 10% (Section 301 forced labor tariff, lower tier)
- **India's Exports to US:** ~$87 billion (2025-26)
- **Competitor Tariffs:** Most Asian competitors face similar or higher rates; India seeking differentiation

### 🇮🇳 Story 3: US-India Trade Negotiations — Deadline Pressure Building

Former Assistant USTR Mark Linscott (Atlantic Council) warned that US-India trade talks are at risk of collapse without sustained high-level engagement. The Supreme Court's February 2026 ruling invalidating IEEPA-based tariffs removed key leverage the US had used in negotiations.

**Current Status:**
- **Framework Agreement:** Announced February 2026 but stalled
- **New Tool:** Section 301 investigations now being used for leverage
- **Key Sticking Points:** Tariff parity, market access, forced labor commitments

**India's Position:**
- Seeks tariff advantage over Asian competitors
- Wants protection for generic pharma (already achieved with 10% tier)
- Pushing for greater services market access in the US
