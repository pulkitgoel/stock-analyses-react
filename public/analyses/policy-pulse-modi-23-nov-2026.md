Two prime ministers laid a foundation stone by video link on Monday morning, and the number attached to it was ₹5,792.36 crore for 669 megawatts.

That is the Lower Arun Hydro-Electric Project, on the Arun river in eastern Nepal, and the company behind it is named: SJVN, through its subsidiary. Nepal's Prime Minister Balendra Shah, on his first bilateral trip abroad since taking office in March, sat with Narendra Modi at Hyderabad House for delegation-level talks and the two leaders virtually laid the stone. Major construction packages have already been awarded. Commissioning is targeted for FY2030-31, and the project development agreement gives Nepal 21% of the generated energy free of cost.

This is the first time in weeks that an India-Nepal engagement has produced a number that maps onto a listed company's order book rather than a communiqué. It is worth being precise about how far that mapping goes.

## The sixteen billion dollars, itemised

India's development assistance to Nepal is ₹15,000 crore, or NPR 24,000 crore — about $1.62 billion at current rates. It splits cleanly:

- **₹3,500 crore (NPR 5,600 crore) in grant assistance** for post-disaster reconstruction and new infrastructure projects. This follows the Rasuwa-Bhotekoshi flash flood, for which Shah thanked India for its humanitarian and technical response.
- **₹11,500 crore (NPR 18,400 crore) as a rupee-denominated Line of Credit** for energy sector projects.

The joint press release is explicit that "specific projects as well as modalities for the provision of development assistance will be finalized through mutual consultations." That sentence is the whole caveat. ₹15,000 crore is a ceiling on a facility, not a signed order book, and the rupee-denominated LoC means the procurement will be sourced in India — which is the part that matters for Indian suppliers.

## What is actually contracted, and what is not

Three tiers, and they carry very different confidence.

**Signed and named.** The 669 MW Lower Arun project. SJVN has confirmed it in its own disclosure, construction packages are awarded, and the foundation stone was laid. That is a real ₹5,792 crore capital project with a named developer, and it is the only hard item in the package.

**Directed but undated.** The two leaders "directed the Pancheshwar Development Authority and the Joint Committee on Water Resources to finalise the Detailed Project Report of the Pancheshwar Multipurpose Project expeditiously and commence work on the implementation of the project." They also agreed on the need to accelerate the DPRs for the Saptakoshi and Sunkoshi multipurpose projects. A direction to finalise a DPR is not a project approval; Pancheshwar has been a DPR under discussion for years and this language does not change its stage, only its stated urgency.

**Structural, no money yet.** Two joint ventures are noted with satisfaction for high-capacity cross-border transmission lines — Inaruwa (Nepal) to New Purnea (India), and Dododhara (Nepal) to Bareilly (India). Both sides also welcomed "the active engagement of Indian companies in the hydro-power sector in Nepal" and agreed to ensure "the expeditious completion of various hydropower projects being developed by Indian companies." Separately, Modi reiterated India's commitment to complete the 900 MW Arun-3 project and its associated 217 km transmission line on schedule — Arun-3 is SJVN too, through SAPDC, and its original March 2025 commissioning date has already been missed once.

## The port line is the one with the widest reach

Buried in item nine of the joint press release is the announcement with the biggest downstream reach: Modi opened a new connectivity avenue for Nepal's transit trade through **Deendayal Port at Kandla in Gujarat**, with **Paradip in Odisha** to follow once "requisite infrastructure is in place."

A landlocked country's trade has to exit through somebody's port, and Nepal's has overwhelmingly exited through Kolkata and Haldia. Adding Kandla to the set reroutes cargo westward — shorter sea legs to Europe and the Gulf, and a different rail hinterland.

The same section covers the rest of the physical layer: new railway lines, bridges, Integrated Check Posts and petroleum pipelines; the foundation stone for a new ICP at Banbasa, with the ICPs at Bhairahawa, Sunauli and Dodhara-Chandani in progress; a new cross-border motorable bridge between Dharchula in India and Darchula in Nepal, inaugurated virtually; and the existing unidirectional B345 Kathmandu-Bhairahawa-Lucknow air route to be made bidirectional.

Eight MoUs were exchanged, covering education, health, agriculture, sports, disaster management and human resource development, and India agreed to establish an offshore IIT campus in Nepal. The two sides also adopted a joint vision document titled "Viksit Bharat and Samunnat Nepal."

## The honest read on the day

The market's verdict on all of this was a 1.75% fall in the Nifty and a 1.77% fall in the Sensex, driven by a global AI selloff and profit-booking after four straight weekly gains. Adani Ports was the only one of these names to close green, and by 0.10%.

That is the correct response. A state visit that produces a facility ceiling, a list of MoUs, a foundation stone and a port access route is a genuine improvement in the bilateral relationship and a genuine negative for nobody — but it is a three-to-seven-year capex pipeline, and capex pipelines do not re-rate a stock on a Monday.

What would change the assessment: a signed LoC tranche with named projects; a Pancheshwar DPR with a cost and a schedule; a tender for the Inaruwa-New Purnea or Dododhara-Bareilly lines. None of those exist yet.
