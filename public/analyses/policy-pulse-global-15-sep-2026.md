## The Oil Shock Is Now a Supply Story, Not a Risk Premium

**Brent is trading above USD 107 a barrel** (November futures at USD 107.44 at the Indian close, WTI October at USD 103.66) and Jarvis Invest reported Brent spiking to **USD 110 intraday**. The reason is physical, not sentiment: **Saudi Arabia's East-West pipeline — the alternative route that bypassed the Strait of Hormuz — has been taken out of service for several weeks by repeated drone attacks**, with press reports pointing to launches from Iraq, and Aramco has given no timeline for resuming throughput.

That leaves Hormuz as effectively the only channel, with traffic described as nearly shut:

- **Iran's position hardened.** Supreme National Security Council secretary Mohsen Rezaee said on X that talks with Washington are out of the question until Iran's conditions are met — a direct contradiction of President Trump's suggestion that Tehran wants a swift agreement. US forces continue to blockade Iranian ports.
- **The IRGC Navy claimed an oil tanker struck a mine in a restricted zone in the southern Strait**; US CENTCOM denied the report. Either way, the insurance and routing cost of every barrel is rising.
- **Houthis attacked civilian targets at Khamis Mushait, Abha and Taif in Saudi Arabia** on Monday and are reportedly digging into positions along Yemen's western Red Sea coast — threatening the other export route.
- **Gulf producers are already pumping below OPEC quotas**, and Chinese inventories have fallen sharply, making China an aggressive buyer.
