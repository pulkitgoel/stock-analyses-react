## Trump Signs "Emergency Tax Relief on Diesel Fuel" — a Midterm Price Signal, Not a Supply Fix

President Trump signed an executive order, **"Emergency Tax Relief on Diesel Fuel,"** onstage at a rally in **Grand Island, Nebraska on Monday, October 5**, four weeks before the November 3 midterms. The order is narrower than the rally rhetoric:

- It directs the **Treasury Secretary** to determine, **within five days**, whether relief is authorised under **26 U.S.C. 7508A** and, if so, to **defer payment** of diesel excise taxes under **26 U.S.C. 4041(a)(1)(A)** and **4041(b)(1)(B)** incurred between **October 5 and December 31, 2026** — deferred **without any penalty, interest, additional amount or addition to the tax**.
- It directs the **IRS** to announce it will not impose penalties under **26 U.S.C. 6715(a)(1)/(a)(2)** when dyed diesel is sold for use or used on the highway in the same window, and to address relief from failure-to-deposit penalties.
- It directs the **Secretaries of Agriculture and Transportation** to facilitate implementation.

The White House fact sheet frames it as *"temporarily allow off-road 'dyed' diesel for highway use and defer the applicable Federal excise tax."* Trump: *"The typical trucker will save more than $100 every time they fill up,"* and the order will *"drive down the cost of all goods, including grocery prices, very substantially."*

**The market-relevant caveat: it does not add a single gallon of supply.** Average US diesel was **$6.32/gallon** on Monday (AAA) — down 13 cents week-on-week but **$2.63 higher than a year ago** — after the Iran war spike, attacks on Russian refineries and Chinese export restrictions. GasBuddy's Patrick De Haan was blunt: *"Taxes aren't the problem, supply is."* States still enforce their own dye rules, and off-road users such as farmers were **already** buying untaxed diesel. The genuine supply-side action was the previously negotiated **100-million-barrel G7/IEA release**, which includes a frontloaded diesel tranche within 20 days.

## IEEPA Refunds Open for 3,700 Court Plaintiffs — CAPE Phase 3 Went Live Today

US Customs and Border Protection **launched Phase 3 of its Consolidated Administration and Processing of Entries (CAPE) system on October 6, 2026** — the mechanism for refunding tariffs imposed under IEEPA that the **Supreme Court struck down**. Phase 3 covers **finally liquidated entries** (generally those liquidated more than 80 days ago) filed by plaintiffs for which the **Court of International Trade has ordered reliquidation**, per CBP's September 15 status declaration to the CIT.

Scale: roughly **USD 20.6 billion** of refunds now in motion against about **3,700 court plaintiffs**. The Federal Register of October 5 confirmed CBP collected **~USD 166 billion** of IEEPA duties between February 3, 2025 and February 24, 2026 and is building CAPE to process **53 million entry summaries**. A separate Rio Times analysis dated October 6 puts the opening at 3,700 plaintiffs.

Two things matter for equities:

1. **The refund is a cash event, and it is plaintiff-only.** Importers that did not litigate do not automatically receive the money — a genuine asymmetry between large, legally-hedged importers and smaller ones.
2. **It is a one-time balance-sheet repair, not a margin change.** The **Section 301 forced-labour tariff regime (10%/12.5% two-tier, 86 countries, in force)** remains the structural baseline.

## Section 301 Overcapacity: The Un-Imposed Tariff Track

USTR's statement after the Milwaukee G20 trade ministerial confirmed only **Mexico and Argentina** signed on to the US-led forced-labour declaration, and that a "handful" of members rejected the excess-capacity pathway entirely. Separately, **USTR is conducting a second Section 301 investigation into 16 trading partners showing signs of excess industrial capacity** — widely expected to lead to new duties in coming months, with **India inside that perimeter**. Trade Representative Greer has already said negotiated tariff caps will be credited against the probe's outcome, which is the mechanism worth tracking.

Trump also told reporters at the White House that the US had **"taken more oil out of the Strait of Hormuz over the previous couple of days than at any point in history"** — a de-escalation signal for the crude complex. He is scheduled to deliver remarks in **Baltimore at 16:00 EDT today**, after this edition goes out.
