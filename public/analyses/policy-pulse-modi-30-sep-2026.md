## The Cabinet Clears PM-DHARA: ₹1.86 Lakh Crore for 135 GW of Evacuation and 50 GWh of Storage

**The Union Cabinet, chaired by the Prime Minister, approved the PM-DHARA Scheme on Wednesday, September 30** — the largest single financing decision of the current energy cycle. The Ministry of New & Renewable Energy set out the structure:

- **Total programme outlay ₹1,86,405 crore**, built from **₹1,36,378 crore for the intra-state transmission system (InSTS) under Green Energy Corridor Phase-III** and **₹50,000 crore for 50 GWh of Battery Energy Storage Systems**
- **135 GW of renewable-energy evacuation capacity** enabled across states and union territories
- **Central Financial Assistance of ₹54,082 crore**, directed at offsetting intra-state transmission charges so that end-user power costs do not absorb the build-out
- **Target completion FY 2032-33**, framed against the government's **900 GW non-fossil capacity target for 2035**; India's non-fossil capacity is about **304 GW today** against the 500 GW-by-2030 goal
- **BESS is to be deployed at the renewable generator end** for grid flexibility — addressing intermittency, congestion, peak-hour curtailment and non-solar-hour demand, which is the specific constraint that has capped Indian renewable utilisation
- **Execution**: greenfield InSTS packages go through **tariff-based competitive bidding** on a build-own-operate-maintain model; brownfield upgrades and network strengthening are executed on a **cost-plus basis**. **State transmission utilities are the implementing agencies** and transmission service providers bid under TBCB
- **Three-level monitoring**: a project monitoring unit at MNRE, quarterly review by the MNRE Secretary, and six-monthly review by the Cabinet Secretary

**Two more decisions were taken in the same sitting**, for a **combined outlay across the three flagship initiatives of ₹2,79,157 crore**:
- The **Rabi Price Policy for the 2027-28 marketing season**, retaining the 50%-over-cost-of-production MSP formula
- An **Intelligent Traffic Management System for the National Capital Territory of Delhi at ₹1,790 crore**

**Timing matters for the tape: the decision was announced after the Indian market closed on September 30**, so it is not in that day's close. The transmission, storage and EPC complex goes into the October 1 session with the quantum fully public — and the sector framing from the day is that India is shifting from adding generation to **ensuring it can be evacuated, integrated and delivered**: EY's Power & Utilities lead flagged "a substantial multi-year pipeline across transmission lines, substations, transformers, grid-flexibility solutions and BESS", while Avaada's chairman cautioned that battery capacity alone does not determine how much new solar must be built.

## Modi Speaks With Trump — as Goyal Sits Across the Table From Greer in Milwaukee

**The Prime Minister spoke by telephone with President Trump on Wednesday, September 30**, in what Modi described as a "productive conversation". The readout said the two leaders **reviewed cooperation in trade, defence, energy and critical technologies**, discussed **regional and international issues — including the West Asia conflict and efforts to advance global peace and security** — and **agreed to maintain close engagement to advance the India-US Comprehensive Global Strategic Partnership**. Indian media noted it was the **first leaders-level contact since the US sanctions round targeting purchasers of Russian crude**, and the first since the two met on the sidelines of the G7 in Evian, France, on **June 17, 2026**.

The call is the political layer over a negotiation running in the same 48 hours: **Commerce and Industry Minister Piyush Goyal is in the United States from September 29 to October 5**, attending the **G20 Trade Ministers' Meeting in Milwaukee on September 30-October 1** and holding a bilateral with **US Trade Representative Jamieson Greer** on the **India-US Bilateral Trade Agreement**. New Delhi's framing is an **interim deal** consistent with the Joint Statement of **February 7, 2026**.

What is on the table, and what is not:

- The February framework's headline commitment: **India to purchase $500 billion of American energy products, aircraft and aircraft parts, precious metals, technology products and coking coal over five years**
- Goyal's stated position is that the pact is **"almost done and dusted"** and will be executed **once Washington gives India the right competitive advantage** — the last-mile issue remains **preferential access over Vietnam, Bangladesh and Thailand**, i.e. a tariff *edge*, not merely a lower headline rate
- **Still unresolved against India**: US legislation authorising higher tariffs on major purchasers of Russian oil, and pending **Section 301 investigations into structural excess capacity**
- **The US side's own litigation risk is live today**: a Court of International Trade panel heard argument on September 30 on the Section 301 forced-labour tariffs — the current US baseline, with **India in the 10% tier** (full detail in the US edition)

## UPI MDR Survives Its First Court Test — 0.4% Lands on October 15

**The Supreme Court on Monday, September 28 refused an interim stay on the Centre's decision to levy a 0.4% Merchant Discount Rate on specified UPI person-to-merchant transactions above ₹2,000**, keeping the **October 15, 2026** rollout date intact. A three-judge bench headed by Chief Justice Surya Kant issued notice to the Union of India and directed counter-affidavits within four weeks; **the case is expected back on October 13, two days before implementation.**

The bench did not endorse the charge — it questioned it. The court asked the government to explain **who pays, who earns, and the legal basis** for the levy, with the pointed formulation that if the MDR is "neither tax nor fee," what exactly is this *expropriation*? The petition challenges the constitutional validity of the **amended Section 10A of the Payment and Settlement Systems Act, 2007**, arguing it gives unguided executive power to decide which payment modes get no-charge protection — noting that the no-charge protection for RuPay debit cards continues without any monetary ceiling.

**What actually changes on October 15:**

- **0.4% baseline MDR** on specified P2M UPI transactions **above ₹2,000**
- **Capped at ₹300** for transactions of ₹75,000 and above
- **Flat ₹5** for railways, telecom, insurance and fuel categories
- **P2P transfers stay free at all values**, and merchant payments up to ₹2,000 stay outside the framework — the Centre told the court **96% of payment-gateway users are exempt**
- The MDR is **borne by the merchant** and cannot be passed to the customer as a separate charge; the Finance Ministry has said UPI apps may not impose platform fees or hidden charges because of the framework
- Chain of instruments: **PSS Act amendment → September 14 notification (zero MDR up to ₹2,000) → September 15 NPCI circular**

**The politics are heating up, not cooling.** The **All India Mobile Retailers Association has called a "No UPI Day" on October 2**, with retailers symbolically covering QR codes and refusing UPI on Gandhi Jayanti; AIMRA told the Finance Minister the MDR implies an immediate net monthly loss of ₹2,000–₹12,000 for a small retailer. **Kerala's gold and silver merchants will protest before central government offices on October 15.** The Congress party has alleged the decision reflects US pressure and favours Visa and Mastercard, and has demanded a rollback. **Finance Minister Nirmala Sitharaman has called the decision "completely professional," with no external pressure.**

**The distributional fact that matters for the equities.** This monetises a service that has run at zero marginal revenue since 2020, but it does so at the top of the ticket. Bernstein's earlier split had banks taking roughly ₹14,000 crore of the pool, apps about ₹7,000 crore and the network about ₹1,000 crore, against an industry pool heading toward ₹22,000 crore by FY28 — and UPI ran **2,451 crore transactions worth ₹29.9 lakh crore in August 2026**, with around 95% of volume below the ₹2,000 threshold. So the revenue is concentrated, the political resistance is concentrated in exactly the merchant classes the government is courting, and the legal challenge is unresolved until October 13.

## The Green-Fuel Push: LNG Trains at Sabarmati, E-Methanol at Kandla

Two dated, quantified green-fuel milestones landed within four days of each other and both sit inside the government's energy-transition capex story rather than the news cycle.

**India's first LNG-powered train was flagged off from Sabarmati in Ahmedabad on September 27, 2026** by Union Home Minister Amit Shah, with Gujarat Chief Minister Bhupendra Patel. The specifics:

- **Two 1,400 HP Driving Power Cars converted to a diesel–LNG dual-fuel system** — the same engine runs on either fuel depending on availability
- Each converted DPC carries an **LNG tank of about 2,200 litres, holding roughly 850–900 kg of usable LNG**; a full fill is expected to cover around **1,600 km**
- **Estimated saving of about ₹23.9 lakh annually** in diesel, with emission reduction as the secondary benefit
- Bundled into the visit: the **₹700 crore WAG-12 locomotive maintenance shed at Sabarmati**, commissioning of the indigenous **Kavach** train-protection system on the Ahmedabad–Palanpur and Ahmedabad–Samakhiyali sections, automatic signalling on two sections, doubling work on Sabarmati–Sarkhej, and a package of **railway projects worth more than ₹1,542 crore**
- Policy frame stated on the day: the Railways used **293 crore litres of diesel in 2015-16 and targets zero by 2030**, with **electrification at 99.6%** (against 92% China, 68% Spain, 60% France, 49% Britain)

**The first port-based e-methanol plant in India had its foundation stone laid at Deendayal Port (Kandla), Gujarat on September 26, 2026** by Union Minister for Ports, Shipping and Waterways Sarbananda Sonowal, Gujarat CM Bhupendra Patel and Assam CM Himanta Biswa Sarma.

- **₹2,300 crore project, 150 tonnes per day (TPD) capacity**, using renewable power, water and biogenic CO₂
- **Joint venture between Deendayal Port Authority and Assam Petro-Chemicals Ltd at a 76:24 capital ratio**; DPA's contribution includes **₹567.32 crore of equity, 75 acres of land, desalinated water and green hydrogen**
- **Phase I: 50 TPD for ₹1,200 crore, targeted January 2027. Phase II: a further 100 TPD for ₹1,100 crore, targeted March 2027**
- **3,500+ direct and indirect jobs**, with the stated aim of supplying green fuel to ships and stimulating the green-molecule value chain around Kandla
- Deendayal Port is one of **three designated Green Hydrogen Hubs** (with Paradip and V.O. Chidambaranar), and already hosts **India's first megawatt-scale green hydrogen plant**, which is being expanded by a further 5 MW roughly a year after construction
