Brent fell 6% today and the reason is not that more oil is flowing. It is that the risk of it stopping has been marked down.

That distinction is the whole trade: a war premium prices barrels at risk, not barrels lost. Take the risk off and the premium goes — quickly, and further than the physical balance justifies.

## First: crude's biggest one-day fall of the conflict

**Brent crude futures fell $6.78, or 6.0%, to $106.65 a barrel by 1357 GMT. US West Texas Intermediate fell $6.37, or 6.1%, to $97.75.** Intraday lows were **$105.86 and $97.12**. On some prints the move was nearer 6.4% earlier in the session.

The trigger is diplomatic and specific:

- **Iranian negotiators held days of talks with Qatari mediators aimed at ending the war with the United States, and have now left Doha. A draft proposal is being circulated, with a follow-up meeting possible**, per an official briefed on the talks.
- **President Masoud Pezeshkian said Supreme Leader Ayatollah Mojtaba Khamenei had granted the government "certain authorities" regarding peace talks.** That is the single most material line — it means the negotiators can commit.
- **Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi said an agreement is possible if Washington "demonstrates goodwill" and is "truly seeking a peaceful solution,"** and that mediators are discussing the proposal Iran tabled at the UN General Assembly in September. Tehran is prepared for "both defence and diplomacy."

Context that makes the fall larger than it looks: **Brent ended October at a six-month high** on fear the conflict would escalate after Tuesday's US midterms. The premium was being paid for a *future* event, and the market has now decided that event is less likely.

**The two-sided part, and it is not small.** Goldman Sachs raised its price forecasts **on Sunday** and said **Brent could reach $140 in 2027 if Gulf output remains 3.5 million barrels per day below its 2025 average.** The war, which began in late February, has already depleted oil stocks. So the correct posture is not "the premium is gone" — it is "the premium has been re-priced against a draft proposal that has not been signed."

## Second: a $115 billion chip deal where the two sides disagree about the price

**Marvell Technology agreed to acquire optical components maker Lumentum in an all-stock deal**, in what would be one of the largest tech tie-ups ever. Marvell's own release puts the **implied enterprise value at $115 billion for Lumentum, approximately $1,132 per share, a premium of about 23.9%** over the unaffected closing price of **28 October** — the last session before speculation began.

Reconcile the numbers before using them, because two very different figures are circulating: **the equity value is roughly $80 billion on the all-stock terms, and the $115 billion is enterprise value including debt.** Both are correct and they are not interchangeable.

The market's verdict is the interesting part. **Marvell fell about 3% and Lumentum fell about 6%, with Lumentum trading well below the implied $1,132** — which is a textbook signal that holders do not believe the deal closes on those terms, or do not want the Marvell paper it is paid in. **Coherent rose about 2%** on read-across.

The strategic frame Marvell is buying: optical connectivity is the part of the AI data-centre build that is not a GPU. **The deal is explicitly aimed at Broadcom**, which has been assembling the same position.

## What this adds up to

**The war premium was a probability, and the probability moved — that is all that happened today.** No barrel of supply was restored. A draft proposal is in circulation, the Supreme Leader has authorised negotiators, and Goldman still models $140 Brent in 2027 if Gulf output stays 3.5 million barrels a day below its 2025 average.

So the honest read is a de-risking, not a resolution. It is enough to own airlines and refiners against it and enough to take the upstream trade off — but the single thing that would confirm it is a **signature**, and the single thing that would reverse it is a **strike headline**, which this conflict has produced repeatedly for nine months.
