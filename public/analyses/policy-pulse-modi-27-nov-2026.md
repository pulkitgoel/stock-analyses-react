For twelve years the Indian government has been told it does not manufacture enough. On Wednesday, Washington said the opposite.

The USTR finding against 14 economies runs to 189 pages. The India section does not allege dumping at a price. It alleges that the government's industrial policy works — that Make in India, the Production Linked Incentive schemes, Atmanirbhar Bharat and even the India Brand Equity Foundation promote "structural excess capacity and production," including "by offering financial incentives to eligible companies based on their incremental sales."

The proposed remedy is up to **25%** on all goods of India, net of existing Section 301 tariffs. If a bilateral trade agreement is concluded, the rate falls to **17%**.

## The finding is about the scheme, not the product

Two numbers in the report do the work, and both are uncomfortable for the government's own framing.

Solar module capacity is put at **154 GW** against domestic demand of **53 GW**. Steel capacity rose **26.2%** between 2019 and 2024. Against that, manufacturing as a share of Indian GDP has hovered between **13.1% and 14.4%** over five years, averaging **13.5%**.

That combination — a small manufacturing share of GDP alongside double-digit capacity additions in specific sectors — is precisely the case USTR is making. It is not a claim that India is a manufacturing superpower. It is a claim that the incentives are building capacity faster than the domestic market can absorb it, in the sectors the incentives name.

The government's programmes are also, by design, sales-linked. A scheme that pays on incremental sales is a scheme that pays for volume, and volume is what the finding is measuring.

## India's answer is procedural, and that is the point

The Commerce Ministry's response, issued on Wednesday evening, is that "India does not have structural excess capacity in any sector," that the department made that case in its submissions during the investigation, that the notice is "currently under examination," and that trade concerns should be addressed inside the Bilateral Trade Agreement negotiations — with a reaffirmed commitment to concluding them early.

No countervailing duty. No retaliation list. No WTO complaint. A restatement of position plus a request to negotiate.

That is a rational choice and it should be read as one: the gap between 25% and 17% is eight percentage points of duty, which is roughly the size of a decent export margin, and the only instrument that closes it is the agreement. But it also means nothing in this file resolves before January, and the comment window runs to **5 January 2027** with hearings from 25 January.

The Indian market read it as unresolved rather than as resolved in India's favour. The Sensex fell 1.17% on Friday with trade-deal uncertainty cited among the drivers, and exporters have begun telling reporters they are opening alternative markets — with steel, textiles, jewellery and pharmaceuticals named as the exposed lines.

Jefferies put chemicals, textiles and capital goods at the top of the hit list. That ordering is the useful one, because it is not the same as the sector list in the finding.

## The Cabinet is funding the same thesis

On the same days the finding landed, the Cabinet cleared two infrastructure approvals that are, in substance, bets on export logistics:

- A **second container terminal at Kamarajar Port** at an estimated **₹4,288.26 crore**, adding **2.0 million TEU** of annual container-handling capacity.
- A **greenfield airport at Doloo in Assam's Silchar** at an estimated **₹2,014 crore**, aimed at Barak Valley connectivity.
- An **India-Singapore MoU on aviation MRO**, which is the services-side counterpart to the goods story.

Container capacity is the one to think about. India runs a structural container deficit — the Cabinet separately approved a ₹10,000 crore Container Manufacturing Promotion Scheme in October to lift domestic capacity tenfold to 7.5 lakh TEU a year, against a global market where China holds more than 90% of supply. Adding 2.0 million TEU of port handling at one terminal, while building the boxes at home, is a coherent import-substitution chain.

It is also, from Washington's vantage, more capacity.

## The one group that did not care

The Nifty India Defence index rose 4% to 9,301.05 with 21 of its 22 constituents in the green, on a day the benchmark fell 1.17%.

The trigger was not a policy. It was a guidance raise.

BEML closed at ₹2,346.10, up 14.99%, its biggest single-day gain since June 2024, after lifting FY27 revenue growth guidance to over 35% from a range of 25-30%. The supporting numbers: Q2 revenue up 42% to ₹1,191.61 crore against ₹839.09 crore, profit down 31% to ₹33.34 crore, order inflow up 130% year on year to ₹6,230 crore including ₹5,400 crore of trainsets for the high-speed rail project, and an order book of ₹21,388 crore — of which only ₹3,421 crore is executable this year. The market chose the top line over the profit line, and it chose the ₹17,967 crore of work that sits beyond FY27.

The ordering inside that table is the useful part. The two smallest and most ammunition-linked names led — Solar Industries at +6.58% and Data Patterns at +4.39% — while Bharat Electronics, the largest defence PSU by revenue and the one with the deepest order book, rose least at +1.72%. A guidance raise at a rail-and-earthmoving PSU lifted the whole complex, and it lifted the highest-beta names hardest. That is a risk-appetite signal, not an order-flow signal.

Omnitech Engineering is the extreme version. It hit its 5% upper circuit at ₹544.55 on a BrahMos Aerospace development order for metallic airframe components and canister subsystems — and a development order is not a contract. The parts are made so the customer can evaluate them, a production order is a separate event quarters later, and Omnitech disclosed no value and said the order falls below its formal disclosure threshold. The stock is up more than 150% this year and down almost 25% in the last month, which is what happens when order flow is the entire thesis.

That divergence is the honest read on Friday: an earnings and guidance event worked, on a day when the policy file — a proposed 25% duty with a January decision — was weighing on everything else.

## What to watch

- **5 January 2027** — comments close on all 14 determinations. **25 January** — public hearings. Implementation "January at the earliest," which means the first tariffed consignment is a February event at best.
- The 25% to 17% fork is the whole trade. Any datable signal on the Bilateral Trade Agreement — a chief-negotiator meeting, a text, an announcement of conclusion — reprices every name in the table above at once.
- **4 December** — Section 232 polysilicon measures take effect at 15% ad valorem plus minimum import prices, on top of the anti-dumping and countervailing duties. This is the one solar item in the file with a date.
- **28-30 November** — October IIP and Q2 FY27 GDP. Industrial production matters more than usual here, because the finding's core claim is about capacity relative to domestic absorption and the IIP is the closest thing to an absorption read.
- **2-4 December** — RBI monetary policy, decision on the 4th, with the repo at 5.25% and the 10-year at 7.2650%.
