## RBI's MPC Opens a Three-Day Meeting With the First Hike Since February 2023 on the Table

**The Reserve Bank of India's six-member Monetary Policy Committee began its October meeting on Monday, October 5, and will announce its decision at 10:00 am on Wednesday, October 7**, with Governor **Sanjay Malhotra** holding a press conference at noon. **The repo rate has been held at 5.25% through four consecutive reviews in 2026**, after cumulative cuts of 125 basis points in 2025.

**A majority of economists now expect a hike — the first since February 2023.** A **Reuters poll conducted between September 18 and 28 found 35 of 61 economists (roughly 60%) expect +25 basis points to 5.50%**, with most also seeing a further increase in December. The published forecasts cluster tightly:

- **SBI Research:** +25bp in October, +25bp in December.
- **Nomura:** +25bp in October and December, taking the repo to **5.75%**.
- **Bank of America:** moved its call forward from December to October and doubled its projected quantum from 50bp to **100bp**, taking the rate to **6.25% in H1 2027**.
- **Union Bank of India:** +25bp now, and one or two further hikes through FY27, to **5.75-6.00%**.
- **EY:** "Chances for a change in the RBI's policy stance and a 25-basis-point hike in the repo rate are high," citing the Fed's September hike, India's comfortable growth outlook, WPI and CPI pressures, and above-trend M3 growth.
- **BofA's Rahul Bajoria:** "We now sense that the RBI has little reason to wait given visibility of growth remains high, inflation appears to be broadening and the need to wait for clarity on the global front declines."

The drivers are all in the skill's watch-list: **August CPI at 4.82%, an eight-month high, with food at 5.95% and rural inflation at 6.13%**; inflation above the 4% target for three months; **WPI at 9.92%**, the fourth month above 9%; **Brent above $100**; the **rupee beyond 96**; the **Fed's September hike to 3.75-4.00%** compressing the interest-rate differential; and **Q1 FY27 GDP growth of 7.8%**, which removes the growth argument for staying accommodative. The counter-case is equally live: inflation remains inside the 2-6% tolerance band and price pressures have not yet become broad-based, which supports a **hawkish hold** rather than a hike.

## GST Council Week: Sitharaman Pre-Announces a Compliance, Refunds and ITC Package for October 7

**Finance Minister Nirmala Sitharaman said on Monday that the next phase of GST reform will focus on simplifying compliance, with "process reforms" coming before the Council shortly.** Writing in a LinkedIn article ahead of the **57th GST Council meeting on Wednesday, October 7 in New Delhi** — with officer-level preparation on October 5 and 6 — she framed the agenda as making **compliance simpler "across registration, returns, refunds, disputes and input tax credit."**

The fiscal backdrop she cited: taxable supplies rose **25.8% year-on-year between October 2025 and July 2026**; **reported B2C sales surged 26.7%** in the post-reform comparison; **gross GST collections reached ₹12.46 lakh crore between April and September 2026, up 11.6% year-on-year**; and net collections after refunds grew 10.4% over the half year. Every month from June through September recorded double-digit annual growth, accelerating nearly 15% across the four months.

The **September print**, released on October 1: **gross GST ₹2.04 lakh crore, up 14.7%** — the third month this fiscal year above ₹2 trillion — with **net revenue up 18.1% to ₹1.77 lakh crore**. The composition is the caveat the skill flags repeatedly: **domestic GST rose 10.1% to ₹1.38 trillion, while GST collected on imports jumped 25.9% to ₹65,525 crore** and net customs-linked GST rose 30.8%. Import-linked revenue accounted for more than half the increase in the headline.

What is expected on the table, per Finance Ministry sources reported by ET — **proposals, not decisions**:

- **Removal of arrest provisions** under the GST law, addressing overreach concerns.
- **Faster refunds: 90% of eligible claims released automatically after a risk check**, aimed at exporters.
- **Input tax credit relief for bona fide recipients** under **Section 16(2)(c)**, and a review of **blocked credits under Section 17(5)**, plus invoice matching and dispute reduction.
- **Registration standardisation** beyond the existing three-working-day automated route for low-risk applicants (output tax liability to registered persons not exceeding ₹2.5 lakh per month, operational since November 1, 2025).
- A **review of GST on the merchant discount rate (MDR) on UPI transactions**, with the new MDR levy taking effect **October 15**.

## SEBI's Project Jagrook Goes Live Today: Broker Websites Must Carry Investor Awareness Messages

**SEBI's October 1 circular — HO/38/24/(15)2026-MIRSD-PODMMC/I/22872/2026, issued under Section 11(1) of the SEBI Act — begins its first compliance phase today.** All stock brokers must now prominently display SEBI's investor awareness messages alongside the existing risk disclosures mandated by the May 19, 2023 circular. BSE published its implementing notice (20261005-37) on October 5.

The phase structure:

- **Websites, October 5 to October 31:** investor awareness messages **and** risk disclosures must both be displayed.
- **Websites, from November 1:** investor awareness messages become **mandatory on the landing page**.
- **Trading apps, October 5 to October 31:** display is **voluntary** — but if a message is shown, the risk disclosures become optional for that day.
- **Trading apps, from November 1:** mandatory, with awareness messages and risk disclosures shown on **alternate days** on the landing page.
- **Exchanges and depositories** must inform their members, publish the circular, display SEBI's messages as provided, and amend their bye-laws, rules and regulations.
- SEBI reserves the right to modify the awareness messages, so brokers must maintain alignment with the latest versions.

This is part of **Project Jagrook**, SEBI's nationwide investor awareness initiative, timed ahead of **World Investor Week 2026**. Two adjacent compliance deadlines also sit in the same window: the **SEBI Master Circular for Debenture Trustees** of September 28, 2026 — which consolidates all debenture-trustee circulars into 17 chapters, rescinds the August 13, 2025 version, and requires implementation of **Regulation 9C** (carrying non-SEBI-regulated activities through a **Separate Business Unit**) **by October 27, 2026**, with a Recovery Expense Fund of 0.01% of issue size capped at ₹25 lakh per issuer — and the **October 31, 2026 deadline** for all SEBI-regulated entities to complete a digital-accessibility audit of their platforms and remediate findings.

## Defence: Project-75(I) Reaches Final Approval Stage With ₹80,000 Crore on the Table, and the IAF Chief Calls Out Tejas Mk-1A

**Defence Secretary Rajesh Kumar Singh said India is in the final stages of clearing a deal worth more than ₹80,000 crore to build six advanced conventional submarines domestically with a German technology partner**, and that a separate proposal for **three additional Scorpene (Kalvari-class) boats at Mazagon Dock Shipbuilders (MDL) in Mumbai** remains under consideration. His words: "**Project-75(I) is at the final stage. It has been submitted and is pending a final decision by the competent authority**" and "Additional Scorpene submarines are also being considered. It is not as advanced as the other one, but that is also under consideration."

The programme structure:

- **Project-75(I):** six diesel-electric attack submarines built in India under a strategic partnership with **ThyssenKrupp Marine Systems (TKMS)** of Germany, with **MDL** as the Indian shipyard and **substantial Indian private-sector participation** anticipated. More than ₹80,000 crore of investment.
- **Capability content:** advanced sensors, improved stealth, modern weapons, and **Air Independent Propulsion (AIP)**, which extends submerged endurance without surfacing.
- **Indigenous legs:** DRDO's indigenously developed **AIP system** is to be retrofitted on a Scorpene-class submarine during a refit expected around **mid-2027**, and the **Naval Physical and Oceanographic Laboratory (NPOL)** has developed an indigenous submarine integrated combat system. TKMS has an option for three additional boats in its own project outlook.
- **Driver:** India is monitoring growing Pakistan-China naval cooperation, including the Pakistan Navy's planned induction of **Hangor-class** submarines.

The rest of the defence tape:

- **MoD signed a ₹810.79 crore contract with Bharat Dynamics Limited (BDL)** for **160 Satellite Smart Anti-Airfield Weapons (SAT-SAAW)** plus associated equipment for the IAF, under Buy (Indian-IDDM). (Executed September 23.)
- **HAL regulatory filing dated October 2:** the PSU will acquire **CAE Canada's 50% stake in HATSOFF**, the helicopter simulator training academy, buying **3,84,04,205 equity shares of ₹10 each**, with completion expected within 60 days of signing. HATSOFF recorded turnover of ₹80.95 crore in 2025-26 and is the country's first Level-D certified full-fidelity helicopter simulator facility.
- **The pushback:** IAF chief Air Chief Marshal **A.P. Singh** said on October 3 that **no Tejas Mk-1A aircraft has been formally handed over despite contracts tying up more than ₹1.1 lakh crore**, with the first deliveries due over two years ago. Fighter squadrons stand at **29 against 42 sanctioned**, and the IAF needs **36-40 jets annually** to arrest the decline. Asked about penalties, Singh said "this time, the delay has been much." He also flagged Pakistan's fifth-generation collaboration with China as "a cause for worry," and Tejas Mk-2's first flight is now expected **mid-2027**.
- **Pipeline:** the Defence Secretary has projected roughly **₹2 trillion of defence contracts to be signed in FY26**, with Indian firms taking the bulk.
