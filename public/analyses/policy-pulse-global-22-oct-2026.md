Two markets broke in opposite directions on Thursday and both were right. Oil went to a five-month high on a war that is being planned rather than fought, and equities sold off because that oil price is a tax on everything else. The ten-year Treasury yield fell 11 basis points — which tells you the bond market did not read this as inflation. It read it as demand destruction.

**Brent crude rose to $112.01 a barrel by 1311 GMT, up $3.19 or 2.9%, its highest level since May 18.** The trigger was a Houthi drone attack on **Saudi Aramco's Shaybah oilfield that killed two people**, alongside strikes on Dammam airport. The State Department ordered some diplomats and the families of US government employees to leave Saudi Arabia. The New York Times reported that **US and Israeli militaries have drafted plans to resume strikes on Iran after the November 3 midterm election**. Iran's Revolutionary Guard described a shift to an "offensive" doctrine, and the UK is reported to be sending warplanes to help defend the kingdom.

## The Offset Nobody Is Pricing

Crude exports through the Strait of Hormuz have climbed back toward pre-conflict levels, with some tankers switching off tracking systems to move undetected. That is a genuine cap.

It also defines the trade. The price is now set by the probability of an interruption rather than an interruption itself, which is the most fragile kind of premium there is. A single de-escalation headline removes most of it, and the positioning that would be caught out is long energy, short airlines.

## Asia Sold Everything, Not Just Chips

**Tokyo's Nikkei 225 fell 2.7% to 62,907.84**, with **SoftBank Group down 6.9%**. **Hong Kong's Hang Seng shed 3.03% to 22,934.68**, down 717.19 points, with the China Enterprises index off 3.20% and the **Hang Seng TECH index down 5.03%**. The Shanghai Composite gave up 1.5% to 3,658.03.

This was not a rotation out of semiconductors into software — the pattern that defined July and August. Tech was hit hardest everywhere, in every market, on the same day. That is a de-risking event, not a rotation.

## The Safe-Haven Signal That Did Not Behave

This is the part worth sitting with. On a day of escalating Middle East conflict:

- The **US 10-year yield fell to 5.009%**, down roughly 11 basis points — a large move for one session.
- The **VIX rose 14.4% to 19.08**.
- **Gold fell 1.77% to $4,076.50.**
- **Bitcoin fell 3.67% to $81,366.**

Gold and bitcoin both fell while the VIX rose. That is not a classic fear trade; it is a liquidation. When investors sell the hedges along with the risk assets, they are raising cash, and the trigger is usually a funding or margin constraint rather than a change in view. The collapse in Treasury yields alongside it says the destination was government paper.

## The India Read-Through

**The Sensex fell 0.62% to 73,249.39 and the Nifty 50 lost 0.54% to 22,687.65**, with FMCG down 1.74% and IT down 1.31%. India's exposure is mechanical: a crude importer running a current-account deficit takes the oil price on the chin, and the rupee is the transmission channel before equities are.

The honest framing is that India fell less than the rest of Asia on a day with no domestic news, which is a relative-strength signal rather than a weakness one.

## What Would Change This

- **A signed agreement or a formal strike deferral.** Either removes most of the oil premium within a session.
- **Hormuz flows continuing to normalise.** Already happening, and currently pushing against the price.
- **A French budget resolution.** The OAT at 5% is a European discount-rate problem that no Asian earnings season fixes.
- **November 3.** The reported strike planning is explicitly sequenced after the US midterms.
