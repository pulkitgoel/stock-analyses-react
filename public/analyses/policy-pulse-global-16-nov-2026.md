The best-performing large market this month is the one with the least exposure to the trade that is being unwound.

India took in ₹84,290 crore of foreign portfolio money in the nine sessions from 2 to 13 November — about $8.9 billion, more than the combined ₹70,704 crore that foreign investors pulled out in September and October. The Nifty is up 9.1% in November through the 13th, and about 13% from its 8 October close.

The same window contains roughly $1.9 trillion of destroyed market value in Nvidia alone, a KOSPI that fell 1.76% today with Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix leading the declines, and a $1.4 trillion initial public offering pricing on Wednesday. These are not separate stories. The money leaving the AI trade had to land somewhere, and India is the large market that was already de-rated.

## India closed higher and the median stock did not

Monday's close was 82,102.85 on the Sensex, up 510.40 points or 0.63%, and 25,291.10 on the Nifty, up 146.80 points or 0.58%. The Nifty touched 25,337.60 intraday.

Beneath that:

- **31 Nifty constituents rose, 19 fell.** This was not a broad rally.
- **Nifty FMCG +1.87%**, Bank +0.89%, Private Bank +0.88% to a record high, PSU Bank +0.51%.
- **Nifty Metal -1.50%, Pharma -1.53%, Realty -1.34%, Auto -1.29%.**
- **Nifty Midcap 100 -0.51% and Nifty Smallcap 100 -1.10%.** The broader market fell while the benchmark rose.
- **Total BSE-listed market capitalisation fell by about ₹1 lakh crore** to ₹509.21 lakh crore, even as both headline indices gained.

That last line is the whole session in one number. Foreign money is buying the largest, most liquid, most index-weighted names. It is not buying the market — it is buying access to the market.

The single largest drag was Tata Motors Passenger Vehicles at ₹262.40, down 8.70%. Its Q2 FY27 print on Friday showed a consolidated net loss of ₹5,531 crore against a profit a year earlier, including ₹6,738 crore of exceptional items, and JLR cut its FY27 adjusted EBIT margin guidance to 2-3% from around 4% with free cash outflow now expected near £1.25 billion. That is an earnings event with a cost and a China demand problem inside it, and it is the exception to the rotation: foreign money does not buy a leveraged global cyclical because it is not an AI stock.

Brent settled the argument for the rest. Around $95.40 a barrel, down 0.4%, after the IEA said the world oil market is likely to be in surplus in the fourth quarter of 2026 with West Asia exports recovering faster than demand. For an economy that imports most of its crude, that is a terms-of-trade gain arriving at the same time as the flows.

## Where the ₹84,290 crore came from

The reversal has four identifiable drivers, and only one of them is Indian policy.

First, crude. A $95 Brent instead of a $107 Brent changes the import bill, the current-account arithmetic and the inflation path at once.

Second, earnings. September-quarter results have been strong enough to stop the downgrade cycle, and the market entered November having already fallen for two months.

Third, the AI rotation. Motilal Oswal's read, cited across the flow commentary, is that Indian equities rebounded from their October lows while most global markets sold off on rate-hike concerns, elevated bond yields and doubts about the returns on AI infrastructure spending. India was the cheap market that had already de-rated — the Nifty fell about 8.9% in the year to 30 September while the S&P rose 14% and the KOSPI nearly doubled.

Fourth, and least verifiable, hope of relief on long-term capital gains tax for foreign investors. SEBI recommended scrapping it on 2 November. The recommendation is non-binding and the decision point is the February 2027 Union Budget. Anyone buying on that basis is buying a recommendation, not a policy.

The honest caveat is the one the flow desks are making themselves: nine sessions is not a trend. Foreign investors are still net sellers of roughly ₹2.1 lakh crore of Indian equities in 2026. November's buying offset about 28.5% of the year's earlier selling. Domestic institutions were net sellers of ₹24,190 crore in the week to 13 November while foreign investors bought ₹33,340 crore — the two have swapped sides, and that is a flow rotation, not a re-rating.

## The AI half: $1.9 trillion, and the IPO that tests it

Nvidia fell about 12% last week and is down another 2.10% today at $159.43. Since its 6 October peak it has lost roughly $1.9 trillion of market value. AMD is down 5.15% intraday, Micron 6.88%, TSMC 6.14%, Broadcom 4.69% — the second sources are being marked down harder than the name with the leadership crisis.

Into that, Anthropic prices its IPO on 18 November and begins trading on Nasdaq as ANT on 19 November. The terms: 825 million Class A shares at $70 to $78, raising up to $64.4 billion, with a further $26 billion private placement from cornerstone investors including Alphabet, Nvidia, AMD, Broadcom, Micron, Microsoft and TSMC — $90.4 billion in total, at a fully diluted valuation of $1.26 trillion to $1.41 trillion for a company that is still loss-making and carries close to $900 billion of computing commitments.

That is the test the AI trade has been missing. Until Wednesday, the argument about AI capex was conducted between public-company balance sheets and private marks. After Wednesday it is conducted on a public screen, with a share price, every day.

## What to watch

- **17 November** — Nvidia's results after the close, and Home Depot and Disney before it.
- **18-19 November** — Anthropic prices on the 18th and trades as ANT on the 19th. The first public mark on a frontier lab.
- **19 November** — the Trump-Xi meeting in Shenzhen. A truce extension removes the scarcity premium from the critical-minerals complex and is a fresher input for Indian equities than anything domestic this week.
- **28 November and 30 November** — October IIP and Q2 FY27 GDP. September IIP was 6.7%, a four-month low, and the GDP consensus is 7.5-8%.
- **Sustained flows, not nine sessions** — the confirmation test for the India trade is whether foreign buying continues once the AI complex finds a floor. If it does not, this was a rotation of weeks, not a re-rating.
