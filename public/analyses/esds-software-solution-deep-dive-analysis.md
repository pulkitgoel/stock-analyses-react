## 2. Business Overview

ESDS Software Solution (incorporated 2005, Nashik) is an AI-enabled **cloud, managed services, data centre infrastructure and software** provider. It is one of only two Indian players offering the full stack of GPU-as-a-Service (GPUaaS), cloud, managed services, data centre infrastructure and software, and the larger of the two by FY26 revenue (Nexdigm report cited in the RHP).

**Revenue mix (FY26)**
- Infrastructure-as-a-Service (cloud + colocation): ₹207.2 Cr — 43.9% of revenue
- Managed services: ₹194.6 Cr — 41.2% (roughly doubled YoY on AI/GPU-related work)
- Software-as-a-Service: ₹70.4 Cr — 14.9%

**Operating assets**
- Five data centres: Nashik, Airoli (Navi Mumbai), Bengaluru, Mohali, Noida (opened Oct 2025); ~75,266 sq ft aggregate
- Two planned: Kolkata and Sahibabad/Ghaziabad
- Asset-light government model: operates 3 of STPI's 6 Tier-3 compliant data centres (Bengaluru, Mohali, Noida)
- Proprietary platforms eNlight and SWARAJ Cloud (India's first "Made in India" cloud, 2011); holds India's only patent for vertical auto-scaling cloud technology (filed in India and the US); Security-as-a-Service (SECaaS) framework

**Customer base (as of June 2026)**
- 2,516 customers in FY26 (1,465 in FY24; 1,714 in FY25)
- 104 government clients on its cloud, 115 banks and financial institutions across 1,045 branches, 113 organisations on its SAP-HANA cloud, six smart cities
- Revenue retention 94.92%
- Average revenue per customer **fell** to ₹18.8 lakh in FY26 from ₹21.1 lakh in FY25 — new customer additions are smaller accounts
- Largest customer was ~15.9% of FY26 revenue. The FY25 top customer (a Russian BFSI entity, 20.15% of revenue, ₹72.8 Cr) collapsed to 2.80% (₹13.2 Cr) in FY26 after sanctions; a new UAE customer contributed ₹75.24 Cr in FY26

**Moat assessment:** The genuine moat is regulatory, not technological — India-resident data on Indian servers under Indian jurisdiction, which matters to BFSI and government clients bound by RBI/localisation rules. That is a real, defensible niche against AWS/Azure/Google. The GPUaaS business, by contrast, is a reseller position with no evident structural moat.

## 7. Order Book & Revenue Visibility

There is no conventional order book to report. What exists instead is one very large GPU commitment and one very large customer advance — and understanding the direction of both is essential:

**The $1.25 billion SharonAI agreement (signed 1 April 2026)**
- SharonAI Holdings (NASDAQ: SHAZ) provides GPU compute capacity; **ESDS and its subsidiaries are the CUSTOMER** paying for it — confirmed by SharonAI's SEC filings (8-K / 424B3), which define ESDS, SPOCHUB Solutions and ESDS Cloud FZ as "the Customer"
- ~8,208 NVIDIA B300 (Blackwell) GPUs and ~17.83 PB of storage, hosted in Australia (NEXTDC facility); revenue start September 2026, with a 16 September 2026 target delivery date
- Five-year term, ~$250 million per year (~₹2,100 Cr), **take-or-pay** (ESDS pays whether or not it uses the capacity); payments monthly in advance; ESDS cannot terminate for convenience in the first 36 months
- **$140 million of letters of credit / bank guarantees** required as security — roughly twice ESDS's pre-IPO total asset base
- ESDS's plan is to resell this capacity to Indian enterprises — "buy wholesale, sell retail". No binding India-side customer contracts have been disclosed

**The ₹1,176.6 crore advance**
- Received by SPOCHUB Solutions (subsidiary) from an overseas enterprise customer for a GPUaaS project
- Sits as a liability until the service is delivered; refundability has not been publicly clarified
- Management has guided GPU revenue to begin from **Q3 FY27**

**The maths to keep in front of you:** ESDS must sell ~₹2,600 Cr+ of GPU capacity annually (at a 25-30% markup) to cover a ₹2,100 Cr annual commitment. FY26 revenue was ₹472 Cr. That is a 5-6x step-up in annual revenue, from a business with no disclosed downstream order book at that scale.

Screener's peer and small-cap data show no order-book line for ESDS — unlike defence/EPC names, this company's "visibility" rests on this single arrangement.

## 16. Final Verdict

**Verdict: AVOID AT CMP — genuinely good core business, but the price and the AI story have run far ahead of what is disclosed.**

ESDS is not a bad company — it is a decent, profitable, fast-growing Indian cloud and data centre business with a defensible regulatory niche, low debt and real cash on the balance sheet. The problem is what is being bought at ₹1,754.

Three things must be separated, and the market is currently conflating them:

1. **The core business** — worth owning at a sensible multiple. FY26: ₹472 Cr revenue, ₹234 Cr EBITDA, ₹121 Cr PAT, ROCE 30%. Even assuming strong 25-30% growth, this core supports nothing close to ₹20,550 Cr of market cap.
2. **The GPU advance** — real cash (₹1,176.6 Cr), but a liability that must be earned back through delivery, and one that distorts every cash flow statistic for FY26.
3. **The SharonAI commitment** — a ~₹2,100 Cr/year **cost** obligation with no disclosed matching revenue. The market has been reading it as an order book. It is the opposite.

Add a 63.5%-margin dormant subsidiary contributing roughly 45% of group profit with the promoter personally holding 1% of it, and the correct posture is not "buy the AI story" — it is "wait for the first listed quarter and the delivery checkpoint."
