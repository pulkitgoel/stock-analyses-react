## Oil Above $95: Renewed US-Iran Strikes Put Physical Hormuz Risk Back at the Centre

Day 187 of the Iran war. US Central Command said it was attacking IRGC targets inside Iran after a tanker transiting the Strait of Hormuz was hit by projectiles on Aug 31. Over the weekend the US struck Iranian rocket launchers on Larak Island (first strikes since late July) and Iran retaliated against US bases in Jordan. President Trump's Sep 1 Truth Social posts escalated the rhetoric: Iran is "DEAD", "stay the course", and a message to countries facing fuel shortages to "buy oil from the United States or get your own".

**Crude reaction (5-week highs, $100 risk back on the table):**
- Brent settled +4.6% to $94.65 on Sep 1 and extended to ~$95.38 on Sep 2 (+0.8%) - highest close in five weeks
- WTI settled +5.2% to $90.22 on Sep 1, trading ~$90.70 on Sep 2
- Iran has declared "not a single drop" of oil, gas or chemical fertilizer will pass through Hormuz; the strait remains effectively closed to commercial shipping (~3 transits/day vs ~85 normal)
- Analysts note the market's biggest fear has shifted from diplomacy back to physical chokepoint risk; EIA base case still assumes the strait eases this month

**Sep 1 US session confirmation:** the Energy Select Sector SPDR (XLE) climbed 2%, with CVX +2.4% and XOM +2.2% - the only strong sector on a down tape.

## Global Bond Rout Compresses Equities; Fed September Hike Odds Jump to 68%

The oil shock is feeding a global bond selloff. The US 10-year yield rose 8 bps to 4.804% - its highest in 20 months; the 30-year added 13 bps to 5.28% and the 2-year rose 6 bps to 4.40%. Overseas, Japan's 10-year hit 3.00% for the first time since 1996 and UK gilts touched levels last seen in 2008.

- Fed Chair Kevin Warsh warned at Jackson Hole (Aug 28) that inflation remains sticky; CME FedWatch now prices a 68% probability of a 25 bps hike to 3.75%-4.00% at the Sep 15-16 FOMC
- New York Fed's John Williams told CNBC that higher yields reflect a strong economy
- Data misses added pressure: ISM manufacturing 54.6 in August (55.2 expected, 55.6 prior); construction spending -0.5% in July (vs +0.1% expected); JOLTS openings 7.27M (up 89K)
