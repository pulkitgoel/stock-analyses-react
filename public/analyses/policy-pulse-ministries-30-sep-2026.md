## The Fiscal Scorecard: 41.9% of the FY27 Deficit Target Spent in Five Months

**The Controller General of Accounts released the April-August monthly accounts on Wednesday, September 30**, and the arithmetic is the backdrop to everything else in this edition:

- **Fiscal deficit ₹7,10,249 crore — 41.9% of the FY27 budget estimate**, against 38.1% of the BE at the same point last year. The full-year target is **₹16.96 lakh crore, or 4.3% of GDP**
- **Net tax revenue around ₹8.38 lakh crore for April-August, 29.2% of the BE** (28.6% a year ago)
- **Total expenditure around ₹20.78 lakh crore, 38.9% of the BE** (37.1% a year ago), with capital spending and subsidies both running ahead of last year's pace
- The same-day context that makes it legible: the **₹2.79 lakh crore Cabinet package** (PM-DHARA, the Rabi MSP policy and a Delhi traffic-management system), of which **₹1.86 lakh crore is front-loaded into the power sector** — see the India edition for the programme detail

**Why it is a market item rather than an accounting one.** The Centre's borrowing programme is the supply side of the Indian bond market at precisely the moment the global long end is repricing: the **US 30-year touched 5.6% on September 29**, and Indian spending is running roughly two percentage points of the BE ahead of last year's pace. That combination — not the deficit level itself — is what decides whether G-sec yields stay anchored, and bank NIMs, insurance investment income and infrastructure financing costs all sit downstream of that answer.

## SEBI at APMI: FPI Onboarding Rails Rebuilt, CAS Untouched, No Exchange Interoperability

**SEBI Chairman Tuhin Kanta Pandey spoke at the APMI 3rd Annual Conference in Mumbai on September 30** and drew a firm line between mandate and market movement: SEBI **cannot force foreign capital into India**, but it is rebuilding the digital rails so that entry is friction-free — a direct answer to the record FPI selling of the past month.

What is actually changing:

- **Registration is being made "very fast, seamless and digital"** jointly with the RBI, via an **updated NSDL front end**, a **unified India Market Access portal**, **common application tracking**, and a shift to **digital signatures and e-Power of Attorney** in place of wet-signature documents
- **The RBI is exploring document certification through foreign commercial banks and integration with the SWIFT network** to compress onboarding further
- **On the Closing Auction Session: "nothing is being considered outside the paper."** SEBI will implement exactly the published proposal — the derivatives settlement-price adjustments are the operative piece, and the consultation comment period runs through October
- **Bond index and bond derivatives approvals are at draft stage**: "they have prepared some draft guidelines and asked for comments, and we are pursuing with RBI to finalise" — no timeline committed
- **No exchange interoperability and no cross-trading:** "There is no such thing going on… if it happens or when it happens, you will come to know"
- **Continuous listing norms get teeth**: verifying the physical presence of listed entities is "well within the means" of exchange assessments, with enforcement anchored to SEBI LODR, company law and FEMA/LRS rules — relevant given that Omaxe was barred for three months for a market-price-sensitive violation and that Lloyds Enterprises settled disclosure-lapse cases this month

## The RBI's Record $200 Billion Forward Book — and the Window That Closed Today

**The RBI's net forward dollar liabilities reached a record $200 billion in August, up $63 billion month-on-month from nearly $137 billion in July**, per data published on **September 30**. The mechanism that produced it is the same one that produced record reserves:

- The RBI absorbed dollars drawn through the **concessional swap facility for foreign-currency deposits and the discounted hedging facility for overseas borrowings** in force between **June 8 and September 30, 2026** — that window closes **today**
- Those measures **attracted $143.5 billion between June 8 and September 18**, of which nearly **$133 billion arrived as foreign-currency deposits**; FX reserves hit a record **$785.7 billion** in the week ended September 4
- Because the absorption was executed through **buy/sell swaps concentrated in three-to-five-year maturities**, the reserve gain created a matching forward liability
- Over the past three weeks the RBI has run **dollar-rupee sell/buy swaps with three-month to one-year maturities**, which bankers read as bringing forward maturities while draining surplus rupee liquidity; FX operations have absorbed an estimated **$20 billion of excess rupee liquidity**
- DBS's read: a portion of the reserves could be earmarked against the forward book, muting the risk that deposit maturities or debt repayments trigger a dollar-demand spike. Near-term priorities, in its words: **"manage liquidity, gradually lower the sizeable forwards book, and support the domestic currency"**

**Why October 1 is the pivot.** The inflow measures that supported the rupee through the quarter expire into a tape of record foreign selling — **FIIs sold ₹10,148 crore on September 30, after ₹9,980 crore on September 29** — so the currency's support has to come from the RBI's own book rather than from policy-induced inflows. That is a different regime for bank funding costs and for import-cost inflation.

## SEBI's 215th Board Rewrites PMS and Settlement — Thirteen Decisions in One Sitting

**SEBI's 215th Board meeting on September 24, 2026 (PR No. 59/2026) approved thirteen items.** Two of them are structural, and both are revenue-relevant for the listed capital-market complex.

**1. SEBI (Portfolio Managers) Regulations, 2026** — replacing the 2020 framework. The stated objectives are industry development, ease of compliance, and consolidation. What actually changed:

- **Portfolio managers may now invest in IPOs and primary-market debt issuances.** This is the single most consequential addition: PMS books gain access to the new-issue pipeline that has, until now, been the exclusive preserve of mutual funds and direct bidding.
- **Exchange-traded derivatives exposure up to 1.25x client AUM**
- **Up to 10% of client AUM in investment-grade, unlisted non-convertible debt** under discretionary PMS, with client consent
- **Foreign securities permitted** under both discretionary and non-discretionary PMS (listed equity, debt, REITs, overseas MFs, ETFs, index funds, foreign government debt) subject to FEMA and LRS
- **A new PRIM route** — a portfolio-manager channel for investing client funds in **direct plans of mutual funds**, including ETFs, index funds and SIFs, with a lower entry barrier than classic PMS. New registrations for PRIM-only entities are permitted
- **Economics**: minimum ticket **₹25 lakh**; net worth **₹2 crore**; **no exit load**; a **25% cap on schemes of affiliated/group AMCs**; **maximum fixed fee capped at 1% of AUM**, with performance fees also permitted
- **Segregation** of activities and clients between the MFD and PRIM routes, except for accredited investors
- Separately, **Eligible Fund Managers** may manage and advise eligible investment funds in overseas securities, with India-linked limits aligned to the FPI framework

The consolidation is aggressive: the regulations shrink from **70 pages to 33 (−53%)**, word count falls **42% to 11,308 from 19,486**, and the number of provisos drops **from 47 to four**.

**2. SEBI (Settlement of Administrative and Civil Proceedings) Regulations, 2026** — replacing the 2018 regulations, effective the day after the 30th day from notification. The core change is a **formula**:

> **Settlement Amount = Base Amount × (S + R + G + A − M) + Legal Costs**

where the base amount links to the minimum penalty for the violation and the factors adjust for stage of proceedings, regulatory history, gravity, aggravating circumstances and mitigating factors. Supporting changes: a **fast-track route** for specified violations and matters up to **₹10 lakh**; settlement permitted at later stages including before the **Securities Appellate Tribunal and the Supreme Court**; settlement notices may issue **before** show-cause notices in certain cases; the window to apply after a show-cause notice extends **from 60 to 90 days**; and the additional amount for refiling a withdrawn application drops **from 50% to 20%**. SEBI found that where applications were rejected or withdrawn and later penalised, the settlement amount averaged **about eight times the final penalty**, and expects that gap to narrow to roughly four times.

**The other eleven items**, briefly: a **common advertisement code** for SEBI-regulated entities permitting brand-level celebrity endorsements with safeguards; relaxation of the call-recording requirement for research analysts and research entities dealing with institutional clients; **FPI access to non-agricultural commodity derivatives**; **depository receipts against REIT and InvIT units**; higher **vault manager net worth norms** and an expanded vault-manager framework; changes to the **accredited investor regime**; and ease-of-doing-business amendments including debt-market rules.

## Tata Sons vs the RBI: The Trusts Attempt a Restructuring Instead of a Listing

**Tata Trusts, which hold 66% of Tata Sons, have proposed merging Tata Electronics Systems Solutions (TESS) and Tata Consulting Engineers (TCE) into Tata Sons** — an attempt to keep the holding company private and avoid the stock-market listing the RBI has effectively directed.

## The Other End of the Tape: OMC Losses of ₹530 Crore a Day, and Rationing at Private Pumps

**On September 28-29, Reliance Industries and Nayara Energy began rationing diesel and petrol at select outlets** as crude costs outran flat retail prices.

- **Jio-BP outlets** have been told to sell up to **50 litres of diesel per customer** at some sites, with **daily caps of 6,000 litres** at others
- **Nayara Energy outlets** are limiting transactions to between **70 and 200 litres**
- **Indian Oil, Bharat Petroleum and Hindustan Petroleum continue to sell without limits** at their outlets, which are roughly **90% of stations**
- The restrictions are **not nationwide**; they are aimed at preventing bulk buyers from filling drums to resell to bulk consumers at a premium
- **Pump prices have not changed since May**, even with crude around $107 a barrel

The margin arithmetic from ICRA is the core of it: **India's crude basket averaged $117.4 a barrel on September 21 against roughly $66 a barrel across FY26.** OMC marketing margins are running at **negative ₹8 per litre on petrol and negative ₹9 per litre on diesel**, with **domestic LPG under-recoveries of about ₹300 per cylinder**, giving a **combined daily loss of roughly ₹530 crore**. ICRA's earlier September 9 estimates of ₹5 per litre on petrol and ₹23 per litre on diesel have since widened.

Supply-side pressure is compounding: **Saudi Aramco reportedly halted crude supplies to Indian refiners** following attacks on its East-West pipeline. **Russia has banned diesel exports** and may extend the restrictions, and the US has considered curbs on outbound shipments — which makes exports more lucrative than domestic sales for Indian refiners, and is a large part of why private retailers are rationing at home.
