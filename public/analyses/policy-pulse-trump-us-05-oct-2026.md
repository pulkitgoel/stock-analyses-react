## Trump Stands Up the "Super Intelligence Force": The AI Rebrand Gets Its Enforcement Body

**President Donald Trump announced the creation of the "Super Intelligence Force" (SIF) on Sunday, October 4**, a White House task force chartered to coordinate the federal government's response to artificial intelligence. The announcement came by Truth Social, a week after the "Inaugurating The Era Of Super Intelligence" executive order that directed federal agencies to retire the term "Artificial Intelligence" in favour of "Super Intelligence" and the abbreviation "SI" in official documents, websites and policy statements.

The personnel is the signal, not the branding:

- **Director of National Intelligence Jay Clayton** chairs the task force — the same Clayton who, as SEC Chairman in the first Trump term, ran the most deregulatory securities agenda in two decades. Clayton's framing: "**The risk of not being first is high... Not being first increases the identified and unidentified risks, particularly from our adversaries.**"
- **FTC Chairman Andrew Ferguson**, **Office of Personnel Management Director Scott Kupor** and **Emil Michael**, the Pentagon's Under Secretary for Research and Engineering, sit on it.
- The task force reports **directly to Trump and White House Chief of Staff Susie Wiles**, coordinates with consumers, public-interest groups, religious organisations, critical-infrastructure providers and AI companies, and must deliver a **report on AI's risks and opportunities within 120 days**.
- The charter language is explicit that its purpose is to mitigate AI risk **"while preventing overregulation and regulatory capture that would stifle innovation and competition."**

That last clause is the one that matters for capital allocation. The SIF is a **permission structure, not a compliance structure**: a body whose stated job is to pre-empt restrictions rather than impose them. It sits on top of the **White House Accord on Super Intelligence**, the roughly 300-word voluntary pact signed at the September 29 White House meeting by executives from Google, Anthropic, Meta, Nvidia, xAI and OpenAI, with four layers of controls and audits and board-level review of auditor reports — and no enforcement mechanism.

The political context is what makes it a policy event rather than a press release. An **AP-NORC poll released October 1 found only 31% of US adults approve of Trump's handling of AI**, and two-thirds of respondents — **65%** — said that higher-than-usual prices are the result of Trump's policies. Several Republican candidates have broken with the President's data-centre expansion line. **Pope Leo XIV** weighed in that concerns raised by AI experts "should be taken seriously" and are not "fake news" — a direct counter to Trump's repeated characterisation of AI-doom arguments as a "hoax" and a "SICK conspiracy." Musk has already begun rebranding, renaming the SpaceX AI unit **SpaceXAI to SpaceXSI** ("No more AI. SI, it's better"), and .si domain registrations reportedly hit **44,000 in September**.

## Greer Puts Tariff Caps on the Table in the Section 301 Excess-Capacity Probe

**US Trade Representative Jamieson Greer said on Sunday that the Trump administration will take account of tariff caps negotiated in trade agreements when weighing potential new duties under its investigation into excessive industrial capacity.** "I didn't want to make any assumptions about the results of a Section 301 investigation," he told reporters at the close of the G20 trade ministerial in Milwaukee.

The distinction matters because there are now **two separate Section 301 tracks**, and only one of them has actually been imposed:

- **The forced-labour tariffs (in force).** A two-tier 10%/12.5% regime on roughly 60 economies — 86 countries, covering an estimated **99.4% of US imports** — imposed after the Section 122 global surcharge expired on July 24. These were argued at the **Court of International Trade on September 30** on the plaintiffs' contention that they are "blatantly pretextual," with possible outcomes ranging from invalidation to remand to USTR.
- **The overcapacity investigation (not yet imposed).** Opened in March 2026, covering **16 economies including India**, targeting structural excess capacity in steel, autos and other sectors. Greer's Sunday remarks are the first signal that negotiated caps could be credited against any duties — the same architecture as the Section 232 tariff-cap deals.

The refund tail of the earlier IEEPA defeat is still running. The **Federal Register of October 5** confirms that Customs and Border Protection collected an estimated **$166 billion** in IEEPA duties between February 3, 2025 and February 24, 2026 — after the Supreme Court's February 20, 2026 ruling in *Learning Resources, Inc. v. Trump* struck the tariffs down — and that CBP is now building the **CAPE (refund) tool** inside its ACE system to process **over 53 million entry summaries** with interest. Small businesses without ACE accounts are being directed to their customs brokers.

## The Midterm Cash Economy: $90 Medicare Checks Are Out, $5,000 Checks Are Promised

**Trump announced on October 2 that one-time $90 payments would go to 20.8 million Medicare beneficiaries**, and on October 4 posted a Truth Social video renewing his pledge to give **$5,000 to every adult US citizen if Republicans retain the House and Senate** in the November midterms. Those claims followed **$500 "Obamacare refund" checks that began going out last week to roughly 950,000 people** who bought unsubsidised coverage on the federal marketplace.

The delivery record is the caveat. The administration floated a **$5,000 "DOGE dividend" in February 2025** that never materialised; proposed a **$2,000 reciprocal-tariff dividend in November 2025** (the Supreme Court struck the tariffs down in February 2026 and no checks were issued); and in 2020 promised **$200 prescription-drug cards to 33 million Medicare beneficiaries** six weeks before that election, which also never went out. No authorising statute and no funding source have been identified for the $5,000 payment.

For equity analysis, the distinction between announced and appropriated is decisive — but the announced programmes that *are* paying out are a real, if small, transfer to households carrying high marginal propensities to consume, landing three weeks before an election.
