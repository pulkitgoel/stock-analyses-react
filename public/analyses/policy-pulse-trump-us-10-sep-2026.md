## Hot August PPI Plus USD 100 Crude Push the 10-Year to a Three-Year High — September Hike Odds Jump to ~66%

The August producer price index, released Thursday September 10 by the Bureau of Labor Statistics, came in **hot on the headline**: final demand prices rose **0.4% month-on-month** (in line with consensus) and **5.4% year-on-year**, a tenth of a point above the estimate. Core PPI rose 0.2%, slightly softer than the 0.3% expected.

The composition is the tell. **Final demand energy prices rose 4.2%**, led by **diesel up 24.1%**, while goods prices rose 1.1% and services just 0.1% (transportation and warehousing +2.3%). Processed goods rose 1.8%. Portfolio management costs fell 1.6% in the month but remain **18.8% higher year-on-year**.

Two things landed at the same time: **US crude topped USD 100 a barrel** and the **10-year Treasury yield hit its highest level since 2023** (4.836%, +3bp) after a Treasury buyback operation disappointed (USD 6bn of longer-dated debt versus expectations of more). Fed funds futures moved to price roughly a **66% chance of a 25bp hike** at the September 15-16 FOMC, up from a coin flip earlier in the week. Weekly jobless claims were 206K versus 205K expected. Equity futures turned negative after the print. Friday's **August CPI** is the last input before the FOMC — consensus 3.4% headline, 2.4% core.

## Trump Signals the Iran War Runs Past the November Midterms — Oil Stays Bid

Speaking at the Republican convention, President Trump said he believed the **war with Iran would end immediately after the November 3 midterm elections**, arguing Tehran is trying to influence the vote, and separately said **oil and gasoline prices will not fall until after the midterms** — while repeating a pledge of USD 2-3 per gallon gasoline. That is the clearest signal yet that the administration is not seeking a near-term ceasefire, which removes the main downside catalyst for crude.

The physical backdrop is tightening, not loosening: US forces **sank five Iranian oil tankers** on September 8-9 (Kharg Island and the Gulf of Oman) after Iranian missile attacks on a US warship; Iran said it struck **10 vessels** near the Strait of Hormuz and announced an **expanded no-go zone extending outside the strait**. The EIA raised its 2026 and 2027 oil price forecasts as inventories drain.

## Canada Trade War Escalates to Outright Import Bans

Canada's retaliatory tariffs on roughly **USD 20bn of US goods** — duties of 15% to 50% on hundreds of products, including US steel items raised to 50% from 25%, motorcycles, cosmetics and cheese — took effect on September 8. Trump responded with **six proclamations on September 8**: two excluding certain **Canadian alcoholic beverages and dairy products** from importation, two modifying the scope of duties on Canadian **motor vehicles and alcohol**, plus adjustments elsewhere.

The practical effect: tariffs were **removed** on road salt and cement, while **new 50% duties land within six days** on aluminium bars and rods, mattresses, golf carts and certain paper products. Trump also singled out Canadian jet maker **Bombardier** ("no more selling Bombardier in the United States — if they want our market, they must build here").

## Leader Stock Trades: Trump's Nine Largest Energy Holdings Gained USD 1.5-4.4 Million During the Iran War

A CNBC analysis of the President's financial disclosures, company filings and FactSet data (published September 9) found that **Trump's nine largest oil and gas holdings gained between USD 1.5 million and USD 4.4 million** between February 27 (the trading day before the war) and August 31. The nine: **Chevron, ConocoPhillips, Exxon Mobil, Kinder Morgan, Marathon Petroleum, Occidental Petroleum, Phillips 66, Valero Energy and Williams Companies**.

Federal records show the accounts **traded heavily across those names through June 29** (the most recent date on which any trade is disclosed), recording purchases and at least **23 sales**. Separately, on September 6 Trump posted on Truth Social that he had made "hundreds of billions of dollars on stocks... for the U.S.A., not myself," sharing an AI-generated image of himself watching Intel stock rise — the post has added to scrutiny of the accounts rather than settling it. CNBC found no evidence that Trump or his managers traded on advance knowledge of policy decisions.
