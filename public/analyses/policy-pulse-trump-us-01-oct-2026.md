## Trump Media Files the S-4: DJT Becomes a Fusion-Energy Company on Paper

**On September 30, Trump Media & Technology Group and TAE Technologies filed a registration statement on Form S-4 with the SEC**, advancing the all-stock merger the two companies agreed to on **December 18, 2025**. The filing is the mechanical step that turns a signed merger agreement into a shareholder vote, and it is the most concrete corporate action at Trump Media since the company went public in 2024.

The structure, as disclosed:

- **TAE becomes a wholly owned subsidiary of TMTG.** A TMTG subsidiary, T Media Sub, merges into TAE. Trump Media remains the publicly traded entity and shares continue to trade under **DJT** on Nasdaq and, now, on NYSE Texas.
- **Roughly half-and-half ownership.** Each company's holders are allocated about 50% of the combined company, per the merger agreement.
- **Co-CEOs.** Kevin McGurn, interim chief executive of Trump Media, and Michl Binderbauer, chief executive of TAE Technologies, are expected to serve as co-chief executives.
- **Target close: fourth quarter of 2026.** The companies reaffirmed the Q4 target and ended earlier spin-off discussions.
- **The money already committed.** Simultaneously with the S-4, the two companies executed an **amended and restated unsecured convertible note**: a **$200 million first tranche already funded by TMTG**, and a **second tranche of up to $100 million** that TAE may request during a draw period that *began with the S-4 filing* and ends on **November 29, 2026** (or on termination of the merger agreement). Requests must be at least $50 million. Total principal is capped at **$300 million plus PIK interest**, which accrues at **7% and is added to principal without compounding**. Accrued unpaid interest stood at **$10,969,863.01 as of September 30, 2026**.
- **What the combined company claims to be.** TAE describes itself as working across **fusion energy, power management and cancer therapy**, and has been developing fusion technology for more than two decades. The press release states the combined company "would be one of the world's first publicly traded companies developing commercial fusion energy."

Trump Media's market capitalisation is roughly **$2.53 billion**. The stock was trading around **$9.10, up 0.44%**, in early Thursday trade — a muted reaction, which is itself informative: the S-4 registers shares and starts a proxy clock, it does not add revenue. The revenue story at TMTG remains the Truth Social advertising base plus the paid Truth API feed launched on August 1, and that feed still carries a live legal overhang from the Intercept and Freedom of the Press Foundation suit.

The strategically interesting part is the pivot itself. A social-media company with a **$2.53 billion** market value absorbing a two-decade-old fusion developer is a bet on the **electricity-demand side of the AI build-out** — the same trade that has re-rated nuclear and grid names through 2026. That makes DJT a speculative, high-variance proxy on a real theme, funded with the acquirer's own convertible paper rather than third-party project finance.

## "Super Intelligence": the Rebrand Order and the White House Accord

**On September 29 the President signed an executive order titled "Inaugurating The Era Of Super Intelligence,"** and the White House simultaneously convened leaders of the major AI companies to sign a parallel industry document — the **"White House Accord on Super Intelligence."**

What the order actually does:

- **It is a naming mandate, not a regulatory regime.** Section 2 instructs executive departments and agencies to use **"Super Intelligence" and "SI"** in place of "artificial intelligence" and "AI" in non-statutory documents, reports and websites, and to cease acknowledging the former terms in applicable settings. It does **not** require agencies to revise previously issued regulations, contracts, grants or historical documents.
- **The definition is inherited, not new.** Section 3 provides that "Super Intelligence" and "SI" mean the technologies and systems already defined as artificial intelligence under **15 U.S.C. § 9401(3)** — the National AI Initiative Act definition.
- **A legislative proposal is due by November 28, 2026.** Within 60 days, the Assistant to the President for Science and Technology — Michael Kratsios, director of the Office of Science and Technology Policy — must submit proposed legislative language establishing a federal definition of "Super Intelligence," including whether it should modify, expand or supersede the existing statutory AI definition, plus conforming amendments to existing statutory references.
- **The private accord is voluntary and short.** It runs just over **300 words** and sets out what the industry described as **four layers of controls and audits**, robust internal controls with internal and external review, and an undertaking that **boards of directors independently review the reports coming from auditors**. Signatories included the founders of the top technology firms, per the signing event — among them Elon Musk, Mark Zuckerberg and OpenAI's leadership.
- **Two other orders landed the same day.** "Streamlining Access to Government Services Through America.gov" established an **AI-powered federal portal** with all agencies directed to integrate public-facing services within **90 days**; a third order addressed disease-carrying pests.

The context matters for positioning. The order was signed weeks before the midterms, in a period when polling shows Americans increasingly concerned about the pace of AI, and the President has repeatedly characterised warnings of insufficient oversight as a "hoax." This is a **rebuttal order** — it asserts that AI risk is overstated and that the technology should be renamed to reflect its upside. For capex, the operative signal is the **direction of travel: no new compliance burden on model developers, no pause, no licensing regime.** That is capex-permissive for the AI infrastructure complex, and the soft, voluntary, board-reviewed accord is far weaker than the pre-announcement speculation about mandatory audits.

The counterweight is the **evidence of political salience**: AI is now a midterm issue, and Congress is not in session again until the week of November 9. Any binding framework is a 2027 story.

## The Stop Insider Trading Act Dies 53-47 — and the Subtext Is Trump's Own Trade Count

**The Senate failed to advance the Stop Insider Trading Act on Wednesday, by a vote of 53-47.** Sixty votes were needed. **Every Democrat and independent voted no.** The bill had passed the House 232-198 in July with the support of 13 Democrats, and the White House had urged its swift passage.

The mechanism of the failure is worth recording precisely, because it determines what happens next:

- **What the bill would have done.** Prohibit members of Congress, their spouses and their dependent children from *purchasing* stocks issued by publicly traded companies; require a member to file **public notice 7 to 14 days in advance of each intended sale** with the Clerk of the House or Secretary of the Senate, published online and withdrawable if the sale does not happen; and impose penalties of **$2,000 or 10% of the value of the covered investment, whichever is greater**, with forfeiture of any profit on a sale.
- **Why it failed.** Democrats objected that the bill would not force members to sell existing holdings, would allow dividends to be reinvested, would let spouses or children trade on a member's behalf, and would permit trading in private companies. A Republican addition of **voter photo-ID language** was called a "poison pill." The White House framed the outcome as Democrats blocking reform.
- **The number that makes it a leader-trades story, not just an ethics story.** Senate Democratic Leader Chuck Schumer, opposing the bill, noted that it did not cover the President — and cited a Bloomberg analysis that **Trump and his money managers executed approximately 28,700 trades between January 2025 and June 2026.** For scale, a December Common Cause study found members of Congress from both parties executed **13,324 trades worth $635.6 million** in 2025, and media reporting in July put Trump's own 2025 total at **21,000 securities trades** that grew his accounts past **$850 million**.
- **The calendar is now closed.** The Senate adjourned for the midterms and does not return until the week of **November 9**. Nothing further happens on congressional trading before the election.

There is no clean single-stock trade in a failed governance bill, and the skill's discipline is to say so rather than manufacture one. What the vote does is **keep two live dynamics intact**: the "copy-trade" reflex that trails disclosed presidential positions, and the **crypto-industry money now flowing through Senate races** — the Winklevoss twins' Digital Freedom Fund PAC is putting almost $3 million into Ohio against Sherrod Brown, and Fairshake has committed at least $30 million to the same race, after spending more than $40 million to defeat Brown in 2024. Crypto policy influence in the next Congress is being bought now.

## Leader Trades: Trump Bought Southern Company and Public Storage in July

The fresh disclosure detail from the July filing — released September 22 — is specific enough to name:

- **Two pure buys, no sells.** The July disclosure shows the President's accounts **bought Southern Company (SO) and Public Storage (PSA)**, with **no sell activity on either name anywhere in the filing**.
- **The larger municipal-bond book.** A CNBC analysis of disclosures filed since the return to the White House found Trump **ended 2025 with 807 municipal bond positions worth between $240.7 million and $797.6 million**, and has since disclosed **at least 243 purchases in 2026 worth between $68.2 million and $233.8 million**. **48 new purchases** were disclosed on September 22 in the July report, including **five tied to healthcare facilities or systems**. Bonds are reported as purchased and not sold.
- **The attribution the White House gives.** Both the White House and the Trump Organization state that the investments sit in **discretionary accounts managed by independent financial institutions**, that the President and his family have no ability to direct, influence or provide input on how the portfolio is invested, and that the outside managers hold sole and exclusive authority over allocation and trading.

The CNBC analysis also flagged that some holdings point *against* the President's financial interest — accounts have bought debt from governments facing federal funding restrictions.

## Boeing's $20bn F/A-XX, RTX's $20.7bn AMRAAM, and "Project Meridian"

**The contract flow into US primes this week is unusually dense, and it is policy-driven.**

- **Boeing won the Navy's sixth-generation carrier fighter.** The Navy selected **Boeing** for the **F/A-XX**, worth **more than $20 billion**, to replace the F/A-18 Super Hornet fleet in the 2030s. Boeing beat **Northrop Grumman**, its only remaining rival after Lockheed Martin dropped out last year. It is Boeing's **second sixth-generation fighter win**, following the Air Force's F-47 award in 2025 — and the company says delivering two advanced fighters in parallel "was always our plan," alongside a new secure manufacturing facility in St. Louis.
- **RTX took the missile award.** The Pentagon announced a contract potentially worth **$20.7 billion for RTX** to build Advanced Medium-Range Air-to-Air Missiles for Navy and Air Force aircraft and for ground-based interceptors.
- **The supporting awards.** Lockheed Martin won a **$723.9 million** Missile Defense Agency contract; BAE Systems received **$137.6 million** for Virginia-class submarine propulsors.
- **And a new study panel.** The Department of War announced **Project Meridian** on October 1 — a panel to study the future of warfare, co-led by **Elon Musk, Palmer Luckey and Newt Gingrich**, operating under the department's Chief Technology Officer, Emil Michael.

The pattern is the same one that has re-rated the US primes through 2026: **a defence budget that keeps producing multi-billion-dollar franchise awards**, and an explicit doctrinal tilt toward autonomy, drones and software-defined warfare. Note the asymmetry — the F/A-XX award lifted Boeing and took a visible toll on Northrop Grumman, which is the correct way to trade a sole-source franchise win.
