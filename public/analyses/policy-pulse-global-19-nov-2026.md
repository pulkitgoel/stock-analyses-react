Three things happened on Thursday and only one of them has a price.

Anthropic priced the largest listing on record. The three most powerful men in the world met in a Chinese lakeside compound and published a communiqué. And gold, quietly, kept falling — which is the item that is actually moving Indian balance sheets this week.

## The listing

Anthropic priced at $83.00 a share, above the $70-78 range, on 980 million Class A shares — 956.4 million newly issued and 23.6 million sold by existing holders — for gross proceeds of about $81.3 billion. Add the $26 billion private placement from strategic investors including Google, Nvidia and AMD and the total capital raised is $107.3 billion, ahead of SpaceX's $75 billion in June. Underwriters hold a 30-day option on a further 147 million shares.

At $83 the company is valued somewhere between $1.4 trillion and $1.5 trillion depending on share count and dilution. The book was covered roughly six to seven times. Offshore grey-market derivatives implied a 24-27% first-day pop.

Three facts sit under that valuation and pull in different directions. Revenue for the nine months to September was $33.24 billion, against $2.5 billion for all of 2025 and $41 million in 2023. Third-quarter revenue was $17 billion, up 1,127% year on year — but up 47% quarter on quarter, against 144% in the second quarter and 132% in the first. The growth rate is falling fast in absolute terms even as the base explodes. And the operating loss narrowed to $1.3 billion for the nine months from $5.2 billion a year earlier, which is the number that makes the valuation arguable rather than absurd.

Against that, commitments of close to $900 billion, with roughly $70 billion due within twelve months. That is the whole AI trade in one line item: contracted future spend booked against revenue that is growing but decelerating.

Nasdaq said ANT would be eligible to trade from 10:00 a.m. ET. As of 10:32 a.m. ET it had not printed — the last quote was $83.00, the offer price. Everything reported about the debut so far is an expectation, including the 24% grey-market indication. The Nasdaq Composite was up 45.90 points or 0.19% at that point while the Dow was down 402.56 points or 0.80%, which is what a market looks like when capital rotates into one trade and out of everything else.

## The summit that produced a communiqué

The Trump-Xi bilateral at Kylin Villa ran into the first trilateral summit with Vladimir Putin, about ninety minutes in total. The APEC artefact is the Shenzhen Outlook, a declaration organised around trade, connectivity and innovation, with China pledging $20 million to APEC practical cooperation and a research centre in Shenzhen. Xi put forward four points on how major powers should get along.

What it did not produce, according to Associated Press reporting from Shenzhen, was an agreement to extend the trade truce that expires on 10 January. One Korean outlet reported an agreement in principle; no instrument or joint statement was published, and the two readouts do not name the semiconductor rules that are the actual dispute. The stalemates over chips and rare earths are where they were on Wednesday.

The concrete item is structural: a new Board of Trade intended to shape a 30-for-30 tariff framework, cutting duties on roughly $30 billion of annual imports in each direction. That is the vehicle for whatever happens before 10 January. It is not a decision.

For an Indian portfolio the read is narrow. Nothing in Shenzhen changed the Section 301 architecture that gates the India-US trade agreement, and nothing changed the 9 January expiry of the 178 China exclusions. The calendar got one day shorter and the substance did not move.

## Gold, and the lenders who said it out loud

Gold is trading near $3,700 an ounce, a one-year low, and on MCX around ₹1.29 lakh per 10 grams. Kotak Neo expects ₹1.20 lakh within six months — roughly a 7% further correction — and describes the move as consolidation rather than collapse.

The Indian problem is not the price. It is the book built on the way up. Banks and non-bank lenders nearly doubled gold loan portfolios year on year in the September quarter, and the segment grew 60.6% year on year, the fastest-growing retail credit category in the system. Gold itself had risen almost 80% into the January-February peak, and has since given back about 25% from that high.

The strain is now measurable. CRIF data shows gold loan delinquencies in the 31-180 day bucket at 1.6% in September against 1.3% in June, and the 31-90 day ratio at 1.3% against 0.9%. Two structural changes made it worse: the RBI's cap on bullet repayment gold loans at twelve months has pushed borrowers into monthly servicing, and falling collateral values are forcing top-up margin calls to stay inside regulatory loan-to-value limits.

The lenders' own numbers are the ones to hold. Muthoot Finance's chief operating officer said 25-30% of borrowers are now not servicing monthly, against effectively all of them under the old bullet structure, and that the readjustment needs another two quarters. IIFL Finance's gold loan head said a further 5-10% fall is manageable, and that beyond 15% "panic may set in."

That is not a forecast. It is a stated threshold from the two largest participants in the market, and it is the most useful risk number published in India this week.

## The India spillover

The Nifty IT index fell 1.10% to 30,919.45 while the benchmark rose 1.14%, which is what a hawkish Federal Reserve does to a sector priced off US demand and dollar revenue. Wednesday's minutes said almost all FOMC participants saw further increases as likely appropriate, with a couple open to more than 25 basis points in December.

The offset is flows. Foreign investors have bought a record ₹94,230 crore of Indian equities in the cash market in November, against domestic institutions selling ₹55,570 crore — the foreign bid is buying domestic cyclicals and selling IT, which is the inverse of the July "reverse AI trade."

And the payment intermediaries took a hit on policy timing rather than earnings. Reports that the 0.4% merchant discount rate on UPI transactions above ₹2,000 could be deferred until after assembly elections in five states in the first half of calendar 2027 pushed One 97 Communications down 7% intraday to ₹1,765.05 and Pine Labs down 5.4% to ₹160.05. The MDR was originally scheduled for 15 October, was deferred past Diwali, and now has no date. NPCI notified the UPI Vyapaar Saksham scheme on 11 November to fund QR and soundbox deployment out of MDR collections for three years after implementation — so the subsidy is designed around a revenue stream that has not started.
