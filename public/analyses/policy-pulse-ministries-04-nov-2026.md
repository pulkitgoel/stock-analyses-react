Two regulators moved today, and neither move is about today. SEBI opened a distribution channel that has been administratively closed for years. The RBI built a standing channel to the banks that price the curve. Both are slow-compounding institutional changes, the kind that show up in volumes two quarters later rather than in a price today.

The price today came from somewhere else, and that part is worth being precise about.

## First: SEBI made NRI onboarding digital, effective 3 December

SEBI issued **Circular No. HO/38/30/12(3)2026-MIRSD-POD2/I/25175/2026** today — **"Review of Know Your Client process for individual Person Resident Outside India clients."** It permits **digital KYC, video verification and reusable KYC records** for eligible **NRIs, OCIs and foreign nationals**, with digital onboarding permitted **from 3 December 2026**.

The mechanism matters more than the headline. Until now the practical barrier to an NRI account was not the eligibility test — it was that verification required physical documents and, at points in the recent past, in-country presence. SEBI had already relaxed the in-India geo-tagging requirement for NRI re-KYC. Today's circular closes the loop: a person resident outside India can now be onboarded without being in India.

**Who actually earns from this.** The distribution chain, not the depository. A reopened NRI channel runs through brokers with existing NRI books and through the registrars and transfer agents who maintain the KYC records. This is the third SEBI relaxation in this sequence this year, and the pattern — each one removing one more physical touchpoint — is what makes it worth tracking rather than a one-off.

The honest caveat: **there is no disclosed size to this.** SEBI has not published an estimate of how many incremental accounts the change unlocks, and the number of NRIs who *want* an Indian brokerage account but were blocked by documentation is unknown. Treat the direction as clear and the magnitude as unquantified.

## Second: the RBI built a standing committee on market plumbing

The RBI constituted a **Technical Consultative Committee for Financial Markets (TCCFM)**, chaired by **Deputy Governor Rohit Jain** in charge of the Financial Markets Regulation Department, with **eight external members**, meeting **at least once a quarter**.

The terms of reference are narrower and more operational than a typical RBI committee: the **functioning, efficiency, liquidity and resilience** of the money, government securities, foreign exchange and related derivative markets; measures to deepen and broaden them; issues in **trading, clearing, settlement, reporting and market infrastructure**; and — the line that matters — *"identify unintended market frictions"* arising from the implementation of RBI measures.

The membership is the signal, because it is almost entirely the dealer community rather than academics: **Ananth Narayan** (former SEBI whole-time member, IIT Bombay), **Shamsher Singh** (Chairman of FIMMDA and Deputy Managing Director, SBI), **Neeraj Gambhir** (Axis Bank), **Shailendra Jhingran** (ICICI Bank treasury), **Aditya Bagree** (Citi India markets), **Nand Kishore** (CCIL), and **Ridham Desai** (Morgan Stanley India).

A committee whose stated job includes finding the *unintended frictions* created by RBI's own measures, staffed by the desks that absorb those frictions, is a feedback loop. It is also the body most likely to shape the next round of FX and G-sec market reform — which is directly relevant to a rupee that has spent the year under pressure and to a hedging market the RBI has been actively tightening.

## Fourth: the statistics scheme nobody will trade on, and why it still matters

The Cabinet approved **VISTAR** — the Viksit Bharat Initiative for Statistical Transformation, Analytics & Research — at **₹4,371.10 crore** for FY 2026-27 to FY 2030-31, under the Ministry of Statistics and Programme Implementation.

The content: **59 surveys** during the 16th Finance Commission cycle against 43 previously; two new metrics (**Headline Index of Services Production** and **Index of Unincorporated Sector Enterprises**); end-to-end digitalisation with AI/ML in processing and validation; alternate data sources including mobile phone, scanner and e-commerce data; the **8th Economic Census**; and a **National Statistical Business Register**.

There is no stock to buy here and this article will not pretend otherwise. But the **Headline Index of Services Production** is a genuine new monthly series for a services-dominated economy that currently has no monthly services output measure — and the **SBR** is the missing denominator for every small-cap industrial claim about the unorganised sector. Both raise the quality of the data that every other name in this article gets valued on. That is the whole read.

**The RBI's first TCCFM meeting** — and specifically whether "unintended market frictions" produces anything on the FX hedging rules the RBI has been tightening, which is the live constraint on corporate treasury behaviour.
