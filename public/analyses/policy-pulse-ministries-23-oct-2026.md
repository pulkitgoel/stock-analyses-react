The Reserve Bank of India has now lent money to the banking system three sessions running, and the market keeps asking for three times what is on offer. That is not a signal of comfort. It is a signal that the operating target has broken and is being repaired by hand.

## First: the liquidity surplus is gone, and the VRR is a patch

**The RBI announced a three-day variable rate repo auction of ₹50,000 crore for Friday, October 23**, with a 9:30-10:00 am window and reversal on Monday, October 26 — the tenor spans the weekend.

The auction results this week tell the story:

|---|---|---|---|

| Allotted (₹ crore) | 1,00,014 | 50,007 | 50,006 |
| Weighted average rate | 5.52% | 5.52% | 5.55% |

Three things sit behind those numbers.

**The surplus has collapsed.** Banking-system liquidity surplus was **₹541.95 crore on October 21**, against **₹4,86,904 crore on September 30** and a record **₹11.16 lakh crore on September 6**. The October average is ₹3.39 lakh crore, down from ₹7.77 lakh crore in September.

**The call rate is pinned near the ceiling.** The weighted average call rate has run **5.65-5.69%**, which is 15-19 basis points above the 5.50% repo and within 6-10 basis points of the 5.75% marginal standing facility. On October 21 banks borrowed **₹2,613 crore at the MSF** while parking **₹1,25,597 crore at the standing deposit facility** in the same session. That is not a system short of cash; it is a system where the cash is in the wrong places.

**The drain is structural, not seasonal.** Core liquidity — banking liquidity plus the government's cash surplus — fell to **₹9.4 trillion on October 9 from ₹14.2 trillion on September 4**, and market participants see it near **₹4.9 trillion by December-end** and approaching **₹1 trillion by March**. The components: the FCNR(B) swap window that ran June 8 to August 31 has matured, roughly **₹1.25 lakh crore** has been taken out through open market operation sales of government securities, foreign exchange reserves are down to about **$726 billion from a $780 billion peak in early September**, the daily cash reserve requirement moved to 99% on October 16, GST outflows hit the October window, and the Jio Platforms IPO pulled subscription money out of the system.

The confirming detail is the auction the RBI walked away from: **it rejected every bid at Wednesday's Treasury bill auction**, where ₹23,000 crore was planned across the 91-, 182- and 364-day tenors. The central bank is lending at 5.54% while the market clears overnight money at 5.69%. It is not easing; it is refusing to pay up.

**The read is neutral for valuations, not positive.** A repo that restores the operating target does not lower the cost of capital. A system heading toward deficit into the December quarter is a headwind for anything that funds itself at the short end.

## Second: SEBI removed one recording obligation and kept the rest

**On Friday SEBI issued a circular removing the mandatory requirement for research analysts and research entities to maintain call recordings of their interactions with institutional investors**, effective **November 8, 2026**, following board approval last month.

The obligation is narrowed, not removed. Records of interactions must still be kept for **all** clients, including prospective clients before onboarding, and can take the form of a physical record signed by the client, call recordings, email from a registered ID, SMS records or any other legally verifiable record. Where the interaction already leaves a digital footprint — email, for instance — a recording is not required. Records run from first interaction until the research service ends, must be preserved **five years**, longer if a dispute is open or if SEBI directs. The Research Analysts Administration and Supervisory Body has been told to amend its rules and notify members.

Also worth dating precisely, because it was reported loosely: **SEBI's early pay-in margin waiver circular of October 22 does not take effect on October 22.** The circular comes into force four months after issuance, making the effective date **February 22, 2027**.

## Third: Jio closes at 22 times, with a record application count

**The ₹30,213 crore Jio Platforms IPO closed on Friday subscribed 22.19 times on NSE data** (BSE's final tally, updated at 5:42 pm, showed 20.38 times). The category split from NSE: **QIB 70.55 times, non-institutional 14 times, retail 2.72 times, employees 2.93 times, shareholders 7.60 times.**

The demand was worth **₹4.51 lakh crore** during the public window at the top of the ₹1,065-1,119 band, and roughly **₹7 lakh crore** once the ~₹2.5 lakh crore of anchor demand is added. Applications crossed **1.34 crore**, the first Indian issue above one crore. Grey market premium was ₹254, implying about ₹1,373 against a ₹1,119 issue price, a 22.7% indicated listing gain.

Subscription built through the week — 3.02 times on day one, 5.40 times on day two, 22.19 times at the close. Allotment is October 26 and listing October 28. It is the largest Indian public issue, ahead of Hyundai Motor India's ₹27,856 crore.

One caveat on the read-through: a record IPO is also a liquidity event. Dealers cited the Jio subscription as one of the drains behind this week's call-rate squeeze, and the ₹1.34 crore of blocked retail applications releases only after allotment. The exchange and RTA revenue is real; the funding cost of it is not free.
