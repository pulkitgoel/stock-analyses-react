## Brent Holds Above USD 108 With Hormuz as the Only Route — The Physical Supply Story Is Unresolved

**Brent settled at USD 108.75 a barrel on September 15, up 2.9%, and West Texas Intermediate jumped 4.4% to USD 105.83 — both the highest settlements since May 19, 2026.** Brent traded around **USD 108.34-108.85** through September 16. That is roughly **USD 40 a barrel above the same point last year**, and it is up about **19% in a month** and **78% year-to-date** on Brent front-month.

The driver is not sentiment. **The closure of Saudi Arabia's East-West pipeline — knocked out for several weeks — has removed the bypass route that normally carries crude around the Strait of Hormuz.** With Hormuz effectively the sole channel and flows already constrained by the US-Iran conflict, the market has no spare routing capacity to absorb a further disruption. Attacks on Saudi energy infrastructure continued into this week.

The second-order effects are now visible in the inflation plumbing: **US diesel has traded at record levels** and gasoline has pushed above USD 4.20, which is precisely why the Fed's decision today became a hike rather than a hold.

## The Fed Decides Tonight — A 25bp Hike to 3.75-4.00% Is 94% Priced

**The FOMC announces at 2:00 pm ET on September 16.** Per CME data cited by WSJ, futures price a **94% probability of a quarter-point hike to 3.75-4.00%** — up from 59% a week earlier. It would be the **first US rate increase since 2023**, delivered by Chair **Kevin Warsh**, and the **30-year Treasury has already closed at its highest since June 2004**.

Because the hike itself is fully priced, the entire trade is in the guidance. Capital.com's Daniela Hathorn framed the binary precisely: *"a 25bp Fed hike is now almost fully priced, meaning the bigger catalyst will be whether Kevin Warsh presents it as a one-off recalibration or signals that persistent inflation requires further tightening."* Barron's notes traders see only a **0.9% chance of rates being held steady through December** and **80.9% odds of at least a half-point of hikes this year**.

## The Bank of Japan Meets September 17-18 and Looks Set to Hike to a 31-Year High

**The BoJ is expected to raise rates by 25 basis points to 1.25% at its September 17-18 meeting** — levels unseen in 31 years — according to sources cited by Business Times. A hike just three months after June's move signals a **faster pace of tightening**, and markets will focus on Governor Kazuo Ueda's post-meeting briefing for how high the terminal rate might go.

This matters beyond Japan. A hawkish Fed on Wednesday followed immediately by a hawkish BoJ on Thursday is the exact combination that historically triggers **yen carry-trade unwinds** — forced deleveraging that transmits into global risk assets and emerging-market equities regardless of local fundamentals. Indian equities, already absorbing **roughly USD 2.5 billion of September FPI outflows**, are exposed to that channel.

## India-New Zealand FTA Clears Ratification — In Force October 19

**New Zealand's Parliament passed the implementing legislation 93-29 on September 16**, clearing the final hurdle. The agreement **takes effect October 19, 2026**:

- **95% of New Zealand's exports to India** will be eliminated or significantly reduced in tariff terms; **57% become duty-free from day one**
- **India receives duty-free access to New Zealand for 100% of its exports**
- **New Zealand commits USD 20 billion of investment into India over 15 years**
- **India retained protections for dairy and several agricultural products** — the sensitive-sector carve-out held
- Two-way trade was about **NZ$3.99 billion (USD 2.29 billion)** in the year to June 2026

**Trade-policy context that matters more than the NZ$3.99 billion:** Commerce and Industry Minister **Piyush Goyal** has said India is negotiating FTAs with **8-9 more blocs** and aims to cover **75% of global trade**, with the **EU FTA already moved to Council for approval**, Chile talks focused on balancing critical-minerals access against agricultural protection, and a **G20 Trade Ministerial on September 30 in Milwaukee** where Goyal meets USTR **Jamieson Greer**. The India-US BTA is described by Commerce Secretary Rajesh Agarwal as "more or less finalised," with the last-mile issue being the preferential tariff edge over Vietnam, Bangladesh and Thailand.

## Global Crypto: The Clarity Act's Failure Is Being Priced as a Sector Event

**Bitcoin closed September 15 at USD 75,590, down 3.30%**, after the US Senate failed to advance the Digital Asset Market Clarity Act 49-50. The differentiation is the notable part: **Ether fell 4.66% to USD 2,398**, **Solana 5.50% to USD 96.87**, and **XRP 9.83% to USD 1.2833** — tokens whose value depends most on pending US regulatory classification fell hardest. Total crypto market capitalisation is around **USD 2.6 trillion**, with Bitcoin near USD 76,000 on Wednesday.

The market is treating this as a **setback for a specific regulatory corner**, not a verdict on the asset class, which is why Bitcoin's drawdown was an order of magnitude smaller than XRP's. The industry's fallback is regulatory rather than legislative: **SEC Chair Paul Atkins and CFTC Chair Michael Selig writing market structure through rulemaking.** Atkins said a day before the vote that his proposed crypto-asset regulation answers the central question of "when does a covered investment contract cease to exist."
